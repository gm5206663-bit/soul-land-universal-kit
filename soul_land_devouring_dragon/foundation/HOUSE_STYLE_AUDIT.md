## 2. THE SAMPLES — how published Soul Land fanfiction is written
Read this session (openings and full chapters from six fics across RoyalRoad,
WebNovel and FanFiction.net; Soul Land / Douluo Dalu, the same genre and fandom):

- *Douluo Dalu: Path to Godhood* (FFN) — opens on a shouted line: "You little
  crippled thief! You stole meat from my shop!" Scene, immediately. Plain
  narration follows: "It was still before dawn. Under the blazing torches, the
  city's food market had already begun to stir with noise and activity."
- *Soul Land: Echoes of Douluo* (WebNovel) — a chapter almost entirely dialogue
  and action tags. Grandpa Jack explains the awakening ceremony IN SPEECH, one
  short line at a time. Time skips are one plain sentence: "Spring passed, and
  autumn came, and a year slipped away in the blink of an eye."
- *Soul Land: Douluo Dalu the Game* (RoyalRoad) — opens mid-action: "The rain was
  making a heavy, rhythmic tapping sound. A youth in twenties was running...";
  sound cues in-line ("Swish, swish, swish."); the world explained as the scene
  moves through it.
- *Doulou Dalu: The Power of a Book* (FFN) — training shown in command form
  ("Twenty push-ups, thirty squats, fifty high knees!"), then the awakening
  ceremony as a scene of voices: "Blue Silver Grass? Useless Martial Spirit!"
- *Douluo Dalu: Seraph of Hope and Light* (FFN) — first person, first line is
  waking up in the new body, no preamble at all.
- *Soul Land: Raiden Shogun Is My Martial Soul* (FFN) — political scene with
  titled-rank talk, every paragraph short and spoken or done.

**The conventions, stated plainly (all six, every one):**
1. A chapter opens inside a scene — a body doing something, or a voice speaking.
   Never a thesis, never a summary of the world.
2. Sentences are short and flat: subject, verb, object. The longest common
   sentence in the samples is about twenty words.
3. Dialogue is the default carrier: people state the world's rules out loud, in
   their own voices, with their own wants. Exposition is delivered as speech.
4. Every scene has a named person who wants something in it, immediately.
5. Concrete nouns everywhere; almost no abstractions; no motifs; no recurring
   imagery system. The "prose style" is plainness.
6. Time and place are stated once, bluntly, then the scene moves ("Nuoding city
   was located in the middle of Fasinuo province...").
7. Beats are small and finish: a chase, a meal, a ceremony, a fight. A chapter
   carries several small finished scenes rather than one long meditation.
8. Chapters end on a hook: a decision, an arrival, a threat, a name.

## 3. THE DIAGNOSIS — what our Devouring Dragon does instead
- Sentence average 44.6, longest 250: the opposite of the samples and of every
  other project in the workspace.
- Dialogue 0.8 per thousand words: a reader of Soul Land fiction meets almost no
  voices, so the world never speaks and never feels like the fandom's world.
- One register carried whole chapters (long interior narration); the human side
  existed mainly in panel summaries, and panels were used as the place scenes
  happened instead of being what a chapter occasionally needs.
- Abstraction as the sentence engine ("the ledger", "the word", "the quiet
  thing"): absent from the samples and from the house corpus alike.

## 4. THE TARGETS (binding from this session; checked at every chapter gate)
1. Sentence average per chapter: **≤ 25 words** (house norm 19–30; ours must sit
   in the house). No sentence over 60 words. No paragraph that is one sentence.
2. Dialogue: **≥ 8–10 spoken lines per thousand words in any chapter that has a
   human-side scene**; the human side is written like Soul Land 2 and SOUL LAND
   NEW are written — named people, voices, wants, exposition in speech. A chapter
   with no human presence at all must be justified by its beat and may not be
   followed by another one.
3. Scene law: a chapter opens inside a scene; at least one hard scene per chapter
   runs in real time (moment to moment); each chapter carries two or more small
   finished scenes rather than one long interior movement.
4. Panel law (author: "when needed"): at most one short human-side scene per
   chapter, and only when the beast side cannot carry the beat — but a chapter
   that has a human-side beat at all ALWAYS gets that scene on the page, because
   voices are how this fandom reads. The scene is written at house dialogue
   density, not summarized.
5. Abstraction limit: the motif words may appear as texture, never as the
   sentence engine; the subject of a sentence is a thing, a body, a name.
6. Beats and canon: unchanged — all locked beats, the soul-beast rule (SB-11),
   the ring-slot law (ER-12), the firewalls, the footers with exact figures.
