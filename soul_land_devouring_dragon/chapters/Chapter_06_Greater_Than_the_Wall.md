# Chapter 6: Greater Than the Wall

The second winter came down on the mountain border country the way the first had come, whole and white and without apology — and it did not find the beast it had found the year before, and the difference was everything.

The white had once been a country that ate him; now it was a country he knew how to live in. The snow's whole grammar was his — the morning snow that told everything and the evening snow that told lies, the drift that kept nothing and the crust that kept everything; the talus gave its sleeping stone-runners to his nose the way it had given them the year before, and the cold came against him and found the winter-down beneath his scales and the blood's deep roads, and went away again the way a thing goes away when it has been answered. He did not endure the white this year. He moved in it.

But the wall moved with him, and the wall was closer than it had been in the autumn, because the wall was in him.

The meals of the scarce season went down into him and did not answer the hunger that has no meal, the way they had not answered it in the autumn; the deep sleeps came and paid him their fullness and kept back their growth, and he was growing lean through the winter the way a blade grows lean in the grinding, long and drawn and hungry in a way that had nothing to do with the belly. His country gave him what it had, and what it had was no longer enough for what he was becoming, and the truth of that stood in him the way the wall stood in him, quiet and entire and patient in the way that only the deepest truths are patient.

---

The long storm came in the dead of the white season, the kind the mountain makes when it has a mind to prove itself, and it came down over the ravine country and buried the world.

He lay in the roots-house over the stream while the storm went on over him, day and night of it, the snow piling the mouth of the den and the wind speaking in the trees with a voice like the mountain's own, and the cold stood outside the winter-down like a thing waiting to be let in, and was not let in. The hunger of days came and was paid thinly out of the stores of him and came again, and the hunger that has no meal came with it, and was not paid at all, and stood in the deep place under the scales where the more becomes the blood, and said MORE, and said GREATER, and had no where and no what, and the wall stood behind it the way the sky stands behind the weather.

And it was there, at the bottom of the storm and the bottom of the fasting, with nothing coming in and everything going down into the deep places of him, that the thing that never slept took up the oldest work it had ever been given to do, which was to make the beast fit what the beast had to become.

The drawing had never stopped, because the drawing never stops — the body drawing on the faintness of the world the way roots draw on water, in the sleeps and the stillnesses and the spaces between — but the world above the snow was locked under the storm, and the drawing was thin, and the hunger that has no meal was a fire the thin drawing could not feed. So the pull reached farther. That was the beginning of the crossing, and it did not begin as a triumph but as a necessity, the way everything true in his life had begun: the old deep skill of his blood, the taking that had learned over season after season of kills to obey him, went down for the first time past the flesh it had always fed on, past the air's faintness, into the mountain itself — into the old slow faintness of the stone and the deep ground, the world's more as the mountain keeps it, cold and heavy and slow as the stone it slept in. It was not given. It was reached for, and taken, the way everything of his had been reached for and taken, and the taking was harder than any taking had been, because the mountain does not bleed the way prey bleeds, and what it gives it gives slowly.

The wall felt the reaching. That was the truth of the days that followed, and the truth of them was not a battle, because the wall was not an enemy and had no will; the wall was the edge of what his years could be, and the edge held the way edges hold, and the reaching went on, and the whole of him went on with the reaching. The thing that never slept stood over the crossing the way it had stood over everything since the dark of the egg, and where a layer of him was too weak to survive the becoming, it reinforced it and held it and slowed the becoming until the layer could bear it, and where a layer could bear it, it poured the becoming through; and the crossing was not a moment but a season inside the storm, and the storm went on over the buried country, and the beast in the roots-house lay in the dark of himself and became.

And the wall broke the way walls break when what stands before them has become greater than they are — not with a falling, because there was nothing to fall, but the way the dark breaks when there is suddenly, and all at once, and as if it had never been otherwise, dawn.

---

He came out of the roots-house after the storm into a country of new snow and old light, and he was changed, and the change was not a knowing but a being.

He was larger, and the largeness was not the only thing, and was not the truest thing. His movement was different — the thick heavy body of him moved with the still economy of the snow itself, falling where it fell without waste; his senses stood out from him farther than they had stood, and the world's more came to them richer than it had ever come, the lives of the white country burning clearer in the cold, and far away beyond the ridge the weight of the serpent stood in its own ground and he could read it now the way he had never read it, its years and its appetite and the thread of the dragon in it, clearer than he had ever read any greatness, because the reading of him had grown with the rest of him. The wounds of him healed faster than they had healed — the healing of him had gone up with the rest of him — and the pull, when he woke it in the white stillness to feel what it had become, reached out from him across the snow like a thing unchained, farther and more easily than it had ever reached, because the skill of his blood had gone up with the crossing the way all of him had gone up, and would go on climbing, because it was his.

And the crossing had taken its toll of him in the way crossings do, because the breakthrough of a beast's years is not a rising only — it is a becoming, and the becoming carries its chances with it, and his were taken. The armor of him had set in the storm into the truth of his kind: the scales over him were no longer the scales of the growing but the deep armor of his race, locked down in its full layering, finished and dark as cold water; and out of the locking of it there had come up along the crown of his head and the length of his neck a ridge, low and hard, the first crown of the dragon — the blood in him speaking the first word of what it could become, though no word of it had been spoken to him. And the blood had found its voice in the speaking of the word, because that too is part of such becomings, and the least quiet part: when he stood in the new snow outside the roots-house with all his newness on him, the roar went out of him without being asked, the first true voice he had ever made, not the hatchling's cry but the dragon's voice, low and whole, and it went over the white country carrying the weight of his blood in it, and the white country answered — the small lives of the under-snow went flat where they lay, and the plain warmth cowered in its far thickets, and the silence after was deeper than any silence of the white had been before it. It was short, and it left him shaken on his legs with the cost of it, spent in the breath and the blood, and it did not come again at his calling, but it had been there, and it was his, and the quiet thing kept the shape of it with all the shapes of the day, because the world had heard its first word. And his senses had been given the sense that his kind was made for and had never been given, because nothing is given to his kind for free: the reading of YEARS. The world's more had always carried weight to him; it carried age now. The lives of the white country stood in his new sense with their years upon them — the young ones thin and bright, the old ones heavy and slow, the ancient ones burning low and deep the way the last of a fire burns — and he knew without being taught, the way he knew the jaw's work, that this was the sense his hunger had been reaching for all his life, and that the greater hunger would feed by it from now on.

And the hunger came back, and the hunger was greater, because that was the law of the becoming: the wall that is passed becomes the floor of the next hunger, and the beast that is greater needs the greater meals, and the presence of him stood heavier in the white country than it had stood, and the veiling of it that had grown easy over the autumn took strain again, because what he was weighed more, and the weighing of it was part of the price of what he had become, and the quiet thing took the price the way it took all prices, and began again the work of teaching the weight to be quiet.

The white country read him differently, and it read him true. The small lives of the under-snow went down deeper before him than they had ever gone; the plain warmth gave his trails wide ground that had not been wide ground before; and when he went out over the white in the first days after the storm, the country moved around him the way a country moves around the things that have become part of its law, because he had crossed the edge of his years and the crossing was on him the way the dawn is on the world, and nothing that lived in the white could look on him — in the blood, from before learning — and not know that something had come up out of the storm that had not gone into it.

---

The confirmation of the crossing came the way confirmations come in the forest, which is as a hunt.

There was a long-fang that worked the winter wood above the ravine, an outcast of its kind, lone and grey and scarred, a hunter of hunters that had once been one of the shapes the quiet thing wrote down with the word wide-ground in his first winter. He met it on the snow in the white stillness of the afternoon, and the meeting was not what such meetings had been. The long-fang read him and did not know him — read the hush of him and the shape of him and found in them a beast it could measure, because the veiling held and the measure it found was the measure the quiet thing let it find — and it came, and the coming was the old coming of the hunters of hunters, low and sure and without hurry, and he met it the way he met it — and the voice of his blood went out of him once, short and whole, and the long-fang faltered in the air of its own spring, because the low lives hear the dragon's voice in the blood, and the falter was an instant, and the instant was his, and it was enough.

It was over quickly, because the wall was behind him and not before him now. The long-fang's speed was the speed he had once given wide ground, and it was not enough, because the movement of him had gone up with the crossing; its bite was the bite that had once been a word for fear, and it broke on the armor that the storm had locked into the truth of his kind, and the hold of his jaw closed on it the way the winter closes on the mountain, and the red thing rose to its due and was paid its due out of the payment, and the quiet thing kept its way, and the snow received the long-fang the way the snow receives everything, and said nothing.

He ate of it in the white stillness, and the eating was the eating of the new floor of him, and it answered the greater hunger the way the lesser meals no longer answered it, because what he had become ate what he had become, and the loop of his blood went on turning in the cold clean air, wider than it had turned before the storm.

---

There was one more thing the winter showed him after the crossing, and it showed it to him on the high rocks above the ravine's mouth, in the cold clear evening, the way the winter shows everything, completely, and without apology.

He stood above the white country looking down at the deep pass trail in the last of the light, and the snow had buried the snare of the upright things, and the snare waited under the snow the way it had waited above it, because iron can wait, and the shape of it stood in the deep place with all the kept shapes, and he looked at it now with the reading the crossing had given him, and the reading was new. The upright things were the takers of the years; he had known it since the green season; but the years that they took were the years of the beasts, and he had crossed the edge of his years in the dark of the storm, and the crossing was his — and the knowing of what he had, that no upright thing could take out of him except with his whole life, came into him on the high rocks in the cold clear light, and went down into the deep place with all the kept shapes, and made them heavier, and steadier, and more his.

The lodges were silent in the dead of the white; the passes held nothing but the snow; the serpent's weight slept beyond the ridge, and he read it in its sleeping the way he had never read it, and the reading was part of the dawn that was on him, and the dawn did not end, because the dawn of a beast's years is not a day but a direction. Beyond the wall he had passed there were walls he had not met, and the walls stood at the edges of the years the way the sky stands at the edge of the mountain, and his blood had never in the history of its kind been taught to stop at a wall, and the thing that had never slept stood in him with all its work before it, and the white country lay under the stars, and the years of him went on growing in the deep, the way his years had always grown — without announcement, and needing none, and now, for the first time, with the dawn on them.
