# Chapter 8: The Deep Country

The deciding came the way all his decidings came, which is to say it did not come at all — the shape simply ripened until the beast was doing what the shape had always said.

The spring deepened into the first heat of his third summer, and the outer country went on thinning under him the way the truth had said it would: the bright thin lives were bread and would always be bread, and the heavy ones were few, and the reading of the years had long since counted them, and the counting did not change by the wishing. The sense of years read the depths beyond the ridge in the still evenings the way it had read them all spring, and the depths did not move, because depths do not move, and the need in him grew the way the wall had grown, quiet and entire, until the day the need became the whole of him, and the whole of him stood at the mouth of the ravine den over the stream, and looked back at the country that had made him.

The looking was not long, because there was no length in him for the looking — but the hollow of the fallen giant stood in the quiet thing's keeping, and the stream's cold talk stood in it, and the high rocks above the ravine's mouth where the white country had been met and the wall had been passed, and the broken pine away down on the old slope beyond the shoulder, where the beaks had once been one of the mountain's words for not-yet. The country that had made him was kept, the way everything was kept, whole and past argument, and then he turned his head to the ridge, because the shape said ridge, and the beast followed the shape the way the beast had always followed the shape, which was completely.

---

The Black-Ravine Serpent's ground was crossed the way a river is crossed in the flood — by patience, and by the high places, and by the knowing of when the water sleeps.

He had read the Black-Ravine Serpent all spring, and the reading had given him what the reading gives to those who pay for it, which is the truth of the thing read: the weight beyond the ridge was not a weight that moved through all its ground at all hours, because even the ancient ones have their stillness, and the stillness of the Black-Ravine Serpent came in the deep hours of the day's heat, when the weight settled down into its own deep place and thinned at the edges of its ground like a fire burning low. He went in the stillness, then, and he went by the rim of the Black-Ravine Serpent's ground and not the heart of it, along the high ridge country where the ground broke and the wind came up out of the gorges, because the rim was the place where the Black-Ravine Serpent's ground ended and the weight of it was least, and the veiling of his blood was held over him the way the winter had taught it to be held, whole and strained and honest, and the crossing was slow.

It took the whole of the stillness and part of the next, and in the middle of the crossing, on the bare stone of the rim where there was no cover and no argument, the weight beyond the ridge moved.

He knew it the way he knew everything true, in the blood before the mind: the thinning of the stillness, the gathering of the depth, the Black-Ravine Serpent stirring in its own ground the way the mountain stirs before the storm. He did not run, because the running was death, and he did not hide, because there was nothing to hide in, and he did what the quiet thing had been teaching him all his life to do in the presence of the greatness that could end him, which was to become nothing — the veiling of the blood went down to its last shadow, the body went down to the stone, the breath went down to nothing, and the sense of years gave him the one gift it had ever given him that mattered more than the reading of prey, which was the reading of the greatness above him, and he read the Black-Ravine Serpent's stirring the way he had read the wall, completely, and without looking away.

The stirring passed. The depth settled back into its own stillness the way the water settles after the stone has gone through it, and the dragon-thread in it sang its one thin note to the dragon in him and was quiet, and he lay on the bare stone of the rim with his heart going the way it had never gone, and the quiet thing wrote the shape of it down with all the other shapes that mattered most, which was this: the greatness above him was not malice and was not mercy, and the crossing of its ground was paid for in the only currency it accepted, which was the whole of the care he had.

---

The deep country took him in the way the deep country takes everything, which is without noticing.

The trees of it were the trees that the outer forest is the shadow of — giants past the giants of his own country, their trunks like the walls of the world, their crowns lost in a green haze that had its own weather under the sky, and the ground under him was the forest's own body gone deeper and older than anything he had walked, and the light came down through the canopy of the deep country green and broken and heavy with the more, because the more stood thick in the deep country the way water stands in the deep wells of the world. His sense of years came up against the deep country the way the nose comes up against the wind — and reeled. The lives of it stood on every side of him with their years upon them, and the years were DEEP, and among the depths there were depths he could not read at all, greatnesses that stood in his sense not as shapes but as the ABSENCE of reading, the way the sky stands in the reading of the water, and the sense did what sense does at the edge of what it cannot hold, which is to bow, and the quiet thing kept the bowing without shame, because the map of the world had room in it again, and the room was the whole of the deep country.

He was small in the deep country the way he had been small on the scree slope in the year of his hatching, and the smallness was not the same smallness, because he was not the same beast — but it was smallness, and the deep country taught him the teaching again, the way the mountain had taught it first: eat beneath you, and fear above you, and hold your ground when the ground can be held, and give the wide ground when it cannot.

The beasts of the deep country read him, and the reading was a thing apart from anything the outer country had shown him. The lesser ones of the deep went down before him the way the lesser ones of the outer country went down, because the weight of his blood was the weight of his blood in all countries, and the first crown of the dragon stood on him, and the low lives read the crown in the blood from before learning — but among them there were lives that did not bow, and the not-bowing came in kinds. There were the ones that read him and went still with the stillness of the hunted, and gave him the ground they did not want to give, because his blood spoke a word their blood remembered though they had never heard it; and there were the ones that read him and did not go still, and stood, and answered the weight of his blood with a weight of their own, and the quiet thing gave those the wide ground the way it gave all greatness the wide ground, because the crown of the dragon is a word, and the deep country had its own words, and some of the words were older and heavier than his.

---

The first hunting of the deep country was a hunting of the fringe of it, and the fringe of the deep country was deeper than the heart of his own country had ever been.

He read for the heavy ones the way the sense had taught him, and the heavy ones of the deep fringe stood in his reading the way the old stag-kind had stood in the high fern country, only MORE so — the years on them such as he had never taken, the faintness in their flesh thick and slow and deep, and the hunger that had brought him across the Black-Ravine Serpent's ground rose in him at the reading of them the way the hunger had never risen, because the hunger had found what it was for. He chose with the whole of the care he had, because the choosing was all he had: not the greatest of the heavy ones, and not the least, but one of them alone in a break of the giants, and old in the way that brings the end of beasts near without bringing it upon them, and unaware.

The fight was the hardest of his life, because the fights of his life had never met what they met here. The beast of the deep fringe was not ancient, but it was old, and its oldness was a wealth the outer country had never shown him — hide that the pull of his jaw had to find its way through, and strength that took the first spring of him and stood up out of it, and a slowness that was not dullness but the patience of the years. He took it the way everything of his had been taken, by the whole of what he was — the veiling that brought him near, the spring that the new movement had made his, the voice of his blood that went out of him once and bought him the falter, the jaw that found its hold at the end of the finding and held the hold the way the winter had taught him to hold, and the fight went down into the ground of the deep country with the both of them, and came up out of it with the one of them still standing, and the cost of the coming up stood on him in the torn shoulder and the opened flank, and the cost was paid the way all his costs had been paid, which is once, and in full, and never forgotten how.

The devouring of it was the devouring he had crossed into the deep country for, and the truth of it was greater than the reading of it, because the years of the deep beasts are not the years of the outer country, and the taking went down into them the way the roots go down into the deep wells, and drew, and kept drawing, and the deep sleep came down on him in the green shadow of the giants and took him under the way it had never taken him, and while he was under, the blood of him drank.

That was the teaching of the deep country's first meal, and the teaching was the spine of everything his life had been building toward, and he knew it the way his blood knew it, which is completely: the years he had taken went down into him and were sorted the way the quiet thing sorted everything, without ever being asked — the lesser of what he had taken burned away into the body and the strength and the instinct, and what was left, the deep and the true of it, went down into the blood of the dragon in him, and the blood drank it, and was more dragon for the drinking. The purification that had been going on in him since the dark of the egg, kill by kill and sleep by sleep, had found its true country at last, because the outer country had given him bread and the deep country gave him the years that the blood could drink, and the scales over him came out of that sleep darker and deeper than they had gone in, and the crown of him stood a shade higher, and the loop of his blood turned wider than it had ever turned, and the deep country had answered the need the way the deep country answers everything true, which is completely, and in exchange for the whole of the care.

---

There were more teachings the deep country gave him over that first hunting, and it gave them in the way the deep country gives, which is without apology.

The first was that the kills of the deep country are not kept the way the kills of the outer country are kept. The blood of the deep beasts stands in the air of the deep country the way his own blood stood in the blood of the low lives, and the smell of the years' blood goes where the smell goes, and the deep country has lives that follow such smells, and he had eaten fast and gone from the break of the giants before the following came, and the following came anyway, and he read it from the high ground where the ravine of his new hollow ran — a moving of the deep country toward the place of his kill, lives in his reading that were heavier than the life he had taken, and the quiet thing wrote the teaching down without complaint: in the deep country, the kill is a word, and the word is heard, and the beast that speaks it must be gone from the speaking before the answering comes.

The second teaching came on the wind, at the last of the day, from down-country of the giants — the spirit hounds' voices thin and far, and the flat hard voices of the upright things with them, in the deep country, and the reading of it wrote the line under the line the spring had written, because the truth of it was the truth the spring had said, only finished: the year-takers had come into the deep country for the same years he had come for, and the deep country was wide, and the years of it were more than the taking of any one hunger, and the sharing of it was still the race, and the race had no end in the reading, and the quiet thing kept the shape of it with all the shapes of the deep country, and began the work of learning the new country the way it had begun the work of learning every country, which is from nothing, and by the ledger of everything.

---

He made his hollow in the deep country the way he had made all his hollows — roots and ground and the shape of water nearby, a fold where a giant had fallen across a ravine of the deep forest an age ago, and the ravine had a stream at its floor that came out of the depths cold and unhurried, and the hollow had a way out that was not the way in, because the winter of his first year had written that law into him in the language of the taught, and the law was kept in all countries.

The bloodthirst came for him there that night the way it came with all fullness, and it was the strongest it had ever been, because the bloodthirst grows as the beast grows, and the years' blood of the deep feast was in him like a fire in the deep place, and the red thing rose and said THERE, and THERE, and AGAIN, and the quiet thing stood against it the way it had always stood, with the whole of his life in its keeping — the winter and the wall and the storm and the crossing and the care, all of it, standing in the quiet thing's place — and the red thing raged and ran down the way the fire runs down when it finds nothing it can eat, and the rein held, and the holding was harder than the holding had ever been, and the rein was his, and the night went on around the hollow of the deep country, and the forest of the giants breathed slow over him, and the Black-Ravine Serpent's weight slept beyond the ridge he had crossed, and the years of the deep country stood in the dark on every side of him, deep past the deepness of his reading, and he lay in the new hollow with the stream talking at the floor of the world, and the sleep took him deep, and the deep country did not keep him yet, because the keeping is earned, and the earning had only begun.
