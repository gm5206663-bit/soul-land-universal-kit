# Chapter 5: The Hunger That Has No Meal

The green world turned the way it had turned before he was born into it and would turn after he was gone out of it, without asking the leave of anything that lived in it. The dark green went pale at the edges; the canopy opened piece by piece to let the sky back in; the cold came down out of the high country at night and went up again in the mornings, farther down each day; and the forest set about its great work of the season, which is fattening everything that can be fattened and hiding everything that cannot.

His second autumn was not his first. That was the difference, and the difference was everything, because his first autumn had been a season endured, and this one was a season met. He knew the country now the way the country knew itself — the fat trails of the fattening prey, the last sweetness of the seep-water, the places where the warmth-with-more gathered before the cold and the places where it scattered; he knew which breaks would hold a meal through the mist mornings and which would hold nothing but the fog; he had his den and his trails and his hours, and the forest had him, and the having was mutual the way it is between a country and the beasts that belong to it. The web of the green world gave to him that autumn the way it gives to anything that has earned its place in the weaving, and he took, and took well, and the taking was quiet and clean and full of the skill of him.

He was not small in his country anymore, and his country had begun to know it.

---

The weight of his blood had grown with his growth, the way everything in him grew, and the growing of it had changed the hunting the way the winter's cold had once changed the digging.

The small lives of the undergrowth went still before him now the way grass goes still before the wind, and farther out than before, and more wholly; the plain warmth scattered at his coming where it had once scattered only at his spring; and even the warmth-with-more, the true people of the forest, had begun to read him before he read them, a wrongness in the air of their own country that said the great-mouthed thing is abroad, and was gone to ground while he was still far. The forest lowered its voice before him, and the lowering was his by blood, and the blood was proud of nothing and knew nothing of pride, and the fact of it was simply this: the hunter's own greatness had begun to warn his dinner.

The thing that never slept took up the problem the way it took up everything, without being asked, and the way it solved the problem was the way it solved everything — by degrees that were not noticed until they were a power. There came a morning in the mist when he was stalking a fern-deer of the green breaks and he felt the small lives of the break going still before him in their long slow wave, and something in the quiet thing's deep place reached down into the weight of his blood and HELD it, the way a beast holds its breath, and the wave did not go out ahead of him, and the fern-deer kept its rooting and its small sureness until the spring ended it. He did not know what he had done. He knew only that the meal had come easier than it should have, and the quiet thing kept the shape of the easier, the way it kept all shapes, for the doing of it again.

The doing of it again was slow, because the hushing of the blood is not a thing the blood loves. It came when his whole attention was given to the stalking, and failed when the attention was a storm; it held over the smallest lives and broke before the greater; when the red thing was up in him it was as if it had never been, because the red thing is the blood's own voice and does not consent to being quieted. Over the autumn he learned it the way he had learned the snow and the waiting and the pull — by the ledger of the eaten and the lost, by the body keeping what the body was paid for keeping. By the time the cold came down to stay, he could walk his own trails with his weight hushed to a shadow of itself, and the forest did not lower its voice before him when he chose, and the choice was his, and that was the first piece of the mastery of himself, though he had no word for mastery and would not have used it if he had.

---

The wall came into his life the way walls come into lives, without arriving.

It began as a slowness. The meals went down into him the way they had always gone down, but the becoming of them was less than it had been; the deep sleeps that had always paid him back in growth paid him back in fullness more and in growth less, and the difference was small at first and then was not small, and the quiet thing kept the shape of the difference the way it kept all shapes, without knowing what it kept. The faintness of the air and of the ground, the world's more that his body drew the way roots draw water, came to him thinner than it had come — or he had grown past what his country could give him, and the truth was the second truth, because the world had not changed and he had. His body had come to the edge of what the small meals and the thin drawing could build, and the edge was not a place and did not have a face, and he could not see it, and he felt it the way his kind feels everything true, which is as a hunger.

It was not the hunger of days, and it was not the hunger of the deep white he had survived in his first year, and it was not the red thing. It was a hunger that had no meal — a want in the deep place under the scales where the faintness becomes the blood, that said MORE and could not say where, that said GREATER and could not say what. The fern-deer and its kindred no longer answered it; the plain warmth had never answered it; even the great eatings of the autumn's fat left it where it was, underneath the fullness, quiet and entire and patient in the way that only the deepest hungers are patient. He stood at the edge of his country in the cold mornings with the whole of his world laid out in the mist beneath him, and the hunger that had no meal stood with him, and his country, which had been the whole of the world to the hatchling, had begun to be what it was, which is a country, and not the whole of anything.

The wall had no name in him. It had no face. But it was there, the way the sky was there, and the quiet thing had written its shape in the only letters it had, which were the shapes of everything else: the meals that filled and did not answer; the sleeps that rested and did not grow; the world grown thin where the world had been thick. Somewhere beneath all the shapes was the truth of his kind, which is that the beast grows until its years harden at the edge of what its years may be, and grows again only by passing the hardening, and the passing is not given. He did not know this in the knowing of words. He knew it in the knowing of the wall, which is the only knowing walls have: by standing before one.

---

The forest, which refuses nothing honestly, gave him the answer the forest gives to the hungers of autumn, which is the culling of the old.

It was a stag-kind of the high fern country, gone ancient in the way of the forest's ancients — the years heavy on it, the weight of uncounted seasons in its horned head, sickness in the deep of it where the sickness cannot be argued with, alone because the old of the forest go alone at the end. It carried the more in its flesh the way the deep vessels carry water, and the reading of it came to him across a whole valley in the cold dawn, and the reading said great and said fading and said ALONE, and the hunger that had no meal woke in him at the reading like a thing woken by its own name.

He went up into the high fern country after it, and the going took the whole of the day and all of the hushing he had learned, because the high country was not his country and knew it, and he came to the stag-kind at the last of the light where it stood in a storm-break with the winter in its breathing, and the quiet thing looked at it, and the hunger looked at it, and they agreed for the first time in the whole ledger of his life, and the agreement was this: now.

The fight was the hardest of his life and was honest to the end of it. The old thing met him with the last wealth of its years — the horns came down out of the cold air with the weight of all the seasons in them and opened his shoulder to the bone, and the hooves drove him back across the storm-break when his hold slipped, and the red thing rose to make the fight its own and the quiet thing held the jaw to its work instead, and the work was long, and the work was paid for, and the end of the work was the hold found and kept at the great throat, the old heart going down beneath him, the long breath of the ancient beast going out into the cold that had come to meet it, and the forest standing silent around the passing the way the forest stands for its own.

The eating of it was the deepest devouring of his life, and the depth had a weight in it that no eating had ever had. The years in the old flesh were not the bright thin faintness of the fern-deer; they were heavy and slow and full, years of the forest itself, and the pull strained at them the way a jaw strains at what it was not built for, and took them slowly, and kept taking, and the taking went on into the night under the cold stars with the body of the ancient beast going down into him the way all things go down into their end, which is into what comes after. When the deep sleep came down on him at last in the storm-break, it was the longest sleep of his life, and the snow of the first cold nights came and lay over him while he slept, and did not wake him, and the forest kept him the way the forest keeps its own through their passings, and asked nothing of him while he was under.

He woke into the cold country grown larger in his scales and heavier in his bones and changed in the deep place where the more becomes the blood — changed toward what the wall is passed by, and not yet across it. The great meal had fed the hunger that has no meal, and the hunger had quieted the way such hungers quiet, which is not into silence but into waiting, because the wall of a beast's years is not passed by one meal however great, but by the whole of the beast becoming greater than the wall, and the becoming was going on, and was not finished, and he knew it in the only knowing he had of it, which was that the hunger was still there, under the fullness, patient, entire, at the foot of the wall.

---

The cold season set in over the mountain border country the way it had set in the year before, and he met it from the ravine den over the stream, with his stores of knowledge in him and his scars on him and the second winter coming at him across the white.

But before the white came wholly, the deep passes gave him the season's last new shape.

He was working the shoulder above the old pass trail in the first hard frosts, where the wind comes down from the country of men, when the reading of it stopped him at the edge of a thicket — a wrongness, small and cold and patient, standing in the run of a trail that the warmth-with-more uses. He read it from outside the thicket the way the quiet thing had learned to read the wrongness, with the whole of the winter and the whole of the green behind the reading, and the reading said: the cold shining death, but changed, because the cold shining death had always until now been a thing that moves in the hands of the upright ones, and this was a piece of it LEFT, teeth of iron folded into the shape of a waiting jaw, baited with the smallness that draws the greater, anchored to the living wood of the forest with the patience of the things that set it. It did not move. It did not smell of anything but iron and the gone-ness of the hands that made it. It waited in the trail the way the wall waits at the edge of a beast's years, without hunger and without malice and without end, until what it waits for comes.

He did not go near it. The quiet thing wrote the shape of it down in the deep place with all the other kept shapes, and the writing said: iron can wait; and under it, the older writing that the ring had written in the green season: the upright ones do not only kill — they take the years, and now the knowing of it was finished into its fullness, because now he knew they could take the years without being there at all. He gave the pass trail its wide ground from that day, the way he gave the black ravine country its wide ground, and the wide ground was not fear and was not flight; it was the map of his world being kept true.

Beyond the shoulder of the mountain, in the cold clear evenings of the setting-in, the lodges breathed their thin smoke again, tended and small against the white country, and he looked at them from the high rocks above the ravine's mouth the way he had learned to look, without the red thing's pull and without the flatness of fear, with the whole of his years standing behind his eyes. The serpent's weight slept in the black ravine country behind the ridge, and his blood marked it the way it had learned to mark it, quietly, from a distance, and the marking was still not finished, and the truth of that lived in him the way all long truths live.

The second cold season lay down over the mountain border country, and the beast that had hatched on the cold scree of a hollow in the mountain a year and more ago stood at the mouth of his ravine den and looked out over the white, and the white did not terrify him and did not welcome him and did not know him except as its own, because the country keeps what survives it and asks nothing more. He was hungry in the honest way of the season, and armored in the way of his kind, and alone in the way that had been given him, and the hunger that has no meal stood in him at the foot of its wall, patient and entire.

The wall had no meal. But the wall had a direction, and the direction was UP, and the beast that stood before it had the whole of the winter to grow toward it, and the whole of the forest to grow in, and a blood in him that had never in the history of its kind been taught to stop at a wall.
