# Chapter 11: The War of the Lists

The first green came up out of the ground, not down out of the sky.

The deep country's thaw ran on the ground's word: the dark water under the stone warmed before the air knew winter was over, the roots loosened in the dark, the shadow of the standing stone thinned a hand's width over the season, and the sky above the giants came green at last, late, and with some show of indifference. The outer country's ledger of the thaw — the sky's green, the birds' word, the grass's hour — failed in the deep, as the outer country's ledger of the cold had failed: the deep country keeps its own weather, and the quiet thing took the deep green down as its own country's green, with its own hours, and filed the outer green under it as the guess it had always been.

The heavy kind moved on the ground's word, not the sky's. The warm breaks filled first with the thick of them, the fern runs ran low with the bright thin ones passing up out of the outer country's last green, and the ledger's pages for the green season stood written before the green was on the sky — the deep country's green was a season that had already started, and had already started taking.

The smoke-line of the winter place stood no more.

He read the going-out on the first still morning: the fires out, the breaks swept clean of the season's bone-ash, the dogs' line gone down-country of the giants with the upright shapes, the whole place given back to the giants' shadow in a day. The map's line — the line it had never held, the line of the things that stayed — was rewritten in the first green: they stayed, and they went, and the going was new. The things that came and gone had gone to their fires at the day's end; these went with the season, and the going of them read in him as the going of readers, because a hunting line that goes into the green of a country it has read goes to work, and work does not go home. Smoke would stand again somewhere down-country of the giants, and the map kept the place of it as the quiet thing keeps a thing it has not met — held at the edge, the way the ledger keeps a line it has not spent. And the winter's last line stood written under the smoke-line, where the shape of the letting-go stands: the race had a winter, and the race had a spring, and the spring's line of taking would stand in the ledger against the winter's line of taken, and would not come far behind.

---

The broad-antler of the high break entered the ledger as the kept-for thing: its hours on the warm breaks, its ground under the standing stone, the heavy years of its kind read by the sense of years and read again, the reading held in the deep place whole, and waiting, and costing — the patience of the marked days, the patience the grey stag's days had paid for and the grey stag's ground had not kept.

The green season's red thing stood in him from the first taking-week, and it stood in the new shape the cold months had left it. The red thing of the fullness had its summer coming — the strength that would boil over the top of the deep meat — and behind it, in the same blood, stood the red thing of the want, sharpened to a needle through the lean weeks, and the green was the season of the taking, and the taking was the one word the want had been waiting for all the cold months, and it stood behind the eyes at every marked hour and asked the question the cold had left it, the one-syllable question, and the choosing answered the way the choosing had answered since the grey stag — not with the holding back, but with the holding to. The want stood sharp in him through the whole of the marked days, kept to the line, the edge and not the rider, and the ledger held the account plain: the want had been carried through the waiting, and the waiting had not been spent.

At the marked hour on the marked ground he went in — and the going-in found the ground already read in other hands.

The blood stood warm under the breaks. The shape of the turn-out stood whole in the reading of the place. The upright shapes and the dogs' shapes stood fresh in the first green light, and the ring-light of the broad-antler — the pale gold, the pale gold itself, nothing darker — stood in the air above the blood a moment and went down. They had been there before him, at the hour he had kept for the taking, and the second line of the war stood taken in other hands, and the quiet thing stood over the broad-antler's blood with the cold coming off the ground and the green coming up through it, and did the thing it had not done in the deep place since the turn.

It rewrote the shape of the list.

The list had been his. The list was his, and it was not his alone, and the two truths stood in the deep place against each other. The first kind of page — the kept-for, the days and the hours and the ground, the faith in the ground — had been a faith the grey stag's ground had spent, and the broad-antler's ground had spent again in the same green morning, and the patience of the kept-for stood in the ledger rewritten as a patience of a different kind: the patience of the choosing. The marking went on; the taking came at the hour the map of their going gave; and the list learned its second kind of page, the page of the line that could be lost, and the taking that was still taken for all the losing — the first line taken darker than pale gold, the second line pale gold, and the list that took them counting the line and not the weight of it, which was the line the quiet thing kept under both takings, where the shape of the letting-go stands, because the line belonged there: the war did not care how heavy the line was, and the war was going to have to learn, line by line, what the list in the blood was made of.

---

**AT THE WINTER PLACE, AT THE STRIKING**

Bren stood in the last ash of the winter place with the list in his hand — a sheaf of paper, the ink of it gone thin where a man had read it a thousand times — and the striking of the camp read down the line of the giants the way a line of men is taken off a field, one after the other, without hurry.

"The deep keeps its own green, and the list is deep," he said, to no one in particular, in the voice of a man settling an account. "Strike it. Two days' march down, past the giants."

"The dogs liked the cold," said Yorre, from the pack-line, the dogs' collars in his hands. "I didn't."

"Then carry less."

The grey stag's ring stood in its keeping on Bren's person, and the keeping of it was the keeping of the season — purple, the darker light, the one that had stood over the blood at the turn of the year. He turned it once in the light and put it away, and the turning was not a deciding; the deciding had been done at the blood.

"Purple buys a border's winter," he said. "That one buys two."

Sella came up from the last fire with soot on her hands and the look she got when the country had shown her something and she had not yet found the shape for it.

"I want the list to carry one more line before we move," she said.

Bren looked at her.

"The deep kills," she said. "You saw the grey stag's ground. You saw the others through the winter. The kills in the deep country are left whole. The flesh, the antler, the body standing where the hunt was, the years taken out of it and nothing else touched. Winter does that to the small kind. This doesn't. This leaves a mark on every kill, the same mark, the same shape, like a name signed."

"The heavy kind die standing when they're struck right," said Yorre, without looking up from the collars.

"When they're struck right, the iron's in them," said Sella. "There's no iron in these. There's just the mark. The whole body, and the mark."

The fire's last coal moved under the ash. Bren stood with the list in his hand and the season in his shoulders, and the two of them looked at him, and in the looking wanted nothing, which was the country's looking at a man who kept a list.

"Then it's a name we don't have yet," he said at last. "And everything in the deep country gets a name, one way or another, before the season's done." He folded the list, and the folding was the decision. "When the list says so, it goes on the list. The list doesn't care what makes the mark. The list cares about years."

"The old ones say the deep country has its own keepers," said Yorre, at last, in the voice of a man saying a thing he did not believe, for the record.

"The list doesn't care about keepers." Bren shouldered his line. "The list cares about years. Two days' march down, past the giants. We make the green ours before the heavy kind learn to avoid it."

They went down-country of the giants with the dogs between them and the light that eats wood on their shoulders, and the winter place stood in the giants' shadow given back to the season, and the smoke of it, the last of the smoke, went up through the grey canopy thin and straight one last time, and stood, and went down.

---

The map of their going did its first green work.

He had kept it through the cold — the going-down and the coming-up of their line, the hours of them against the season, the dogs' voices carried further in the cold air and carried further still in the green — and the keeping of it came back to him now as a window: a window of hours, wide as their two days' march, the width of their going, and in the window the thick-paw of the green run stood on his own list, marked with the patience the winter's losing had taught.

He took the thick-paw at the window's middle, at the hour their line was deep and the dogs' voices were a day down-country of the giants, and the taking came on the ground and the hour and the mark, and the green run gave him the fight it had to give and no more.

The green was wide, and the wide green was where the heavier presence lived — the crossing's weight, grown through the deep meals, the weight the winter's lean frame had let him carry on the hush. In the green, on the open run, with the heavy kind moving all around him in the thick of their season, the veiling took its green-season strain at the moment it mattered: the hush thinned, and the low lives of the run felt the weight come up out of the ground the way the cold had come up, and two of them turned their heads at the passing of him before the turning of it cost him the stillness — and the stillness cost him the time, and the time cost him the thick-paw's last stride, the stride that put it into the run's deep end, and the jaw-work held the rest the way the jaw-work always held, because the jaw's work is the line the choosing is made of, and the line did not give.

The voice did not go out. The taking came on the ground and the hour and the jaw, and the voice stood in him all the way unspent, and the quiet thing wrote the line under the voice's lines where the winter's price stands: the winter had bought a summer's silence, and the silence, kept, was worth more than the calling spent.

The meal of the thick-paw was the green season's first deep meal, and the deep meal gave the red thing of the fullness its summer back — the strength boiling over the top of the meat, the old red, the red that rode in the running and asked to be given the head. And behind it, in the same meal, stood the red thing of the want, the want the cold months had sharpened to a needle and the green season had put meat back in, and the two reds stood in him at the same hour — the boil and the whisper, the one that charged and the one that waited — and the choosing stood against both, and found the standing against both was the same standing, because the choosing was never against the red thing; the choosing was against the choosing's own being taken, and it was not taken, and the meal was the meal, and the taking was the taking, and the ledger held the difference the way the ledger holds everything: the two words of the red thing named at last as one thing, and the line the choosing stood on written in the deepest place it writes, where the shape of the letting-go stands, because the line belonged there.

At the meal's end he did the deep country's craft: the rest of the kill left as a word of its own, the body set, the word placed, the answering called to where he was not — the word of the kill his to speak, not only his to fear.

---

The green deepened into the deep country, from below, first, and the map's last layer held in the green the way it had held in the cold: the standings of the greatnesses fixed in the deep place, the walking of the green the walking between them, the small life walking the country of the sleeping greatnesses without haste and with the whole of the care, and one of the depths standing where it stood, and the distance of it the distance of a thing that does not move.

The thread beyond the ridge sang in the green air. It had sung in the cold; the green changed nothing in the song, which was the line the quiet thing kept for it — the song did not season, the song did not age, the song went up the road the road was going, and the road had no end in the reading, and nothing in the green asked the song to be more than it was, and the keeping of the song was the letting-go of it.

The wall at the thousand stood where it had stood: far, unaged, unfelt — a direction in the deep place the way the ridge was a direction in the map, and the season came and went around it, and the coming and the going were the season's and not its, and it stood.

In the deepest place the two lists stood against each other in the green: his list, kept in his blood, with the broad-antler's line struck off by other hands and the thick-paw's line taken in his, and the other list, kept in hands he had never seen, with lines he would never know, written over the same breaks, the same heavy kind, the same hours of the green — and the war of the lists running its green season: not to an end, but to the next taken line, and the next, and the next.

He stood at the edge of the green run with the word of his taking left in the grass, and the green deepened around him, and the two lists in his blood went on being written — his line by his jaw, the other line by hands he had never seen — and the season asked its question over the whole of the deep country's green, out loud and without a voice: the next line, and the next, and which of the two lists would take it.
