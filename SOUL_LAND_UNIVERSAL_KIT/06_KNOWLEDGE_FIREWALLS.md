# 06 — KNOWLEDGE FIREWALLS

Who knows what. The single most common continuity break in fan fiction is a character
behaving as if they know something they haven't learned.

**Standing rule: old trust stays real. Private secrets stay protected. Public consequences
must breathe.**

---

## 1. THE FOUR LAYERS

Every piece of information in your serial sits in one of these:

```
1. HARD TRUTH        — verified canon, universe rules, explicit user decisions,
                       established on-page facts
2. APPROVED DIVERGENCE — user-approved changes from source
3. STORY STATE       — everything that has happened on the page
4. CHARACTER KNOWLEDGE — what each person knows, believes, misreads, or has never heard
```

Layer 4 is the one that breaks. Layers 1–3 are about the world. Layer 4 is about *minds*,
and minds are not synchronised.

**Knowledge firewall: a character never behaves as if they know what they haven't learned.**

---

## 2. THE FIREWALL FILE

Maintain `codex/KNOWLEDGE_FIREWALLS.md` with two lists per POV character or group:

```
## <Name> MAY KNOW
- (each public fact they have actually witnessed or been told, with the chapter)

## <Name> MUST NOT KNOW WITHOUT EARNED DISCLOSURE
- (each private fact, with what earning it would require)
```

Update it every chapter. It is short, and it prevents the most expensive class of error.

---

## 3. WHAT COUNTS AS EARNED

A secret is earned by:

- being **told** by someone who knows, on page
- being **witnessed** directly, on page
- being **deduced** from information the character actually holds, with the reasoning shown
- being **recorded** somewhere the character has legitimate access

A secret is **not** earned by:

- the author needing the character to know
- the reader knowing it
- the character being smart
- "they would obviously have figured it out"

That last one is the trap. Smart characters still cannot know things they have no route to.

---

## 4. PUBLIC VS PRIVATE — THE BREATHING RULE

**Public facts cause public consequences.** If your OC's rank is measured in an official
assessment, the institution records it, people react, and opportunities change. Suppressing
that to protect a plot is the anti-nerf violation from `05_POWER_LAW.md`.

**Private facts stay private.** A hidden bloodline, a concealed identity, a family secret —
these do not leak because the plot needs them to. They leak when someone earns them.

The tension between those two rules is where most of the good drama lives. A character with
a public power and a private identity is a permanent engine: everything they do publicly
risks the thing they hide.

---

## 5. THE PARALLEL PROSE RULE

The reader may know things the characters don't.

A canon event can be shown in parallel prose — a scene from another character's POV — while
your OC remains ignorant. This is not a firewall breach. It is dramatic irony, and it is one
of the best tools available in fan fiction, because the reader knows the source material and
is waiting for the collision.

What you must not do is let your OC *react* to the parallel scene as though they saw it.

---

## 6. THE AUDIT

Every chapter:

```
- Does any character act on information they have no route to?
- Does any character fail to react to something they definitely know?
- Did a public event fail to produce a public consequence?
- Did a private fact leak without an earned route?
- Is the firewall file updated?
```

The second question is as important as the first. A firewall is not just about secrets
leaking — it is about known things being *used*. A character who knows a fact and never lets
it affect a decision has been written as a prop.
