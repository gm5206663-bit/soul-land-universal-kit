# 04 — CANON AND BUTTERFLY

How your divergence and canon coexist. Distilled from the SL4 protocol, which was written
after an author correctly complained that canon was being treated as a checklist.

---

## 1. THE ERROR THIS CORRECTS

Canon is **not** a list of events to mention in a footer.

A canon scene must be written with its causes, its emotional pressure, its characters' adult
motives, its public/private boundary, and its consequences. If you can only summarise a
canon beat, you have not written it — you have filed it.

Equally: your OC is **not** inserted into every private canon scene. But when they are
naturally present in a public or shared scene, their existence must change something —
reactions, records, choices, timing, or emotional residue.

**Do not suppress natural butterfly effects to protect a prewritten outline.** If your OC is
publicly present and recorded, people react. That is the point.

**Do not force butterfly effects where your OC has no access.** Private canon beats stay
private unless an earned route opens them.

---

## 2. THE EIGHT QUESTIONS

For every canon chapter you touch, record all eight:

```
1. What exactly happens in canon?
2. Who is present?
3. What is public information?
4. What is private or family-only?
5. What emotion or pressure drives the scene?
6. What changes after the scene?
7. Where can your OC naturally touch this?
8. Where must your OC NOT intrude?
```

If a beat is private, the reader may see it in parallel prose — but your OC does not learn
it without an earned route. See `06_KNOWLEDGE_FIREWALLS.md`.

---

## 3. THE SEVEN BUTTERFLY CATEGORIES

Use these instead of random changes. They are a checklist: if a chapter touches canon and
none of these fire, ask why not.

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Presence** | Your OC is physically present in a public scene and changes how people react. |
| **Absence** | Your OC's absence, or another's, changes routine. |
| **Relationship** | A friendship or rivalry changes messages, worry, choices, interpretation. |
| **Record** | Institutions record your OC differently because they exist under that file. |
| **Resource** | Family money, access and status shape what options are available. |
| **Training** | A lesson learned earlier changes an action later. |
| **Environmental** | The current setting leaves body-level or circumstance-level effects. |

The **record** category is the most underused and the most powerful. Institutions write
things down. A report filed in chapter five, read by a clerk in chapter fifteen, acted on in
chapter thirty — that is canon-shaped consequence with no combat required.

---

## 4. WHAT BUTTERFLIES MUST NEVER DO

- Replace the canon protagonist.
- Steal a canon character's private emotional pillars.
- Hand your OC canon secrets they did not earn.
- Break canon events purely for drama.
- Put your OC where logistics say they would not be.
- Turn a talent or system into an all-purpose solution.
- Make your OC's power override higher canon bloodlines by assertion.

---

## 5. THE PARALLEL RULE

Canon runs as itself. Your OC lives inside it.

The world does not centre on them. If a canon event happens off-page and your OC never hears
about it, that is correct and it should stay that way — the reader can see it in parallel
prose without your OC knowing.

This is the discipline that separates "a story set in Soul Land" from "Soul Land rewritten
around me."

---

## 6. THE COVERAGE FILE

Keep one per arc: `foundation/CANON_LEDGER.md`. One row per canon beat touched:

```
| Canon beat | Source | Public/Private | Butterfly category | Effect on page | Chapter |
```

If the "effect on page" column is empty for every row, your serial is set next to Soul Land
rather than in it. That is failure mode one, and the ledger is how you catch it early.
