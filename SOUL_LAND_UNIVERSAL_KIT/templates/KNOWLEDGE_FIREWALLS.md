# knowledge_firewalls.md — Template

Copy to `codex/KNOWLEDGE_FIREWALLS.md`.

A firewall is a fact one character knows and another does not. Fan fiction collapses when a
character reasons from information they have no way to possess. Every firewall below is a
place the story must not leak.

---

## Layer 1 — Canon characters do not know about the OC

The OC does not exist in canon. No canon character may reason about them as though they had
read the source material. They may learn things the ordinary way: being told, seeing,
deducing.

**Test:** for every inference a canon character makes about the OC, name the in-world source.
If you cannot, it is a leak.

## Layer 2 — The OC does not know the future

An OC with foreknowledge must not act on it silently. Either they have no foreknowledge, or
they have it and it is a visible burden with visible cost and visible failure. Silent
foreknowledge is plot armour wearing a costume.

**Test:** for every decision that conveniently anticipates an event, name what the character
knew beforehand and how.

## Layer 3 — Ordinary characters do not know extraordinary things

A village hunter does not know what a Titled Douluo can do unless someone told them or they
saw it. Common knowledge is narrower than the author's knowledge, and it is narrower in
specific, classifiable ways.

**Test:** is this something a person of this rank, in this place, would have heard?

## Layer 4 — The reader is not a character

The reader knows things the viewpoint character does not. That is dramatic irony, and it is
allowed. What is not allowed is the narration quietly adopting the reader's knowledge and
letting the character benefit from it.

## What counts as earned

A character may cross a firewall only by one of:

1. **Being told** — by someone who knows, on page.
2. **Seeing** — witnessing the thing directly, on page.
3. **Deducing** — from evidence the reader has also seen, with the reasoning shown.
4. **Investigating** — spending time, resources, or risk, with the cost shown.

Off-page revelation is not earned. "He had heard rumours" is not earned unless the rumour is
shown arriving.
