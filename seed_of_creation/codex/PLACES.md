# PLACES

Geography is continuity. A forest that is nine miles wide in chapter 3 cannot be a day's walk
across in chapter 11, and nobody notices until a reader does.

**State a measurement once, in this file, and derive every later mention from it.** If prose
needs a distance it gets a round felt number spoken by a character; the exact figure lives here.

**Warning specific to this era:** `SOUL_LAND_UNIVERSAL_KIT/01_CANON_SPINE.md` describes the SL1
world — Spirit Hall, sects, forests, the Star Dou Great Forest as a hunting ground. **That is not
this era.** The gap-era plane is a technological Federation with spirit guides, battle armor,
ranked fixed soul shells, a Spirit Pagoda in every city, and soul beasts near extinction
(receipts #28–#32, #42). Reading the kit's spine as a picture of this world is the single easiest
continuity error available in this project. The kit's spine remains authoritative for *mechanics*
(rank ladder, ring colours); it is not authoritative for *this era's geography or institutions*.

## THE DOULUO DIVINE REALM

**Canon status:** `[canon]` — named in source, and the setting of Soul Land 2.5.

**What it is:** the higher-dimensional plane the gods of the Douluo cosmos ascended to, ruled by
a council of five God Kings. Once part of the Divine Dragon Domain.

**Extent:** before the turbulence it held 18 first-class gods, 216 second-class, 1,847
third-class, and roughly ten thousand God Officers and Deity Patrol Hunters `[fan]` — one source,
so treat the counts as atmosphere. Hui Mie's expansion plan was to grow it so it could cover more
planes and absorb more elemental force in turn `[fan]`.

| Measurement | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Dimensional tier | God Officers to First Class Gods are fourth-dimensional; God Kings control fifth-dimensional space | `[fan]` #21 |
| Time ratio to Douluo | 1 divine day = 1 Douluo year | `[canon]` #20 |
| Status after act one | **gone.** Swept away by the Time-Space Turbulence with every god in it | `[canon]` #15 |

**Notable features:** the Committee Chamber, where the five God Kings meet; the **Castle of
Destruction**, Hui Mie's domain, before which Huo Yuhao and Tang Wutong confront him; the
**Forbidden Land of the Gods**, where the Golden Dragon King was sealed and from which it broke
out. No name is recorded for Sheng Ming's domain — a negative finding, so do not invent one and
call it canon.

**What happens here:** all of C1–C7 in Lock 3. The war, the fusion, the turbulence, the escape of
the Golden Dragon King, the disappearance.

**Why it matters after act one:** it is gone, and it stays gone. It functions for the rest of the
serial as an absence — the place everyone on Douluo has a story about, and nobody can reach. The
protagonist is the only character on the plane who has *been* there, and per firewall Layer 3
nobody can confirm a word he says about it.

## THE HIDDEN PLACE — where his twenty years happened

**Canon status:** `[design]` — invented for this project, permitted by canon, stated nowhere in it.

**What it is:** *undecided, and it must be decided before chapter one if act one shows it.*
Canon supplies no such place, so whatever it is, it is ours, and it has to do three jobs at once:

```
1. Run on the divine ratio, so twenty subjective years pass while Douluo counts twenty days.
   Without this, Reading A collapses — see CONTINUITY.md §1.
2. Be concealable from a Divine Realm that has Law Enforcement Gods whose remit is judging gods
   and maintaining its rules. A god hiding a child inside the realm is not concealment, it is a
   cover-up with witnesses.
3. Explain why he knows the realm but not the plane. He was raised somewhere he could see or
   hear the Divine Realm, and nowhere that could teach him Douluo.
```

Candidate shapes, none locked: a fold in Sheng Ming's own life-origin; a sealed garden kept
inside the Castle of Destruction's grounds; a minor plane Hui Mie took during his expansion
campaign, which would make the protagonist's childhood *literally* inside his father's crime.
That last one is the strongest because it makes the hiding place a consequence of canon rather
than an addition to it — and it means the place is gone too, destroyed with everything else the
expansion touched.

**What it costs to leave undecided:** nothing yet. Act one cannot be drafted without it.

## THE DOULUO PLANE (Douluo Star)

**Canon status:** `[canon]` — named in source.

**What it is:** the mortal plane. In this era it is ruled by a **Federation**, technologically
dominant in soul guidance, and organised around the **Spirit Pagoda**, which has a pagoda in
every city and eighteen major-city pillars called the Eighteen Pillars of Heaven `[canon]` shape,
`[fan]` detail.

| Measurement | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Governance | a Federation; a Parliament with factions (an Hawk Faction is named in SL3) | `[canon]` #42, `[fan]` detail |
| Continents | at least Douluo, Star Luo and a **Dou Spirit Continent** hosting Spirit Pagoda HQ | `[fan]` #42 — one source, verify before any journey is written |
| Time since the Pagoda's founding | ~1,000 years at the point canon describes the extinction state and the Silver Dragon waking | `[fan]` #32 |
| Planar offices | **Plane Master: Tang Hao. Life Core: A Yin, merged into the Golden Ancient Tree at Shrek Academy** | `[canon]` #33 |
| Threat | the Abyss Plane, being annexed and plotting; it later nearly destroys the Federation | `[canon]` #33, #35 |

**Notable features:** technology canon's SL1 world does not have — soul guidance shells ranked to
twelfth grade, of which the God-Killer grade destroyed Shrek City; battle armor in word-classes;
the Spirit Ascension Platform, which can raise a soul spirit only to about peak black grade
`[fan]`; the Star Dou Battle Network `[canon]`, a combat simulation Tang Wulin duels in.

**What happens here:** the entire serial after act one.

## SHREK ACADEMY AND SHREK CITY

**Canon status:** `[canon]`.

**What it is:** the continent's premier academy, ten thousand years old in SL1's terms and twenty
thousand by SL3's, with the Tang Sect beside it and Sea God Lake and Sea God Island at its heart.

**Why it matters to this serial, in one line:** **A Yin is the tree there.** The plane's Life Core
is merged into the Golden Ancient Tree on the Academy's grounds (`[canon]` #33), and that tree
dies in the Academy's destruction (`[canon]` #36) — an event whose date is `[disputed]` but which
both readings place inside SL3's era, i.e. at this serial's far horizon.

**Status during the early gap era:** standing, whole, unaware. Firewall F8 applies in spirit here
too: the protagonist may not arrive knowing what will happen to it.

| Measurement | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Fate | destroyed by two God-Killer fixed soul shells fired by the Holy Spirit Cult; the Ancient Golden Tree and Yun Ming die; 20+ Titled Douluo dead | `[canon]` #36 |
| City casualties | over 12 million dead, ~2 million injured; under a third of the city left standing | `[canon]` #36 |
| Aftermath | the crater floods and becomes part of Sea God Lake; **the Life Seed is placed at its centre to grow into the new tree of life** and purify the radiation | `[canon]` #36 |
| Complicity | secretly supported by the Qiangu family of the Spirit Pagoda and powerful Federation factions | `[canon]` #36 |

## THE SPIRIT PAGODA

**Canon status:** `[canon]`.

**What it is:** the era's defining institution, founded by Huo Yuhao to give a peaceful
alternative to killing soul beasts for rings, grown over ten thousand years into the strongest
power across three continents, with a pagoda in every city.

**Why it is the natural first setting for this serial:** it is an institution whose entire
business is *pricing souls* — ranking them by colour, manufacturing them from genes, upgrading
them on a platform, selling them. The protagonist is a soul that cannot be classified, priced,
matched to a spirit soul, or sold. That is not a metaphor the author imposes; it is what the
institution does, meeting the one thing it cannot do.

| Measurement | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Founder | Huo Yuhao, ~10,000 years before SL3 | `[canon]` #27, #31 |
| Reach | every city; eighteen major pillars; HQ on the Dou Spirit Continent | `[canon]` shape, `[fan]` detail #31, #42 |
| Original purpose | mediate peace between humans and soul beasts | `[canon]` #31 |
| Later | slaughtered countless soul beasts with Federation support, for artificial spirit-soul research | `[canon]` #31 |
| Constraint | artificial spirits need soul beast genes; the gene pool depleted as beasts went extinct; non-beast-gene spirits were pitifully weak and unsaleable | `[canon]` #30 |

**Firewall F8 governs the protagonist's view of it.** He arrives roughly a thousand years after
the founding, *before* the betrayal, and must be allowed to hope first. A character who arrives
already knowing the institution will become complicit in extinction is a character the author has
handed a reader's hindsight. See `KNOWLEDGE_FIREWALLS.md`.

## THE STAR DOU GREAT FOREST

**Canon status:** `[canon]`.

**What it is:** the last great stronghold of the soul beasts. In this era the Silver Dragon wakes
there, angry that humans are causing the beasts to go extinct `[fan]` #32, and Di Tian the Beast
God — a Golden-eyed Black Dragon King of roughly 890,000 to 900,000 years — is there `[canon]` #39.

**Why it is listed but not recommended as an opening:** the user's `blue_silver/` serial already
owns this ground, at length, and repo law makes that project **craft reference only, never canon**
for this one. Opening here risks treading the same emotional territory in the same voice. It
remains available later in the span, once the protagonist has a distinct relationship to the
dying beasts — which his mother's law gives him, and which Blue Silver's protagonist does not
share.
