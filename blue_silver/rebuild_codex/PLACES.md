## Blue Silver Valley / Blue Silver Emperor Valley

**Canon status:** `[canon]` `[user ruling 2026-09]` — this IS canon Soul Land 1. Never tag it
`[design]`, never write "canon nowhere names it," never write "never asserted as canon."
That wording was revoked and must not be re-added.

**What it is:** the destination. Nine great ones rooted in a ring; the floor entirely Blue
Silver grass.

| Measurement | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Length | **nine miles** | [on page] Ch14:15 |
| Floor | nine miles, wall to wall, dense layered growth | [on page] Ch14:31 |
| Clearance kept by the forest | nine miles — the deep band stops dead at the edge | [on page] Ch14:68, :96 |
| Inhabitants | nine great ones; eight remain after year 746 | [on page] Ch14 |

**What happens here:** the cull is revealed — there had been **400**, and **391** went back
into the soil in single seasons. **The line was harvested, not dwindled.** `[on page]` Ch14

He refuses the whole valley. The old king dies at year 746 at the end of a natural life —
the only one of the 391 to do so — and he takes not one year. **Refusal 2.**

**Why it matters:** the valley is not a refuge he finds. It is a crime scene he walks into,
and the moral test is whether he joins the economy that made it.

## The glade

**Canon status:** `[design]` this project's invention, inside canon Star Dou Forest.

**What it is:** where he breaks soil and spends most of his life before year 714.

**Notable features:** a stream, a scree slope, a bank. Ground-listening opens here in Ch2.
Flood silt laid at ~year 30 — the ground he is later born again into.

**Who comes here:** men cutting grass for cordage (Ch2); the shadow cat, driven in by
hunters (Ch4); the Spirit Hall surveyor (Ch5); the night-walker (Ch7); the found one (Ch8).

## Grid nineteen

**Canon status:** `[design]` — a Spirit Hall survey designation, not a canon place.

**What it is:** the administrative identity of the glade. The reason he survived four
centuries is that a piece of paper said *surveyed and unresolved*.

| Measurement | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Marked surveyed-and-unresolved | year ~193 | [on page] Ch5:138 |
| Stays unresolved | 147 years (193 → 340) | derived |
| Reclassified exhausted habitat | year 672 | [on page] Ch12 |
| Sealed pending | ~400 years old, never justified | [on page] Ch10 |

**The surveyor's note:** *"if anybody out of the Hall comes through grid nineteen in the
next fifty years I want them to read the note before they read the map."* `[on page]` Ch5:140

**Why it matters:** the whole survival mechanism of the serial is bureaucratic. Not
concealment, not power — a note nobody read.

## The seal

Not a place, but it is what protects one.

Berrit Ohn files the permanent consolidated seal in her own name at ≈2590 DC, covering nine
grids in eleven. `[on page]` Ch15:253

**The valley is protected by paperwork.** Not a ward, not a barrier, not a guardian. A
thirty-one-year-old records clerk with no rings followed her own brief to its end and
understood what she was reading. This is deliberate and load-bearing: the serial's whole
argument is that mercy survives through records, not through force.
