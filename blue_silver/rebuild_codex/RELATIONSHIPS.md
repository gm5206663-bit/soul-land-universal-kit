## The arithmetic of loss

Every bond in this serial ends. That is not pessimism, it is the subject: a being with a
775-year life watches short lives end, repeatedly, and has to decide what to do with that.

The answer the serial gives is *keep*. Not avenge, not hoard, not feed. Keep.

**Kill count: zero, across all fifteen chapters and 775 years. Permanent.** `[user ruling]`

## Deep dives

### The mantis (61–104) — the first bond

Jade-green hunter. Arrives year 61, stays forty-three years, dies year 104 of a natural life.

The killing happens at year 98 — while the mantis still lives. That ordering is load-bearing:
he learns what killing costs *before* he loses the thing he loves, and the mercy law is
founded on arithmetic he did in the presence of a friend.

**Never retcon the mantis's death into a killing.** It is a natural death and the serial's
first proof that he can bear one.

### The shadow cat (163) — the first enemy

Several-thousand-year cat, driven into the glade by hunters, hides in his crown and eats his
small tenants. He takes its foreleg. **It survives.**

Thirty years later the same officer writes up its leg on a scree slope — that is the
surveyor, and it fixes Ch5 at ~193. `[on page]` Ch5:44

This is the closest the serial comes to violence, and it stops at maiming. Do not escalate it
retrospectively.

### The night-walker (301–468) — the teacher

Ancient, last of its kind, former keeper of the seam. Invents ground-language. Taps the
keeper's knowledge into him four words at a time over decades. `[on page]` Ch7:102

Taps two blows into the earth to say goodnight. `[on page]` Ch7:197

Dies at year 468 at his edge. **REFUSAL 1:** he does not draw its returned years into the
well. The reason available to him at the time: *a keeper keeps.*

### The found one (402–630) — the one who answered

Small grey grazer from the deep fringe. Answers at year 508 — forty years after the
night-walker died, forty years of him answering alone.

Invents the eleventh word, which took eleven years to agree on:

> a single blow, hard, once — *I am here, I am answering, I am one of the ones who stays.*
> `[on page]` Ch10:84–89

Dies of age at 630. He stamps the eleventh word into the packed soil over two graves.
`[on page]` Ch11:253

### The liar (592) — the test of the door

A mid-forest hunter that learns the drum. Is let in. Kills two. Is caged eleven days and
**released**.

This is the law of the door, and it is the hardest thing in the serial to write correctly:
the cost of mercy is paid by someone other than the merciful. Two small ones die so that the
rule stays a rule.

**Do not soften this and do not give it a fourth option.**

### The nine great ones and the old king (735–746)

Eight remain after the old king dies at 746. It dies at the end of a natural life — **the
only one of the 391 to do so** — in a valley made of harvested great cultivations.

**REFUSAL 2:** *He did not take a single year of it.* `[on page]` Ch14:186

The two refusals are the moral spine. Neither may be softened, retconned, or given a fourth
option. `[user ruling]`

### A Yin (753–) — the naming

Youngest of the nine great ones by ~40,000 years. The only one who felt the cull's signature
and told no one.

Speaks his eleven-word drum back to him, stamps the eleventh, and **names him Home**.
`[on page]` Ch15

Her canon fate is never staged on page. `[user ruling]`

## The humans

Nine named humans, each with a face, a rank and a decision. Full dossiers in
`rebuild_codex/CHARACTERS.md`.

The relationship that matters is not between him and any one of them — it is the chain:

**the surveyor's note → the thin man's letter → Kesh's testimony → Vase's eleven pages →
Hallum's four lines → Ohn's seal.**

Six people who never met him, across four centuries, each doing a small correct thing with a
piece of paper. That chain is what protects the valley. He is alive because of bureaucracy,
and the serial knows it.
