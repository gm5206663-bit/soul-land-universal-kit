# Chapter Fourteen — The Ground That Remembers

```
◆ STATUS — Chapter 14: years 735–753
KIND      : Blue Silver Grass, common line. A King of the grass line, in the line's valley.
AGE       : ~765 at the chapter's close
CLASS     : ten-thousand-year class, ≈10,000 carried. Stage Three. Still rooted.
REVEAL    : the line did not dwindle. The line was culled. The ground knows, because the
            ground is where they all went back.
REFUSAL   : the second refusal, at the largest scale available to him. He does not feed.
```

---

The valley was nine miles long and it was full of grass, and the grass was the same kind of
thing he was.

That is not a small statement and it took him four years to be certain of it. He had spent
eight centuries as the only one of himself in the world — the only Blue Silver Grass in the
Star Dou middle band that had ever cultivated, the only plant anything had ever kept a
boundary, the single deep-blue blade in a forest of ten thousand species that had, entirely
by accident, become a person. He had no framework in which another one of himself was
possible, and when the root-mass came down into the valley soil in the spring of his
seven hundred and thirty-fifth year and the ground under him answered in his own register, the
answering was so far outside everything he had ever felt that he spent a whole season
reading it as a mistake.

It was not a mistake.

The valley floor was Blue Silver. Not a bank, not a meadow, not a hundred feet of low
ground — the whole floor, nine miles of it, wall to wall, growing in the dense layered
structure he had spent two centuries developing against the deep band's leaning, with the
same silver veins and the same hard base and the same held-and-let-go tension under contact.
And in the middle of it, in a ring, stood the nine.

They were not grass.

They were the great ones.

---

He had no word for what they were, having eleven words and none of them near enough, and
the word the world would eventually have used was Emperor — but they were not that either,
not anymore, and the difference is the whole of this chapter.

Nine of them. Rooted. Immobile. So old that the cultivation coming off them did not
register as pressure the way the old one's had not, and did not register as weather either.
It registered as *the valley*, because they had been standing in it so long that the ground
and the growing and the nine great bodies were one continuous object, and the object had
been there before the flood that laid down the silt he was born in, before the old one's
round, before the deep band had a seam to lean on.

They did not speak. That was the second thing, and it was worse than the first.

The nine were alive. They were the most alive things he had ever been near, by a margin
that made the serpent's ten thousand years feel like a candle, and they did not answer him,
and they did not turn toward him, and they did not do anything at all except stand in a
ring in the middle of a valley of their own grass and be rooted in the way that a thing
which has been rooted for an age is rooted — completely, permanently, past the point where
rootedness is a condition and becomes an identity.

They were not keeping the valley. That was the third thing, and it took him six years, and
it was the first piece of the truth.

The valley did not need keeping. The boundary that stopped the deep band's great things
dead at the valley's edge was not a kept boundary — it was a *pressure*, the accumulated
weight of nine ancient cultivations sitting in one place for so long that nothing in the
forest would come within nine miles of it. The valley kept itself. It had always kept
itself. The nine were not guards.

They were what was left.

---

The old king was dying, and it was the only one of the nine that had anything to give him.

It stood at the ring's northern point and it had been standing there for a very long time,
and it was old in a way the other eight were not — not older, *further along* — and in the
ninth year after he came down into the valley soil, in the deep of the cold season, it
reached for him through the ground.

Not the way the old one had tapped. Not the way the found one had stamped. It reached the
way a root reaches, and it came into his root-mass from below and it did not ask, and it
did not wait for an answer, and it gave him the valley's memory in the only form the line
had ever had for giving anything.

Through the ground. All at once.

---

There had been four hundred of them.

That was the first number, and it came down into him through the soil the way everything
in his life had come down into him, and he had no concept of a number and the number was
not a number to him — it was a *shape*, a ring of nine that had once been a ring of four
hundred, standing in this valley, in this grass, with the deep band stopped dead nine miles
out in every direction and no need to keep anything because there was nothing in the world
that could come at four hundred.

And the ring had thinned.

Not quickly. That was the cruelty of it and the thing he would have called cruelty if he
had had the word. The ring had thinned over an age — one here, three there, a gap at the
western point that stayed a gap for four hundred years, and the thinning had felt, from
inside the valley, like age. Great things die. Great things die slowly and at the end of
enormous lives and go back into the ground and the ground gets rich and the ring gets
smaller and that is what time does.

The ground knew otherwise.

That was the whole of the reveal and it had been under him the entire time, in the soil he
had woven himself into in the spring of his seven hundred and thirty-fifth year, in the
richness he had noticed coming into the country in the last two years of the going — the
richness that was not scattered, that was *in lines*, that was in a pattern.

He read the pattern in the eleventh year, and what the pattern said was this:

The dead had not gone back into the ground at the ends of their lives.

---

A soul beast that dies of age returns its cultivation slowly. It settles. It goes back over
seasons, the way the old one's went back over four days, the way the found one's went back
into the packed soil over a winter. The ground takes it gently and the ground gets rich in
a soft irregular bloom, and that is what four hundred feet of soil over an ancient grave
looks like from underneath.

The valley's soil was not like that.

The valley's soil was rich in *lines* — narrow, deep, vertical, running down through nine
miles of ground in a pattern that repeated, and each line was a place where a great
cultivation had gone back into the earth all at once, in a single season, in the middle of
what should have been an enormous life. Not settled. *Cut*.

He counted them by reading, over three years, root by root, and the count came up through
him the way every true thing in his life had come up through him, and the count was three
hundred and ninety-one.

Three hundred and ninety-one of the four hundred had gone back into the valley's soil in a
single season each, and not one of those seasons had been the end of a natural life.

The line had not dwindled.

The line had been *harvested*, slowly, over an age, one great body at a time, by something
that came into a valley that nothing in the forest could enter and took a member of the
ring and left, and came back, and took another — and the valley had gone on keeping itself,
because the valley did not know it was being emptied, because a pressure that stops the
deep band dead at nine miles does not register the difference between three hundred and
ninety-nine great cultivations and nine.

The nine were not what was left of a line that had aged.

They were what was left of a cull.

---

The old king gave him all of it in one season and then it went, and the going was the
quietest thing in the whole of the serial.

It did not die in the ring. That is worth saying, because it is the same thing the found one
had done, four hundred miles away, at the end of a life two hundred and twenty-eight years
long — it left its place. The great rooted body at the northern point of the ring came out
of the ground in the spring of his seven hundred and forty-sixth year, and it could not
move, and it did not try, and what it did instead was let go of the valley floor and put
the whole of itself down into the soil under the ring, and the going-back took one season,
and the soil under the northern point got rich in a soft irregular bloom.

At the end of a natural life.

It was the first one in three hundred and ninety-one.

He felt the whole of it, and he did not feed.

That is the second refusal and it was harder than the first, and the reason it was harder
is that this time he understood what he was refusing. The old one's ten thousand years had
been an accident of proximity — a dying animal forty feet from his crown, going back into
soft ground, available to a root-mass that had no reason in the world to decline it. This
was a King of his own line, at the end of an enormous life, putting itself into the soil
under a ring of its own remaining great ones, in a valley nine miles across that was made
of nothing but the returned substance of its own dead.

The whole valley was food. Nine miles of it. Three hundred and ninety-one great
cultivations, cut down in the middle of their lives and gone back into the ground, lying in
the soil under him in lines he had spent three years counting.

He did not take a single year of it.

He had four words and a drum of eleven and no self clear enough to hold a principle, and the
principle had been written for him anyway, four hundred miles away, in the eleventh year
after the liar, in the dark, by a thing that could not have explained it:

*a keeper keeps.*

A keeper does not take. And the line's dead were the line's dead, and the valley was the
valley, and what had been done to the ring had been done by taking, and he was not going to
be another thing that took.

---

*The report went into the Hall's registry in the autumn of his seven hundred and
fifty-first year, and it was four lines long, and it was the first document in nine
centuries that named the valley.*

*It was written by an assessor named Tefer Hallum, who had stood at the boundary of that
country for two days with an instrument case that would not read past it, and who had come
back to the southwestern post and dictated the whole of what he had seen to a clerk in
under a minute.*

*"Valley, southwestern grid, unentered," he said. "Vegetation continuous across the floor,
nine miles, will not come out of the soil."*

*"Cultivation reading."*

*"None. That's the thing." Hallum rubbed his face. "There's no reading. My case reads to
sixty thousand and it reads nothing, and there are nine — nine — structures at the centre of
it that are not structures, they're growth, and my hand went cold standing two hundred feet
from the boundary."*

*"Cultivation estimate."*

*"In excess of sixty thousand years each." The clerk stopped writing. "Write it down."*

*"Ma'am, if that's right—"*

*"Write it down and write the recommendation." Hallum was already putting his coat on.
"Recommend no approach. Recommend the grid be sealed."*

*"And the party. Six men went out with you."*

*"Four of them wouldn't cross." He looked at the clerk for the first time. "I wouldn't
cross. I've assessed in the deep band for nineteen years and I stood at that line for two
days and I did not cross it, and I'm not going to pretend to you that I know what's on the
other side." He took his case off the desk. "Seal it. Seal the whole grid. And put my name
on it, because in ten years somebody with a quota is going to want to open it and I want
them to have to read my name first."*

*They sealed it. The valley had been sealed by nothing at all for an age and was now sealed
by a Hall designation as well, and the designation went into the registry next to grid
nineteen's exhausted-habitat note and the walker entry and a dead records man's eleven
pages, and nobody in the Hall connected any of them, because they were four hundred miles
apart and one was about a dead walker and one was about grass and one was about a valley
nobody could enter.*

*Somebody did, eventually.*

*In the valley, at the end of the seven hundred and fifty-first year, a King of the Blue
Silver line stood rooted in the soil of his own dead with a hundred feet of low ground
around him, and eight great ones stood in a ring with a gap at the northern point, and the
ground under the gap was rich in a soft irregular bloom, and the deep band stopped nine
miles out and had always stopped, and three hundred and ninety-one lines ran down through
the soil in a pattern that said the same thing over and over.*

*He had three hundred and forty years left and he did not know it.*

*And in the spring of his seven hundred and fifty-third year, at the eastern edge of the
valley, in the blue-silver grass that grew up to his own crown's height, something moved
that was not wind.*
