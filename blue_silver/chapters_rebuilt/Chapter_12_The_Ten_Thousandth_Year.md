# Chapter Twelve — The Ten Thousandth Year

```
◆ STATUS — Chapter 12: years 650–700
KIND      : Blue Silver Grass, common line. Keeper of the seam. From this chapter: a King.
AGE       : ~700 at the chapter's close
CLASS     : ten-thousand-year class. The gate crosses in his six hundred and sixty-fifth
            year — Stage Three of the King form. He is still rooted. That is the whole
            problem of the rest of the book.
THREAT    : the assessor's report is finally read. The grid is reclassified. A black ring
            is now possible in grid nineteen, and a black ring is worth more than a city.
```

---

The well had been filling for five hundred years, and in the winter of his six hundred
and sixty-fifth year it was full, and the gate took him.

It did not arrive. That is the thing about the gates that no one who has not crossed one
ever believes: there is no arrival. There is no light and no sound and no moment. There is
a long slow accumulation — every year of growth folded in, every scar of the flood and the
serpent and the eleven nights and the four hours, every winter of holding against the
leaning, the whole seven centuries of a thing being alive and careful — and then one season
the accumulation is of a kind that the world's arithmetic recognises, and the arithmetic
moves, and what was a thousand-year thing is a ten-thousand-year thing, and the season in
which it happened was ordinary in every respect except that one.

What changed in him was not power.

That is the part he would have got wrong if he had been asked, and he would have been asked
soon enough. Power went up, of course; the well was deep and it had always been available
to the root-mass and there was a great deal more of it now. But the gate did not change
what he could do. It changed what he *was*, and the change was in the world rather than in
him:

The forest could feel him.

Not the way it had felt the boundary, or the seam, or the hundred feet of low ground that
would not come out of the soil. Those were places. This was a *presence* — a ten-thousand-
year cultivation signature sitting in the middle band of the largest forest on the
continent, and it registered in the ground the way the deep band's great things registered,
and it registered in the same register, and from the winter of his six hundred and
sixty-fifth year every ancient thing within four hundred miles of grid nineteen knew that
there was a King at the stream bend.

They did not come to look. That was the first surprise. The great things of the deep had
been leaning on his seam for two centuries and had never once crossed it, and they did not
start now. What they did was *route around him* — the same way the mid-forest half-light
hunters had routed around the glade for two hundred years after the cat, the same way
twelve men with nets had worked within four hundred feet of the best-kept boundary in the
Star Dou middle band without once looking at it.

The forest had been making room for him since the ninety-eighth year of his life, slowly,
one pressure at a time, without ever being told to.

At ten thousand years it stopped being avoidance and became deference, and the difference
was that deference is given on purpose.

---

The other thing the gate did was put a number on him.

He had no idea about numbers. He had four words and a drum of eleven and no concept
whatsoever of the economy that his entire existence sat inside — and from that winter he
was, objectively, in the most valuable single bracket the human world had, because a
ten-thousand-year soul beast yields a black soul ring, and a black soul ring is the fourth
tier of five, and the men who take them do not take them from anything they can avoid.

The registry caught up in the spring of his six hundred and seventy-second year.

It took seven years and it took a war. Not a war in the forest — a war in the Hall's
budget, two provinces over, in which somebody who had been a junior records clerk when the
assessor's eleven pages went in was now a senior records clerk with a shortage and a
quota, and who went looking through the sealed-pending designations for anything that could
be reclassified as a resource. He found grid nineteen. He found the vegetation note, four
hundred and fifty years old. He found the walker entry, dead for a hundred and fifty. He
found the assessor's report, which he read twice, and then he went and got his superior,
and the superior read it, and the superior said the thing that had been said about him
every time anybody had looked at him in seven hundred years:

"It's grass."

"The report says the boundary is maintained by the vegetation."

"The report also says the man didn't think it was a soul beast and says he knows what he's
suggesting, and if he'd been sure we'd have sent somebody in two hundred years ago." The
superior put the eleven pages down. "Plants don't cultivate. That's the first rule."

"Then what's keeping the grid."

"A dead walker's territory and a hundred low-class animals with nowhere else to go." He
pushed the file back across the desk. "Reclassify it as low-yield fauna habitat, open the
hunting on the small stock, and leave the vegetation alone — it's not worth the assessor's
time and it's certainly not worth a team."

They opened the hunting on the small stock that summer.

---

It was the worst year of his life and he did not know it was a year, having no word for
one.

They came in parties of four and six, contracted, working the middle band's grids for
everything low-class and mobile, and they worked grid nineteen for eleven weeks because
grid nineteen had been reclassified as low-yield fauna habitat and low-yield fauna habitat
is exactly what a contracted party works when the good grids are taken. They took the
refugees. Not all of them — the door was a door and the door had a hundred voices and the
hundred voices knew the sound of men with cages better than they knew the eleventh word.
But they took thirty-one small things out of the low ground in eleven weeks, and they took
them at the edge of the woven mass, where the loosened layer was, because that was where
the small things came out at dusk to stamp.

They learned the drum.

That was the thing. It took them three weeks and it was not clever — a contracted hunter
stands still at the edge of a glade at dusk and watches what the small things do, the same
way a four-hundred-year ambush predator had stood still at the same edge eighty years
before and worked out what the stamping was for. The stamping was the asking. The asking
opened the ground. And a man who could stamp could stand at the edge of the only place in
the middle band where the deep band's refugees came out on purpose.

He felt all thirty-one go.

He could not stop it. That is the plain fact and it is the fact that made the rest of the
book: a King of the grass line, ten thousand years of cultivation, the keeper of the best
boundary in the Star Dou middle band, the thing every ancient creature within four hundred
miles routed around — could not move. The gate had made him a King and the gate had not
given him legs, because he was grass, and grass is rooted, and the whole of his kingdom was
a hundred feet of low ground that he could not take with him.

He could have killed them.

That is worth saying, because it is the first time in six hundred and seventy-two years
that the option had ever existed. The root-mass could have taken a man's ankle the way it
had taken the cat's foreleg. The weave could have closed over a body the way it had closed
over the liar. There were six of them in his ground in the ninth week and they were
standing on the packed soil over two graves with cages at their belts, and every root in a
hundred feet of low ground was his, and he did nothing.

He did nothing because the law was the law and the law had been written in the eleventh
year after the liar, in the dark, without malice, by a thing with four words and no self
clear enough to hold a principle:

*keep even the liar. Cage the act, not the animal. The door stays a door.*

The door stayed a door. Thirty-one small things went out of it in cages.

---

*They stopped coming in the twelfth week, and the reason was not him.*

*It was that the small stock ran out. Eleven weeks of a contracted party working a
low-yield fauna habitat will empty it, and by the end of the season grid nineteen had
nothing left in it worth a net, and the parties went to the next grid, and the
reclassification that had opened the hunting was quietly marked exhausted and nobody in the
Hall ever looked at it again.*

*Which is how the whole of the second half of his life was decided — by a budget shortfall
two provinces over, and a senior records clerk with a quota, and the fact that a hundred
feet of low ground in the middle band of the largest forest on the continent was worth less
to the largest institution on the continent than the cost of sending a team to look at it.*

*They never sent a team.*

*And in the glade, in the winter of his six hundred and seventy-second year, a King of the
Blue Silver line stood rooted in a hundred feet of low ground that had gone quiet again,
and the door had eighty voices left instead of a hundred, and the seam went taut and let go
at dusk the way it had for two hundred years, and the ancient things of the deep band
routed around grid nineteen on purpose now, and the whole of the human world had filed him
under exhausted habitat.*

*He had forty-odd years left at the stream bend.*

*He did not know it. He had no word for leaving, and grass does not leave, and the going —
the thing that would take him out of the middle band and across four hundred miles of
country to the valley of his line, in the last decades of his life — had not been earned
yet, and could not be earned by anything he had ever done.*

*It would be earned by the one thing he had never once done in six hundred and seventy-two
years.*

*It would be earned by wanting to go.*
