## The arithmetic of loss (the theme beneath every bond)
Written into him by the mantis's death, taught again by the sanctuary, and now kept whole
through the old one's own passing (Chapter 9): **everyone he loves will end, and he will
not.** Two alones frame his life — the first alone (before the mantis, all there was) and
the second alone (a room with the shape of her still in it); after the last vigil there is
also a kept ground where the last of the old one's kind lies, and he is the keeper alone on
the line. The adaptation rewrites body, memory, and bloodline, but never this. That
deliberate limit keeps the talent honest and shapes the wound A Yin must one day know to
truly reach him. **[wire]**

## Deep dives

### The mantis (years ~40–90) — the first bond
A jade-green mantis of the grass carpet, one of a hundred, a soul-beast insect whose whole
life happened on top of him. She saw the impossible grass first as shelter and warmth, then
as home; she was the first creature who chose his stillness.
- What she gave him: the first *wanting her to stay*; the first deliberate act of his life
  (the blade-roof over her in the storm); the sanctuary he did not know he was becoming.
- The fire year (~70): she could fly and did not leave — stayed, laid her egg-case in the
  cool mud at his base, climbed his crown and held on. He spent every blade he had to be
  between her and the fire, and nearly died. Almost-losing her became his first great fear.
- Her death (~90): she declined as her kind do and did not wake. He kept her husk, folded
  against his crown — the first thing he ever kept that was not a lesson. Her death wrote
  the arithmetic into him.
- Her line: the egg-case hatched in the first green after the fire; her young went into the
  grass, each alone. He keeps the empty shell. Whether any of her line finds its way back
  to the one still place that held its mother is a held possibility, not a promise. **[wire]**
- Never spoken by her: her mind was motion and heat. She had no words, and needed none. A
  name was never earned before she died.

### The night-walker, "the old one" (years ~140–375 — gone) — the second bond, the teacher
An original keeper of the boundary between the middle band and the deep band, last of its
kind, its years in the high tens of thousands (unstated). It crossed his ground for a
century without looking at him — and then, one moonlit night (~140), lay down at his edge.
- What it gave him: acknowledgment (first, the weight of being *known* by another power of
  the forest); a true two-way ground-language (deliberate taps read, root-pulses answered —
  his first shared speech, wordless); the vigil winter (~175), when it curled at his edge
  and he stayed green through the white silence and it woke to *here, you stayed*; the
  deep-country memory (the grass once had kings and queens — myth-level, no canon claim);
  and finally the keeper's whole knowledge, tap by tap: the boundary's body, the soft
  watercourse door, the deep band's rhythms, and the keeper's truth — *the work is mostly
  not the fight; it is the being there.*
- What it cost him: the fear that is not for himself; the certain knowledge that it will not
  always be there.
- The serpent night (~240): the old one broke its foreleg defending him. The leg never set
  right; it could no longer walk its round. The keeping passed to him: *you are the keeper
  now.*
- Going quiet (~300–355): in the years after the eater it tapped less and less, until a
  full season passed with none. He kept sending his pulses into the silence, and it
  answered with only its breath. On the morning the sickness broke it tapped its last two
  blows — the oldest, *you are the keeper now* — and spent its final speech. It did not tap
  again; only its breath remained.
- The last vigil (~360–375): its breathing faded by degrees through the long quiet, and on
  a spring night, with no warning, it did not resume — the last of its kind had finished
  its going. He kept the vigil; his green went thin for a season (grief not adapted away);
  and he let the ground have its body whole rather than drawing its returned years into
  his well. Where it lay, the densest silver-green in the glade grew — a kept place.
- Now: he is the keeper AND the King of the seam (the whole of its knowledge carried in his
  roots and crowned at the King gate, Chapter 12) — and the deep-country memory it passed to
  him has come true: the direction it turned its head toward every dawn is the Blue Silver
  Valley, and in Chapter 13 he went to it. What its kept place and carried knowledge become
  is story-earned. **[wire]**

### The sanctuary and its small lives (years ~100–)
He became, without a word for it, a place that keeps things. Fledglings, rodents, ground
birds press into his deep green and are safe there; he is their defender, and a place that
keeps things is a place that must fight for them.
- The fledgling: saved from the hawk (~105–115) when his blades first held a line. It came
  home to him every dusk of its long life (the middle band's beasts age slow). It died old
  in his deep green (~255). Unlike the mantis, he gave it back to the soil — the first of
  his kept lives to end in his keeping, kept as memory and pattern, its place kept.
- The taken ones: the shadow cat took some before he understood. He could not save them all;
  the holes in the pattern of his ground did not fill. He learned the second lesson of the
  arithmetic by being a keeper who could not keep everyone.

### The shadow cat (years ~120) — the first enemy
A lean, scarred, several-thousand-year mid-band hunter, drawn by his dense essence. It
raided the sanctuary and took the sheltered. He fought for the first time — roots tripping,
blades seizing and cutting — was shredded to his root, and outlasted it. It fled maimed and
never returned; its kind learned the place by the water is not a place to hunt. He did not
kill it. He has never killed anything.

### The deep serpent (years ~176–240) — the enemy from below
The circling presence he first felt at ~176 was a ten-thousand-class serpent of the deep
band, drawn by his essence, that spent decades tunneling beneath the boundary. It broke the
old one's foreleg and came for his root. He spent two hundred years of kept essence in one
night — roots caging, blades biting, every torn root regrowing — and drove it off, wounded,
not dead. The night made him the keeper and cracked the well open in him. Its lesson traveled
the deep band: this ground is held. **It returned healed, at the crest of his becoming
(Chapter 12)** — the deep band's oldest blade sent to test the King gate itself — struck
at the moment of the crossing, and was met by a keeper grown into a King: caged in the
living ground, spoken to, and released. The deep band withdrew its testing. Whether it
comes again, or the deep sends something greater, is story-earned. **[wire]**

### The root-eater, "the eater" (years ~260–295) — the enemy beneath
An armored, patient deep-band burrower drawn by the now-open well — the sweetest root in the
middle forest. It fed on his fine roots from below for years, unreachable by blades,
unbeatable by presence. He could not out-fight it, so he out-kept it: in a hard winter he
baited it past his heart on a trail of sweet new root into his oldest deep net, closed the
roots behind it, held it through the cold, and released it in the thaw. It did not return.
The deep learned he holds below as well as above.

### The found one (years ~352–~590s) — the first kept of the deep
A small gray grazer of the deep band's fringe — the smallest, weakest kind of the deep's
people (species unstated, original **[design]**). Driven out of the deep by the same rot
that sickened the glade, and hunted by its great ones while it was weak, it ran until it
could run no more and crossed the boundary — the one line in the forest that was kept — to
be taken in, not to take.
- What it gave him: the knowledge that the deep is not one thing — it has its small and its
  lost as well as its serpents and eaters — and that the keeping was never a wall against
  the deep, but a keeping for the small and the lost of every dark that comes to it. The
  boundary, he learned, is a door as well.
- What it cost him: everything, in the end. It was not a hunter; the sanctuary's small
  lives accepted it in a season — and it became the voice of the door.
- Its arc (~352–~590s): grown steady and unafraid, it slept against the silver-green over
  the old one's place, crossed back into the fringe and returned (the deep letting it pass
  because it carried the green), ANSWERED him in ground-language (Ch10 — the first two-way
  speech of his alone-keeping), spoke for the green to the small and lost of the fringe,
  baited the hunters on the night of the breach, learned the arithmetic of loss (Ch10),
  and grew old teaching the drum to the fringe young (Ch11). It drummed its last *here* and
  passed against the old one's place (~590s); given to the ground, not drawn into his
  well. Its teaching outlives it — the door is now a hundred young voices. Grief carried,
  not adapted away. **[carried wire]**

### A Yin, the Blue Silver Empress (the road is open; the meeting is the near next beat) **[canon]**
The hundred-thousand-year Empress of the grass line, rooted in the deep forest in this era.
She loves every blade of Blue Silver Grass; her domain reads the grass across its reach. She
noticed him when he stopped being ignorable: at the King gate (Chapter 12) he crossed the
rank her domain can sense as a King, not a blade, and his bloodline sensing recognised her
line in return — she has READ him for the first time, a two-way wire. Since the going he
stands in her line's home valley (Ch13–14), and in Chapter 14 the wire became a road: his
word went down through the valley's continuous root-mass in the old one's ground-grammar
and she answered with one deliberate pulse — *here*, the found one's last drum-word —
carried up from the direction of the core [design staging within her canon grade]. The
meeting itself
remains ahead, and the bond's texture is deliberately not pinned: she is elder and kin, and
possibly the one who names him. To truly reach him she must know the wound the arithmetic
made — that everyone he loves ends, and he does not. Everything before that is earned,
never staged early.

## Relationship principles (laws)
1. Every bond is caused on-page; nothing is asserted in footers only.
2. Grief is real and not adapted away; loss changes him permanently.
3. The talent responds to the whole of his life — bonds are not decoration, they are part of
   the engine (the mantis made the sanctuary; the old one made the keeper; keeping the last
   vigil without taking from the old one's body shaped the keeper alone; A Yin will be the
   meeting).
4. He never kills. Enemies are turned, outlasted, driven off, caged and released.
5. Warm not edgy: he is humble, curious, a natural center of whatever small society he
   finds — never a mystery the world tiptoes around.
