# FOUNDATION — how we write this serial

## Language law
- **English only. No Chinese characters in any file.** Names in pinyin (A Yin, Tang San),
  terms in the common English forms (Soul Land glossary lives in WORLD.md). This is a hard law
  — the reader cannot read Chinese characters.

## Canon law
1. Every scene is checked against the source before it is written. **Novel and donghua are
   the source of truth, per scene.** Fan-wiki and fan lore are pointers only — never canon.
2. When we do not know a canon fact, we mark it unknown and resolve it before staging. We
   never write a plausible-sounding canon scene from memory and call it verified. (This was
   the fatal habit of the old project.)
3. The story stages canon from the era we own (deep pre-SL1, Star Dou). Any fixture that
   canon later defines (the SL1-era Blue Silver King; A Yin's human-form era) is a **guard
   rail** — approach, never collide. Guard rails live in WORLD.md.

## Character law (the OC lives inside the world)
1. Ordinary life is story: place, neighbors, seasons, hunger, limits.
2. No invented rules to bench or elevate him. If he cannot do something, the cause is real
   and visible on the page.
3. Every character — beast or human — has verifiable logic and interior depth.
4. We never put words in a canon character's mouth that they could not say.

## The Adaptation talent — full doctrine (carried from the old project, made plant-shaped)
**AUTHORITATIVE SOURCE: `ADAPTATION_TALENT.md` (2026-09). The doctrine below is the short
form; the stage system + mutation model in ADAPTATION_TALENT.md governs the serial.**

The talent was originally defined (with the user) for a human child. Its **core law** carries
over untouched; only the body it lives in changes.

- **Scope:** it affects his ENTIRE existence — body, mind, senses, energy, skills, identity,
  relationships, environment, and **bloodline**. Nothing is exempt. (User's own emphasis:
  "it effects everything, every single part.") It is also the reason a Blue Silver Grass can
  be a soul beast at all: his very being is being rewritten by it, slowly, from the sprout.
- **Not a system voice.** It shows as sensation, instinct, recovery, changed need, corrected
  growth — never as a UI or an inner narrator explaining "your talent means…"
- **Does NOT give:** free energy, instant immunity, universal copying, omniscience,
  auto-training. Everything still costs. For a rooted plant the "cost" is visceral: hunger,
  vulnerability, long stillness, parts of himself he must shed to change.
- **Bloodline is the talent's deepest work — but never toward royalty.** A common grass has
  no royal line and he never gains one: the Sunweave mutation is the common line's own real
  change (module M1). The talent effects everything and refines/stabilizes that mutated
  common line across his life — NOT toward the Emperor's blood, never royal, never dark,
  never dragon. Any rise toward the Blue Silver King is the CANON wild-grass evolution at
  ~10,000 years (the line's own grown crown), driven and carried by the talent — earned,
  priced, and slow in feeling even if fast by beast-law.
- **Gains-ledger law:** every consolidated gain changes his later perception, difficulty,
  speed, and cost — forever. Gains are tracked in the bible, never frozen, never forgotten.
- **Early expression as a grass (working picture, to confirm):** roots that "remember" soil;
  leaves that harden toward what burned them; growth that leans toward what fed it; a body
  that slowly becomes stranger than common grass — until the forest starts to notice.

## Serial discipline (kept from the old project's method — the good parts)
- A living bible (`bluesilver_bible/`) and this foundation. Every chapter updates both when a
  fact moves (two-copies law: when one file changes a fact, the copies change in the same
  turn).
- Each chapter stages at least one scene from a **non-protagonist point of view** — a beast,
  a rival, a companion — sourced from the world we've built, so the legend of the impossible
  weed grows in other eyes, never only in his own narration.
- Every chapter footer records: position (age/tier/place), what canon was touched and how it
  stayed true, what changed in the world because of his presence, and open wires.
- A verify pass runs after each chapter (structure, canon rails, language law, pronoun/
  naming consistency).

## Prose laws (locked with Chapter 1)
1. **Tense:** body narration is in the **simple past tense** throughout the plant's lived
   passages and the narrator's zoom-outs — the voice Chapter 1 actually established and
   every chapter since has held. *(2026-09 audit: an earlier line of this law read "present
   tense"; all six published chapters were written and accepted in past tense, so the law
   is corrected to the prose, not the prose to the law.)* The only present-tense writing is
   the short italic synopsis under each chapter title, which previews the chapter's
   situation outside time; body narration itself never shifts tense. One consistent tense,
   never a jarring shift.
2. **POV:** limited, near-sensorial, from inside the blade. The world is felt, not
   described by an omniscient eye: light as warmth/tide, the stream as ground-tremor and
   wetness, weather as pressure and knock, creatures as rhythm and shadow. He has no eyes
   and no ears in the human sense; prose never cheats him sight or hearing. Sighted
   descriptions are allowed only in non-protagonist scenes or narrator zoom-outs where the
   canon discipline permits.
3. **Pronoun and name:** the blade is **"he"** from the start (identity/voice choice),
   though he has no name and no gendered body. Prose calls him the blade, the weed, the
   sprout, the impossible one. Nothing names him until the story earns it (likely A Yin).
4. **No thought-words for the early mind:** until language enters him, his interior is
   rendered as sensation, appetite, memory, fear, warmth — never as English sentences in his
   head. The narrator may translate a feeling ("he did not know the word for alone") but may
   not let him think in words.
5. **Time-keeping:** a grass does not count years; the narration counts events (first cold,
   first bite, first flood). Explicit calendar ranges belong in chapter footers and the
   serial log, not the prose. Round felt counts may surface in prose as the narrator's
   approximation of his stored record ("a hundred winters of keeping", "two hundred years
   of kept essence") — never as precise age statements. **Exact figures (actual age, carried
   cultivation, class, ring) appear in the chapter-top STATUS PANEL only — never in the
   narrative paragraphs (see STATUS_PANEL.md).**
6. ~~**Every chapter footer**~~ **REVOKED by the rebuild.** No chapter footer. Bookkeeping
   (position, canon touched, world changes, adaptation signs, wires left open) lives in the
   kit — `REBUILD_CONTINUITY.md` — never in the chapter file. The apparatus had outgrown
   the story; that was one of the five diagnosed failures.
7. **Chapter-top STATUS PANEL — SLIM FORM (rebuild).** Each chapter opens with a short
   fenced panel under the title carrying only: year range, KIND, AGE, CLASS, THREAT (and
   BOND / LOSS / LAW where the chapter turns on one). Not the eleven-field system window.
   Exact figures are permitted **inside the panel only**; prose carries round felt counts
   spoken or thought by characters, never ledger numbers. It is meta-text for the reader;
   the story voice never sees it.
