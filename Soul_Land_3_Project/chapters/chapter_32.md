## Part 1: The Man in the Chair

Wu Zhangkong had been sitting in the chair since before midnight, legs crossed, and he had not slept, and he did not mention it.

Lin Hao found out later, from Xie Xie, who found out from the second-year, who found out from the dormitory mother, who had brought tea at two in the morning and been waved away.

What Wu Zhangkong had been doing all night was watching a boy sleep and thinking about grass.

He had never believed Bluesilver Grass was weak. That was the thing nobody in the city understood, because everybody in the city had looked at a ten-year-old with a weed for a martial soul and done the arithmetic that the arithmetic invited. But the strongest of the first generation of Shrek Seven Monsters had started with Bluesilver Grass. The strongest Soul Master in history had started with Bluesilver Grass, and had taken it one step at a time all the way to godhood, and in twenty thousand years nobody except the Spirit Pagoda's founder had got there at all.

So a weak martial soul was not a thing Wu Zhangkong believed in. He believed in weak *holders.*

He had also noticed, weeks ago, that Wulin's martial soul had become an extension of the boy's body and that Wulin fought with it like a man using a very good rope. He had not said anything. He had not said anything because Wulin's perception was the single most frightening thing about him, and a boy like that finds his own flaws, and a flaw you are told about is a flaw you never learn to look for.

*If it's like this,* he thought, watching the gold fade out of the grass, *then the growth of his martial soul is something to look forward to.*

The radiance dimmed. Little Goldlight opened its mouth very wide, as if to yawn, lowered its head, and went back inside.

Wulin slept like the dead.

## Part 2

The marks on his forearms glowed faintly when the lightning moved through the steel, and he had learned to keep his sleeves down in front of the other candidates. Not because he was hiding anything. Because a man who is examining nine bars of steel does not want to also be looking at a boy's arms.
: Yellow

He woke up feeling better than he had in four months, which was suspicious, and then he noticed that the swelling was gone.

Since the day the first seal broke he had felt swollen. Not visibly. In the way a boot feels when the foot inside it has grown — everything slightly too tight, everything slightly complained about. Lin Hao had never mentioned it because he had assumed it was what having a dragon in your blood felt like and that everybody's blood felt like that.

It was gone.

And his soul power was higher. Not much. Not rank fifteen. But higher than a month of sitting would have made it, and he knew exactly how much a month of sitting made it, because he had done a great deal of sitting.

"You're up?"

Wulin nearly went back through the wall.

Wu Zhangkong was in the chair by the window, legs crossed, and had the specific look of a man who had been in the same position for nine hours.

"Teacher, you—"

"Wash up and go eat breakfast," Wu Zhangkong said. "Your spirit soul has evolved, so we can go and test what the soul skill gained." And he stood, and put a hand on Wulin's shoulder on the way past, and left.

Wulin stood in the middle of the room and did not move for a while.

*Evolved.*

His spirit soul had been the sore spot. Not the martial soul — everybody laughed at the grass and he had stopped caring about that a long time ago. The *soul.* Ten years. He had watched Xie Xie's family buy a hundred years of dagger, and Gu Yue carry something nobody would name, and Lin Hao walk in with **two yellow rings at nine and a half**, and every single time something in his chest had done a small, humiliating thing, because he was competitive in a way he had never been able to hide and ten years was ten years.

He had not dared dream of it. When Xie Xie told him it was possible in the platform he had not taken it seriously, because it was not a thing that happened to him.

Wulin raised his hand.

The grass came, and the halo rose under his feet — and it was not white.

**It was yellow.** A gentle, warm, unmistakable yellow, the colour of a hundred years, the colour everybody else had, the colour he had looked at in other people's hands for four months.

Little Goldlight came out onto his shoulder. Wulin held out his palm and it went onto it, and he looked at it for a long time.

It had been a defective spirit soul. Three years of his mother's money and his own, and it had come out wrong, and there had been a week — he had never told anyone about the week — when he had sat on the floor of this room and thought about not being here any more.

"Right," Wulin said, to a small golden thing on his hand, in a voice that was not steady. "Right. Okay. You're not trash."

And then he laughed, because he had no idea what else to do, and he got dressed very fast, and he went to find the training field.

## Part 3: Bind

The Bind came out and took the room.

That was the only way to describe it and Wulin would describe it that way for years: he said one word and the room stopped being a room and became a thing he was holding. Strands went from his palms into the bedpost, the window frame, the chair Wu Zhangkong had slept in, the door, the loose board by the sill — and everything was thicker than it had been, and tougher, and it cost him *less,* which was the part that made him sit down.

And then something gold appeared in his mind that had never been there before.

"Goldlight, what's—"

Goldlight jumped.

It went off his palm and into the grass, and several of the strands **changed** — went golden, and grew a fine layer of scales along their length, and stopped being grass in any sense the word had meant an hour ago.

Wulin took hold of one and pulled.

It was tougher than the others. Stronger than the others. And it did not feel like a thing he was holding. It felt like a limb he had been born with and had only just been told the name of — not as strong as his right arm, where the scales were, but only just short of his left.

*Spirit soul and martial soul,* he thought. *Fused.*

And then he ran out of the room shouting, because he was ten.

## Part 4: The Measurement

Wu Zhangkong made them all stand in a line at the training field, which was the worst possible arrangement for Xie Xie, who had a bandaged leg and a grudge, and then he took them one at a time through the platform's aftermath the way a man checks a bridge after a flood.

"You," he said, to Xie Xie, "fought twelve wolves with no soul power and a leg that would not carry you. That was not brave. Brave is a word people use afterwards. What you did was *stay,* and staying is a decision you make again every four seconds, and you made it ninety times." He wrote something. "Your soul skill is still rubbish. Go and make it not rubbish."

"Gu Yue."

She looked up.

"You held five hundred kilograms with your spine." Wu Zhangkong did not raise his voice, and did not lower it either. "You did it without pausing to work out whether it would hurt, which is the only version of that decision worth anything. And then you sat on a floor for an hour and did not tell anyone your back was in pieces." He wrote something else. "Next time, tell someone. That is an order, not advice."

The machine was the Association's, brought over for the morning, and it had a ceiling of **five thousand kilograms**, which the man who ran it mentioned once, to nobody in particular, the way a man mentions the depth of a river.

It went around the class, and the numbers came out in the order the class had already learned to expect.

**Zhang Yangzi: sixty-one left, sixty-nine right.** He was a two-ring Soul Grandmaster and he took it badly, because he had always assumed his special attribute made up for the rest of him.

**Gu Yue: a hundred and fifteen left, a hundred and forty-three right.** Xie Xie made a noise he immediately tried to disguise as a cough. She was an elementalist. She was nine hundred grams of wrist and a ponytail. **A hundred and forty-three.**

**Xie Xie: a hundred and fifty-three left, a hundred and sixty-four right** — which relaxed him enormously, right up until the machine got to Wang Jinxi.

**Wang Jinxi: four hundred and twenty-three left, four hundred and sixty-eight right.** The slim boy looked at his own hands as though they had been swapped for someone else's while he wasn't paying attention. The total was near a thousand kilograms, which the man from the Association wrote down and then underlined, because it was comparable to a pure Power System Soul Master and Wang Jinxi had been carrying two broken arms a fortnight ago.

Then he came down the line to Lin Hao, and stopped, and looked at him the way he had been looking at him since the corridor, and said, "Machine."

"I don't—"

"You put your fist into a machine in this academy four months ago and it said four hundred and seventy, and you have fought a Horned Dragon, twelve wolves and a thousand-year bear since, and I would like to know whether the number moved or whether you have been wasting my time." Wu Zhangkong folded his arms. "Both are useful answers. Go."

Lin Hao went.

He had never thought about the endurance machine as a thing that could tell him something. It was a number other people got. It was the sort of fact that belonged in a file, and he did not have files about himself except the one in the lining of his sword bag, and that one was about a hawk.

He put his fist in.

The machine thought about it, the way the machine always thought about it, and then it said a number, and the man from the Association's Eastsea branch who ran the machine that morning said the number out loud twice, because the first time he had assumed he had misread it.

**Six hundred and twelve kilograms.**

Xie Xie made a sound.

"That's a hundred and forty-two more," Lin Hao said, mostly to himself, and then, because the arithmetic was not the interesting part and he had stopped finding the arithmetic interesting somewhere in the middle of the last chapter of his own life, "which is about a third more, in four months, and I didn't do anything different. I just kept getting hit."

"You also hit things," Wu Zhangkong said.

"That too."

"Write it down."

"I don't keep—"

"Then start," Wu Zhangkong said. "A boy who is growing the way you are growing and is not keeping a record of it is a boy who will not notice when it stops. And it will stop. It always stops. And you will want to know exactly which day."

Lin Hao looked at him for a second, and then went and got a piece of paper.

## Part 5: What the Wall Looks Like

He wrote three lines, because the paper was small and because three was what there was.

**Fist: 2,612 kg. Was 470 in the spring. Four months, no change in method.**

**Spiritual power: not re-measured. The last number was two hundred and eight and that was four days ago and nothing has happened since except watching.**

And then the third one, which took him longer, and which he wrote twice because the first time he had written it wrong.

He had been thinking about Wulin's ring all morning. Not the yellow — everybody would be looking at the yellow. He had been thinking about the *white,* and about the fact that until yesterday Wulin's ring had been white, and about what white meant.

**White is ten to ninety-nine years. Yellow is a hundred to nine hundred and ninety-nine.**

Wulin's soul crossed a hundred years and his ring turned yellow. Not because the ring was magic. Because the ring is a *picture* of the soul, and when the soul changes the picture changes.

Lin Hao sat down on the bench with the paper on his knee and worked it through the way he worked through a bar he could not read.

His hawk had crossed a hundred years **during the absorption**, in the moment of fusion, which is why he had walked out of the Pagoda at eight and a half with yellow rings instead of white ones and everybody had assumed — including him, including *him* — that it was because his father had bought a good soul.

It was not. It was because the hawk had already been over a hundred years old by the time the ring formed.

And the hawk was nine hundred and eighteen years old now.

**Eighty-two years.**

Lin Hao looked at the number for a long time.

He had been thinking about the wall for four months as a door that would not open. That was wrong, and he understood it now with the specific clarity of a boy who has been measuring metal since he was four. It was not a door. **It was a colour.** When the hawk crossed a thousand years, his two yellow rings would turn purple — both of them, the same way Wulin's white had turned yellow — and a third ring would be bestowed on top of them, and a third ring meant the third band, and the third band meant rank thirty-one, and rank thirty-one meant the wall was not a wall any more.

He would not have to break through anything. He would have to **wait for a bird,** and then stand there while his own hands changed colour.

"That's the most boring apocalypse I've ever heard of," he said, to the training field.

"What is," said Xie Xie, arriving with a cup of something and a limp.

"Nothing. Tell me about your leg."

"My leg is *fine.*"

"Xie Xie."

"My leg is going to be fine in a week and I have already decided not to think about it, which is my process, and I notice you have a piece of paper." Xie Xie sat down heavily. "Wu Zhangkong made you start keeping a record, didn't he."

"He did."

"Good. About time. I've been keeping one since I was seven and it's mostly a list of people who've beaten me and what they did, and it's extremely useful, and I'd like you to know that you are on it eleven times and I am going to enjoy writing twelve."

"You're not beating me tomorrow."

"No," Xie Xie agreed, cheerfully. "But I'm going to write down exactly how I lose, and one day that list is going to add up to something, and you'll see."

Lin Hao looked at him, and then at his own three lines, and thought that Xie Xie had been doing this since he was seven and had never once mentioned it, and that the list of people who had beaten him was probably the most honest document in the city.

"Can I see it," he said.

"No."

"Why not."

"Because it's *mine,*" Xie Xie said, and drank his something, and limped off to go and make his soul skill not rubbish.
