## Part 1: One Minute, Spent Right

The first blade fell, and the rest followed it one after another, a metal clamor filling the yard like a shop at full shift.

"Get ready. The one-minute countdown begins now."

The gears of Tang Wulin's mind turned, and turned fast, and came out with the spine of it: "We need to cross through the guillotine gates within thirty seconds. The shortest path has six gates... Gu Yue, test the power of the blades."

A cool head was the whole trial, and the class had one each and a spare.

Xie Xie was already coiled to sprint. Xiaoyan stared at the gates, pale. And Gu Yue, calm as a closed book, held out her hand, palm to the sky — blue light condensed into a two-meter icicle and shot through the bottom of the nearest guillotine.

Clang!

The blade turned most of it to fine powder and split the thickest part in two.

"The descent of one blade produces about three thousand kilograms of force," Gu Yue announced — an analysis only her spiritual connection with the elements could price.

"Can you do it?" Tang Wulin asked.

"I can protect one person."

"How about you, Xie Xie?"

Leaping from the ground: "I'm fine."

And then Gu Yue said, "How about I protect Xu Xiaoyan — and Lin Hao carries me on his back. It shouldn't be a problem with his physical strength. Xie Xie can then follow right behind us. That should work."

Wulin considered it — his own plan had been the more logical one, and hers spent the seams on carrying, which nobody could account for; he eyed her suspiciously anyway, brother to the boy being volunteered. A slight blush appeared on Gu Yue's face and was put away again just as fast.

"Ten... nine... eight..."

No time to change anything. She mounted Lin Hao's back; a wave of her hand wrapped green light around Wulin, Xie Xie, and Xiaoyan; and in the last of the countdown the class's sixth voice spent its two seconds, flat, where only the class could hear it.

"I'm the margin," Lin Hao said. "Nothing goes wrong, you never see me. Rows in threes."

"Three... two... one... begin!"

## Part 2: The Catch

Gu Yue chanted, hands raised, clinging to Lin Hao's back with her legs. Yellow light — a stone pillar shot up under the first gate and took the blade with a clang that cracked it web-wide.

"Quickly!"

And Xiaoyan's fear lost the argument it had been having with four years of trust. She clenched her jaw, leaned, and DASHED — through the first gate to join Xie Xie, who had flashed through it like a rumor.

Lin Hao ran the seams with Gu Yue on his back; Wulin held the line beside him — and directly under the guillotine, the stone pillar collapsed.

The gigantic blade plunged down at the two of them.

Gu Yue shut her eyes and wrapped her arms around Lin Hao's neck as if she had no intention of leaving it — her heart racing, probably because of the tense situation. Lin Hao noted it the way he noted everything, at speed and without comment: the rows breathed in threes, and so did he.

And Wulin was already moving — a step, a lunge, his shoulder under the falling iron his brother and his friend stood beneath.

*Isn't this just three thousand kilograms of force? And weakened by the pillar at that.*

His blood roared. The golden dragon claw appeared on his raised right arm, thrust above him, struck the enormous blade —

— and held it aloft.

On the sidelines, Shen Yi was dumbstruck. Countless students had faced this trial; every one had approached it differently; this was the first in its history to brazenly GRAB the blade. Over a thousand kilograms of iron, three thousand of falling force — she nearly shut her eyes against the blood she was sure was coming.

Clang!

Tang Wulin caught it easily, squatting a little under the load. "Up!" — and his arm filled with strength and threw the blade high, and Lin Hao stepped through the gate with Gu Yue on his back, and the captain cleared his own beside them.

"Boss, are you even human!?" Xie Xie, staring in horror.

"Gu Yue, you don't need to do anything else. I can do this by myself."

He could, and did, and it was that simple: the second gate's blade dropped and was caught and thrown up; the third, the fourth, the fifth — Xiaoyan and Xie Xie passing under each held ceiling before him — the golden dragon claw treating the academy's dreaded guillotines like toys. He walked between the gates at a stride, catching half-ton slabs of falling iron the way a farmer lifts gates for his family.

## Part 3: The Margin

Nothing went wrong. That was the whole point of the margin: it was not supposed to be seen.

Lin Hao ran the rows beside the team's route, in the seams of the falling grammar — threes, with the fall, never against it — arriving at each gate a breath before the class reached it, positioned for the failure that never came: a pillar crumbling early, a blade loosed out of time, a foot snagging on the recess lip. For six gates he was the second answer to six questions the first answer had already solved, and the trial never learned his name.

Once — only once, at the fifth gate, under the iron Wulin held sky-high while Xiaoyan and Xie Xie slipped through — he put his palm flat against the held blade for half a second. Not helping. READING. Up the arm, the way metal's opinions arrive: the release, the track, the counterweight, the spring. Six gates' worth of falling iron, priced in half a second each, on the way through.

At the far end, six gates done, the class reformed around their captain — and the margin delivered its maintenance report, because a margin that stays silent about what it has read is just scenery.

"Your sixth gate drags," Lin Hao said, to Shen Yi, in the voice he used for numbers. "Half a beat, every third fall. The spring's tired — metal's tired, it rings flat. It'll start hanging soon, and a hanging blade is a lying blade; the next examinee will read the rhythm and the rhythm will change on them." A beat. "I can show your smiths, if they'd rather see than believe."

Shen Yi looked at him. The file — three winters thick, one morning richer by a sword-line and a golden ring — acquired its third page before noon.

"...I'll pass it to the hall," she said, in the voice of a woman adding a line item to a budget she had thought was closed.

## Part 4: Fourteen

"Fourteen seconds!" Shen Yi called, and the number rang across the yard like the blades had.

Wulin swung his arm, flexed his shoulder, and let the claw go. And under the flex, quietly, the morning's other bill came due: six catches, sustained, held, thrown — the gold had been drinking blood essence the whole time, and his stomach announced the invoice to the entire yard, long and eloquent.

Xie Xie's grin arrived before anyone's dignity could organize. "Captain. You can bench a guillotine. You cannot bench breakfast."

"I ate," Wulin said, with dignity, "three of everything."

"That was BEFORE you became heavy machinery."

The arithmetic of the trial had already happened in the scoring: thirty seconds for six points, and every three seconds early one more — and fourteen seconds early by sixteen, which the cap trimmed to a full ten apiece. Five for five, three trials deep, and the wager — the duel that only happened if they failed — riding quietly behind the numbers, alive.

Shen Yi watched them bicker toward the next door and let her proctor's face do her interior for her: *this kid is looking more and more like a monster.* The trial was built to test adaptation, reaction, courage — most examinees went through one at a time, because nobody plans cooperation in a minute; most examinees under fifteen looked at the guillotines and could not move; the first trial killed the many (spiritual power too weak), the second passed the most (a simple battle), and the third weeded half of the brave remainder on courage alone. For class zero it had been the easiest morning of their lives. Tang Wulin had single-handedly dealt with everything, propping up the heavens while the others crossed under his arms — and the sixth one, the part her file kept underlining, had spent the same fourteen seconds running a seam through the falling iron so that nothing could go wrong, and then told the academy which of its springs was lying.

"Follow me," she said. "There are more trials."

"Of course there are," said Xie Xie, to nobody, with love.
