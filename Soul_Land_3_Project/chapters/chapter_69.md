## Part 1: The Platform

The spirit ascension platform was a door, and the door cost contribution points, and Lin Hao had contribution points, because he had been making metal for a month and a first-grade thousand refining paid eleven hundred.

He had gone in before. He had gone in at Eastsea, when he was ten, and he had come out with a ledger full of wolves and a hawk that had grown. He had not been back since.

The platform at Shrek was bigger. It had tiers — elementary, intermediate, high — and the tiers were about how old the soul beasts were, and the high tier was where the ten-thousand-year things were, and the high tier cost more contribution points than Lin Hao had.

He had gone in at the intermediate tier, because that was what he could afford, and because the intermediate tier was where the thousand-year things were, and because he had been getting ready for a thing since he was six and he did not know the thing was in there.

---

## Part 2: What Was In There

The intermediate tier was a forest, and the forest was full of thousand-year soul beasts, and Lin Hao had been in a forest full of thousand-year soul beasts before, and he was not frightened.

He had been in there for two hours. He had fought four soul beasts and won, and the hawk had grown, and the ledger had grown, and he was about to go out, because he had earned enough contribution points and the forest was not the thing he had come for.

"Four," Lin Hao said, to nobody, because he said things out loud when he was alone and had been doing it since he was six. "That's four. That's enough for the points. I should go out."

He did not go out.

And then the hawk went still.

The hawk had gone still before. It had gone still before weather, and before the jiao — no, not the jiao, he did not know the word yet — before something. It had gone still in the forest at Eastsea, and he had gone toward it then, and it had been a wolf, and he had won.

He went toward it now.

The stillness was not like the wolf. The wolf had been a hunger. This was not a hunger. This was a tiredness, and it was old, and it was deep in the forest where the thousand-year things did not go, because whatever was there was older than the thousand-year things and the thousand-year things knew it.

Lin Hao went toward it, because the hawk had gone still, and because he had been getting ready for a thing since he was six and the thing was in there.

---

## Part 3: The Flood-Dragon

It was in a pool.

It was a dragon. Not a true dragon — it was a jiao, a flood-dragon, the thing a dragon is before it is a dragon, and it was ice and water, and it was ten thousand years old, and it was dying.

Lin Hao had seen a dragon before. He had seen the Golden Dragon King in Wulin, in the gold, in the scale. He had seen the bone dragon in Wang Jinxi. He had never seen a jiao.

It was long, and it was the color of deep water, and it had ice along its spine, and it was in a pool that had frozen around it, and it was dying, and it had been dying for a long time.

The hawk went still.

The jiao opened its eye.

Lin Hao had been getting ready for a thing since he was six. He had not known what the thing was. He had made horseshoes, and straps, and a strap that did not know he was better than the bottom, and he had registered a badge at eleven, and a woman in a hall had said blacksmithing was the most appropriate profession for creating powerful battle armors.

And now he was standing in front of a ten-thousand-year ice-and-water jiao in a frozen pool in the spirit ascension platform, and the jiao was dying, and it had opened its eye, and it was looking at him.

The hawk went still.

"You're tired," Lin Hao said. He said it out loud, because he said things out loud when he was alone, and because the jiao was looking at him and it seemed like the thing to say. "You've been dying for a long time."

The jiao did not answer. It could not. It was ten thousand years old and dying and had been dying for a long time, and it had opened its eye, and it was looking at him, and it was tired.

And the jiao, which was ten thousand years old and dying and had been dying for a long time, surrendered itself to him.

It did not fight. It did not hunger. It chose him — the way a spirit soul chooses a soul master when it is tired and old and ready to be something else's, and the choosing was not a surrender to a stronger thing, it was a choosing of a thing that was ready, and Lin Hao was ready, and he had been ready since he was six.

---

## Part 4: The Absorption

The absorption began.

It was not like the hawk. The hawk had been a settling, a thing finding a place. This was not a settling. This was a flood, and it was ice and water, and it was ten thousand years old, and it was going into him, and it was not going to be quick.

Lin Hao had absorbed a spirit soul before. The hawk had taken a day. This was not going to take a day.

He understood, standing in the frozen pool with the jiao going into him, that this was going to take a long time, and that the jiao was ten thousand years old and he was eleven, and that a ten-thousand-year soul going into an eleven-year-old was not a thing that happened quickly, and that he was not going to walk out of the platform today.

The hawk went still. The jiao went in. The pool froze around him.

Lin Hao had been getting ready for a thing since he was six. He had not known what the thing was. And now the thing was going into him, and it was ice and water and ten thousand years old, and it was going to take a month, and he was not frightened, because he had been getting ready for it since he was six.

---

## Part 5: What Lin Hao Wrote

He could not write. He was in the pool, and the jiao was going into him, and he could not write.

But he had written, before, on the way in, because he had a habit and the habit was the thing he did.

*Three hundred and thirty-eight. One thousand four hundred and ninety. Forty.*

*I went into the platform to earn contribution points. I fought four soul beasts and won. The hawk grew. The ledger grew. I was about to go out.*

*And then the hawk went still.*

*I went toward it. It was a jiao. A flood-dragon. Ice and water. Ten thousand years old. Dying. It opened its eye and it surrendered itself to me. It chose me. I have been getting ready for a thing since I was six and I did not know what the thing was, and the thing was a ten-thousand-year ice-and-water jiao, and it chose me, and it is going into me, and it is going to take a month.*

He had written that before he went into the pool. He had not known he was writing the last thing he would write for a month.

The hawk went still. The jiao went in. The pool froze around him.

Lin Hao had been getting ready for a thing since he was six. He had not known what the thing was. And now the thing was going into him, and it was ice and water and ten thousand years old, and it was going to take a month, and he was not frightened, because he had been getting ready for it since he was six, and the boy who had been getting ready was not the boy who had had the cake, and the thing was going to change him, and he was not frightened, because he had been getting ready for it since he was six.
