## Part 1: The Tenth

Shen Yi walked to the three elders and bowed, and stood aside.

"The tenth trial," she said, "is also known as the Three Hall Review. These three elders will evaluate your performance across the trials and give each of you a comprehensive score, added to your total."

Three Hall Review. The words went down the row of five like cold water down a collar — because the middlemost of the three, grey-haired and still as a closed door, was bad enough; but on his left stood Elder Cai, the Silver Moon Douluo, the examiner who had failed them twice and enjoyed it once.

Wulin glanced at Gu Yue. Her brows were drawn. But her eyes, as always, held that stubborn light.

*She's only short two points,* he thought. *This trial—*

"Tang Wulin," said the grey-haired elder, voice deep as a well. "Step up."

## Part 2: The Reads

Wulin bowed and stood at attention, and the old man studied him the way Lin Hao had seen men study metal — not unkindly, entirely without mercy.

"Full marks for seven trials, zero for two," the elder said. "Brave. Wise. A capable leader — we have been watching you the entire time. Your performance has been remarkable. Simply outstanding." A pause. "However, we will hold your score until later. We want you to take a makeup trial."

A makeup. Wulin thanked him, confusion and all, and stepped back. Seventy points; he was safe whatever happened.

"Xie Xie."

Xie Xie stepped up like a man approaching his own verdict.

"Your talent is slightly higher than average. Your situational awareness is poor." Xie Xie's face began its preparations for doom. "However, you have the courage to charge head-first in the face of danger. On the whole, satisfactory. Six points."

Sixty-seven. Xie Xie thanked the elder with the dignity of a man whose scaffold has just turned into a floor, and came back trying not to look relieved. He had feared a negative number most of all; some of his trials had been, in his own private accounting, embarrassing.

"Xu Xiaoyan."

She went up small and came back tall. "Your talent is average," the elder said, "but you have absolute control over the astrological attributes of your variant martial soul. You work well with your team, you have a clever mind, and you are never discouraged by your own weaknesses. Your potential is quite impressive. Seven points."

Sixty-seven, and a shy smile, and for a moment the roof was almost warm.

"Lin Hao."

The fifth examinee walked to the mark and bowed — correctly, the way you bow to a customer who has already paid.

The grey-haired elder looked at him for a long moment. Beside the elder, Elder Cai's page — a real page, paper, worn soft at one corner — turned.

"Seventy points," the elder said. "Full marks for seven trials, zero for two, and the two zeros are their own story. We have watched you herd your class through fire, farm the edges of every fight you were too strong to be seen winning, and close a door on a legend with one stroke — Sword Intent holding the seam, four elements in a single line, and not one ring lit above what the work needed. There are teachers in this academy who could not have kept that discipline. There are none who could have kept it at thirteen." The page at Cai's side turned again, unhurried. "Like your captain, your score is held. You will both take the makeup trial. What we want to see," and here the old man's voice went dry as a forge, "is what you do when the work is your own."

*Both.* Wulin and Lin Hao, seventy and seventy, the tie held closed like a coin in a fist.

"Gu Yue."

Gu Yue went up and bowed, chin level, and the grey-haired elder said nothing at all. He looked to his left.

Elder Cai did not stand. She didn't need to.

"Gu Yue," she said, "you're arrogant and insolent. You are gifted — I'll grant the gift, it's cheap to grant. But you lack all situational awareness, and your actions have damaged your own team. You are impulsive. You are unreasonable. I do not think you are suitable for Shrek Academy." A beat, exactly long enough. "One point. Your total is fifty-nine."

## Part 3: The Number

Fifty-nine.

The number sat on the roof like a stone on a scale. Gu Yue raised her head and glared at Elder Cai, biting her lip, trembling — and standing. Proud. Not asking.

"Tang Wulin, Xie Xie, Xu Xiaoyan," the grey-haired elder said. "You are accepted into the academy. Tang Wulin, you will take the fifth trial again in a moment. Gu Yue — you have fifty-nine points. You can go now."

The stone dropped the rest of the way.

Everyone had always said it: if only one of them made it into Shrek Academy, it would be Gu Yue. Every teacher, every classmate, every odds-maker in their year — Gu Yue, the six elements, the once-a-generation mind. And she was the one standing at fifty-nine with her chin up and the door closing.

An arm went around her shoulders. Wulin pulled her in, trembling as she was trembling, and looked straight at the three elders, and his voice came out in a way none of them — not the class, not the teachers, not once in three years — had ever heard it.

"I don't accept this."

Zhuo Shi — though the class did not yet know his name — sat back inside his own stillness, genuinely taken aback. He would not have been surprised to hear it from Gu Yue. From the captain, the steady one, the boy with seventy points and a safe bed at the finest academy on the continent — from *him*, the words had a different weight.

"Why do you not accept it?"

"Respected elders." Wulin's voice shook, and held. "May I ask if Shrek Academy is fair?"

A cold smile moved on Elder Cai's lips. "Nothing is fair in this world. If you want fair, you need strength. Justice is fought for — it is not freely and willingly given. Of course, that is on the condition that you are powerful enough to make everyone sit down and hear it."

Wulin stood still. Then he nodded to her, and bowed — a small, precise bow, the bow you give a teacher of the hard kind.

"Thank you for the advice. I've learned from it. Then I have nothing more to say." He straightened. "Elders — I renounce my Shrek Academy admittance. One day, I will come back. One day, when I'm strong enough, I'll come back and enforce the justice myself."

He turned to Xie Xie and Xu Xiaoyan, stern as a captain on a deck. "You two don't follow me. This is Gu Yue's business and mine. Stay, and cultivate properly."

Xie Xie looked at him. Then he stuck his hands in his pockets and strolled over, unhurried, grinning like a knife.

"Since my talent's only average, the academy clearly won't miss me." He put a hand on his captain's shoulder. "And Captain — you made a nobody with a mouth into part of a shape that beat everything this school could throw at it. You want to know when I decided? You put the class ahead of your own score in every single trial, and I've been keeping count since the first week. I'll follow you for the rest of my life. We'll come back when we're strong enough to *demand* justice."

"I never wanted to be a soul master anyway," Xu Xiaoyan added, arriving at Wulin's other side as if remarking on the weather. "My old man forced me. And the pressure here is far too high. Let's go home."

"You guys—" Wulin's voice failed him entirely.

He turned to Shen Yi instead, and bowed low. "Teacher Shen. I'm sorry. It seems we aren't qualified to be Shrek's students after all. It was my dream — the dream broke. May I ask where Teacher Wu is? We should tell him we're leaving."

Emotions warred openly across Shen Yi's face.

## Part 4: The Fifth's Answer

Four of five stood in a row against the roof's door, and every eye on that roof swung to the fifth.

Lin Hao had not moved. He stood where the review had left him, at the mark, hands loose, the way he stood at the forge between heats — and when he spoke, it was not to his class, and it was not heat at all.

"Elder Cai." He addressed the page itself. "One point. That's the gap. Name the price of the point — I'll pay it."

The roof went very quiet.

"Points aren't coins, blacksmith," Elder Cai said.

"Everything is coins," Lin Hao said. "You priced our character at ten. You priced a calm butchery at six. You priced her at one — that's not a grade, that's an insult with a number on it, and I've been insulted by professionals." He did not raise his voice; the roof's acoustics did the work, and the flat forge-carry reached every elder on it. "So either the number is honest and she's unfit — and every trial we just survived says otherwise — or the number is a test, and the price of the point exists somewhere. I'm asking for the counter. Name it."

For one unguarded moment, the page in Cai's hand was still.

"The makeup trial," the grey-haired elder said at last, and there was something in the deep voice that might, in a different man, have been delight, "is for Tang Wulin and Lin Hao. Not for Gu Yue. There is no counter to name, examinee. The trial is the trial."

"Then I'll take the makeup," Lin Hao said. He looked at Wulin — at his captain, arm still around Gu Yue, dream broken and set down like a full crate. "You go enforce justice your way. I'll stay and make them count honestly in front of everyone — that's mine. Between the two of us, she gets both."

Wulin looked back at him. Three years looked back. Whatever he might have said, he didn't; he nodded, once, the way you accept a tool you know will hold.

## Part 5: Grandteacher

"Good," the grey-haired elder said gloomily, into the silence. "You're all really stubborn. That stubbornness must have been taught to you. Very good. Nicely done. Now leave. Get out, all of you."

The class — mid-departure, half-bowed, one foot each into their different futures — froze.

Shen Yi darted a meaningful look at the five. "This elder," she said, quietly, quickly, "is senior brother Wu's teacher. Which makes him your *grandteacher*. Hurry up and pay your respects."

*Grandteacher.* Teacher Wu's teacher. The stillness at the center of the roof reassembled itself in every head at once: no wonder the old man sounded like a colder version of their teacher's silences.

Wulin tugged Gu Yue's sleeve and bowed. "I pay my respects to Grandteacher." Xie Xie and Xiaoyan followed. Gu Yue, nudged, bowed last of all.

Zhuo Shi's expression lightened a few shades, and not one degree warmer. "Do you all think you're something amazing? Renouncing everything for the sake of your companions, huh? You *fools*. Why did you even come here? To show off your stubbornness? Your pride? You know how to fight, but do you know how to beg? Did Wu Zhangkong teach you this stubbornness? It really is like teacher, like student — a whole bundle of stubborn fools—"

Shen Yi's mouth-corner twitched. *Aren't you the one who taught senior brother? Do your words not apply to yourself, Grandteacher?*

"Grandteacher," Xu Xiaoyan said, in her smallest, most disarming voice, "may I ask you a question?"

"Hm?" Zhuo Shi's eyes shifted to her.

Her eyes welled, reddened, brimmed. "Grandteacher — is this grandma more amazing than you?"

The question hit the old man like a dropped anvil. He side-eyed Elder Cai. "…We're about equal."

The tears spilled over, and Xu Xiaoyan, throat stiff with feeling, fired the second barrel: "It's said that once a teacher, forever a father. We've treated Teacher Wu like a father — and Teacher Wu surely treats you like one — so you're our grandfather. You're a *Title Douluo*, and a battle armor master! How can you watch your grandchildren be bullied? We would have passed with points to spare if not for this grandma. Unless—" the pause of a born closer, "—you're afraid of her?"

"Me? Afraid of *her*?" The words were out before the old man's dignity could catch them.

"You're not afraid of her," Xiaoyan said, sweetly, wetly, "and yet you don't care that your granddaughter is being driven out?"

Zhuo Shi stood dumbstruck — he, the famed stubborn ox of Shrek's inner court, he who cowed seniors with a look, out-flanked by a thirteen-year-old with tears on her cheeks and her class's whole fortune in her slingshot.

"That's right — the girl has a point!" he burst out, wheels visibly leaving the ground. "A granddaughter driven off this roof under my own watch, and I'm to stand here like a lamp? *CAI YUE'ER!* What is this? What are you playing at, bullying my granddaughter where I can see it?!"
