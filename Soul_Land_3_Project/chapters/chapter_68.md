## Part 1: The Eleventh Birthday

He turned eleven on a Tuesday, and nobody made a fuss, because nobody at Shrek knew it was his birthday, and he had not told anyone.

He had been eleven for about four hours when he realized he had not thought about it until he woke up, and that the not-thinking was the thing that told him he was not the boy who had had the sixth one.

The sixth one had been a cake. His mother had made a cake, and his father had been home, and Wulin had been there because Wulin was always there, and there had been a knife with eleven marks in it because Lin Hao had been the kind of boy who needed a mark for every year.

He was eleven now, and there was no cake, and there was no mark, and he was sitting on the floor of a working-student dormitory at the greatest academy in the world, and he had not told anybody it was his birthday, and the not-telling was not a sadness, it was just a thing that was true.

He had been getting ready for a thing since he was six. He had not known what the thing was. And now he was eleven, and the thing was a battle armor, and he was going to make the metal for it, and he was not the boy who had had the cake.

---

## Part 2: The Job

Xie Xie found him at the forge, because Xie Xie found people, and because Xie Xie was hungry, and because Xie Xie had learned that Lin Hao was the one who took the forging jobs.

"There's a job," Xie Xie said. "A first-grade thousand refining. Somebody's paying eleven hundred for it."

"The Academy pays a thousand."

"So somebody's paying a hundred more," Xie Xie said. "And there's a one percent fee on student transactions, so it's really eleven hundred minus eleven, which is still more than a thousand, and I want you to know that I did the arithmetic."

Lin Hao had been a blacksmith since he was six. He had made horseshoes, and straps, and a strap that did not know he was better than the bottom, and he had come to Shrek and found the forge behind Spirit Ice Plaza on his first day, and a woman in a hall had said blacksmithing was the most appropriate profession for creating powerful battle armors.

"Who's paying," Lin Hao said.

"Somebody named Yuanen," Xie Xie said. "He's a working student. He's a mecha maker. He wants a first-grade thousand refining and he'll pay eleven hundred and cover nothing, and you cover the transaction fee, and I want you to know that I think that's a bad deal but it's still more than a thousand."

Lin Hao took the job.

## Part 3: The Badge

The Shrek Blacksmith's Association was a building, and it had a clerk, and the clerk had a machine.

Lin Hao had gone to register, because a working student who took forging jobs had to be registered, and because a fourth-rank Grandmaster had a badge and the badge had to be registered at the Association of the place you were working.

"Do you have a blacksmith's badge?" the clerk said. "I'll begin your registration. In case you're not aware, Shrek Blacksmith's Association goes by rules similar to those governing other Blacksmith's Associations across the continent. The greater the blacksmith, the greater their authority."

Lin Hao gave him the badge.

It was orange with four yellow stars. It was the badge of a fourth-rank blacksmith. He had been carrying it since he was ten, since the day he had passed the examination at Eastsea, and he had not looked at it much, because it was a thing he had and not a thing he thought about.

The clerk took the badge with a hand that did something the clerk did not mean it to do.

"This badge's color scheme," the clerk said. "You're a fourth-rank blacksmith."

"Yes."

The clerk looked at Lin Hao. Then he looked at the badge. Then he looked at Lin Hao again, because he had not encountered a fourth-rank blacksmith this young before, and because fourth-rank blacksmiths were not hard to find at Shrek but they were all older, because blacksmithing took a long time, and because the boy in front of him was eleven.

The clerk put the badge in a slot on the machine.

"Lin Hao," the machine said. "Male. Born in Glorybound City. Member of Eastsea Blacksmith's Association. Fourth-rank blacksmith. Second place, junior division, Skysea Alliance Tournament blacksmithing competition." The machine paused, because the machine had a record and the record had an age on it. "Age eleven."

The clerk looked at Lin Hao for a long time.

"You're eleven," the clerk said.

"Yes."

"You're a fourth-rank blacksmith and you're eleven."

"Yes."

The clerk had been a clerk at the Shrek Blacksmith's Association for nineteen years, and he had registered a great many fourth-rank blacksmiths, and not one of them had been eleven, and he did not say anything else, because there was nothing else to say.

## Part 4: What the Badge Was

Lin Hao had been carrying the badge since he was ten.

He had not thought about it much. It was a thing he had, like the hawk, like the ledger, like the marks on his forearms. He had passed the examination at Eastsea because Mu Chen had put him up for it, and he had passed it because he had been making metal since he was six, and the badge was a thing that said he was a fourth-rank blacksmith, and he had not thought about what the badge was.

And now a clerk at the greatest academy in the world had looked at the badge and looked at him and said *you're eleven,* and Lin Hao understood, standing at the registration desk, that the badge was not a thing he had. The badge was a thing that said what he was, and he had been carrying it for a year without looking at it, and the clerk had looked at it and seen what it said.

*The most appropriate profession for creating powerful battle armors,* the woman in the hall had said.

Lin Hao was eleven. He was a fourth-rank blacksmith. He was going to make the metal for a battle armor, and he had been getting ready for it since he was six, and the badge was the thing that said he was on the road.

---

## Part 5: What Gu Yue Knew

Gu Yue found him at the forge after the badge was registered, because Gu Yue found him at the forge the way the tide finds the shore, without deciding to.

"You registered the badge," she said.

"Yes."

"The clerk told the whole Association."

"The clerk told the machine," Lin Hao said. "The machine told the clerk. I don't think the clerk told anybody, because the clerk spent nineteen years registering fourth-rank blacksmiths and none of them were eleven, and I think the clerk is still standing at the machine."

Gu Yue was quiet. She had a way of being quiet that was not empty.

"It's your birthday," she said.

Lin Hao looked at her.

"How did you know."

"You told me your birthday in the forest, four months ago, when you told me everything," Gu Yue said. "You told me your parents' names and your birthday and the thing about the knife with the marks in it. You told me everything and then you left, and I kept it, because I keep things." She paused. "You're eleven."

"Yes."

"Nobody made a fuss."

"No."

Gu Yue was quiet for a while. Then she reached into her robe and took out a small thing, and held it out, and it was a whetstone, and it was not a cake.

"I did not know what to get," Gu Yue said. "You already have the badge. You already have the hawk. You already have the sword. I did not know what to get a boy who has everything and needed a mark for every year, so I got you a whetstone, because a sword needs one and you have everything except the thing that keeps the thing you have sharp."

Lin Hao took the whetstone.

"It's not a cake," Gu Yue said.

"No," Lin Hao said. "It's better. A cake is for a boy who needs a mark. A whetstone is for a boy who has a sword. I am not the boy who had the cake."

"I know," Gu Yue said. "That's why I got it."

Lin Hao had been getting ready for a thing since he was six. He had not known what the thing was. And now he was eleven, and he had a whetstone, and a badge, and a road, and a girl who had kept his birthday for four months because she kept things, and he was not the boy who had had the cake.

---

## Part 6: What Lin Hao Wrote

He wrote it that night, on the floor of the working-student dormitory, because there were five of them and four beds and the floor was still his.

*Three hundred and thirty-one. One thousand four hundred and seventy-eight. Forty.*

*I turned eleven today. Nobody knew. I did not tell anybody. The sixth one had been a cake, and my father had been home, and Wulin had been there, and there had been a knife with eleven marks in it. I am eleven and there is no cake and there is no mark, and I am not the boy who had the cake.*

*I registered the badge today. The clerk looked at it and looked at me and said you're eleven. I have been carrying the badge since I was ten and I have not looked at it much. It says I am a fourth-rank blacksmith. It says I am on the road.*

*I took a forging job. Eleven hundred for a first-grade thousand refining, minus a one percent fee. Xie Xie did the arithmetic. I have been making metal since I was six, and a woman in a hall said blacksmithing is the most appropriate profession for creating powerful battle armors, and I am going to make the metal for a battle armor, and I am on the road.*

He set the pen down, because there was one more thing.

*I am eleven and I am not the boy who had the sixth one. The boy who had the sixth one needed a mark for every year. I am eleven and I do not need a mark. I have been getting ready for a thing since I was six, and I did not know what the thing was, and now I know it is a battle armor, and I am going to make the metal for it, and I am on the road, and the road is long, and I am not frightened, because I have been at a lot of bottoms and I understand now that the bottom is not a place, it is a thing you stand at until you understand it.*

He closed the book.

The lock at his temple lay gold across his cheekbone, and the marks on his forearms were raised, and the hawk turned over in him — *one thousand four hundred and seventy-eight* — and did not pull toward anything, and Lin Hao lay on the floor of a slum dormitory at the greatest academy in the world on the night of his eleventh birthday, and he was not the boy who had had the cake, and he was on the road, and the road was long.
