## Part 1: Downstairs

Wu Zhangkong took them downstairs.

Not metaphorically. Down — out of the class-zero corridor with its wards and its quiet, down two flights, into the part of the academy that smelled of sweat and floor polish and had a **soul barrier** around every field because ordinary students put stray soul skills into walls.

**Three floors. Four fields to a floor. Thirty to fifty metres across.** Full of children from every grade.

"Are we being demoted," Zhang Yangzi said, to Wang Jinxi, in the voice he used when he was not actually asking.

Wang Jinxi gave him a look.

"You really do talk a lot of nonsense."

They had been fighting in the platform for three months. Bears. Lions. A spider that had smoked Wulin's grass. And they had been good at it — good enough that Wu Zhangkong had stood in a corridor with the sun going down and called them a functional team, which was the nearest thing to a medal that any of them was going to get from him.

And now they were standing on an ordinary floor among ordinary children, and Lin Hao understood before anybody said it.

"You want us to fight each other," he said.

Wu Zhangkong looked at him. "The platform teaches you to survive a thing that wants to kill you," he said. "It does not teach you to work with a person who does not want to be worked with."

---

## Part 2: What The Body Decided

They tried the fusion four times.

The first time, Wang Jinxi's **Bone Dragon King** came up and Zhang Yangzi's **Shadow Phantasm Eagle** came up, and the two of them went toward each other the way they had gone toward each other for three months — and Wang Jinxi's body **pushed.**

Not a stumble. A rejection. Lin Hao watched it happen from twelve metres and saw the exact moment it went wrong, which was not a moment in the technique at all. It was a moment in Wang Jinxi's *shoulders*, and it happened before either of them had done anything.

Zhang Yangzi landed on his backside.

"**Wang Jinxi.**" He was up and shouting in one movement. "*What is the meaning of this?!* Why won't you fuse with me?!"

"I didn't do anything!"

"You *did* something—"

"**Calm down.**" Wu Zhangkong said it once, and Zhang Yangzi calmed down, because that was what that voice did. "This isn't something Wang Jinxi can control."

He looked at Wang Jinxi for a while. Wang Jinxi was standing very still with his hands at his sides and his face doing something that Lin Hao recognised, because he had seen it on his own face in a mirror about a year ago, which was the face of a boy working something out that he was not going to like.

"Jinxi's martial soul has probably **mutated,**" Wu Zhangkong said. "So your two martial souls aren't compatible enough to use the soul fusion skill any more."

There was a silence.

"Mutated," Zhang Yangzi said. "When."

"I don't know when. Probably some time in the last month." Wu Zhangkong's voice did not soften, which was the only kindness he had. "It isn't a fault. It isn't a mistake. Souls do it."

Wang Jinxi looked at Wulin.

It was a short look and a strange one, and Lin Hao caught it, and Wulin caught it, and neither of them said anything, because they had both understood at the same moment that the look was not about them.

## Part 3: The Two Days

Wang Jinxi did not come to the dormitory that night, or the next.

He was not angry. That was the part Zhang Yangzi could not sit with — he kept waiting for the anger, because anger was a thing you could argue with, and it did not come.

On the second evening Lin Hao found him on the steps behind the training building, which was not a place anybody went, and sat down two steps below him, because that seemed like the right height.

"You're not coming back," Lin Hao said.

"No."

"Is it the fusion."

"It's not the fusion." Wang Jinxi looked out at nothing. "The fusion is how I found out. It isn't the reason."

Lin Hao waited, because he had learned that Wang Jinxi needed the waiting.

"Do you know what I found in the two days?" Wang Jinxi said. "I couldn't sleep. I couldn't meditate properly. My heart was—" he stopped, and chose a plainer word, because that was the kind of person he was. "It was loud. And I worked out that the reason it was loud was that I'd been cultivating next to Wulin for three months and I'd stopped being able to tell which parts of me were mine."

"That's not—"

"It is." Wang Jinxi turned his head a few degrees. "Bloodlines affect each other. Martial souls affect each other. The stronger the soul power, the stronger the bloodline. I'm a rank **higher** than Wulin right now, and I'm already being pulled. So what happens when he comes past me?" He said it without any bitterness at all, which was worse. "And you've got three rings. You came past me four days ago and you didn't even notice you were doing it."

Lin Hao opened his mouth.

"Don't," Wang Jinxi said. "That's the thing. You didn't notice. That's not your fault — you weren't doing it at me, you were just being larger than me, and that's the problem." He looked back out at nothing. "I'd become somebody's. I'd become Wulin's, or yours, and I wouldn't even feel it happen, and one day I'd be standing in a room doing something because the person next to me wanted it and I wouldn't be able to tell you why."

---

## Part 4: The Hardest Part

"I've thought about it for two days," Wang Jinxi said. "It's a hard decision. It hurts. I don't have another choice."

"You could stay and just—"

"No." Wang Jinxi said it gently, which was somehow the firmest anybody had ever said no to Lin Hao. "That's the thing you don't understand yet, and you will. You can't *half* stand next to somebody. Either you're next to them or you're not."

Lin Hao sat on the step with his **hawk-gold lock** falling across his cheekbone and the **marks on his forearms** faint and raised under his sleeves, and he thought about a formation he had built three days ago in a dormitory, with a door in it that was two boys, and he understood that the door was gone and that no amount of cleverness was going to put it back.

"What do I tell them," he said.

"You tell them the truth."

"They'll take it badly."

"Zhang Yangzi will take it badly," Wang Jinxi said. "Wulin will take it well, and that will be worse."

---

## Part 5: What Zhang Yangzi Did

He came around the corner of the training building at a run, and Lin Hao had exactly one second to decide whether to say something, and decided not to, and got up and went the other way.

He was twelve metres off when Zhang Yangzi reached the steps.

He did not shout. That was the surprising part, and Lin Hao heard it because he was listening, and he was listening because he had not entirely left, and he knew that and was not entirely honest with himself about it.

"**Jinxi.**"

And then Zhang Yangzi put his arms around him, on the steps behind a training building, in the ordinary afternoon, in front of whoever was walking past, and held on.

Lin Hao did not hear what was said after that. He went back inside, because there are things you do not take from people even when you could.

---

## Part 5-B: What They Did

He told them in the dormitory, at the hour when all of them happened to be in it, which he had arranged without arranging it, and he said the true thing in four sentences the way Wang Jinxi had told him to, and then he stopped talking, because he had learned that the silence after a thing like that was the part that did the work.

Wulin took it well.

That was the whole of it, and it was worse than Lin Hao had been expecting, exactly as promised. Wulin nodded once, and said *"he's right, isn't he,"* — not a question, the way Wulin said most things — and then he got up and took his own bowl off the shelf and put rice in it and sat back down, and his hands did not shake, and his face did not do anything at all, and Lin Hao watched a boy who has been putting money in a tin for four hundred Fridays absorb the second departure of his life without a single visible seam.

And Lin Hao understood, watching it, that this was not composure. This was a person who had decided a long time ago that the people he loved were going to leave and that the correct response was to not make it harder for them.

**Wulin has never once asked anybody to stay. Not Na'er. Not Wang Jinxi. Not me. And that I have been calling that steadiness for two years, and that it is not steadiness, and that I do not know what it is, and that I am going to find out before I let it happen a third time.**

Xie Xie took it the way Xie Xie took everything, which was loudly and at length and with a joke in it that he abandoned halfway through. *"Well. Good. Fine. That's— the fusion was never— he was always going to—"* and then he stopped, and said *"I'm going to go and clean my daggers,"* at nine in the morning, and went, and nobody said anything about it, and he came back forty minutes later and was completely fine, and was not.

Gu Yue said one thing.

"He is not the first person to leave a room because you were standing in it," she said. "And you are going to spend a long time deciding whether that is a compliment. I would like to save you the time. It is neither. It is a fact about weather."

And Zhang Yangzi — who had come in from the steps eleven minutes earlier with his eyes red and his voice entirely normal, who had been told nothing and had worked out all of it, who was staying in a class that had just lost the only person he had ever fused with — sat down at the end of the bench and said, "Is anybody going to say the thing."

"What thing."

"That there's five of us now, and that the five of us are going to a tournament in eight weeks, and that he would want us to be decent at it." Zhang Yangzi put his hands flat on the table, one on top of the other, the way he did when he was holding something still. "That's the thing. I'm going to say it, since nobody else has the sense to, and because he is my friend and not yours, and I would like somebody to notice that I am the one who gets to say it."

## Part 6: What The Formation Became

Wu Zhangkong stood in the middle of the field with his arms folded and looked at the five of them.

"You're a door short," he said.

"Yes."

"So stop being a formation." He said it the way he said everything, flat, and it took Lin Hao three seconds to understand that it was not a criticism. "A formation is a shape you put people in. You built one and it worked and it broke, and the reason it broke is that people aren't shapes." He unfolded his arms. "So don't build another one. **Learn what each of them will do when it goes wrong.** That's the thing that survives. That's the only thing that survives."

Lin Hao stood in an ordinary field among ordinary children with three purple rings going slowly dim around his boots, and he thought about a ledger with a column in it marked **WHAT THEY WANT**, and he understood that he had been keeping the wrong column.

He had been keeping what they wanted.

What he needed was what they would **do.**

He went and got the book.

---

He wrote it that night, and he did not cross anything out, because nothing in it was wrong.

**The door is gone. Wang Jinxi left because standing next to us was costing him himself, and he was right, and I did not see it coming, and I am the one who reads things.**

**So the column is wrong. What people want is not the useful part. What people DO when it goes wrong is the useful part.**

*Wulin stays. He always stays. He is the only one I have never had to predict.*

**Xie Xie goes left. He went left at the bear and he will go left again, and the arithmetic will be his and it will sometimes be wrong, and he will go left anyway.**

**Gu Yue makes the decision nobody else can make in under a second and does not explain it and does not need to.**

**Zhang Yangzi will be angry for about nine days and then he will be fine and he will never say that he wasn't.**

*And Wang Jinxi is not in the column any more, because he is not in the room, and that is the first line I have ever written in that book that I would rather not have.*

He closed it.

The hawk turned over in him — **one thousand one hundred and seventy-one** — and did not lean toward anything, and Lin Hao lay on his back in a room that was too small and thought about a boy on a step who had said *you can't half stand next to somebody,* and about the fact that he had three rings and could not think of a single thing they were for that anybody else needed.
