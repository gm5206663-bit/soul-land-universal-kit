### ╱╱ THE LAKE ╲╲ *(Huo Yuhao)*

Breakfast was Yuhao's treat and Wang Dong ate double, and then — because the pill could wait for evening and bodies could not — they walked off breakfast along the lakeside path, and the Sea God Lake opened before them like a held breath: man-made, vast, blue as a rumor, mist standing over the far water where the inner court's roofs sat on the island like a secret inside a secret.

The red dot came out of that mist.

It came *running on the water* — a figure in red, hair red, mask red, eyes red, wrapped in heat that bent the air, crossing the lake at a speed that made the water angry. Yuhao's new eyes read the details; his old instincts read the *wrongness*: "**Wang Dong. Behind me.**"

The blue wings snapped open — and curled like paper in a fire. The heat came down on them as a sea: grass going yellow-brown in a ten-meter ring, soul power sitting on their throats like a hand. Wang Dong fainted mid-syllable, wings folding. Yuhao's ice answered from behind his sternum and was spent in a single second, and the red-masked palm came down at his face, and a last Soul Shock dissolved in the heat like a snowflake in a furnace, and he thought — with a strange calm — *so this is it.*

*(Two hundred meters away, on the near-shore path, a forge crew was setting fresh iron fittings, and a courier's moisture-sense — idly mapping the water's skin — went taut like a snare. "Furnace backdraft," Jiang Che said, in his father's burning-site voice, and took the nearest journeyman to the ground as the world's temperature rose. Inside his coat, an empress was already awake: "Something old and burning, and it is not sane. Down. NOW.")*

The world went white.

### ╱╱ THE FINGER ╲╲ *(the shore — witnessed)*

This part belongs to no one present, which is why the lake tells it best.

The boy's eyes went white — ten-thousand-year-ice white. The ring at his feet turned **gold**, sunrise-gold. A hundred meters of lakeshore froze between one breath and the next, and the running figure was flung from the water's skin like a spark from a struck flint. A voice that was not an eleven-year-old's — a voice like a glacier being lazy — said into the air:

"**You dare touch the one brother chose. You dare wake brother from his nap. Then die.**"

One finger. One line of ice-blue light. The red figure answered from the floor of her fall — six rings rising, **two yellow, two purple, two black**, a great **Evil Fire Phoenix** screaming up behind her, a fire-thread no thicker than a finger meeting the light — and the phoenix threw its own body between them, and the collision howled steam, and the red figure trailed ice-blue into the lake and vanished under.

The boy's body turned to pursue — and stopped, and yawned from somewhere beneath its own skeleton. "*Annoying. Someone's coming. Hmm — this little fellow's body has improved nicely. Brother can spare a bit more strength then. I dodge.*" Gold faded to white, white to nothing, cold folded away like a fan; and Huo Yuhao fell beside Wang Dong like a cut puppet as figures came flashing over the water — fast beyond fast, led by a white-robed old man who crossed the last thirty meters between one step and the next.

Behind the path-stones, a courier held a fox's claws in his shoulder and did not move until the last white robe was gone.

"*Little tide,*" the fox said, in the voice of archives being correct. "*You will not speak of this. Not to your sect, not to your roommates, not to me unless I ask. The thing that wore your little sect-brother is older than every book in this academy. And the burning one is a wound, not a weapon — I have seen her kind of wound before, and it is not for you to carry. The path is still broken. You are, today of all days, a boy who fixes things.*"

### ╱╱ THE CLASS ╲╲ *(the class - ensemble)*

Class One, that afternoon, was theory: martial-soul taxonomy until lunch, soul-beast year-marks after - the courses the well-born ignored and the woodshed boy drank like water, front row, taking everything.

And all around him, the rumor mill ground its grist. The monitor and the strongest fighter had vanished from morning classes and returned at midday looking like they had fought a summer and lost; the lake mist had gone white, said a cousin's roommate; inner-court seniors, said others; a duel, said Wang Dong's admirers, glossing his rings. Nothing true survived the sealing order - and something *better* than truth grew in its place: the class had watched its weakest boy outlast them all under iron, and now its weakest boy had walked off whatever the lake had been, and a class of ninety-one geniuses began, without deciding to, to be proud of its monitor. Children are accurate barometers of the unknowable.

Jiang Che sat four desks behind and one row left of all this, and took notes on two subjects: the taxonomy, and - in a courier's shorthand, in a margin - the rumor mill itself. What the class believed. How fast. Which names carried weight. A sect was an economy; information was coin; and a third disciple who had been raised in a hall where you learned the patient's condition before the family did was, quite quietly, beginning to invest.

### ╱╱ THE SECRET ROOM ╲╲ *(the inner court — Ma Xiaotao)*

On the lake's heart-island, in a sealed room, the red woman woke.

"*So hot… so uncomfortable…*" — and then, wondering: "…Why is it not hot anymore?"

She sat up. The soaked clothes had dried on her; the mask came off in one hand, and under it was a pale, cold-featured face of about twenty — and a figure that made the room smaller. Her eyes, the blood-red gone out of them, were a faint, bewildered pink.

"You're awake." The white-robed elder — the one who had carried her from the shore — sighed from somewhere old. "**Xiaotao**. This time you nearly made a mistake beyond mending. Two outer-court students almost died under your evil fire. What shook you out of it — do you remember?"

She didn't. Red, a killing threat, resistance — then here. And the elder, examining what he found in her channels, went very still: the fire was *pressed*. Not fought — **pressed**, laid down under something cold so pure it felt older than fire itself. "Like the absolute-ice breath of the ancient ice dragons," he said slowly. "Purer than your phoenix evil-fire. It has done what none of my methods ever did."

The search that followed was quiet and total — and fruitless. Not a guardian of either boy: the special invite was an orphan of ordinary talent, vouched for by the Tang Sect; the other boy's great family was not an ice family. No student or teacher in the academy held an extreme-ice soul. The source of the cold simply did not exist, anywhere the academy could look.

"Then I'll say what I have said," the elder said, at last, gently, as to a daughter. "If an extreme-ice soul could be found — a man, and a match — the marriage would settle the phoenix fire at its root. Otherwise the fire caps you forever: cultivation slowed, life at risk. Xiaotao — the academy's best genius in centuries cannot be spent on pride."

"I'm not marrying anyone," said **Ma Xiaotao**, ears red, chin set, "and especially not a martial soul I don't like. I'll hold it down myself."

"Xiaotao—"

"*Myself.*"

The elder sighed the sigh of teachers everywhere and did not press — and so the academy gained, that day, two secrets: a lake that had frozen from nowhere, and a quiet standing search, in the world beyond the walls, for an extreme-ice man who did not know he was being looked for.

### ╱╱ THE WALK ╲╲ *(Huo Yuhao — with both his brothers)*

They were returned to the white dormitory by a teacher who healed them, sealed them with silence under pain of expulsion, and left each a green pill as an apology from the powerful to the small.

Wang Dong woke first, furious with the holes in his morning; Yuhao woke second, with a black-out where a fire-god should be and the taste of impossible cold on his teeth; and Jiang Che — who had signed off the path repairs early and come straight up — was third in the room, and the triangle did what the triangle now did: both boys turned to the third bed before they turned to each other.

"You were on the shore path," Yuhao said.

"Behind the stones. I saw steam, and then I saw the inner court carry two boys away. That's the official version." He looked at them, one and then the other, and did not spend one question of the hundred he had. "It's also mine. Lunch is on the sect. Both of you — you look like boiled laundry."

And it was on that walk — the three of them, and Wang Dong explaining what a **Soul-Raising Pill** was with the bounce of a boy whose near-death had been converted by adult economics into treasure (a rank's worth of soul power; one per lifetime; fewer than twenty a year — "we are the luckiest people to almost die, *historically*") — that Yuhao, digging out his whole fortune to fund lunch, said the plain thing: he was an orphan; this was the entire estate; bedding cost money, and that was why his bed had none.

Wang Dong stopped walking. Weather crossed the prince's face.

"Say the sorry part for me," Jiang Che said quietly. "I'll stand witness."

"…I'm sorry, Huo Yuhao. I bullied you and I didn't know what your life was." A breath. "*From now on your food's on me.* It's a sect thing. It's basically law."

"It's really not—"

"*It's basically law.*"

And Yuhao let himself be fed — the second letting of his life — while on his other side the courier made no noise at all about the fact that he had packed, that morning, an unordered second lunch for exactly this, and would now be eating it himself. Some payments only go one way. Some thirds of a triangle hold the other two sides up by saying nothing, loudly.

### ╱╱ THE TWO-LEDGER NIGHT ╲╲ *(room 108, after the audit)*

“You did the thing,” Yuhao said, mid-pushup. “The audit thing.”

“I asked two questions.”

“You filed a grievance against the *concept of institutions,*” said Wang Dong, oiling a blade. “…Are you okay?”

And that — from the prince, unsolicited — was the thing the day needed: someone asking about *him.*

“I’m angry,” the courier said. “And I’m using it. But tonight I’m mostly angry that the answer was always going to be a pill and a threat, because that’s what institutions are, and my brothers were *cooked.*” Breath. “I’m okay. I’m being okay. Ask me at breakfast.”

“Copy that,” said the room — both, at once — and the fox said nothing. Some nights the archive stands down.

### ╱╱ THE STALL ╲╲ *(the gate — evening)*

Tang Ya was waiting at the gate with the grill and the fish and — produced from her sleeve with the flourish of a woman executing a plan — **a second white porcelain bottle.**

"*Another* one?" Wang Dong's voice cracked. "That's — those are Mysterious Water Pills — that's the *second* —"

"Your senior brother won two," said the Sect Master, with dignity, "and the sect has voted, and the sect's vote is final: the second one goes where the first one went." She pressed it into Yuhao's hands and then, immediately, into a posture of bodyguarding. "And TODAY, Xiao Yuhao, your teacher guards the stall personally. Let any blind fool try it now."

The blind fool had already tried it. There were thirty-odd customers waiting at the gate, and first in line, radiating dignity and bruised ribs, stood a mountain in a sixth-year uniform.

"I'm here to *buy fish,*" Xu Sanshi announced, before Tang Ya could speak. "I queued. *Personally.* Two fish. For — for someone. Not that it's anyone's business."

"You queued," Tang Ya said, with the delight of a hawk identifying a mouse wearing a bell, "for Jiang Nannan."

"…The fish is not for her. The fish is for me. I simply chose to purchase it in an orderly fashion—"

The fish was for Jiang Nannan. Everyone knew the fish was for Jiang Nannan — everyone had known since yesterday — and the mountain took his two fish (fifth and sixth off the grill: Wang Dong's silver had come first, then the Sect Master's *quality control*, then the mountain's), paid, and left with the stiffness of a man who had queued forty minutes for a girl who would not say his name.

The stall sold out again. The sect's ledger fattened again. And at the table beside the grill — where a courier, off shift, ate his own packed lunch and kept the Sect Master's books balanced in a small neat hand — three boys sat shoulder to shoulder without arranging to, the way they now did: the gray one in the middle because he was the middle, and the other two on either side because that was simply where their nights lived.

From inside a coat, one word, for one ear, at the sight of the sold-out ledger: "*Adequate.*"

And when the stall was packed and the coals were drowned and the five of them - six, counting the mountain, who had lingered to help carry the table for reasons he declined to explain - walked back through the gate together, the lake lay to their left, dark and flat and keeping its secrets, and under the dark flat water an island held a sealed room, and in the room a girl with pink eyes sat awake, pressing her own fire down with nothing but stubbornness, and not one of the six knew she existed.

The jar held its lid. The year had begun.
