### ╱╱ THE PILLS ╲╲ *(room 108 — ensemble)*

The pill debate happened on the floor of room 108, lamplit, three beds occupied for once at the same hour, and it was — as the best arguments in that room always were — between the two boys with the third one listening.

"Both at once," Yuhao said, eager, the two treasures in his palm like a boy holding his own future. "Senior brother gave me the second one, and the green one — why wait?"

"Because you'd waste them." Wang Dong said it the way princes say things that are simply true. "The first Mysterious Water Pill's force is still in you — it works with your meditation for days. Take the second now and they overlap; take the green one now and your channels are still soft from the first. Ten days. Let the first finish. Then the second — more marrow-cleansing, more rank. *Then* the Soul-Raising Pill, into a body that's been rebuilt twice, and you get everything it has." A shrug. "It's just manners. You don't serve the next course into a bowl that's still full."

"...Say the sorry part again, I want to hear you be humble twice in one week."

"*Now* I'm never saying it again."

And Wang Dong — already rank twenty-one, channels aristocratic and pristine, the perfect vessel *today* — bit into his own green pill there and then: a smell like ten thousand flowers opened in the room at once, a green nectar swallowed, shell and all, and a blue-green light settling over his bed like a tide while the other two boys meditated in self-defense against the sheer *plenty* of it. By morning he would be twenty-two or twenty-three, and he said so, smugly, around a yawn, and mentioned — casually, the way princes mention the things they want most — that first place in the new-student assessment would earn him something he wanted. Neither of the other two had to ask what.

Jiang Che, third bed, said nothing about pills. He had none — had given his claim away before it existed — and wanted none, and the wanting-none was not a wound and not a pose. But he did not sleep either, and the not-sleeping had a temperature to it, and a fox who had lived four years against that particular chest knew the temperature by name.

"*Little tide.*"

"Sleep."

"*You are angry.*"

"...Yes."

"*At whom?*"

The pause went on long enough to have a texture.

"Tomorrow," said the courier, in the voice his mother used for diagnoses. "Not tonight. Tonight they sleep."

And then, because he was who he was, he added the other thing — the thing the anger had been grinding against all evening like a whetstone: "*And tonight I break through.*"

The fox went very still on the blanket.

"*Say that again.*"

"Twenty-five has been a wall for a month. I thought it was the bottleneck. It is not the bottleneck." His hands flexed once — the Jade Hand's warmth, the forge callus, three days of this academy's food in him and a fire under it. "It is a door I have not pushed, because at home there was no reason to hurry. Here there is every reason. Tonight there is the best reason."

"*Your brothers nearly died today.*"

"Exactly," said Jiang Che, and sat down on the boards, and closed his eyes. "The jar will try again. I am done being rank twenty-five in a school that cooks first-years."

### ╱╱ THE ANGRY AUDIENCE ╲╲ *(Jiang Che — the next day)*

He went alone, which was itself a message. Not to the dormitory office. Not to Zhou Yi — this was above her classroom's ceiling and below her payroll. He went during the theory hour, when every desk in the administrative hall was guaranteed occupied by the people whose job it was to be answerable, and he asked the junior clerk, politely, for the record of the previous day's lakeshore incident, *as the work-crew witness of record*, and sat down to wait with his hammer over his shoulder and his temper folded up small and hot in his chest like a coal in ash.

The senior administrator who finally saw him — a soul-tool hall teacher with the weary eyes of a man whose mornings were usually about broken windows — lasted six minutes.

"The record says the work crew saw steam," Jiang Che said, level. "I am the work crew. I saw the steam, and I saw what came after: two first-years carried off this academy's own lake-shore unconscious, and healed, and handed pills, and instructed to forget." He let that sit. "I have kept the instruction. I am keeping it now. That is not why I'm here."

"Then why—"

"Two questions, and I'll leave you the rest of your morning." The courier counted them off the way his father counted supplies. "One. Where in the enrollment contract does it say the academy's own students may be cooked on the academy's own shore by the academy's own —" the word chosen precisely, a courier choosing words for a living — "*personnel*, and the remedy is a pill and a threat of expulsion?" And before the man's face could finish assembling its official shape: "Two. If the boy had died — the rank-eleven one, the orphan, the one with no family to ask — who would have been answerable? Not to me. I'm nobody. To *anyone*."

The silence that followed was the silence of an institution discovering that a first-year had walked in carrying not a grievance but an *audit*.

The answer, when it came, was honest, which was worse than a lie would have been. "The inner court governs itself," the administrator said. "What happened at the lake is inner-court matter. The outer court's role was — is — your classmates' recovery." A beat. "The academy is grateful they're well. The pills were generous."

"The pills were a *receipt*," said Jiang Che, and stood, and shouldered the hammer. "Thank you for your time. You've answered both questions."

He was most of the way to the door when the administrator, unable to help himself: "You're the trial one. The hammer. Zhou Yi's file said—" and then stopped, because the boy turned, and the boy's face was calm, and calm was somehow the worst thing in the room.

"Zhou Yi's file is right," the courier said. "I'm the one who fixes things. It's on me now to know *which* things this academy fixes — and which it just seals." And he left, and behind him a middle-grade administrator sat very still for a while, and then, against every instinct of his career, wrote a short memo to a superior he trusted about a first-year who had asked the second question, because someone in the building should have it on record that it had been *asked*.

*(Natural ripples, ambient: an institution now has a boy in its lowest year who audits it. The memo exists. Nothing will come of it — this month. But files, as this academy keeps proving, have long lives.)*

### ╱╱ THE SECT'S ANSWER ╲╲ *(the fox — midnight, before the months)*

"*You went to the administrators,*" the fox said, on the third bed, in the dark.

"I went to the administrators."

"*And they told you the inner court governs itself.*"

"They did."

The fox was quiet for the length of a decision. Then: "*Good.*"

He turned to look at her — moonlight-white, gold-eyed, seated on his blanket like a small white verdict.

"*You misunderstand me,*" she said. "*I am not pleased that you were dismissed. I am pleased that you went, and I am pleased by what you did not do. You did not shout. You did not threaten. You did not leak the thing you are not permitted to leak — you spent your anger like a craftsman, on two questions that cannot be un-asked. That is the correct use of a fire, little tide.*" One tail curled. "*Now hear the other half, because a beast who has watched a hundred courts will tell you what a boy who repairs paths cannot yet see: the academy is not one thing. The jar is not one thing. The old man who froze — no. The teachers who came running across the water — they healed your brothers with their own hands, and the tree-mender did it twice in two days for children not of his own class. The girl in red is a wound the academy carries because it does not know how to cure her, and it hides her because hiding is cheaper than admitting the jar leaks. That is not evil. That is an institution, which is a beast with ten thousand stomachs and no single throat to blame.*"

A pause.

"*So the anger stays. File it. Do not let it rot into hatred — hatred is a fire that eats its own lamp. And then, since you are who you are, do the thing you already decided to do.*"

"Which is?"

"*Get stronger than the jar.*" The fox yawned, cavernous, ancient. "*You are angry that your brothers could die and the academy's answer was a pill. Very well. Become the answer. A sect of five with a healer who outgrows the academy's cure is worth more than any file in that building. You cannot fix an institution, little tide. But you can make it irrelevant to the people you love. That is the craftsman's revenge.*"

He was quiet for a long time. Then: "The pills tonight. Wang Dong's rank, Yuhao's ten days. And me?"

"*You,*" said the fox, "*have a hammer, a sect, a forge shift, and the strongest foundation in your year. You have been at this academy for three days and it has already tried to kill your brothers once. Cultivate like it will try again.*"

"Fair." And he had — was — would. The meditation ran all night. Somewhere in the small hours the wall of twenty-five, which had never really been a wall, gave way the way river-ice gives way: not a crack but a whole quiet sheet settling downstream, and the soul power behind it flooding into the new room. **Twenty-six. Before dawn.** The AT received the academy's richest day — iron, fear, fire-sight, grief, anger, the pillar-feel of five — and compounded it into what it always compounds things into: more of him.

"*Also,*" she added, settling, "*the ruffling. It is after midnight.*"

The clause held. The candle was out. The empire filed it under *different night* and purred, and the boy who could not fix an institution but could absolutely outgrow one went to sleep doing arithmetic.

### THE LAKE-NIGHT ARGUMENT *(before the breakthrough)*

At midnight the courier sat up: "I'm going to break through tonight."

"…It's *midnight,*" said the furs.

"It's exactly midnight. It's the correct time."

"Rank walls don't care about the *time—*"

"They care about me being done being rank twenty-five in a school that cooks first-years. Watch or sleep. No advice."

"When have I ever — you gave me advice about the *plums,* Yu."

"The plums were a *moral crisis—*"

"The plums were *two plums—*"

"*One* plum—" and the prince, flat, doomed, half-asleep: "*If you two argue about plums during his breakthrough, I'm getting the fox to adjudicate—*"

"*I heard that,*" said the fox.

And the room laughed — at midnight, on the worst day — and a boy who can laugh at midnight is not clenched, and an unclenched boy is a river, and rivers do not negotiate with walls.

### ╱╱ THE THREE MONTHS ╲╲ *(Class One — ensemble; the montage)*

The months turned the way Zhou Yi's months turned: like a grindstone.

**The stall became an institution.** Twenty fish became thirty; thirty hit its ceiling because Yuhao would not spend his cultivation on commerce; a line formed daily at gate-open; one fish per customer, price never raised — and the boy who could not win a fight became, by unanimous culinary acclamation, the most beloved first-year at the outer court. Jiang Nannan queued once, bought once, thanked once, and left once; Xu Sanshi, having failed to conquer her with fish, was conquered by it instead and became the stall's most faithful regular — six evenings in seven, buying one fish, eating it alone with the dignity of a mountain at a memorial.

**Wang Dong's promise came due.** Rank twenty-two — then twenty-three before the month was out, the pill compounding an already gilded foundation — and the whole class quietly recalibrated around the fact that the second-strongest boy in the room was getting stronger *faster*.

**Jiang Che grew like a river in flood season — and the class clocked it, then stopped being surprised by it.** Twenty-six the first night. **Twenty-seven by the month's turn; twenty-nine by the second month's end** — the AT eating the academy whole: the Xuantian Gong's superior circulation multiplying his absorption (a technique generations beyond his family's basic method); three Shrek-grade meals a day feeding a furnace raised on commoner rations; Zhou Yi's iron-courses as pure adaptation-data; the forge-shift loads rebuilding bone and tendon; the mend-work itself as spiritual exercise; and under it all the pressure — the lake, the jar, the brothers who could have died — which the AT, per its nature, did not experience as stress but as *specification*. The mutations deepened with the ranks: vine-density crossing into a tier his father would not recognize; self-re-knit measured in heartbeats; the dew-lens reading a room's soul-power signatures like weather-maps; two storm-light strands become a streak. Twenty-nine, with the thirty-bottleneck ahead — the rank-wall where the third ring must come — and for once the courier was not calm about a wall: he was *planning* it.

And through it all he ran the triangle and the ledger both. Mornings: the forge shift, the Jade Hand past its burns by week six. Evenings: the mend — the iron days' aches and the soul-tool yard's scalds flowing through room 108's door, because the word had gone round the class the way words do: *the third bed fixes things.* Zhou Yi said nothing about her first-year running an unlicensed infirmary out of a dormitory; Zhou Yi, it turned out, had a file that said *flagged*, and watched, and approved of load-bearing students. And in the margins of all of it, the courier's shorthand ledger grew: the class's alliances, the seniors' pecking orders, the stall's economics, the rumor-mill's seasons — the information-inheritance of a sect being built one quiet page at a time.

And Yuhao — **Yuhao.** The pills taken in Wang Dong's prescribed order; the marrow cleansed a second time; the rank climbing past the teens; the moving Xuantian circulation that turned every lap of every iron day into cultivation; the Purple Demon Eyes fed at every dawn. But that was only the measurable part. The unmeasurable part was this: in the first week, Class One had watched the weakest boy in the academy outlast them all under iron; in the second, they watched him take a monitor's duties nobody else wanted; and by the third month, the question of who led Class One had stopped being a question. Nobody challenged him. Not because he could beat them — most of them could — but because the class had discovered it did not *want* to. Zhou Yi's iron had forged them a body. Huo Yuhao had forged them a spine.

**Sixty-seven.** When the three months closed and the count came in, the academy's own projection — *a third will remain* — lay in ruins. Sixty-seven of ninety-one. The high offices were, the teachers whispered, genuinely shocked; no class in living memory had held together like this under Zhou Yi. The official explanation was Zhou Yi's methods. The teachers who actually taught Class One knew the unofficial one, and it had a fish stall, a butterfly, and a boy who slept on bare boards and never once mentioned it.

The assessment was next. The whole year knew it. And on the last night of the three months, five people sat around a grill that had already been drowned for the night, and the Sect Master looked around at her sect — five, plus a mountain in the seventh seat he had stopped pretending was temporary — and said, cheerfully, the thing everyone was thinking:

"First place. The whole year. Wang Dong takes first, and the fifth seat is sealed properly, and *then* we're really five."

Wang Dong looked at the grill. Then, briefly, at the third bed's occupant — the strongest leg, the one both of them turned to — and then at Yuhao.

"What if I can't take first?" he said, and it was the first unarmored sentence the prince had produced in three months. "The one who takes it should be... it should *matter*."

"It will matter whoever takes it," said Jiang Che, and put a fish — his own portion, the sixth of the evening, bought and paid for like everyone else's — onto the prince's plate. "Eat. You're rank twenty-three," and here the courier smiled the mild smile the class had learned meant trouble for somebody, "and I passed twenty-nine last week. The wall's at thirty and I'm already picking the ring. Worry about *me*."

The fox, in the coat on the nail, said one word, for one ear, and it was not *adequate*.

"*Finally.*"
