# Chapter 8: The Bell After the Sky

Lan Xiao learned that some silences were heavier than alarms.

The three-day crisis had passed. Lan Xuanyu was breathing steadily in his room. The gold and silver lines were gone from his face. Nana had explained what she understood, and what she did not understand remained large enough to fill the spaces between every adult word.

Two energies.

A spinning balance.

Danger at breakthroughs.

Rank twenty.

Nan Cheng sat beside Lan Xiao after Nana returned to Lan Xuanyu's room. Her hands were folded so tightly that the knuckles had lost color. The apartment was quiet, but it was not the safe quiet of a home after bedtime. It was the quiet of adults listening to the wall between their child and disaster.

"Can we really not ask anyone?" she whispered.

Lan Xiao shook his head slowly.

He wanted to say they could. He wanted to be an ordinary father with an ordinary answer: hospital, senior teacher, Spirit Master association, federation medical center, proper records, proper help. But ordinary answers did not survive the memory of a baby found in the Extreme North with impossible vitality, nor the private test that had turned a simple family story into a secret they had carried for years.

"If we ask," he said softly, "we will have to explain too much."

Nan Cheng closed her eyes.

Too much meant the child sleeping in the room. Too much meant the egg he had once been. Too much meant the lie that had become their legal truth because love had needed paperwork.

"Then we trust her."

"For now," Lan Xiao said.

It was not distrust. It was not trust either. It was the narrow road between them.

Inside Lan Xuanyu's room, Nana sat by the bed and placed her hand against the child's back.

Lan Xuanyu was still asleep. His face had softened into the round innocence that made the golden and silver danger seem like a cruel joke told by fate. Nana's power entered him carefully, flowing through meridians that were too tender to deserve the burden placed upon them. She did not use force. Force was easy. Control was the difference between saving and breaking.

She sorted from his back down to his legs, then returned through his arms. The two energies near his chest circled in the shape she had woven, touching only where they must, separated by motion where they could not yet coexist.

Familiar.

The feeling returned again, sharper now that the immediate danger had passed. Nana's brows tightened. She knew this golden power. She knew the silver too. Or she knew a grief shaped like them.

When her fingers reached Lan Xuanyu's right hand, her body trembled.

A dark blue ring rested on his thumb.

It was a child's ornament at first glance, quiet and unimportant, the sort of thing adults might notice only when telling a child not to lose it. A faint pattern lay on it like sleeping water beneath night. Nana looked at it, and something inside her that had been empty for years suddenly struck a wall.

Cold.

Pride.

Sorrow so deep it made breathing feel borrowed.

Her fingertip touched the ring.

Silver light gathered.

The ring loosened from the thumb it had refused to leave for anyone else. It slid into Nana's palm and became heavier than a ring should ever be.

She stood without thinking.

The room blurred silver.

High above Zi Luo City, night wind pulled at her hair.

Clouds moved beneath the stars. The city below slept in layered lights: apartments, roads, quiet rooftops, academy buildings, transit lines, ordinary people wrapped in ordinary dreams. Nana held the dark blue ring in her hand and felt a name rise from broken memory.

Holy Heaven Dividing Halberd.

The ring unfolded.

A long halberd appeared, dark blue and cold, its blade carrying a loneliness that did not belong to metal. Patterns flowed over it. The air around Nana twisted into a vast cloud vortex, and for one heartbeat she was not a quiet home tutor in a borrowed room.

She was grief with a weapon.

She raised the halberd.

The sky tore.

Only a line. Only one stroke. But the night above Zi Luo City opened in a deep blue trace that made satellites fail to understand what they were seeing and made warning systems decide that understanding could wait until after screaming.

The city alarm began.

It did not sound like school bells or household reminders. It rose harsh and official, rolling between buildings and into bedrooms, dragging adults upright before their minds had caught up.

In the Yan apartment, Yan Shuo woke so fast that his blanket flew off the bed.

For one wild instant, he thought the alarm was inside him.

Heat rushed to his shoulders. The Dawnflame Kite jolted awake in his chest, wings of feeling beating against his ribs without shape or sound. Dawnfeather Mantle tried to answer before he had even stood. Fire-light flickered along his forearms and vanished when his breath caught.

"Shuo!"

The door opened. Yan Ke stood there in night clothes with the posture of a man who had already checked three exits in his head. Lin Mei arrived half a breath later, hair loose, eyes clear.

"Emergency broadcast," Yan Ke said, though his device had not finished loading the details. "Stay away from the windows."

Yan Shuo obeyed because the alarm made disobedience feel smaller than usual.

Outside, other apartments lit up. Voices moved through the walls. A baby cried somewhere. The city's protective systems continued to blare, then shifted into an announcement too formal and too vague to be comforting.

Unidentified energy fluctuation.

Remain indoors.

No confirmed impact zone.

Await further notice.

Yan Shuo pressed both hands to his chest. "Is it a soul beast?"

"Not likely inside the city," Yan Ke said.

"A terrorist?"

Lin Mei's eyes sharpened, then softened because he was seven. "We do not know. That means we do not invent answers. Sit. Breathe."

"I'm breathing."

"Lower."

He lowered it.

The heat that had jumped toward his hands drew back. His shoulders loosened. The Dawnflame Kite tucked itself closer, restless but no longer flinging warmth everywhere. Something in Yan Shuo's body treated the alarm like pressure: not an enemy to burn, not a thing to run from, but weather to stand under without losing shape.

In, low.

Hold.

Out, slow.

The mantle did not form again.

Lin Mei saw the change and said nothing until he had managed three breaths without shaking.

"Good."

Yan Shuo looked toward the window despite being told not to. The curtains were drawn, but a faint blue-white pulse of city light crossed the fabric as emergency signals changed along the street.

"Lan Xuanyu lives near here," he said.

Yan Ke's fingers paused over his device.

"Many people live near here."

"But he does."

"And his parents will keep him indoors like we are keeping you indoors."

Yan Shuo did not like that answer because it was probably true and did not let him do anything.

Across the city, Nana stared at the torn trace in the sky until the alarm pulled her back to herself.

The halberd became a ring again.

Silver light folded around her.

When she returned to Lan Xuanyu's room, the child still slept. Lan Xiao and Nan Cheng had rushed to the doorway, faces pale.

Nana placed the dark blue ring back on Lan Xuanyu's thumb.

"The alarm?" Lan Xiao asked carefully.

Nana lowered her eyes. "It will pass."

That was not an answer.

Lan Xiao knew it was not an answer.

He also knew that the city was not destroyed, his son was breathing, and the woman in front of him had just returned from somewhere with sorrow still wet in her gaze. He swallowed the questions that would only make their home more dangerous.

The alarm lasted through the night.

By morning, Zi Luo City's news channels had learned many ways to say almost nothing. An unexplained energy wave had appeared in the upper atmosphere. No casualties were confirmed. No attack source had been found. Citizens were advised not to spread rumors. Experts were investigating.

Children spread rumors anyway.

Yan Shuo read three before breakfast and had his device taken away.

"I was researching," he protested.

Yan Ke placed the device on the far side of the table. "You were reading a message from Bao'er saying a giant space fish bit the clouds."

"It could have been a small giant space fish."

Lin Mei slid a bowl toward him. "Eat. Your mantle flared in your sleep."

Yan Shuo looked betrayed by his own arms. "It did?"

"Briefly. Not enough to burn anything. Enough to prove alarms count as training pressure whether we like it or not."

The Dawnflame Kite pulsed once, quieter now that food was near.

Yan Shuo ate.

At the Lan apartment, Lan Xuanyu woke with hunger large enough to frighten every ordinary breakfast in the kitchen.

"Mommy! I'm hungry!"

Nan Cheng came out carrying a plate and trying not to look as if she had barely slept. Nana had brought the food before Lan Xuanyu woke, saying only that he would need it. The long pieces on the plate smelled rich and strange, filled with a deep warmth that did not belong to common meat.

Lan Xuanyu ate one bite and forgot to ask questions.

"Delicious," he said around full cheeks.

Nan Cheng watched him with the expression of a mother counting breaths.

"Slow down."

"I am slow."

"You are faster than last time."

Lan Xuanyu considered this and slowed just enough to avoid losing the plate.

After breakfast and rest, Nana brought him to the cultivation room.

"Today I will teach you to look inside," she said.

Lan Xuanyu sat cross-legged in front of her, very serious because Teacher Nana's lessons were sometimes games and sometimes not games, and he was beginning to learn the difference by the way adults stood outside the door.

"Your two Blue Silver Grasses are like two hot-tempered children," Nana explained. "They must not fight inside your body. I have helped them move around one another. In the future, you must maintain this movement with your own Spiritual Power."

Lan Xuanyu closed his eyes.

He saw the golden and silver vortex near his chest.

It was beautiful.

It was also frightening in a way beauty should not be. The two colors circled, intertwined, close enough that his heart seemed to listen to them. He did not understand how much danger Nana had hidden from him. He understood only that Teacher Nana's voice stayed beside him and made the impossible shape feel like something he might learn.

"So I have to stop them fighting every day?"

"Yes."

"Like being class monitor for my own body?"

Nana paused.

Then she smiled. "Something like that."

Lan Xuanyu frowned. "Ye Lingtong is class monitor. She always looks angry. I don't want my body to be like her."

"Then guide it gently."

He nodded as if this were a very important moral lesson.

The remaining break days settled into two different kinds of training in two nearby homes.

Lan Xuanyu trained with Nana twice a day. He learned to feel the vortex, to keep it spinning, to notice when one side tugged harder than the other. He ate food that made his bones warm and his sleep deep. His footwork became less like running and more like water deciding where a stone was not.

Yan Shuo trained with his mother and father. He learned that Dawnfeather Mantle did not like arrogance, that the shoulders were not the same as the fists, and that a yellow ring made adults write more forms rather than fewer. He learned to hold the mantle through eight breaths, then nine when he did not grin, then seven when he grinned too early and had to pretend seven was part of the plan.

He also learned that Lan Xuanyu's replies had become shorter.

Training.

Eating.

Teacher Nana says I have to rest.

Yan Shuo accepted these answers with great dignity, which meant he complained only twice at dinner.

When the semester break ended, Zi Luo City's alarm had become a story adults discussed in lowered voices and children decorated until it was useless. The sky had been bitten by a space fish, cut by a giant sword, slapped by an invisible Titled Douluo, or attacked by pirates who forgot to land.

Yan Shuo liked the giant sword version best.

He did not know how close it had come to truth.

The first morning of the new term smelled of washed uniforms, breakfast steam, and children pretending they had not forgotten half their classroom manners.

Lan Xuanyu arrived at the school gate with eyes bright from a break that had been terrifying for adults and wonderful in several ways for him. Lan Xiao saw him off, then looked for one breath longer than usual before leaving. Nana was waiting at home. Nan Cheng had kissed his forehead twice.

Qiu Yuxin stood at the classroom door as always, counting her children back into the world of lessons.

"Good morning, Teacher Qiu," Lan Xuanyu said, bowing politely.

"Good morning, Xuanyu." Qiu Yuxin's smile warmed. Then her eyes paused. "You seem taller."

Lan Xuanyu looked pleased at once. "Teacher Nana said I must eat properly."

"Teacher Nana?"

"My home tutor."

Qiu Yuxin had heard a little from Nan Cheng's leave explanation and decided she did not need to know more at the classroom door. "Then remember to study properly too. Go in."

From the opposite classroom, Ye Lingtong's voice rang out.

"Lan Xuanyu!"

Lan Xuanyu turned with the reluctant expression of a child who had discovered that some problems returned after vacation.

Ye Lingtong stood in the doorway of Class A. Her hair was tied neatly. Her back was straight. Her eyes were fiercer than they had been before break, which was impressive because she had started from fierce.

Behind her, Huang Qifan was telling three boys that Battle Bear was not fat, it was muscle in advance.

Yan Shuo stood a little to the side with his school bag on one shoulder and his face arranged into a seriousness that fooled no one who knew him. Several attack-type classmates had formed a half circle around him without admitting they had done so. Children were drawn to new rings the way moths liked light, and Yan Shuo now had the most dangerous kind of light: one that belonged to someone their age.

Ye Lingtong glanced once at Yan Shuo, then back to Lan Xuanyu.

It was not a friendly glance.

Yan Shuo lifted two fingers in greeting anyway. "Morning."

"I wasn't calling you," Ye Lingtong said.

"I heard my friend's name. My ears work."

"Then use them to hear this: today Class A will win."

Huang Qifan thumped his chest. "Definitely!"

The thump sounded a little too much like someone testing whether his new strength made interesting noises.

Lan Xuanyu blinked. "Win what?"

Yan Shuo's serious face cracked. "You don't know?"

"Know what?"

Ye Lingtong looked offended that her future victory had not been properly feared. "Afternoon actual combat. Class A against Class B."

Lan Xuanyu glanced toward Teacher Qiu.

Qiu Yuxin, who had been enjoying the children's thunderclouds with professional calm, said, "Into class first. Worry after attendance."

The new term began without ceremony.

Lessons did not wait for children to feel emotionally prepared. The second bell rang, and Qiu Yuxin stood at the front of Class B with a smile that made several students sit straighter.

"I hope none of you spent the entire holiday forgetting how to cultivate," she said. "We will test soul power and Spiritual Power this morning. In the afternoon, we will have an actual-combat class with Class A. The winning class will be rewarded. The losing class will run five laps."

A groan moved through Class B.

Lan Xuanyu felt a small chill and then remembered Teacher Nana's footwork. The chill became excitement.

Qiu Yuxin tested him first.

The soul-power apparatus showed Rank 12.

"Improved," Qiu Yuxin said. "Good."

The Spiritual Power test showed 32 points.

"Thirty-two," she said, pleased. "Your control has improved a great deal. Continue working hard."

Lan Xuanyu smiled.

What neither Qiu Yuxin nor the watching classmates knew was that part of his attention was quietly holding the golden and silver vortex near his chest. The number was only the public number of a child doing two things at once with a secret no teacher in the room could see.

Qiu Yuxin wrote the result down. She was happy with the Spiritual Power. She was less happy with the soul-power speed. For a child recorded as innate full soul power, Rank 12 still carried worry beneath praise.

Across the corridor, Class A's tests were louder.

Gong Yinghao liked order, but attack-type children treated testing like a competition even when told it was not one. Huang Qifan flexed before his soul-power test. Ye Lingtong told him flexing did not change numbers. Yan Shuo stood quietly until three classmates checked whether he was sick.

"I'm concentrating," he said.

"You?" one boy asked.

Yan Shuo decided not to answer because the question was rude and partly fair.

When his turn came, Gong Yinghao watched with more attention than he had given most of the class.

Yan Shuo placed his hand on the soul-power apparatus.

Rank 12.

The number appeared cleanly.

Whispers rose behind him despite the teacher's look.

Then came the Spiritual Power test.

Yan Shuo sat still. The testing cap felt cool. He had learned not to chase the sensation outward, not to let the Fire Phoenix flare just because people were watching, not to let pride brighten the shoulder heat before the machine finished doing its job.

In the dark behind his eyes, the Dawnflame Kite tucked close. Breath lowered. Heat spread wide, not sharp.

Thirty-six points.

Still Spirit Origin Realm.

Gong Yinghao's eyebrows rose.

"Good," he said, and then, because teachers knew when a single word was too little and too much, added, "Very good. Record: Yan Shuo, Rank 12, Spiritual Power 36. First ring stable."

The whispers changed shape.

Rank 12 like Lan Xuanyu.

Spiritual Power higher than thirty-two.

Yellow ring.

Fire Phoenix.

Yan Shuo did not grin. His mother had warned him that grinning during official measurements made adults think one had spare energy to be assigned more exercises.

He waited until he sat down before letting the corner of his mouth rise.

Ye Lingtong saw it.

Her jaw tightened.

She had worked hard during the break. Her Celestial Body control had improved. Her father had given her methods to keep her soul skill from scattering the moment Lan Xuanyu's strange pressure touched it. She had returned expecting the road in front of her to contain one main stone named Lan Xuanyu.

Now there was another stone beside the road, bright with fire feathers and a public number teachers liked.

Yan Shuo was not her enemy.

That did not make him less annoying.

By afternoon, both classes had gathered in the combat training area.

The rules were simple enough for first graders and sharp enough to make them nervous. Each class would send one student. The loser left the field. The winner could continue unless the teacher replaced them for safety or strategy. The class that lost first would run five laps.

Gong Yinghao stood before Class A with three names heavy in his mind: Ye Lingtong, Huang Qifan, Yan Shuo.

That was a good feeling.

It was also troublesome.

Ye Lingtong was proud and dangerous when provoked. Huang Qifan had returned with a yellow 100-year ring and wanted to prove Battle Bear was impressive. Yan Shuo had a stronger martial-soul label, a legal Spirit Pagoda record, and a mantle skill that was promising but not yet as obedient as a teacher liked.

Across the field, Qiu Yuxin was thinking too.

Class B had Lan Xuanyu.

Class A had too many reasons to aim at him.

She glanced at Yan Shuo. The boy stood in Class A's line, trying not to look at Lan Xuanyu too often and failing. A friendship across classes was sweet at lunch and difficult in combat class.

Gong Yinghao made the first call.

"Class A, Huang Qifan."

Huang Qifan stepped out with such force that the floor seemed personally challenged.

Qiu Yuxin looked at him, then at Gong Yinghao's expression. Huang Qifan was not Ye Lingtong. He was not Yan Shuo. That meant Gong was testing the water while saving sharper pieces for later.

If she sent an ordinary child, Class B would start by losing morale to a yellow-ring strength type.

If she sent Lan Xuanyu, he could gather early advantage before Yan Shuo entered.

"Class B," she said, "Lan Xuanyu."

Lan Xuanyu walked out to cheers.

Yan Shuo's hands tightened for a moment, then loosened. "Don't lose before I can complain," he called.

Lan Xuanyu turned and smiled. "I'll try."

Ye Lingtong made a sound of impatience.

"Begin," Gong Yinghao said.

Huang Qifan roared.

His small body swelled under Battle Bear's transformation, becoming broader and stronger in a way that made several Class B children step back even from the sideline. A yellow ring rose beneath him.

"First soul skill, Bear's Strength!"

His arm thickened. His momentum came straight forward, heavy and honest and full of faith that strength solved the problem in front of it.

Lan Xuanyu took one step back.

Yan Shuo felt the step before he understood it.

It was the same not-being-there rhythm from the courtyard, but cleaner now. Lan Xuanyu did not retreat like a frightened child. He let Huang Qifan's line become obvious, then moved away from where the line thought he belonged.

Silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass coiled around his left hand. Water gathered. The ground beneath Huang Qifan's foot flashed with ice.

Huang Qifan slid.

Lan Xuanyu turned around him, light and direct, then landed on his back with an ice awl pointed down.

"Don't move," Lan Xuanyu said, sounding surprised by how calm he was. "You lost."

For a breath, even Class A forgot to protest.

Then Huang Qifan did it for them.

"You cheated!"

Ye Lingtong stepped forward before Gong Yinghao could speak. "He used actual combat technique. Don't embarrass yourself."

Huang Qifan's face turned red.

Yan Shuo looked from the ice mark on the ground to Lan Xuanyu's feet. His chest felt hot, not with anger but with the clean sting of being shown something useful.

Footwork could beat strength before strength finished arriving.

Dawnfeather Mantle, he realized suddenly, was not only for charging forward. If its feather-light could correct shoulders and ribs, it could also make an angle one breath earlier. Maybe not now. Not perfectly. But someday.

His body stored the thought before he did.

Ye Lingtong had already moved.

"Teacher Gong, I'll go."

Gong Yinghao hesitated for less than a second. Ye Lingtong was the Class A monitress. She had prepared for this. If he refused her now, her pride might explode in a less supervised location.

"Go."

She stepped onto the field.

Lan Xuanyu faced her with less ease than he had shown Huang Qifan. Ye Lingtong did not move like a bear charging down a hallway. Her balance was trained, her eyes fixed, her anger folded tightly enough to become focus.

"Lan Xuanyu," she said, "this time I won't lose."

"You said that last time," he answered before thinking.

Several children gasped with the happiness of witnessing dangerous honesty.

Ye Lingtong's face flushed.

"Begin!"

A faint dragon cry sounded as Ye Lingtong's Celestial Dragon martial soul emerged. Her yellow ring lit, and white light covered her body. This time, the light did not scatter outward wildly. It pulled close to her skin, turning into a silvery-white layer that made her steps sharper.

Yan Shuo straightened.

She had improved.

He did not like her bullying Lan Xuanyu. He also could not deny effort when it stood in front of him. Ye Lingtong's first rush was cleaner than Huang Qifan's entire charge. One hand guarded, one hand sought, feet placed to take away escape.

Lan Xuanyu raised his left hand. Water formed a thin shield.

Ye Lingtong broke into it.

Lan Xuanyu spun.

For a moment water became ice, ice became mist, and the two children passed close enough that several teachers leaned forward. Lan Xuanyu slid along the edge of Ye Lingtong's grasp. Ice awls cracked against her elbows and shattered rather than piercing. Ye Lingtong bent backward with startling control, then drove a kick through the opening with a speed that made Qiu Yuxin and Gong Yinghao shout at the same time.

"No!"

Lan Xuanyu was in the air.

Danger sharpened around him.

Yan Shuo felt it hit the field before he understood what he was feeling.

The soft hidden heaviness he knew from Lan Xuanyu's side suddenly gained an edge. Gold flashed. Not sunlight. Not fire. Something deeper and colder than fire though it shone like it could burn the world if it grew old enough.

Lan Xuanyu's right hand lifted.

Golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass wrapped his arm.

He caught Ye Lingtong's leg.

Ye Lingtong went limp.

It was not a normal loss. Even children understood that. Her strength vanished all at once, like a string cut inside a puppet. The fierce momentum that had filled the field collapsed into a small body falling wrong.

Gong Yinghao rushed forward.

Qiu Yuxin followed.

Lan Xuanyu stood frozen, golden grass already withdrawn, face pale with confusion.

Yan Shuo had not moved.

That surprised him afterward. He had always moved too quickly toward trouble. This time his body had locked itself in place, knees bent, breath low, heat drawn inward around the center of his ribs. The Fire Phoenix in him had crouched. The Dawnflame Kite had tucked so close it felt like a warm feather hidden under storm clouds.

Not fear.

Not exactly.

Recognition without understanding.

He breathed out only when Ye Lingtong gasped and moved.

"What happened?" Huang Qifan whispered.

No one answered because no one knew.

Then children began doing what children did when fear needed a costume.

"Did she let him win?"

"Again?"

"Maybe the monitress likes Lan Xuanyu."

A girl repeated it louder, delighted by the shape of the rumor before noticing Ye Lingtong's face.

Ye Lingtong's eyes filled with tears.

"I don't!" she shouted. "I don't like him!"

She shoved Lan Xuanyu as she passed him, not hard enough to injure, hard enough to make hurt choose a target.

"Lingtong!" Gong Yinghao called, following as she ran from the field.

The whispers tried to grow behind her.

"Shut up," Yan Shuo said.

Because he was not usually quiet, the word struck harder than it should have.

A boy beside him blinked. "What?"

"She didn't let him win. She fell weird. That's not the same."

"How do you know?"

Yan Shuo looked at the place where Lan Xuanyu had stood, then at the doorway where Ye Lingtong had vanished.

Because something had crouched inside him.

Because the air had felt like old thunder.

Because laughing at someone after they fell wrong tasted bad.

He could not say any of that.

So he said, "Because letting someone win doesn't make your face look like you swallowed a lightning bug."

Several children considered this. It was not a scientific explanation, but it was vivid enough to slow the rumor.

Lan Xuanyu looked at Yan Shuo from across the field.

Yan Shuo did not know what expression he was making. He tried to make it normal and probably failed.

Qiu Yuxin checked Lan Xuanyu once, then looked toward the Class A line. Gong Yinghao was still gone after Ye Lingtong. The other assistant teacher had begun restoring order. The combat class stood at the edge of becoming chaos.

Qiu Yuxin made the decision quickly.

"Lan Xuanyu, rest."

Lan Xuanyu blinked. "Teacher, I can still fight."

"You have completed your round. Rest."

In canon's old path, without Yan Shuo in the line and with Class A already shaken, Qiu Yuxin might have let him continue until ten wins made the whole academy talk. Here, she looked at the waiting yellow ring on Yan Shuo's side, at Lan Xuanyu's pale face, at the impossible fall no one could explain, and chose safety before glory could become stupidity.

Lan Xuanyu obeyed, though he looked back once.

Yan Shuo lifted his eyebrows as if to say, Running away?

Lan Xuanyu spread his hands as if to say, Teacher.

Rules, Yan Shuo thought, were very annoying when they were correct.

Class B sent another student. Class A recovered enough to send an ordinary attack-type boy and win. The rhythm of the class returned unevenly. Children fought, lost, won, complained, and discovered that five laps sounded worse every time someone else got knocked down.

Gong Yinghao returned with a face that had become teacher-calm through effort. Ye Lingtong did not return to the line.

He looked once at Qiu Yuxin. She looked back with the polite expression of someone who had removed Lan Xuanyu from the board before he could become either miracle or accident.

"Class A," Gong Yinghao said, "Yan Shuo."

Yan Shuo stepped forward before his heartbeat finished celebrating.

A yellow ring rose beneath his feet.

The combat area warmed.

Dawnfeather Mantle formed over his arms, shoulders, upper back, and chest in close fire-light feathers. It was brighter under the academy lights than it had been in his family practice room, and for one dangerous second pride tried to pour through his hands.

He swallowed it down.

Shoulders, not fists.

Wide, not sharp.

His opponent was a Class B control-side boy with Vine Grass. Not a genius, not famous, not someone anyone would tell stories about unless the story involved losing to fire. But he looked determined, and his teacher had clearly told him not to be frightened just because a ring was yellow.

"Begin."

Vines snapped forward.

Yan Shuo did not burn them at once.

That surprised almost everyone, including himself. The mantle tightened along his forearms and let the first vine slide along feather-light rather than catch his wrist. Heat licked the surface, enough to make the vine loosen, not enough to blaze uncontrolled. He stepped diagonally. The mantle pushed at his shoulder, corrected his balance, and almost pushed too hard.

Almost.

He bent his knee before it could happen.

The hidden small correction made the difference between a clean advance and an embarrassing fall.

A second vine caught his ankle.

Yan Shuo grinned.

The mantle flared with the grin.

Lin Mei was not there to sigh, but he heard the sigh anyway.

He forced the brightness down. Fire-light pressed close. The vine smoked, released, and Yan Shuo arrived inside the control student's range with one palm stopped a careful finger-width from the boy's shoulder.

"Touch," Gong Yinghao called. "Yan Shuo wins."

Class A cheered.

Yan Shuo tried not to look too proud and failed in a way that made several classmates cheer louder.

Across the field, Lan Xuanyu clapped with both hands and smiled.

Yan Shuo's pride shifted. It became warmer and less sharp.

The next Class B student used a small Wind Bell martial soul that made Yan Shuo's fire feathers flutter in all the wrong directions. He nearly overstepped twice. Once, his mantle thinned at the chest because he poured heat toward his elbows. The Dawnflame Kite pulsed in irritation. His breath corrected before panic could.

He won, but not beautifully.

The third time Gong Yinghao looked at the feather edges, which were beginning to fray, and raised a hand.

"Enough. Yan Shuo, withdraw."

Yan Shuo turned so quickly the mantle sparked. "Teacher, I can still fight."

"You can still make mistakes too. Withdraw."

Several Class A children groaned. Yan Shuo wanted to join them. Then he remembered Lan Xuanyu being removed, and adult rules became twice as annoying because they applied to everyone.

He lowered the mantle before it collapsed on its own.

"Yes, Teacher."

As he returned to the line, he passed close enough to Lan Xuanyu's side that their eyes met across the gap between classes.

"Two wins," Yan Shuo mouthed.

Lan Xuanyu held up two fingers, then added three more with a small smile.

Yan Shuo stared.

Lan Xuanyu lowered one finger, looking apologetic.

Four.

Yan Shuo decided this was still unacceptable.

By the end of the period, Class B won.

Not the same way it would have in a world without Yan Shuo. Lan Xuanyu's early victories cut through Huang Qifan and Ye Lingtong and left a strange silence behind them. Yan Shuo's two wins kept Class A from feeling helpless and gave the attack-side children something to shout about. But Ye Lingtong was gone, Huang Qifan had lost first, and Yan Shuo had been withdrawn before his mantle frayed into something unsafe.

Class A ran five laps.

Yan Shuo ran them with his jaw set and his shoulders still warm. Huang Qifan ran beside him muttering that ice on the ground should count as cheating if bears were not allowed to bring shoes with claws. Several Class A students agreed because losing made rules easier to redesign.

Ye Lingtong did not run. She had gone to the infirmary and then home early under teacher permission.

Yan Shuo looked once toward the empty place where she should have been.

He did not like Ye Lingtong much.

He liked empty places less.

After school, fame moved faster than homework.

Lan Xuanyu had defeated Huang Qifan and Ye Lingtong. He had used strange footwork, water that became ice, and a right-hand flash no one could properly describe. Some children said Ye Lingtong had let him win. Some said his Blue Silver Grass was secretly not Blue Silver Grass. Some said Teacher Qiu had removed him because Class B was too strong. That last rumor lasted until someone pointed out that Class B still had to work hard after he left.

Yan Shuo became part of the story too.

Yan Shuo's Rank 12 and Spiritual Power 36 were confirmed. Yan Shuo's yellow ring was real. Yan Shuo's fire feathers could melt vines without setting the practice hall on fire, which disappointed several children. Yan Shuo told people to stop saying Ye Lingtong liked Lan Xuanyu, which made two classmates ask whether Yan Shuo liked Ye Lingtong instead.

Yan Shuo chased them until Gong Yinghao made him stop.

At the Lan apartment, Lan Xuanyu ran inside with the breathless energy of a child carrying both victory and confusion.

"Teacher Nana!"

Nana came out of her room. Her expression softened the moment she saw him.

"You're back."

Lan Xuanyu took her hand and told the whole story in the way children told important stories: out of order, with gestures, and with at least three repeated explanations of the best part. He described Huang Qifan swelling like a bear. He described the ice. He described Ye Lingtong's kick, the danger, the golden grass, and how she suddenly went limp.

Then his excitement faded.

"Teacher Nana, I didn't mean to hurt her. Why did she fall like that?"

Nana's hand rested on his hair.

"Your martial soul restrains hers," she said after a moment. "When her intent was against you and she touched that power, the restraint became strong. If one day she has no ill intent and fights beside you against the same enemy, she may not be harmed. She may even benefit."

Lan Xuanyu frowned.

"But she always wants to beat me."

"Then do not force closeness. But remember: winning is not the same as understanding."

He thought about this very seriously.

"Yan Shuo told people not to say she liked me."

Nana's eyes shifted slightly. "Your friend?"

"En. He is in Class A. He has fire feathers now. They are pretty. He won two fights. Teacher made him stop too."

Nana listened.

"Do you want to fight him?"

Lan Xuanyu looked down at his hands.

His left hand felt like water and silver grass. His right hand still remembered the golden strength that had made Ye Lingtong fall. He liked Yan Shuo. He wanted to compare. He did not want his friend to look like Ye Lingtong had looked.

"I don't know," he admitted.

Nana's voice was gentle. "Then wait until you know how to hold back and how to protect yourself."

Lan Xuanyu nodded, relieved that waiting could be an answer.

In the Yan apartment, Yan Shuo's report was less orderly and more defensive.

"I won two," he said before his parents could ask anything. "Teacher stopped me before I lost. That is different from losing."

Yan Ke nodded gravely. "A very important distinction."

Lin Mei looked at the faint redness along Yan Shuo's shoulders where the mantle had pressed too long. "And why did Teacher Gong stop you?"

"Because he has too many opinions."

"Try again."

Yan Shuo sighed. "Because the feather edges were fraying."

"And?"

"Because I flared when I grinned."

"And?"

"Because school floors are harder than family mats and I almost proved it with my face."

Yan Ke covered his mouth with one hand.

Lin Mei did not smile, which was heroic. "Good. You noticed."

Yan Shuo sat on the floor because chairs seemed too formal for suffering. The Dawnflame Kite was tired but pleased, curled in his chest like a small ember-bird after a long day.

"Lan Xuanyu was strange," he said.

The room quieted in the way adults quieted when a child touched the edge of another family's secret.

Yan Ke asked carefully, "Strange how?"

"His feet are still annoying. Better than before. And when Ye Lingtong was going to hit him, his right hand..." Yan Shuo stopped. The memory did not fit properly into words. "It felt heavy. Not like his usual heavy. Sharper. Cold-gold."

Lin Mei sat down across from him.

"Some martial souls restrain other martial souls," she said. "Some children have things they do not fully understand. You do not chase another child's secret because it startled you."

Yan Shuo looked at her. "But if it is dangerous?"

"Then teachers and parents handle it. Your job is to keep your own shape and not make danger worse."

He did not like that answer either.

It was also probably true.

"I told them not to laugh at Ye Lingtong."

Lin Mei's face softened.

"That was well done."

Yan Shuo looked away quickly because praise after a serious conversation felt embarrassing.

His device chimed later that evening.

Lan Xuanyu: Teacher says I should wait before comparing with you properly.

Yan Shuo stared at the message.

Yan Shuo: My teacher also stopped me. Adults are forming an alliance.

Lan Xuanyu: Maybe rules protect us.

Yan Shuo: Rules protect boring people too.

Lan Xuanyu: You ran five laps?

Yan Shuo: I ran them heroically.

Lan Xuanyu: Did you win?

Yan Shuo considered several versions of the truth.

Yan Shuo: Class B won. I won two. You won more but were removed earlier, so the comparison is invalid.

Lan Xuanyu: Okay.

Yan Shuo: You agreed too fast.

Lan Xuanyu: Teacher Nana says winning is not understanding.

Yan Shuo stared at that one longer.

Then he typed more slowly.

Yan Shuo: Then next time we understand first and win later.

Lan Xuanyu: Okay.

Outside both apartments, Zi Luo City continued discussing the night the sky had cried alarm.

Inside one home, Nana sat by the window and looked at a moon she did not remember leaving behind in another life. Inside another, Yan Shuo lay awake with his shoulders sore and his chest warm, replaying a golden flash, a footstep that was not where it should be, and the feeling of his fire crouching without surrender.

He did not know what Lan Xuanyu was.

He did not know what Nana had held above the city.

He did not know why some pressures felt less like enemies than mountains that had always been beside the road.

He only knew school had returned, and the world had become wider while still expecting homework by morning.

That seemed unfair.

It also seemed exciting.

When he finally slept, the Dawnflame Kite slept with him, tucked close beneath the dream of wings that were not ready yet.
