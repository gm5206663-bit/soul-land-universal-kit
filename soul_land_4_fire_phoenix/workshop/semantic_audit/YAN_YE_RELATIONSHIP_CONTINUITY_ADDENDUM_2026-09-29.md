# Yan Shuo / Ye Lingtong Relationship Continuity Addendum

**Date:** 2026-09-29  
**Status:** **ACTIVE characterization lock for the existing C55–C60 author-review line; not new-chapter authority.**

## Author decision

Ye Lingtong already carries a **private, age-appropriate crush** on public Yan Shuo.

It is not a declared relationship, mature romance, public knowledge, rescue debt, team merger, or permission for either child to decide the other’s future. It is an emotionally consequential private fact: she is drawn to him and affected by him, while pride, rivalry, embarrassment, self-respect, danger, and the fact that public Yan Shuo is a boy all make the feeling difficult for her to admit even to herself.

Yan is not licensed to presume, claim, or resolve that feeling. At the C55/C56 edge, he can recognize Ye’s pride and protect her dignity without knowing the private name for every reaction.

## Why generic “familiarity” was wrong

The prior C55 correction accurately repaired Song/Luo’s failure to recognize Ye, but it mistakenly described Yan’s distinction as only “longer familiarity.” That flattened years of established, asymmetric emotional history.

Yan is, in Ye’s lived view, not simply another Elite Junior Class peer. He is the unusually capable, disciplined, visibly handsome Fire Phoenix boy who rose into the class-monitor role; someone she has watched, competed beside, and been seen by through some of her worst public moments. His public strength, intelligence, restraint, and social position do not erase Ye’s own competence. They make her wish to meet his eyes at her best rather than wounded, frightened, or forced to run.

## C55 writing requirements

1. **Recognition is not the whole beat.** Song and Luo recognize Ye as their fellow Elite Junior Class peer. Yan recognizes her too, but Ye’s sight of him carries private relief, attraction, pride, shame, and the instinct to hide vulnerability.
2. **No flattening.** Do not call Yan merely a familiar academy friend, a slightly closer classmate, or an interchangeable rescuer.
3. **Dignity is causal.** Yan’s bounded aid and non-claiming answer matter because he neither mocks her worst moment nor tries to own her route. Ye chooses the wash herself.
4. **No romantic shortcut.** No confession, reciprocal declaration, mature framing, physical escalation, possessiveness, public reveal, or conversion of tactical help into a debt.
5. **No character theft.** Song’s shield geometry, Luo’s terrain reading, and Ye’s decision remain essential. Yan’s emotional weight cannot erase their roles or turn the scene into Yan soloing a rescue.
6. **C56–C60 consistency.** The subsequent weather/dawn/human-visibility/trace-ethics packages may preserve the fact that Dorm336 does not pursue or bait Ye, but they must not manufacture a second encounter or resolve the private feeling. C59/C60 must keep Ye’s wash unmarked and off-route.

`late_arc_rebuild/drafts/Chapter_55_What_We_Leave_Open.md`, its contract, and its audit now carry this lock. C55 gives Ye an unspoken private reaction to seeing Yan at her worst and preserves Yan’s refusal to turn that reaction into a claim.
