# Chapter 53 — A Path Borrowed From Fire

Liu Feng vanished into green shadow.

He did not run like someone fleeing. He moved like a spear answering its own throw. White light flashed in his hand as the White Dragon Spear appeared, and the moment his toes touched a low-hanging vine, his body rose lightly over roots and leaves. The vine bent under him, sprang back, and Liu had already crossed into the next patch of dim forest.

Three hundred meters.

No farther.

Lan Xuanyu watched until the layers of leaves swallowed Liu's figure. Only then did he guide Qian Lei back against the massive tree.

"Lean here."

Qian Lei obeyed with rare silence.

Lan raised his left hand. Silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass unfurled from his palm, pale lines gleaming against the green shade. Moisture gathered in the air. One thin ice awl appeared, then another, then a ring of sharp cold points took shape around the tree's base and the two boys.

The ice did not look impressive compared with the firestorms of the previous qualifier.

That was why it was useful.

No blaze. No loud impact. No signal sent far through the forest.

Only quiet, angled protection.

Lan set the ice awls where sudden movement would have to cross them and kept his body half-turned toward the direction Liu had taken. He did not stare at one point for too long. The forest had too many possible mouths.

Qian closed his eyes.

His hands still hovered near the Coin of Summoning, but his face changed. The joking looseness disappeared, leaving the strange alertness Lan had learned to trust. Qian Lei's battle power was unreliable in the simplest sense, but his sense for danger had always been keen. Perhaps summoners had to learn that. A door that opened to unknown beasts either made a person foolish or made him feel changes before others did.

"I don't sense any dangerous aura nearby," Qian whispered.

Lan nodded. "Alright."

He did not lower the ice.

Qian opened one eye. "You believe me, but not enough to remove the spikes?"

"I believe you enough not to panic. The spikes stay."

"Reasonable," Qian said, and shut his eye again.

Among the Elite Junior Class, Lan and Qian had the strongest Spiritual Power. Lan's Spirit Sea had opened properly, and Qian's perception had always stood out even before his reward. In a forest like this, where sight ended at leaves and sound bent around trunks, Spiritual Power mattered as much as eyesight.

It told them there was no immediate pressure.

Immediate was not the same as safe.

The forest waited.

A short while later, leaves trembled in the direction Liu had gone. Lan's ice awls tilted slightly. Qian's hand tightened over the coin.

Liu Feng slipped back between two hanging vines and landed without disturbing more than a few dead leaves.

"No soul beasts nearby," he said quietly.

Lan let the ice awls settle but did not dismiss them.

Liu looked past the ring of cold points, then at the dense, pathless green around them. "Xuanyu, we don't have a map. We don't know where we are. How do we tell which direction is deeper into the Star Dou Forest and which direction goes outward?"

That question mattered more than the empty scout report.

If they chose wrongly and moved outward for half a day, they might survive and lose. If they rushed inward too quickly, they might meet something that ended all seven days in one breath. If they wandered without method, the forest and other teams would both profit.

Lan smiled faintly.

"We can tell by the soul beasts' strength."

Qian opened both eyes. "That sounds comforting and terrifying at the same time."

Lan continued, "The farther out the region is, the weaker the soul beasts should be. If we're really in the Peripheral Region and our luck isn't especially bad, we should mostly encounter ten-year or hundred-year soul beasts. We can handle those."

Liu nodded once.

"If we meet thousand-year soul beasts, we have to be very careful," Lan said. "And if the soul beasts keep getting stronger, it means we're moving deeper. If they grow weaker and fewer, it likely means we're moving away from the core."

"So the beasts become our map," Liu said.

"A dangerous map," Lan replied.

Qian Lei lifted his thumb. "Good. Very good. Anyway, we'll listen to you. Wherever you say to go, we go."

Lan glanced at him. "That includes running."

"I am excellent at obeying running orders."

"And hiding."

"A noble survival technique."

"And not grabbing points just because you see them."

Qian's thumb lowered halfway. "Your trust wounds me."

"Then remember it."

Qian made a pained expression, but did not argue. The rules of this round were too sharp for greed to be harmless. Last kill took points. Teams could kill teams. Seven days meant a mistake made on the first morning could become another team's harvest on the sixth.

Lan narrowed his eyes and looked into the forest.

"Summon a soul beast. We'll use it to find a path and start moving in one direction."

Qian turned his head so quickly that the back of it almost touched the tree.

"Summon one right now?"

"Yes."

"My first summoning still has an idle period. Fifteen to twenty minutes before I can summon again, depending on the situation. If I use it now and something really appears..."

"You have Replicate as a contingency," Lan said. "And this assessment lasts seven days. If we want to survive that long, we can't count on never using Soul Power. We need to find safe places to recover and recharge."

Qian looked down at the Coin of Summoning.

Lan said, "A summoned beast can walk ahead of us. If something attacks, it bears the first strike instead of Liu or you. That gives us time to judge, retreat, or fight calmly. Using a summon first is not wasting it. It is buying information."

Qian stared at him for a breath.

Then his whole face relaxed.

"You don't have to explain," he said cheerfully. "I'll do whatever you say anyway. It's wonderful not using my brain."

Liu Feng gave him a flat look. "You say that too proudly."

"Because I have chosen a commander. This is called efficient division of labor."

"It is called laziness."

"A lazy person who survives seven days is still a genius."

Lan almost smiled.

Qian tossed the Coin of Summoning out.

It spun once in the dim forest light. Soul Power gathered, and two yellow Soul Rings rose from beneath Qian's feet.

Lan's eyes paused on them.

They were still yellow.

Not purple. Not a thousand-year breakthrough. Nothing so exaggerated.

But the color was clearly darker than before, richer and heavier at the edges. The reward had not become a boastful number in anyone's mouth, but its effect was no longer abstract. After proper Spirit Pagoda processing, Qian's rings had reached the five-hundred-year realm, and the enhancement showed in the darker yellow light.

Qian noticed Lan noticing and grinned. "Handsome, right?"

"Summon first. Be handsome later."

"Cruel commander."

A Gate of Summoning rose before them.

Lan lifted his right hand this time. Golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass flowed outward and wrapped around the Gate. Gold lines brightened across the grass. The gate's aura steadied under the familiar enhancement.

Qian blinked. "Xuanyu, why aren't you using your silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass to help my summoning anymore? Dong Qianqiu's power should have a huge effect, right?"

Lan froze for half a breath.

The forest did not change. The Gate hummed. Qian was only asking a practical question.

That did not stop the memory from arriving with cruel timing: the accidental brush, Dong's furious chase, the arm-hold in public, his own childish counter, the beating outside Teacher Nana's door, and the apology that had not made the situation less complicated.

Lan shook his head.

"I offended her," he said. "If we summoned in that direction now, she probably wouldn't appear. Also, she should be taking Shrek Academy's qualifiers too."

Qian's expression became strange for exactly one instant, the expression of someone who wanted to ask ten questions and valued his life enough to ask none.

"Across another planet?" Liu Feng asked.

Lan nodded. "The qualifiers should be happening at the same time across the Federation. Shrek has its own algorithm to prevent different planets from learning the tests in advance. Even though the universe separates us, the timing should be controlled."

Qian looked impressed despite himself. "Shrek is really strict."

"It has to be," Lan said.

As for whether Dong Qianqiu could pass, Lan had never worried too much. She was Teacher Nana's disciple, and she had followed Teacher Nana for even longer than he had. Her strength, control, and temperament were not ordinary.

That thought ended there.

The current gate was in front of him. His current teammates were beside him. The forest would not spare them because another girl on another planet might be fighting her own assessment.

A low roar came from the Gate of Summoning.

Heat rolled out.

The three boys' expressions changed at almost the same time.

A fiery-red figure emerged.

It was roughly five meters long. Red scales covered its entire body, hard plates overlapping from head to tail. Spikes rose along its back. Its head was massive, low, and blunt, and smoke leaked from its mouth with every breath. Heat shimmered faintly around it, making nearby leaves curl at the edges.

Qian Lei stared.

Liu Feng stared.

Lan stared too, but for a different reason.

"Ground Fire Dragon Lizard," Lan said.

Not an ordinary Ground Fire Lizard.

An evolved close relative. A type of Ground Dragon. Weaker than a Ground Fire Scarlet Dragon by a large margin, but much stronger than the lesser lizards that had swarmed them before. Its defense was not its greatest advantage, but its attack range was wide, and it could spit fireballs.

After the last qualifier, summoning something from that family felt like the Soul World had a sense of humor.

A poor one.

Qian Lei's face twisted. "Of all things, we summoned a relative of those monsters?"

"It is on our side," Lan said.

"For now," Liu added.

"Don't help," Qian muttered.

Still, he raised a hand and waved with forced boredom, as if commanding a five-meter fire lizard was not a relief after fearing something useless might emerge.

The Ground Fire Dragon Lizard obeyed. It lowered its massive head and charged forward.

Vines snapped aside. Low branches broke. The scales along its head and shoulders shoved through the forest's tangled growth, opening a crude path where none had existed. Heat from its body brushed the plants, and smaller leaves shriveled before touching its scales.

"Move," Lan said.

Liu Feng took the front behind the summoned beast, White Dragon Spear ready and body light enough to avoid the broken vines snapping back. Qian Lei stayed in the middle, his attention connected to the Ground Fire Dragon Lizard. Lan fell to the rear.

Golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass extended from Lan's hand and wrapped around Liu Feng's waist.

The connection was not pulled tight. It rested there like a promise.

If danger appeared, Lan could enhance him immediately.

If Liu had to retreat, Lan would know from the shift before sight caught up.

If the forest tried to split them, the line would object.

The Ground Fire Dragon Lizard pushed onward.

Qian's darker yellow rings turned steadily beneath him as he controlled the summon. The improvement from his ring realm showed again. The lizard's duration was longer than before — roughly a minute and a half. A minute and a half did not sound like much in a seven-day test, but in a pathless forest, behind a summoned Ground Dragon that burned vines by walking, it was distance, safety, and information.

They chose one direction and moved straight.

The forest, strangely, seemed peaceful.

Once the first tension eased, the air revealed layers of plant fragrance. Damp bark, crushed leaves, wild flowers hidden somewhere under the canopy, and the clean breath of ancient green mixed together. Light filtered down in broken pieces. The ground was uneven but alive rather than barren.

For a moment, if one ignored the rules, the killing, and the possibility of being eaten, the simulated Star Dou Forest was almost beautiful.

Qian inhaled. "This place smells expensive."

"Don't get relaxed," Liu said from the front.

"I said expensive, not safe."

The Ground Fire Dragon Lizard moved faster than any of them would have risked alone. Its head scale was the hardest part of its body, and the fire property there made it perfect for breaking the way. Vines that touched its front withered. Thin creepers blackened. Leaf walls split apart under heat and impact.

Lan counted time silently.

A minute was nearly gone.

The lizard continued straight.

Then, just as the summoning duration was nearing its end, one of the thicker vines ahead moved.

It did not sway.

It sprang.

The vine lashed down from the side like a living rope and wrapped around the Ground Fire Dragon Lizard's head.

Qian's eyes widened. Liu Feng's spear lifted at once.

"Plant-type soul beast," Lan said sharply.

Such soul beasts were rare in Soul World compared with the beasts students usually met, but rare did not mean weak enough to ignore. The vine tightened around the lizard's head, and for a heartbeat the path stopped.

Then the Ground Fire Dragon Lizard answered with fire.

Its body flushed a deep, metallic red. Scale after scale brightened until it looked as though strips of heated iron had been laid beneath its skin. A sizzling sound cut through the forest.

The vine around its head turned yellow.

Then brown.

Then it withered.

A strong plant aura burst from it, bitter and green, as the hidden plant soul beast suffered and began to withdraw its vines.

Fire restrained wood.

Lan felt the principle fit into the map they were building.

The Ground Fire Dragon Lizard was not strong compared with the Scarlet Dragons, but here in the Peripheral Region, against weaker plant-type soul beasts, it was exactly the right tool. Its dragon-type body gave it strength beyond ordinary Ground Fire Lizards, and its fire made the forest's grasp hesitate.

The surrounding thick vines separated as though a curtain had been pulled aside.

The lizard rushed forward.

"Follow!" Lan ordered.

They did.

Liu flashed ahead behind the summoned beast. Qian ran with the awkward determination of someone very aware that the safe road had an expiration time. Lan kept the rear, golden grass still around Liu's waist, eyes sweeping for the plant soul beast's next attempt.

No second attack came.

The brief window was too valuable to waste.

With Soul Master speed, following a path-breaking beast, they moved far faster than they could have while testing every root. The Ground Fire Dragon Lizard cleared a line through the forest, and the three boys used every breath of its remaining time.

By the time the Gate of Summoning floated beside Qian again and the lizard's body blurred backward into it, they had advanced roughly a kilometer.

Then the summoned beast disappeared.

The sudden loss of heat made the forest feel colder.

Lan raised his hand.

They slowed at once.

"Higher ground," he said.

Liu found it first — a slight rise where roots broke through the earth and the sightline between trunks widened by a few steps. Not safe, but less blind. They climbed it and stopped.

Qian sat down almost immediately, breathing harder than the others. He did not complain. He closed his eyes, controlled his breathing, and began recovering Soul Power.

Lan watched the forest while Qian recovered.

The first experiment had given them several answers.

No strong soul beasts nearby at the starting point.

Weak plant-type soul beast after moving.

Fire had restrained it easily.

The region still looked like the outer side rather than a deeper zone.

Direction not proven, but usable.

When Qian's breathing steadied, he opened his eyes and summoned again.

This time, the Gate of Summoning activated a little faster.

Lan noticed.

Liu noticed too.

Qian noticed them noticing and sat up straighter.

"Yes, yes, admire me quietly."

"The gate is faster," Lan said.

"A little," Qian admitted, then failed to hide his grin. "After the ring realm improved, the activation feels smoother. Still not fast enough, but better."

For someone like Qian Lei, who had little direct battle power when a summon was unavailable, that slight shortening mattered. The space between one summon and the next was the space where enemies could discover he was a summoner with no beast in front of him.

Shorter meant safer.

Safer meant more chances to be useful.

They continued inward.

Summon, move, rest.

Test the forest.

Measure the danger by what answered.

The rhythm was not glorious. There was no dramatic charge, no instant pile of points, no heroic roar. It was methodical, cautious, and far more suited to surviving seven days than chasing the first shadow that moved.

After another recovery period, Qian lay back against a root and stared at the canopy.

"I really want to go to the Spirit Ascension Platform soon," he said from the bottom of his heart.

Lan looked at him.

Qian lifted one hand as if reaching toward an imaginary future ring. "If my first Soul Ring could reach a thousand years, I wonder how much improvement it would bring. Wouldn't it be wonderful if I could summon without stopping until all my Soul Power ran out?"

Liu Feng snorted. "You dream beautifully."

"Dreams are free. Platform tickets are not."

"What good does thinking about it do?" Liu said. "If it were that simple, why would we work so hard?"

Qian rolled his eyes. "Because the world refuses to appreciate summoners properly."

"The world appreciates summons after they appear," Liu said. "Before that, it appreciates quiet."

Lan did not interrupt immediately.

Qian's wish sounded like greed, but it was also a correct direction. The Lucky Wheel reward had already proved how sensitive his martial soul was to ring age. Darker yellow rings, longer duration, faster activation — every small improvement changed the usefulness of the Gate.

"It isn't impossible," Lan said at last.

Qian sat up. "Really?"

"Not now," Lan added.

Qian sat down again.

Liu looked amused.

Lan continued, "Your cultivation isn't enough yet. From what we've seen over the past few years, several factors affect your summoning: your Soul Power rank, your Spiritual Power rank, and the strength of your Soul Rings."

Qian's joking expression thinned into attention.

Lan said, "If your Soul Power rank becomes high enough, you should eventually be able to summon more continuously. But the strength of whatever appears still may not be certain. That uncertainty is part of your martial soul."

Qian nodded slowly.

"If you want to reduce the summoning interval and make the gate respond faster," Lan continued, "you need to improve those foundations. Soul Power, Spiritual Power, and Soul Ring strength. Especially Spiritual Power. From the way your perception, control, and activation have changed, Spiritual Power clearly has a strong relation with your summoning."

Qian looked at his hands.

The forest was quiet again, but this time he did not look as if he wanted it to stay quiet forever.

He looked as if he had been shown a path.

Not a straight one.

Not an easy one.

But one that belonged to him.

Lan rose and looked deeper into the trees.

The Star Dou Forest would not give them a map.

So they would make one from danger, distance, and every beast the Gate borrowed from chance.
