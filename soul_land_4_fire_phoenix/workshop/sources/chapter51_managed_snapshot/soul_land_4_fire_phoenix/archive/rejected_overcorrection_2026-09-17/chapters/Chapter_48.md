# Chapter 48 — The Line in the Stars

"It was the final exam of the sixth semester."

Yin Tianfan said the words once.

Then he stopped.

The office was quiet enough that Lan Xuanyu could hear the faint hum from the wall panels and the soft fading click of the testing device as its number disappeared from the display.

Five hundred and five.

The number had already vanished, but Lan could still feel the changed weight of it inside his head. His thoughts had more room now. The world had more edges. Even Yin Tianfan's silence seemed to have shape: old pride, old pain, old unwillingness wrapped together so tightly that Lan could not tell where one ended and the other began.

Yin looked out the window.

The night outside Heaven Luo Academy had settled deep. At this hour, the buildings no longer felt like classrooms and offices. They felt like sleeping beasts, full of hidden corridors and quiet breathing. Somewhere far away, students who had survived the qualifier were eating, sleeping, complaining, or pretending not to be afraid of the next round.

Lan sat straight and did not interrupt.

Yin Tianfan had always been loud.

He was loud when he laughed, loud when he scolded, loud when he cheated a simulator's angle and called it education. Even when he whispered, there was usually a kind of round, shameless confidence behind his voice.

But now the silence belonged to him too.

That made Lan listen more carefully.

After a long moment, Yin exhaled.

"You probably cannot imagine it," he said, "but when your teacher first entered Shrek, I was not like this."

Lan glanced at his teacher's stomach.

He did not mean to.

His eyes simply moved.

Yin's head snapped back.

"What was that look?"

"Nothing, Teacher."

"Nothing? Your eyes just insulted twenty years of my life."

Lan immediately lowered his gaze. "I was respecting history."

"You were doubting history."

"Only slightly."

Yin pointed at him. "Good. Honesty is important, but too much honesty gets disciples beaten."

The heaviness eased by a fraction.

Then Yin leaned back in his chair, and a crooked smile touched his face. "I really was handsome. Tall enough, thin enough, sharp enough. My results were good, my mouth was better, and the girls in the Space Command System were not blind. Some of them were very pretty. If not for Shrek's rule forbidding romance before eighteen, hmph..."

Lan's mouth twitched.

"Teacher," he said carefully, "the final exam?"

"I am giving background."

"This background seems dangerous."

"Only because you are too young to appreciate your teacher's youthful glory."

"I am almost eleven."

"Exactly. Too young."

Yin waved a hand, as if throwing away the useless tragedy of Lan's insufficient age.

Then his smile faded again.

"Fine. The final exam."

His fingers tapped once on the table.

"By that time, we had already studied for several years in Shrek's Space Command System. Do you understand what that means?"

Lan nodded slowly. "The curriculum was far ahead of ordinary academies."

"Far ahead?" Yin snorted. "Ordinary academies teach you how not to crash. Shrek teaches you why crashing can be useful, when it is unforgivable, and how to make someone else believe you are about to crash when you are actually cutting his escape route. By eighteen, we had already finished more than what most advanced academies could provide. The next question was whether one could enter the inner court."

Inner court.

Even the words had weight.

Lan had not seen Shrek Academy yet, but everything around it seemed to bend people's hearts. Ji Hongbin's sternness changed when Shrek was mentioned. Yin Tianfan's laughter cracked. Ling Yiyi's smile hid knives. Qian Lei had cried and refused something he could barely explain because of the word Shrek.

Lan had wanted Shrek before.

Now the word felt less like a school and more like a door that tested whether a person was ready to become what he claimed to be.

Yin continued, "I wanted that door. I thought I deserved it. My command scores were excellent. Some teachers admired me. Some disliked me. They all agreed on one thing: my style was dangerous."

"Dangerous how?"

"Unexpected moves. Feints inside feints. Using bait, using false weakness, using harassment, using terrain that no sensible person would treat as a battlefield. If there was a clean road and a dirty road, I usually chose the road where the enemy thought no one would dare walk."

Lan was silent.

He thought of the false magma pool.

He thought of hiding where the Ground Fire Scarlet Dragon's body and the heat pressure made others' instincts scream danger.

He thought of Qian Lei's Replicate, thirty seconds, one terrifying bird, and two eyes becoming doors to victory.

Yin saw his expression and smiled without joy.

"You recognize part of it. Good. You should. You are my student."

"Teacher Ji called it a shortcut," Lan said.

"Old Ji calls breakfast a test of foundation if you eat too quickly." Yin snorted. "But he was not completely wrong. A tactic can be brilliant and still become poison if the commander forgets what it costs. Back then, I did not believe that. I thought victory proved everything."

Yin's gaze drifted again.

"The exam was set as a large simulated space battle. Every student in the Space Command System had to participate. Three students formed one group. Each of us commanded a fleet, and the three fleets together became a joint fleet. Groups fought other groups. The final grade came from command operation, survival, battle result, and contribution."

Lan's newly opened Spirit Sea seemed to respond to the words.

Not with power.

With imagination.

Inside his mind, the office table became a star map. Three small fleet markers moved through darkness. Another nine markers circled outside them, forming a net.

Yin said, "My two teammates were not as strong in command as I was. That was obvious. At the time, I thought the teachers arranged it so my talent could be shown more clearly."

Lan looked up.

"Was that wrong?"

Yin's mouth twisted. "Not entirely. That was one reason. But there was another reason I refused to see."

"They wanted to test how you led weaker teammates."

Yin's eyes paused on him.

For a second, the old man's face held something almost gentle.

"Yes," he said. "You understand faster than I did."

Lan did not feel proud.

He thought of Qian Lei standing before Ling Yiyi's offer and refusing to leave his team.

He thought of Liu Feng quietly holding the line with no complaint.

He thought of Yan Shuo choosing Dorm336 over individual certainty, of Song Yichen planting his shield where Luo Haoran told him even when the ground itself wanted to kill them.

The qualifier had been cruel.

But in some ways, it had already asked the same question Yin Tianfan's final exam once asked.

What is a teammate?

A tool?

A burden?

A path?

A line?

Yin continued.

"At the start, everything went smoothly. I predicted the first contact angle, avoided the obvious battlefield, and used asteroid belts to hide our signal echoes. We eliminated one careless group quickly. The score rose. My confidence rose even faster. Then the real trouble began."

"The coalition?" Lan asked.

"Three teams," Yin said. "Nine student-commanded fleets total. They knew I was dangerous. They also knew that if I scored too high, their own chance of entering the inner court would become smaller. So they joined hands before I could break them separately and surrounded us."

His finger drew a slow circle on the table.

"Nine fleets outside. Three fleets inside. My two teammates looked at me. Do you know what I felt?"

"Pressure?"

"Excitement."

Lan's brows moved.

Yin laughed once. "That is the problem, Xuanyu. A normal commander should first think: how do I keep my people alive? I thought: finally, a stage worthy of me."

The office seemed colder.

Yin's voice became steady, almost like he was giving an after-action report.

"I made them chase shadows. I sent small units to provoke their left flank, then withdrew through a radiation belt where their sensors blurred. I ordered my second teammate to expose a transport formation as bait, and when the enemy tried to swallow it, I detonated a false supply node behind them. I used my first teammate's carrier group as a decoy to pull two enemy fleets out of formation, then cut the gap with my main attack force."

Lan could see it.

Not perfectly, but enough.

Small lights moving in the dark.

A commander laughing because the net around him had become material for him to weave with.

"The enemy was stronger in total numbers," Yin said. "But coalitions formed from fear have weak joints. They did not trust each other. I hit those joints again and again. Harassment. Interference. Fake retreats. Communication spoofing. Ambushes from ugly angles. Provocation. Every time they tried to tighten the encirclement, I made one side suspect the other of moving too slowly."

"And your teammates?"

Yin's finger stopped.

"They obeyed."

Two words.

He did not hide behind them.

"They obeyed me every time. When I needed one of them to draw fire, he drew fire. When I needed the other to hold a lane with half a formation, he held it. They lost ships. They lost supply craft. Their fleets became thinner and thinner, but they did not refuse me. They believed I had a plan."

Lan's hands tightened on his knees.

Yin looked at him. "I did have a plan. That was the terrible part. It worked."

He continued.

"After a long fight, the three opposing teams could no longer bear the losses. Their joint line was cracking. They contacted me. They said they wanted to negotiate. They promised that if I let them keep enough score to avoid a miserable ranking, they would cooperate with me against the remaining groups."

Lan said, "Did you believe them?"

"Of course not."

"Then why agree?"

Yin's smile was thin. "Because the moment a desperate enemy believes negotiation has begun, his defense changes. Weapons cool. Formation opens. Command attention moves from survival to bargaining. That is a battlefield opening."

Lan did not speak.

Yin's eyes darkened.

"I accepted. I used friendly communication channels, confirmed a temporary alliance structure, and asked them to adjust formation so our fleets could merge safely. They did."

His voice lowered.

"Then I attacked."

Lan's imagined star map flashed white.

"I had already hidden assault craft near their exposed command routes. My main guns were charged under maintenance cover. I struck their flagship cores first. Before they understood what happened, I cut their retreat and destroyed all three teams' fleets."

The silence after those words was not like the silence before.

Before, Yin had been searching memory.

Now, memory had arrived.

Lan asked softly, "You won?"

"Against them, yes. Completely. Beautifully, if you only judge by tactics. Nine fleets tried to swallow me. I made them negotiate, then erased them. At that moment, I felt..."

He closed his eyes.

"I felt unstoppable."

Lan's stomach felt heavy.

"What did your teammates say?"

"That was where I truly failed."

Yin opened his eyes again.

"They were not happy. They had supported me from the beginning. They had paid the price for my tactics. But when they saw me break a promise immediately after accepting it, one of them asked why I did that. The other said that even in a simulated war, a commander who made an agreement had to understand what his word meant."

"And you?"

"I told them war never has too much deception. I told them we did not owe mercy to people who had tried to surround us. I told them victory with the least cost was the best proof."

Yin's face became expressionless.

"They said I was wrong. They said ugly things."

Lan did not ask what.

He thought he already knew the shape of them.

Maybe: you never saw us as teammates.

Maybe: you only used us.

Maybe: you are not a commander.

Maybe: you are just someone who wants to win.

Yin's fingers curled.

"I was proud. I was angry. I could remain calm under enemy pressure, but I could not tolerate my own teammates accusing me after I had just won an impossible battle. That weakness had been there all along. I simply did not see it until I had enough power to make it unforgivable."

Lan's breath slowed.

Yin said, "I turned my fleet on them."

The words were quiet.

They were worse because they were quiet.

"Their fleets were already badly damaged. I knew the gaps in their formation because I had commanded them the whole time. I knew which ships had lost shields. I knew which carrier group had no interceptors left. They were never my match in direct command."

His voice roughened.

"It did not take long."

Lan saw it again.

Three allied lights in the dark.

One light turning.

Two damaged lights unable to escape.

Then silence.

"The simulation became clean," Yin said. "No arguments. No accusations. No one beside me."

Lan did not realize he had stopped breathing until his chest tightened.

He inhaled slowly.

He had known Yin Tianfan was strange.

He had known his teacher's methods were ruthless in the simulator. The first time he had piloted a warship simulation under Yin's supervision, he had been attacked from behind by his own teacher and then told that awareness had improved. Yin could smile after doing outrageous things and call them lessons.

But destroying enemies through deception was one thing.

Destroying teammates because they objected was something else.

Lan looked at Yin Tianfan and, for the first time, did not see only the fat, sly, brilliant teacher.

He saw the student who had once believed victory could justify standing alone over everyone's wreckage.

"Teacher," Lan said softly, "did you regret it immediately?"

Yin was quiet.

"A little," he said. "Not enough."

Lan's eyes lifted.

Yin looked at him without hiding.

"That is also part of the truth. After I destroyed them, I knew I had been rash. I knew I had acted from anger. But I also thought: so what? Look at the result. Look at the command talent. Look at what I achieved. If Shrek was truly Shrek, surely it would understand the battlefield."

He laughed once, bitter and short.

"Shrek understood better than I did."


The test ended.

Yin Tianfan ranked first.

He said it without pride now.

"First place. Undisputed. My score was the highest. My operation record was dazzling. The teachers could not deny my command ability."

Lan's fingers relaxed and tightened again.

"Then they expelled you."

"Yes."

Yin's shoulders sank a little.

"At the time, I was furious. I shouted that it was a simulated battlefield. I said no real soldiers had died. I said my enemies had formed a coalition to suppress me first. I said deception and ambush were part of war. I said a commander should be judged by victory."

"What did they say?"

Yin's gaze became distant.

"My favorite teacher came to see me. He did not say my tactics against the enemy were worthless. He said Shrek could accept cunning. It could accept ambush, surprise attack, strange routes, ugly but effective methods. War is not a children's game."

Lan listened.

Yin said, "But he also said there was a line."

A line.

Lan felt the word settle.

"He said a person who could not forgive allies, who could destroy teammates over a disagreement after they had trusted him through danger, was not fit to command a fleet. Today it was a simulation. Tomorrow it could be soldiers. A commander who treats allies as disposable obstacles may win one battle and ruin an army."

Yin's mouth pressed tight.

"He said the me at that time was not a hero. I was an ambitious careerist with talent and no restraint. If Shrek handed me a real fleet, I might bring disaster to everyone under my command."

The office hummed softly around them.

Lan thought of Qian Lei's pale face when he refused Ling Yiyi.

He thought of Liu Feng's simple words: he will wake.

He thought of Yan Shuo telling Song and Luo that second place belonged to all three.

He thought of himself beneath the Dragon's body, calculating exactly when Qian Lei should call the Emerald Demon Bird.

Had he used Qian?

The question came sharply.

Then he answered it honestly.

Yes.

He had used Qian's ability. He had used Liu Feng's trust. He had used the environment, the enemy's wounds, the timing, the summon imprint. He had used everything.

But Qian had known the risk and followed because they were teammates.

Liu had stood with them.

Lan had not thought of abandoning them for a cleaner personal score. He had not treated their objections as obstacles. He had waited until all three could act.

Still, the line was closer than he liked.

A commander always used people.

The difference was whether he remembered they were people.

Yin watched him, and perhaps he saw the thought arrive.

"Good," Yin said.

Lan looked up.

"If this story only makes you shocked, then it is useless. If it makes you afraid of all cunning, it is also useless. You must understand the difference."

"Between tactics and betrayal," Lan said.

"Between cost and sacrifice without consent. Between asking a teammate to hold a dangerous line because the battle requires it, and throwing him away because his damage does not hurt you. Between deceiving an enemy and becoming someone your allies can no longer trust."

Yin leaned forward.

"Xuanyu, you have command talent. You are calm for your age. You see angles other people ignore. In the qualifier, you dared to hide beneath a Dragon and turn impossible danger into opportunity. I am proud of that. Do not become ashamed of being clever. A battlefield will not reward children for being clean."

His voice hardened.

"But remember this: if your cleverness needs you to cut off the hands holding you up, then it is not command. It is self-destruction with a better-looking report."

Lan lowered his head.

"I understand."

"Not fully," Yin said. "You are too young to fully understand. That is why I am telling you now, before Shrek teaches you with a knife."

Lan did not argue.

Yin leaned back again.

"After the expulsion, everything collapsed. Shrek erased my honor. I could not continue through the academy path. Other advanced Space Command programs knew my record and would not accept me. For a while, I thought the entire world was wrong."

"How long?" Lan asked.

"Two years," Yin said.

He did not make it funny.

"Two years of anger, nightmares, and unwillingness. I replayed that exam again and again. At first, I changed nothing except the academy's judgment. Then I changed small things. Then, eventually, I finally changed myself in the replay."

His fingers rubbed the table edge.

"I found those two teammates later. I apologized. Truly apologized. Not because it would fix my record. Not because anyone forced me. Because one day I finally understood that they had trusted me, and I had answered trust with guns."

Lan's chest felt tight.

"Did they forgive you?"

Yin was silent for a while.

"One did. One did not. Both had the right."

Lan absorbed that.

It sounded simple.

It was not.

Yin continued, "I still wanted a stage. I could not return to Shrek. I could not enter a good academy route. So I joined the military from the bottom. Ordinary soldier. No special halo. No Shrek graduate certificate. No inner-court recommendation. Just work."

Lan could imagine that less easily than the star map.

Yin Tianfan, who liked to cheat angles and mock everything, starting from the lowest rung because the door he wanted had closed behind him.

"It took me many years," Yin said. "Crew member. Technical post. Tactical assistant. Small ship officer. More posts than you need to remember. I crawled for nearly twenty years before I finally commanded a small fleet and reached major general rank. People called me the Elusive Fox. They thought it was praise."

"Wasn't it?"

"It was. And it wasn't." Yin smiled faintly. "A fox can slip through many traps. It cannot escape its own tail. My Shrek record followed me. When I reached the point where larger command authority might have been possible, the old stain blocked the road again. No matter how many operations I completed, no matter how many plans worked, promotion stopped."

He looked toward the window again.

"So I came here. Heaven Luo Academy. Vice president. Teacher. Fat old fox."

"Teacher," Lan said, "you are not old."

Yin turned back slowly.

Lan added, "Not compared to Teacher Ji's expression when he sees Qian Lei speak."

For one breath Yin stared.

Then he laughed.

It burst out of him suddenly, round and rough and alive.

"Good! Very good. Spirit Sea made your mouth sharper. Old Ji should fear this."

Lan smiled faintly.

The laughter faded, but it left the air easier to breathe.

Yin's voice softened.

"Xuanyu, this is why I teach you dirty tricks. Not because principles are useless. Because principles without ability can only watch disasters happen. Ability without principles creates disaster. You need both."

Lan nodded.

"Yes, Teacher."

"If you learn space warship command from me, you will learn how to win with ugly methods. I will not apologize for that. The universe is not polite. Pirates, enemies, unknown creatures, disasters — none of them will wait for you to arrange a fair duel."

Yin pointed at him.

"But when you command, your own people are not expendable numbers. Sometimes they will die. Sometimes you may have to order a route that kills someone to save more. War can be that cruel. But if you ever become comfortable with allies dying because it makes your report beautiful, then remember me. Remember the first-place student who was expelled because he won and still failed."

Lan bowed his head deeply this time.

"I will remember."

Yin watched him.

Then, very quietly, he asked, "Do you still want to learn from me?"

Lan looked up at once.

There was no hesitation in his answer.

"Yes."

Yin's eyes flickered.

"Even after hearing this?"

"Especially after hearing this," Lan said. "If you only taught me tactics and not the mistake, I should be more afraid."

Yin did not speak for several breaths.

Then he coughed and looked away.

"Children say frightening things after breakthroughs."

"Teacher?"

"Nothing. Eat more later. Spirit Sea burns brain fat."

Lan glanced at Yin's stomach again.

This time Yin caught it immediately.

"Do not think what you are thinking."

"I did not say anything."

"Your silence is rebellious."

Lan lowered his head to hide his smile.

The old rhythm returned for a few breaths.

Then Yin drew it back to the place he had wanted from the start.

"Xuanyu."

"Yes?"

"You now have a real chance to enter Shrek. Not a dream. Not a childish boast. A real chance. Your team is champion of Heaven Luo. Your Spiritual Power entered Spirit Sea. Your command performance has been seen."

His eyes grew bright.

"When you arrive there, you will have to choose a direction sooner or later. You should enter the Interstellar Command faculty."

Lan did not answer immediately.

Yin continued before silence could become refusal.

"Do you understand what that means? A Soul Master can be strong. A Mecha Master can shake a battlefield. A Battle Armor Master can become a hero. But a fleet commander can decide the fate of countless people across the stars. You have the mind for it. You have the patience. You have the courage to wait beneath danger until the exact moment. If you walk this path, you may go farther than I ever could."

His voice caught slightly.

"You could finish what I lost. Enter the inner court's Space Command route. Stand where I failed to stand. Let your teacher see that the path did not end with me."

Lan's heart stirred.

He saw, for a moment, not the office but an endless black sea filled with stars.

Warships moved through it like silver fish. Their lights were small, but each carried lives. A commander's decision could open a path for all of them or close it forever.

He liked that world.

He had liked it before he understood it.

He liked the simulator even when Yin shot him from behind.

He liked the way a battlefield became lines and timings and choices. He liked the feeling of a plan unfolding because people trusted him enough to move.

But another memory rose with it.

Space pirates.

Helplessness.

The feeling of being too weak inside a great machine when true danger arrived.

And somewhere behind that memory, a golden light he could not explain to others.

Individual strength mattered.

Ji Hongbin's lessons mattered.

Mecha mattered.

Battle armor mattered.

His martial soul and bloodline were strange; if he neglected them, the strangeness would not politely wait for him to have time.

Lan opened his mouth.

Before he could speak, the office door burst open.

"Silly boy!"

Ji Hongbin strode in with a face dark enough to make the room temperature seem to drop.

Lan froze.

Yin Tianfan's expression also froze, but only for a fraction of a breath. Then he put on innocence with a skill that made Qian Lei's earlier attempts look childish.

"Old Ji," Yin said warmly, "why are you back so quickly? Are the children trained already?"

"They are running foundation drills under Mu Zhongtian's supervision," Ji said. "And you are running your mouth under mine."

Yin frowned. "That sentence is ugly."

"Your routine is uglier."

Lan looked from one teacher to the other.

"Routine?"

Ji pointed at Yin. "He told you a true story. Then he arranged every emotional step to lead you to one answer. Regret, lost inner court, dead dream, beloved disciple, stars, Federal fleet. Then he asks you to choose the Space Command faculty while your heart is soft."

Yin slapped the table. "What do you mean routine? I spoke from the heart!"

"Your heart has hooks."

"Hooks are also part of command."

"Exactly."

Lan blinked.

Yin coughed. "Xuanyu, ignore him. Old Ji is narrow. In his eyes, if you do not become an invincible individual hero with perfect foundation and battle armor, you have wasted your life."

Ji's gaze sharpened. "Do not twist my words."

"Then stop interrupting my educational confession."

"Educational? You lured me away so you could sell him the ending before he had time to think. What happened to fair competition between teaching paths?"

"I merely used the battlefield opportunity."

"You just finished telling him not to do that without principles."

"My principle is that my disciple should have a glorious future."

"Your principle is that your disciple should fulfill your regret."

Yin opened his mouth.

Closed it.

Then opened it again. "Those are not mutually exclusive."

Ji laughed without warmth. "At least you admit it."

Lan sat very still.

For some reason, this argument made the story more real, not less.

Yin had told the truth.

Ji did not deny that.

But truth could be arranged like a fleet.

Even grief could be given a formation.

Yin Tianfan saw Lan's expression and scratched his nose.

"What I said was true," he muttered.

Ji said, "True does not mean complete."

Yin's eyes narrowed. "Old Ji."

"Should I tell him how you left the military?"

Yin stood up so quickly his chair nearly hit the wall.

"Life is already difficult. Some old matters do not need to be dug up in front of a child who just broke through Spirit Sea."

Ji crossed his arms.

"Then stop trying to recruit him by making your wound bleed on command."

"Bleed on command? That phrase is cruel even for you."

"Accurate."

Lan almost heard Yan Shuo's voice in the word and had to press his lips together.

Yin stepped around the desk, took Lan by both shoulders, and began steering him toward the door.

"Xuanyu, go back and rest. You still have the next selection. No one knows what Shrek will test. Recover well, train well, and remember your teacher's lesson. Especially the part about Interstellar Command being broad, magnificent, and suited to geniuses with beautiful minds."

Ji followed behind. "Remember the part where you do not decide tonight."

"Also remember that Old Ji becomes jealous when disciples admire the stars."

"Remember that your Soul Power is Rank20 and your body foundation still needs work."

"Remember that warships have cannons."

"Remember that cannon fire does not help if your own body collapses."

"Remember that a fleet commander does not need to punch everything personally."

"Remember that a commander who cannot survive assassination will not command long."

Yin pushed Lan through the doorway.

"Enough. Too many remembers will damage his new Spirit Sea."

Ji stopped in the doorway and looked at Lan.

His expression softened by the smallest amount.

"You did well," he said. "But he is right about one thing. You must have principles. He is wrong about deciding your future tonight. Go think."

Yin leaned out from behind him. "Think about Interstellar Command."

Ji turned.

Yin immediately retreated behind the door.

The door closed.

Lan stood in the corridor alone.

Then, behind the door, Yin's muffled voice said, "Old Ji, if you break my table, the academy will charge you."

Ji's reply was too low to hear clearly.

Something thumped.

Lan decided that resting elsewhere was wise.


The corridor outside the office was mostly empty.

Mostly.

Qian Lei leaned against the wall at the far end, looking as if he had been tragically abandoned by food, fairness, and all teachers. Liu Feng stood beside him with his spear case on his back and sweat darkening the edge of his collar.

When Qian saw Lan, he straightened immediately.

"You survived the old men's secret argument!"

Lan walked over. "You survived training."

"Barely. Teacher Ji left us with Teacher Mu and a set of drills designed by a person who hates knees."

Liu Feng said, "You complained for half of it."

"That was breathing regulation."

"It was not."

Qian ignored him and leaned closer to Lan. "What did Vice President Yin say? Is it true he was expelled? Is it true he commanded a fleet? Is it true Teacher Ji and he were Shrek students? Did he reveal treasure? Forbidden tactics? Secret meals?"

Lan looked at Qian's eager face.

For a moment, he saw the star map again.

A commander could arrange truth.

He could also choose what not to use.

"Teacher Yin told me," Lan said slowly, "that a commander must have a line he cannot cross."

Qian waited.

"And?"

"And he tried to convince me to choose Interstellar Command."

Qian's eyes brightened. "Did he offer benefits?"

"Not exactly."

"Then he is losing his touch."

Liu Feng studied Lan's expression. "Was it serious?"

Lan nodded.

Qian's smile faded a little.

"How serious?"

Lan thought about it.

"Serious enough that if I ever treat either of you as disposable, you should hit me."

Qian blinked.

Liu Feng's gaze sharpened.

"You won't," Liu said.

It was not comfort.

It was judgment.

Lan accepted it.

"I won't," he said.

Qian crossed his arms. "For the record, if you destroy my fleet in a simulation because I complain, I will haunt every meal you eat."

Lan smiled faintly. "Only meals?"

"And every Lucky Wheel draw."

"That is serious."

"Extremely."

Liu Feng's mouth curved by a fraction.

The three of them began walking toward the dormitory.

Lan did not tell them the whole story.

Not yet.

It was Yin Tianfan's wound, and wounds were not snacks to pass around a corridor. But he carried the lesson with him, and he could feel it changing the way he looked at his own two teammates.

Qian Lei walked on his left, still complaining under his breath about knees.

Liu Feng walked on his right, quiet and steady.

Lan had used both of them in battle.

He would use them again.

They would use him too.

That was not wrong.

A team was not made of people who never relied on each other.

A team was made of people who could be relied on without being discarded.

When they reached Dorm333's hallway, several doors opened a crack.

News moved through student buildings faster than official announcements. Lan had woken. Lan had broken Spirit Sea. Dorm333 was champion. Dorm336 was second. The Lucky Wheel was coming. Every fragment made people curious.

Before Qian Lei could perform a champion wave, a familiar calm voice cut across the corridor.

"Close the doors. Rest or train. No crowding."

Yan Shuo stood near the bend of the hallway with Song Yichen and Luo Haoran behind him.

He looked more rested than the night before, but only slightly. His copper-red hair was tied and contained under public restraint, the color muted by the corridor light but still too vivid to look ordinary. His uniform was neat. His posture was class-monitor straight. His eyes, however, moved first to Lan.

Lan felt it with his new Spirit Sea before he processed it with sight: concern pulled tight, relief controlled, warmth hidden behind discipline.

Public corridor.

Public identity.

Public words.

"You are walking normally," Yan said.

Lan nodded. "Mostly."

"Mostly is not a medical category."

Qian immediately said, "It should be. Many of my conditions are mostly."

Song Yichen nodded solemnly. "I support this classification. I am mostly alive."

Luo Haoran adjusted his glasses. "You are loudly alive."

"After what we survived, loudly alive is a virtue."

Yan did not look away from Lan. "Teacher Mu said you tested at five hundred and five."

"Yes."

"Spirit Sea," Yan said.

"Barely across the door," Lan answered. "You are still far ahead."

A few students listening behind doors inhaled softly.

Yan's SP962 had become one of the academy's whispered numbers. Lan's SP505 was a miracle for him, but everyone knew the class monitor's sea was wider.

Yan's expression did not change.

"A door is a door," he said. "After entering, you can walk."

Lan looked at him.

The sentence was simple.

It felt like more.

Qian broke the moment because he existed.

"Speaking of walking, we are walking toward reward glory soon, right? Teacher Mu said Lucky Wheel. I can feel destiny preparing to apologize for my suffering."

Song's eyes brightened. "You get to draw first?"

"Champion privilege," Qian said with fake modesty. "We accept this burden on behalf of history."

Luo said, "Dorm336 is scheduled after administrative review."

Qian paused. "Administrative review? That sounds like private reward inflation."

Song straightened. "We are second place. We deserve inflated things."

Yan said, "We deserve clean medical recovery and a reward draw that does not set Song on fire."

"I was only set on fire emotionally."

"That is also dangerous."

Lan turned to Yan. "Your draw is later?"

"Separate timing," Yan said. "Shrek wants the records clean. Second place with our score triggered more review than the ordinary top-three route."

There was no pride in his voice.

There was no false modesty either.

Lan nodded. "It should. Your route was real."

Song grinned.

Luo's expression softened.

Yan's eyes held Lan's for a breath. "So was yours."

Neither asked the details that could not be asked in a corridor.

Neither gave an answer that could not be given.

The doors nearby stayed mostly closed now, but attention pressed behind them like heat behind paper.

Yan stepped aside.

"Go draw well."

Qian clasped his hands. "Class monitor, please bless us with second-place pressure."

Yan looked at him. "Do not touch the wheel before Xuanyu decides the order."

Qian staggered back. "Why does everyone assume I will sabotage destiny?"

Liu Feng said, "Experience."

Song said, "Instinct."

Luo said, "Probability."

Lan said, "All of the above."

Qian clutched his chest. "Betrayal. From all directions."

Yan's lips moved by a fraction.

Then he looked at Lan again.

"Don't let a reward scatter your team," he said.

Lan paused.

Yin Tianfan's voice seemed to echo under the corridor lights.

A commander must have a line.

Lan answered quietly, "It won't."

Yan nodded once.

That was all.

But as Lan walked past with Qian and Liu, he felt the answer settle more firmly inside himself.


Mu Zhongtian arrived before Qian Lei could begin a formal speech to the dormitory about champion dignity.

This was fortunate for everyone.

"Simulation cabin room," Mu said. "Now."

Qian's face lit up. "The Lucky Wheel?"

"Yes."

"Teacher Mu, before we go, as champion representatives, should we prepare remarks?"

"No."

"A pose?"

"No."

"A unified team expression?"

"If it is silent."

Qian considered that. "Difficult, but not impossible."

Liu Feng looked relieved when Mu began walking.

Lan followed, feeling the change in his own body with every step. Spirit Sea had not made him stronger in the obvious way Yan's Fire Phoenix awakening had changed a room. There were no purple rings around him, no wings, no Ultimate Fire pressure that made inferior flames bow.

But his perception had become finer.

The hallway seemed full of small currents: students' breathing behind doors, the residual heat from the lamps, Liu Feng's spear-like soul power, Qian Lei's restless excitement, Mu Zhongtian's tired pride.

And behind them, farther down the corridor, Yan Shuo's restrained warmth turned back toward Dorm336's room.

Lan did not look back.

He did not need to.

Some lines could be felt without being announced.

The simulation cabin room waited under bright lights.

For most students, returning to the cabins so soon after the qualifier would have brought back dread. Lan felt a flicker of it when he saw the smooth shells arranged in rows. The last time he had entered, he had come out with heat in his bones, a score that made teachers stare, and a lesson he was only beginning to understand.

Qian Lei, naturally, saw only reward.

"Beloved cabins," he said, patting one. "Today, no Dragons, no magma, no betrayal, only prizes."

Mu Zhongtian's expression twitched at the word betrayal.

Lan wondered if Mu had heard enough of Yin's story to understand why.

Liu Feng lay down in his cabin without comment.

Lan did the same.

The lid lowered.

Darkness came.

Then Soul World opened.

The familiar training ground formed around them. The air was clear, the ground broad and clean, and the sky above had the unreal brightness unique to a constructed world. It looked safe.

Lan trusted that about as much as he trusted Qian Lei near an unguarded reward box.

Ling Yiyi was already waiting.

She wore the same gentle smile that had once made Qian Lei's soul nearly leave his body with hope.

This time, when Qian saw her, his reaction was complicated.

He straightened first.

Then he remembered the early-admission offer he had refused.

Then he remembered she might control rewards.

His face passed through admiration, grievance, caution, greed, and diplomacy in quick succession.

"Sister Ling," he said at last, with a smile carefully assembled from all five. "We meet again."

Ling Yiyi's eyes curved. "Qian Lei, why do you look as if I owe you money?"

Qian's smile trembled. "Not money. Destiny."

"Destiny is not reimbursed through Shrek Academy's finance office."

"Can it be reimbursed through the Lucky Wheel?"

"That depends on your luck."

Qian immediately looked at Lan. "Captain, enhance my luck."

Lan said, "I do not have that soul skill."

"You enhanced my summons. Luck is only a more abstract summon."

Liu Feng said, "That sentence is illegal."

Ling Yiyi laughed softly.

Mu Zhongtian entered the training ground projection beside them. His expression became more formal when he saw Ling Yiyi.

Ling Yiyi looked at Dorm333 and inclined her head slightly.

"Congratulations," she said. "Lan Xuanyu, Qian Lei, Liu Feng — your team obtained first place in Heaven Luo Planet's qualifier with an extremely outstanding score. Twenty thousand three hundred forty-one."

Qian's chest rose.

Ling Yiyi continued, "According to the consolidated data I have seen, your score is also first across the entire federation's qualifier stage."

Mu Zhongtian's breathing stopped for a moment.

"First across the entire federation?"

Even though the score had already shocked him, the words still landed differently. Heaven Luo Academy had produced strong students before. It had produced promising candidates. But first across all qualifier zones was a weight that reached beyond school pride.

Ling Yiyi nodded. "Usually, Mother Planet students occupy the most eye-catching positions. This time, Heaven Luo Planet has given Shrek a very interesting answer."

Qian opened his mouth.

No sound came out.

Liu Feng looked at Lan.

Lan's own heart lifted, then steadied.

First across the federation.

It was too large a phrase to hold immediately.

Ling Yiyi's smile deepened. "Your academy's Dorm336 also created an abnormal result with nine thousand eight hundred forty-six. Their route is being reviewed separately because the Fire Phoenix suppression record and team survival pattern are not ordinary. But even with that added anomaly, your twenty thousand score remains far ahead. You three have brought Heaven Luo Planet a very rare honor."

Mu Zhongtian's eyes reddened slightly before he controlled himself.

He looked away, coughed, and tried to sound stern. "Remember this honor during training. Do not become arrogant."

Qian finally recovered speech.

"Teacher Mu," he whispered, "if first across the federation cannot make us arrogant for even one minute, what is the purpose of youth?"

"Survival."

"A narrow answer."

Ling Yiyi looked amused, but her eyes lingered on Lan.

Lan Xuanyu stood quietly in front of her, face bright, eyes clear, hair still carrying the softness of a boy not yet eleven. He looked gentle now. Almost harmless. Even after entering Spirit Sea, his aura was not heavy in the way Yan Shuo's public Fire Phoenix pressure could be heavy. Lan's strangeness hid deeper.

Ling Yiyi had reviewed the assessment recording.

The boy in front of her was the same boy who had remained calm under the body of a Ground Fire Scarlet Dragon, measured an impossible timing window, used Qian Lei's terrifying Emerald Demon Bird summon without losing his nerve, and turned a death trap into a score that made the ranking system look absurd.

Gentle face.

Cruel calculation when needed.

Not cruelty toward people.

Cruelty toward problems.

That distinction was important.

It was also worth watching.

What Lan Xuanyu and his teammates did not know was that their score alone would normally have been enough to make many people argue for direct admission. First across the federation at their age and background was not a small matter. But Shrek's higher levels had not allowed the matter to become simple. Some wanted to see whether the team could repeat judgment without the same terrain and same one-time summon opportunity. Some wanted to watch Qian Lei's choice after refusing temptation. Some wanted to watch Lan Xuanyu's foundation catch up with his command. And now, because Heaven Luo had also produced Dorm336's Fire Phoenix route, the entire batch had become more interesting than a single score sheet.

So the reward remained a reward.

The road remained a road.

Ling Yiyi smiled as if none of that weight existed.

"Are you ready to draw your prize?"

Qian Lei took half a step forward.

Liu Feng caught the back of his collar.

Qian stopped.

"I was only expressing readiness physically."

Lan looked at Ling Yiyi with wide, sincere eyes.

"Sister Ling."

Ling Yiyi's smile paused.

Mu Zhongtian's eyelid twitched.

He had seen that tone before.

Qian Lei immediately recognized hope and stood straighter.

Lan asked, "Since our score is first across the entire federation, are there any additional rewards?"

Ling Yiyi's smile became a little wary. "The Lucky Wheel draw is already the reward for first place."

"I mean beyond Heaven Luo first place," Lan said earnestly. "Federation first should be different, right? Maybe another spin? Or a small supplementary draw? Or a badge that can later become a draw?"

Qian's eyes shone brighter with every word.

Liu Feng silently shifted his grip from Qian's collar to his sleeve.

Ling Yiyi looked at Lan.

The boy's face was beautiful in a clean, harmless way. His eyes were large and bright, and he had just entered Spirit Sea after deep meditation. If someone did not know him, they might think he was simply excited and hopeful.

Ling Yiyi knew better.

This child was negotiating.

Worse, he was negotiating while looking cute.

She suddenly understood why some examiners called him troublesome after reviewing the recording.

"No," she said.

Lan blinked. "No?"

"No. For fairness, every champion team receives the arranged reward. We treat the rules equally."

Lan thought for a moment.

"But receiving a champion reward is already unequal compared to teams that did not rank first."

Ling Yiyi's expression stiffened.

Qian Lei whispered, "Good angle."

Lan continued, "If inequality based on result is allowed once, then an additional difference for federation first would still be result-based fairness."

Mu Zhongtian covered his mouth with one hand.

It might have been a cough.

It might not.

Ling Yiyi looked at Lan with narrowed eyes.

"Then would you like to give up the champion draw to make everything perfectly equal?"

Lan immediately shook his head.

"That would be unfair to us. If another planet's champion draws, we should draw too."

"Then one draw."

"What if the wheel itself feels moved by our score and voluntarily gives another?"

"It will not."

"Can wheels not be moved?"

"This one cannot."

Qian raised a hand. "May I attempt emotional communication with the wheel?"

"No."

"Sister Ling, you answered before seeing my sincerity."

"I saw enough."

Liu Feng murmured, "Probably wise."

Lan sighed softly, as if accepting a tragic but reasonable outcome.

"Then one draw."

Ling Yiyi stared at him for another breath.

The gentle boy had retreated.

The captain who knew when to stop had returned.

Two-faced, she thought.

No.

Not exactly.

Layered.

That was more accurate and more troublesome.

"Prepare," Ling Yiyi said.

She lifted her right hand and pressed down lightly in the air.

Pink light spread from beneath her palm.

It did not unfold like an ordinary door. It bloomed, petal by petal, each piece of light curving outward until a doorway stood in the middle of the training ground. The edges shimmered with soft radiance, pretty enough that Qian Lei immediately leaned forward and suspicious enough that Liu Feng immediately leaned back.

Beyond the doorway, Lan could not see the Lucky Wheel yet.

Only light.

Reward light.

Shrek light.

A path that looked gentle and probably was not.

Ling Yiyi gestured toward it.

"Enter. Draw your prize."

Qian swallowed.

"Captain," he whispered, "remember: if luck is a formation, place me in the safest position."

Liu Feng said, "The safest position is behind Xuanyu and away from touching things."

"That is oppression."

Lan looked at the pink doorway.

Yin Tianfan's story still rested in him. Ji Hongbin's warning rested beside it. Yan Shuo's corridor words rested somewhere quieter: Don't let a reward scatter your team.

A line.

A team.

A road to Shrek that did not become simpler just because a door opened.

Lan stepped forward.

"Together," he said.

Qian Lei and Liu Feng moved with him.

The pink light swallowed the three of them.
