# Chapter 36: The Year Between Wings and Stars

The first week after Yan Shuo returned, Heaven Luo Academy learned that wings were not a reward.

They were homework.

Teacher Ji Hongbin wrote the first training schedule himself. He did not decorate it with encouraging words. He did not praise the miracle. He did not call the full Fire Phoenix awakening beautiful, although every adult with eyes had understood that word before they understood the numbers.

He wrote:

Lift.

Balance.

Landing.

Heat containment.

No full integration without approval.

Then he underlined the last line twice.

Yan Shuo read the sheet, looked up, and asked, "Teacher Ji, is that because it is dangerous?"

"No," Ji Hongbin said.

Yan blinked.

"It is because it is unnecessary."

That answer did more to steady him than a softer one would have.

For three days after the sealed field test, the adults forbade any proper sparring. Gao Shuren from the Spirit Pagoda came every morning with equipment boxes, heat plates, pressure markers, and the expression of a man who had slept less than his machines. Mu Zhongtian managed the school side, Yan Ke managed paperwork with the hostility of a parent who had discovered forms could reproduce, and Lin Mei managed the thing no form could measure.

She stood behind Yan every morning and combed copper-red hair that no longer wished to obey the old mask.

Under ordinary light, with the aura folded tightly and the school cut kept practical, the red could be muted. A deeper copper. A restrained phoenix warmth. Still explainable if no one looked too long.

But when soul power moved, red-gold answered inside each strand.

Not a color painted on top.

A flame remembering where it lived.

Lin Mei tied the hair back with a plain black band, then added a thin inner clasp etched with heat-dulling patterns.

"Does it hide it?" Yan asked.

"No," Lin Mei said.

Yan's shoulders lowered.

"It makes people argue with themselves before saying anything stupid. That is enough for now."

Yan touched the clasp. "That sounds like Father's paperwork strategy."

"Your father wishes his paperwork were this elegant."

From the doorway, Yan Ke said, "I heard slander. I am choosing family harmony over legal action."

Yan laughed.

The wings answered under the skin.

Not fully. Not visibly. Only a pressure between shoulder blades, a red-gold warmth along the back, a faint violet shadow under the right breath.

Lin Mei's hand landed on Yan's shoulder immediately.

"Again," she said.

Yan inhaled. Held. Let the heat fold inward instead of outward.

The pressure calmed.

This was the new life.

Not hiding power until it became smaller.

Teaching power to wait without becoming ashamed.

On the fourth day, Ji Hongbin put Yan Shuo in the field alone.

Song Yichen stood at one boundary with his Stoneplate Tortoise shield ready. Luo Haoran stood on the other, eyes narrowed as if the air had lines only he could see. Lan Xuanyu was not in the testing circle, but he stood beside Liu Feng and Qian Lei with the blue cord wrapped once around his wrist.

Yan noticed it before noticing anything else.

That was not good field awareness.

It was very good heart awareness, which Teacher Ji probably did not count on any official record.

"First rule," Ji said, snapping Yan back to attention. "Base body. No soul rings. Cross the field."

Yan waited.

Ji's brows lowered. "Did the cocoon burn your ears?"

"No, Teacher. I thought you meant with suppression weights."

"I mean walking."

Qian Lei whispered, "Teacher Ji has finally discovered cruelty at its purest form."

Liu Feng said, "Walking is hard when the ground is embarrassed by you."

Teacher Mu did not even look at them. "Both of you, ten laps after testing."

"I was praising him!" Qian Lei protested.

"Twelve."

Silence returned.

Yan walked.

That was all.

No ring. No mantle. No visible wing. No full integration.

The field still reacted.

The pressure markers under Yan's feet brightened in a sequence no ordinary Rank34 Soul Elder would have produced. The body moved too cleanly. The shoulders no longer carried the slight protective tension from before the cocoon. The back distributed force through a hidden wing-root frame even when the wings did not appear. Each step landed with a warmth that spread, returned, and vanished.

Gao Shuren muttered, "Base body output exceeds ordinary three-ring prediction by forty-seven percent before martial soul release."

Ji Hongbin glanced at him.

Gao corrected himself. "Conservatively."

Yan reached the far side and turned.

Ji said, "Again. Faster. Still no rings."

This time Yan ran.

Not Phoenix Step. Not a named technique. Not yet.

Just running with a body that had spent a month learning that balance could include the sky.

The first ten meters were too smooth. The eleventh was too high. Yan's foot left the ground longer than expected, and the body tried to become flight before the mind asked permission.

Song's shield lifted half an inch.

Luo's fingers twitched.

Yan corrected before either had to move.

The landing was rough, but it held.

Ji Hongbin wrote one word on his tablet.

"Body-memory," Gao read over his shoulder. "Positive?"

"Too positive," Ji said. "His body learns faster than his habits. That is dangerous."

Lan's gaze sharpened.

Yan looked away before the worry in it became a conversation.

The first lesson of the new year was simple: base Yan Shuo was already not normal.

Teacher Ji made sure everyone understood it.

On the fifth day, Lu Qianxun asked for a match.

He did it properly. He did not sneer. He did not call it a challenge in front of the whole class. He walked to Mu Zhongtian after afternoon training and said, with the pride of someone who had been forced by talent to learn manners, "Teacher Mu, I request a controlled direct-combat comparison with Monitor Yan."

Mu Zhongtian looked tired immediately.

"Why?"

Lu Qianxun's jaw moved once. "Because if I do not see the gap with my own eyes, I will imagine the wrong one."

That answer was honest enough that Mu did not refuse.

The match lasted seventeen seconds.

No full three-spirit Fire Phoenix integration was allowed.

No Purple Flame Eidolon Wing was allowed.

No high-output Ultimate Fire was allowed.

Yan Shuo used only normal martial soul release, Dawnfeather Phoenix Mantle at low intensity, and Skybone Phoenix Frame for movement.

Lu Qianxun released the Golden Lion with all three rings shining. The lion pressure rolled forward with the old dominance that had once made half the class forget to breathe.

Yan stepped into it.

The Fire Phoenix did not roar.

It simply unfolded enough for the field to remember another kind of king.

Red-gold mantle feathers breathed over Yan's shoulders. The copper-red hair under the academy clasp brightened once, and every heat marker near the center line turned warning orange. Yan did not ignite high-output flame. He did not need to.

Phoenix Step began as a correction rather than a technique name.

Foot.

Breath.

Frame.

Heat.

Yan crossed the lion pressure at an angle Lu had not expected. Skybone Phoenix Frame took the recoil. Dawnfeather Phoenix Mantle wrapped the shoulder. One palm stopped a fist. One wing-shadow that was not the third skill but only the body remembering wings shifted Yan half a step outside the lion's best line.

Lu Qianxun's third ring brightened.

Ji Hongbin said, "Stop."

Lu froze.

Yan's hand rested two fingers from Lu's throat, not burning, not pressing, only present.

The silence after the match was heavier than shouting.

Lu withdrew first and bowed his head once.

"Understood."

Yan lowered his hand. "You were faster than last term."

"Not enough."

"No," Yan said, because respect did not require lying. "But faster."

Lu looked at him for a long moment, then nodded.

From the side, Luo whispered, "Seventeen seconds. I feel cheated. I wanted at least thirty."

Song answered, "You wanted entertainment. That was measurement."

"Measurement should consider audience morale."

Lan Xuanyu did not joke.

He watched Yan with a look that held pride, worry, and something too young to be named safely.

Later, when the field emptied, he found Yan beside the water dispenser.

"You did not use everything," Lan said.

Yan took the cup slowly. "Teacher Ji would have buried me under training weights."

"That is the only reason?"

Yan looked at the blue cord on Lan's wrist.

"No."

The word was the same answer as the one given on the field after the cocoon, but it carried a different shape now.

Lan waited.

Yan said, "If I use everything when I do not need it, then I am saying the base of what I became is not enough. It is."

Lan's eyes brightened.

Not with envy.

With understanding.

"Teacher Ji told you that?"

"Teacher Ji told me not to be stupid in eleven crueler words."

Lan smiled.

Yan wanted to ask about the gold and silver pressure Lan had kept hidden for years. Wanted to ask why his own rank stayed low when his body felt like a sealed sea. Wanted to ask whether Lan ever got tired of standing between being underestimated and being protected from answers.

But they had rules.

Say what you can.

Say when you cannot.

Yan drank the water instead.

"Your training is worse," Yan said.

Lan looked down. "You noticed?"

"Everyone notices. They just think it is normal because you keep walking."

For a moment, Lan looked almost angry.

Not at Yan.

At the truth.

"I have something I must wait for," he said.

Yan nodded. "Then wait properly. Do not carve yourself hollow to prove you are patient."

Lan's fingers tightened around the cord.

"You sound like Teacher Ji."

"Take that back."

This time Lan did laugh.

Yan's fire warmed and stayed steady.

That became the shape of the next year.

Not one miracle after another.

Work.

For Yan, the academy built a training field inside a training field. The outer array measured flight path, heat spread, pressure leakage, and wing-root recoil. The inner line was marked with Song's shield positions and Luo's call points.

Teacher Ji named nothing until a movement survived three failed attempts.

Dawn-Iron Landing earned its name after Yan fell badly enough to scorch a circle into the floor and still rose with both knees intact.

"Again," Ji said.

Yan, face red from embarrassment and heat, said, "Teacher Ji, I think the floor lost."

"The floor does not need to pass the exam."

Song lifted his shield. "Landing line ready."

Luo pointed two fingers left. "Wind lane wrong. Your right wing is lying to you."

Yan glared at him.

"It is," Luo said. "Beautifully. But lying."

The next fall became the first proper Dawn-Iron Landing.

Phoenix Step came from ground drills. At first Yan jumped too far. Then not far enough. Then the heat arrived before the foot, which Ji called "announcing your funeral route to the enemy." Eventually the step became what it needed to be: not flight, not teleportation, only a ground-burst that used Skybone Frame to return recoil and Dawnflame's mantle to keep the shoulder line from tearing.

Wingbeat Burst took longer.

Full wings made every old formation wrong.

Song's shield angles were built for teammates on the ground. Yan kept leaving the roof of Song's protection. Luo's scouting lines were built for movement across the field. Yan kept turning the field into three dimensions and then looking offended when Luo called him impossible.

"You fly like a thrown knife," Luo complained one afternoon.

"That sounds useful."

"Thrown knives do not return to formation."

Song planted his shield into the ground. "Again from launch. Return to the turtle point before attacking."

Yan looked at him.

"Turtle point?"

Song's face did not change. "If Luo is allowed to name wind lanes, I am allowed to name shield points."

Luo put a hand over his heart. "I have never been prouder."

Three-Feather Guard was born because none of them liked the first name, Turtle-Mantis-Phoenix Emergency Survival Net, and Teacher Mu refused to write that on a training report.

Purple Flame Eidolon Wing was tested only three times in the first two months.

The first test made four target markers lock onto an empty place where Yan's wing had been half a breath before.

The second test split a summoned practice construct along its soul-power cohesion line without destroying the core.

The third test left Yan sitting on the floor with both hands braced behind him, sweat rolling down his face, unable to stand for twenty breaths.

Ji Hongbin crouched in front of him.

"Conclusion?"

Yan swallowed. "Do not use it for ordinary fights."

"Second conclusion."

"If I have to use it, use it to end the exchange."

"Third conclusion."

Yan looked at the target marker that had turned toward the wrong place and stayed there.

"It is not a stronger flame. It is a wrong memory."

Ji's expression did not soften.

His voice did.

"Good."

Full three-spirit integration happened only once that season.

Adult shields. Spirit Pagoda observers. Ji Hongbin at the field edge with killing intent directed at anyone who suggested increasing output. Song and Luo outside the line but inside the formation map, because Yan had insisted that if the form could not remember the team, then it was not his strongest state, only his loudest one.

Dawnflame Phoenix Kite appeared above the left shoulder, red-gold and alive.

Dawn-Iron settled into spine, wing roots, and feet until the field plates sank.

Purple Flame slid along the right wing like twilight under fire.

For five seconds, Yan Shuo became the center of three living foundations.

The heat array screamed.

Then Yan shut it down.

Not because it failed.

Because it worked.

That was scarier.

Afterward, Teacher Ji wrote another line on the schedule.

Full integration: decisive use only.

Yan copied it into his own notebook.

Then under it, smaller, he wrote:

Base must be worthy.

A year passed by being counted in failures that became techniques.

Lan Xuanyu's failures looked different.

His problem was not that he could not grow.

It was that he could.

Too close to Rank20, too often, with danger waiting behind the threshold.

He trained under Ji Hongbin until his body shook. He trained under Yin Tianfan until the simulation pod taught his hands to move before fear could speak. He ate rare ingredients that disappeared into his body as if feeding a bottomless sea. His physical quality rose until ordinary class comparisons became meaningless. His Spiritual Power crossed five hundred a year before the end of the three-year span and entered Spirit Sea Realm. By the final term, school records placed it over eight hundred — SP800+ on every serious report.

Qian Lei, who took pride in Spiritual Power whenever possible, finally broke past six hundred and complained that Lan had ruined the joy of numbers.

"Six hundred should feel heroic," Qian Lei said. "Instead, I say it and everyone looks at Xuanyu."

Liu Feng answered, "Then say it when he is not in the room."

"I tried. I still remembered him."

Liu Feng had become thinner and sharper, his speed no longer the speed of a desperate spear but of someone learning where the opening would be. Qian Lei had become rounder, louder, and much harder to dismiss. Their Dorm333 remained strange, lucky, unlucky, and impossible to grade by normal rules.

The Elite Junior Class shrank to twenty-one students.

Eliminations stopped after the numbers became too small and the remaining students too valuable. The old fear of being removed changed into a different fear: that staying meant the academy expected them to become useful in situations ordinary students would never touch.

Of the students measured against Lan's stubborn Rank19, the others had passed the second ring threshold long ago. Lu Qianxun reached three rings and wore the achievement like armor that no longer fit as comfortably as it once had.

He was still proud.

He was still strong.

He was no longer the ceiling.

The academy's reports grew more complicated because Yan Shuo existed.

Lan Xuanyu remained the most terrifying comprehensive problem: Rank19 on paper, Spirit Sea above eight hundred, body quality absurd, command talent rising, self-created martial-soul fusion dangerous enough that teachers wrote around it rather than through it.

Yan Shuo became the other impossible column: Rank39 before twelve, SP962, full awakened Fire Phoenix, low-output Ultimate Fire stable, true flight usable in training, three purple living foundations, and a note from Ji Hongbin that said, in handwriting sharp enough to cut paper:

Do not evaluate as ordinary Soul Elder.

Gao Shuren added his own sealed appendix after the yearly Spirit Pagoda retest.

Dawnflame Phoenix Kite: three thousand one hundred years.

Dawn-Iron Phoenix Roc: three thousand nine hundred fifty years.

Purple Flame Eidolon Bird: six thousand four hundred years.

Growth without external shortcut. Compatibility-driven maturation under full Fire Phoenix foundation.

Yan Ke read the bill, then the report, then the bill again.

"I liked it better when the numbers were smaller."

Lin Mei said, "No, you didn't."

"Emotionally, no. Financially, yes."

Yan stood in front of the mirror that night while Lin Mei adjusted the heat-dulling clasp again. The person looking back still wore Yan Shuo's school uniform. Still held the shoulders in the public way. Still knew which expression made classmates decide a question was too awkward to ask.

But the hair was copper-red even under restraint.

The eyes caught dawn-gold when the room light moved.

The face was too refined now, too clear around the edges, too much like a flame that had learned manners.

"Mother," Yan said quietly, "how long can this hold?"

Lin Mei did not lie.

"Long enough if we keep changing how it holds."

"That is not an answer."

"It is the only honest one."

Yan nodded.

In the glass, the private truth stood behind the public name, alive and waiting.

The academy's final pre-vacation day arrived with heat in the training field and stars in Lan Xuanyu's eyes.

He had asked Ji Hongbin for leave before and been refused with a scolding severe enough that even Qian Lei stopped mocking him for half a day. This time, after three years of almost no real rest, Lan asked again.

Yan saw him before the request.

The two of them met outside the side hall where the afternoon light made the corridor windows shine.

Lan had grown taller, nearly one and a half meters now, with the softer roundness of childhood replaced by a cleaner elegance. Sweat still darkened his collar from training. The blue cord was on his wrist, worn enough now that the ugly knot had become familiar.

Yan leaned against the wall, arms folded.

"You are going to ask today."

Lan stopped. "How do you know?"

"You walked like someone entering a battlefield and hoping the enemy is reasonable."

"Teacher Ji is not the enemy."

"That is what makes him more dangerous."

Lan's mouth curved, then faded.

"I need to go to Heaven Dou Planet."

Yan knew the safe parts.

Teacher Nana.

Rank20.

Danger if handled without her.

Not why. Not how. Not the truth under the gold and silver pressure. Those belonged behind Lan's own sealed doors.

"Then ask," Yan said.

Lan looked at him. "If he refuses?"

Yan's fire warmed under the uniform, a small controlled thread.

"Then ask again. Properly. Not like someone begging for rest. Like someone protecting the next step."

Lan stared for a breath.

"You sound different."

"The wings made me wise."

"No, they didn't."

"They made me taller in arguments."

That one made Lan laugh, and the corridor became easier to breathe in.

Then Lan said, quieter, "When I come back, I should have my second ring."

Yan looked at the cord on his wrist.

"Then come back."

Lan's fingers touched the knot.

"You said that like it was simple."

"No. I said it like it mattered."

Neither of them reached for the other.

They were in a public corridor. They were still young. They had too many secrets between them and not enough safe words.

But Lan nodded as if Yan had put a hand on his shoulder anyway.

Ji Hongbin agreed to twenty days.

Not a month.

Twenty days.

Lan nearly cried from relief and was offended at himself for almost doing so.

After Lan left, Ji Hongbin walked out of the training ground with the expression of a man who had given permission and then immediately begun suffering from it. He muttered under his breath about wasted potential, Rank19, and whether three years of work could be undone by a bottleneck that should not exist.

Then he looked up sharply.

The roof was empty.

Or seemed empty.

After he turned away, a figure dressed in black stood silently where his gaze had passed. The man's eyes were clear and deep, watching Lan Xuanyu's tired back until it disappeared toward the dormitory.

He seemed to want to remember something.

Nothing came.

Elsewhere in the academy, Ji Hongbin went straight to Yin Tianfan's office and did not knock.

Yin Tianfan had his feet on the desk and a glass of Heaven Luo hard liquor in his hand, which meant he was either relaxed or pretending relaxation was a military tactic.

"Manners," Yin said. "Have you heard of them?"

"Have you heard of shame?" Ji replied.

"Rarely. It sounds unpleasant."

The argument began exactly where old arguments between them always began: with insults that had grooves worn into them by use.

Then Ji said Lan's name, and the room changed.

"He is one month from twelve," Ji snapped. "The old minimum for that place was Rank20 before twelve. Now anyone serious knows Rank30 is the real competitive line. He has Spirit Sea, twin martial souls, that self-created fusion, absurd control, and he is still Rank19. Rank19."

Yin took a slow drink.

"For Interplanet Command, Spiritual Power matters. Two hundred below twelve is the requirement. Three hundred is already good. How much does our precious disciple have? Over eight hundred. In that system, he is not weak. He is a monster wearing a Soul Power problem."

"I am trying to make him a Soul Master."

"And I am trying to make him a commander who can decide battles before Soul Masters reach the battlefield."

"You cannot command your way through every threshold."

"And you cannot punch your way through every star."

They glared at each other.

Then Yin said, more quietly, "You gave him leave."

Ji's anger deflated into worry.

"Twenty days. If he cannot break through properly after this, then maybe your road is the one he must take."

Yin Tianfan stopped smiling.

For all their quarrels, neither of them liked that sentence.

Lan told Qian Lei and Liu Feng before leaving.

Qian Lei was delighted to learn that vacation still existed as a concept.

"I'm going home to eat," he announced. "Then sleep. Then eat again to recover from sleeping."

Liu Feng looked at him. "There is a cross-academy trial next semester."

"I will spiritually support it from a dining table."

"You are Rank25."

"A majestic Rank25."

"I am Rank26 and still planning to push toward Rank27."

Qian Lei patted his stomach. "Different martial souls require different nutrition philosophies."

Lan smiled despite himself.

Liu Feng turned to him. "When you return, you should have your second ring. What will your second soul skill be? What Spirit Soul will you use?"

Lan froze for half a breath.

Yan, standing a few steps away with Song and Luo, saw it.

So did Liu Feng.

So did Qian Lei, though Qian chose kindness by pretending his curiosity had tripped over hunger.

Lan's first soul ring had appeared on its own. He had never fused a Spirit Soul. Lan Xiao and Nan Cheng had told him repeatedly not to speak of it.

"I don't know yet," Lan said. "You'll see when I come back."

Liu Feng nodded.

Qian Lei leaned close. "If it summons food, tell me first."

"It will not summon food."

"You say that without hope."

Yan waited until Dorm333 moved on before speaking.

"Say what you can," Yan murmured.

Lan glanced back.

"I did."

"Good."

Luo watched them from the side and whispered to Song, "They have entire conversations made of half sentences."

Song said, "Efficient."

"Terrifying."

The twenty-day vacation began the next morning.

Lan Xuanyu went home.

He had only returned a few times each term in the past three years, and home had changed with his father's promotion. The new place was larger, bright and practical, with a training room, study, guest room, and enough space for Nan Cheng to miss her son in more rooms than before.

She came out of the kitchen the moment he called for her.

By the time Lan Xiao returned, the family warmth had already filled the house. Tickets were booked quickly despite the cost. Heaven Dou Planet. Heaven Dou City. Teacher Nana's address, obtained through Qian Lei's Gate of Summoning and Dong Qianqiu's communication route, sat safely in Lan Xuanyu's terminal.

He was so close to Rank20 that meditation felt like standing before a door with both hands tied.

Tomorrow, they would leave.

That same night, far from the Lan family's dinner table, Young Noble Le finished the final concert of his tour.

Three years had turned him from a rising singer into a name almost no one on Heaven Luo Planet failed to recognize. He still had only five songs. Fans did not care. Each live performance changed subtly, carrying emotions no imitation could reproduce.

Le Qingling hugged him after the performance with joy bright enough to cover worry.

"Vacation starts tomorrow," she said. "Dragon King Planet? The beaches?"

Tang Le looked at the stage lights as if listening to something beneath them.

"No," he said. "I want to take an interstellar ship to Heaven Dou Planet."

Le Qingling stared. "Heaven Dou Planet? Why?"

Tang Le did not know.

That was the problem.

"A whim," he said.

Le Qingling immediately decided that whims were dangerous if allowed to travel alone.

"Then I am going too."

Tang Le nodded.

The next morning, the space center swallowed families, luggage, staff announcements, security gates, and childhood dreams.

Lan Xuanyu nearly forgot to look like someone on vacation.

On the giant screens, warships and spacecraft rotated in clean holographic glory. He named them for Nan Cheng before she asked.

Star Luo Mark I Mothership. Three thousand two hundred meters. A city in space. The strongest core of a Federation fleet, capable of destroying a planet if its main firepower focused fully.

Dragon King rank guardian vessels. More than one thousand meters long. At most four guarding a star-rated mothership, forming the core of an interstellar fleet.

Lan Xiao listened with the pride of a father watching his son's eyes point at the future.

"It seems my son really will become a spaceship commander."

Lan Xuanyu straightened. "I definitely will."

Nan Cheng asked why he liked spaceships so much.

Lan answered without hesitation.

Because spaceships were strong.

Because the Federation's fleets were the greatest weapons civilization had built.

Because no individual Soul Master, no matter how powerful, could stand against the main cannon of a real interstellar warship.

Because if there was a strongest power, he wanted to understand it from the command seat.

Lan Xiao hugged Nan Cheng's shoulder and smiled.

He did not know that one day, that answer would be tested much sooner than any parent would wish.

Lan sent one message before boarding.

Twenty days. I will come back.

Yan Shuo received it in the academy training field between a landing drill and Luo yelling that the east lane was not decoration.

He typed back:

Then come back with both eyebrows.

Lan answered:

Why eyebrows?

Yan replied:

Teacher Ji values measurable results.

Lan sent a laughing face.

Yan looked at it for longer than necessary, then closed the terminal before Song could pretend not to notice.

The Lan family boarded economy class.

Tang Le, Le Qingling, and the bodyguards boarded last, masked and hooded, rising by lift to the extravagant first-class cabin with its bedrooms, study, theatre, bath, and small pool. The distance between economy and first-class was almost absurd.

The distance between people meant to meet was sometimes less obedient.

The spacecraft took off smoothly.

Lan Xuanyu felt the pressure of launch and found it almost gentle compared to Yin Tianfan's simulation training. He watched the structure, the response systems, the likely location of power lines and control routes. The civilian craft was not a warship, but he could see the shadow of transport-class military design inside it.

Fourteen days of the twenty-day vacation would be spent traveling there and back.

He did not mind.

He was at Rank19 peak.

Every breath said Rank20 was waiting.

Teacher Nana was waiting beyond it.

On the first day, excitement kept him awake.

On the second, he studied the cabin layout until Nan Cheng ordered him to rest.

On the third, he meditated.

A masked figure came quietly to the economy class entrance and looked at him.

No passenger noticed.

No alarm sounded.

Tang Le's gaze rested on the boy with his eyes closed, and warmth surfaced in it without permission. Confusion followed. Thought followed confusion and found no memory waiting.

He stood there for a while.

Then he left as quietly as he had come.

Lan Xuanyu woke later with a bloated stomach and the very ordinary need to use the toilet.

Ordinary things were cruel like that.

They chose the edge of history with no respect at all.

He unbuckled and went to the small economy-class restroom.

He was still inside when the alarm screamed.

The sound tore through the spacecraft.

Lan braced one hand on the wall.

A forced announcement followed, strained but calm by design.

Unknown spacecraft interception.

All passengers remain seated.

Safety belts forcibly locked.

Do not panic.

Lan's first thought was not panic.

It was assessment.

Interception.

Unknown craft.

Civilian route.

Space pirates?

Outside the restroom, two flight attendants whispered in voices too frightened to stay quiet.

Five miniature meteorite-class surveillance spaceships — 5 small pirate ships, exactly the kind of number a civilian craft should never meet.

A civilian spacecraft unable to deal with them.

A space warship stored below.

No pilot.

The pirates were already firing.

Not bargaining.

Not kidnapping.

Trying to destroy them.

The spacecraft shook hard enough that Lan's shoulder hit the wall.

In first-class, Le Qingling clutched Tang Le's arm and shook. Tang Le comforted her softly while bodyguards moved with professional urgency. Soon the lower doors opened and twelve mechas launched: eight purple from Tang Le's side, four yellow from the spacecraft's own security.

Mechas could fight in space for a short time.

They could not fix the fact that five pirate spaceships had come to kill.

Lan opened the restroom door.

The attendants stared at him.

He moved before age could become an argument.

He unbuckled one attendant's safety belt and gripped her wrist.

"I am a student of Heaven Luo Academy's Interplanet Command System," he said, voice urgent and steady. "I major in space warship piloting. Bring me to the warship. Now."

The attendant looked at his face and saw a child.

Then she felt his Spiritual Power, his focus, and the strange steadiness of someone who had been trained to think while fear shouted.

"You can pilot?"

"Yes."

It was not the whole truth.

It was enough truth for people who were about to die.

Lan thought of the Horizon Building years ago, of standing helpless while danger fell from above and someone else had to save him.

His parents were on this ship.

This time, he had a way to move.

The attendant ran.

Lan followed.

The lower deck smelled of metal, pressure seals, and emergency heat.

The warship waited there like a silver-grey answer no one had expected to use.

Twenty-five meters long, 25m by the deck readout. Eighteen-meter wingspan, 18m across the spread. Basic model. Rapid-fire Soul Guidance cannon at the front. No heavy weapons, no antimatter missiles, no luxury, no forgiveness.

To Lan Xuanyu, it was beautiful.

He climbed into the cockpit with movements learned thousands of times in simulation and never once in reality. The canopy began to seal over him. Controls woke beneath his hands.

The communication channel snapped open with the captain's voice, furious and afraid.

"Who activated the warship? Identify yourself!"

Lan took a breath and lowered his voice.

"Captain, I am a space warship pilot. I heard there was no one able to pilot the craft. The shield is failing. Open the lower deck and let me join the battle."

"Designation? Unit?"

"There is no time," Lan said. "If the shield breaks, everyone dies. Trust me."

For one second, the captain had to choose between protocol and extinction.

He chose life.

The lower deck opened.

Lan's helmet locked into place. His Spiritual Power poured into the control system, and the warship answered like a body with sharper bones than his own.

No pain reduction.

No restart.

No teacher ending the simulation after failure.

His fingers moved.

Thruster systems ignited.

Orange fire burst behind the warship.

From first-class, Tang Le stood by the window, long blue hair lifting like a cape in the invisible pressure of something waking inside him. His right hand touched the glass, and faint gold flashed beneath his palm.

The captain watched the monitor as the small warship slid, then shot from the lower deck into space.

The launch angle was clean.

Too clean for a frightened amateur.

"A seasoned pilot," the captain breathed, hope arriving before sense could stop it.

Outside, the twelve allied mechas were already locked against pirate forces. More dark yellow mechas poured from the five enemy ships. Fire crossed the void in lines too bright and too final.

Lan Xuanyu entered space for the first time in a real warship.

His Spiritual Power spread through the controls.

The battlefield opened.

Four pirate mechas peeled away and turned toward him.

On Heaven Luo Planet, far below the sky Lan had left, Yan Shuo's terminal remained silent after the laughing face.

In the training field, red-gold wings folded at his back as he looked up once without knowing why.

Song called, "Landing point ready."

Luo called, "West lane clear."

Yan lowered his gaze.

"Again," he said.

Between wings and stars, both boys moved toward danger neither could explain to the other yet.
