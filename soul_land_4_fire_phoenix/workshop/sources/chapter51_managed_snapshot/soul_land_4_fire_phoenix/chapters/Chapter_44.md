# Chapter 44: The Door That Chose Three

Qian Lei's joy had nowhere safe to stand.

One moment, Shrek Academy had opened like a golden gate in front of him. The next, the gate had teeth.

Lan Xuanyu watched the change happen on Qian Lei's face: the wide, disbelieving happiness; the greedy little sparkle that belonged to a boy imagining his family shouting themselves hoarse with pride; the sudden blankness when Ling Yiyi's words reached the part of him that was not greed, not vanity, not fear, but team.

Later assessments required teams of three.

If Qian Lei registered early, Lan Xuanyu and Liu Feng might not be able to continue.

It was a simple sentence.

That was why it was cruel.

Liu Feng reacted first.

"Why?"

The word came out sharp enough to make Qian Lei flinch.

Liu Feng took one step forward, the White Dragon Spear still in his hand though its tip had lowered. His face was pale from exhaustion and false magma heat, but his eyes were bright with anger.

"Why can't the two of us keep testing? If later rounds need three people, then arrange it differently. Or let us fight three opponents as two. We can do it."

Ling Yiyi's smile softened into something that looked almost regretful.

Almost.

That one word kept Lan from trusting it.

"The academy's assessment structure is not changed on the spot," she said. "The later tests are arranged around fixed team units. One person from your team being admitted early is already a rare success."

"Rare success?" Liu Feng repeated. "For him, yes. What about us? We survived too. Xuanyu led us through everything. We killed the two Scarlet Dragons together. How can the result be that only one person goes on?"

Qian Lei's face twisted.

Lan put a hand lightly on Liu Feng's shoulder.

Not to stop him.

To tell him he was not alone.

Then Lan looked at Ling Yiyi.

"Sister Ling," he said, voice controlled, "if the rule is teams of three, can Liu Feng and I continue as a two-person team under penalty? Or be assigned a substitute? We don't need special treatment. We only want the chance to keep taking the test."

Ling Yiyi looked at him for a long breath.

Lan did not look away.

Behind her, sunlight moved through leaves that had no business being peaceful. The partition separating Dorm333 from Dorm336 shimmered faintly. Beyond it, Yan Shuo stood with Song Yichen and Luo Haoran in their own pocket of green valley, all three still streaked with traces of ash and red light. They could not hear every word, but they could see enough.

Lan saw Yan's head lift when Liu Feng stepped forward.

He saw Song's shield hand tighten.

He saw Luo's eyes move from Ling Yiyi to the partition to the empty space around them, as if searching for the trick in the geometry.

There was always a trick.

Ling Yiyi said, "I am sorry. These are the rules."

Qian Lei whispered, "Sister Ling..."

His voice sounded small.

Ling Yiyi turned to him. "You must decide immediately. If you are willing, I will take you to complete registration."

"Wait," Qian Lei said. "You mean if I go, they—"

"They may not be able to participate in the following assessments," Ling Yiyi said.

May.

Lan caught the word and stored it.

Yin Tianfan had once told him that a battlefield was not only made of attacks. It was made of omissions, timing, and the words people thought you were too frightened to measure.

May was not must.

But sometimes may was a blade with a polite handle.

Liu Feng's shoulder trembled under Lan's hand.

"This is unreasonable," Liu Feng said.

Ling Yiyi did not argue.

That made it worse.

She raised one hand.

Light gathered around Lan Xuanyu and Liu Feng.

Qian Lei's eyes widened. "No, wait—"

Lan moved on instinct. Golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass flickered from his right hand toward Qian Lei.

The light cut between them.

Not violently.

Precisely.

The vine struck brightness and dissolved into harmless sparks.

Lan had time to see Qian Lei standing alone, mouth open, one hand half-raised toward them.

Then the green valley disappeared.

Heat returned.

Lan landed on one knee in red-black ash.

Liu Feng struck the ground beside him, rolled, and came up with spear ready before realizing there was nothing left to stab.

The volcanic valley had gone quiet.

The false magma pool lay still in the distance. No Scarlet Dragons remained. No Ground Fire Lizards crawled from the rocks. The air still smelled of heat, but the killing pressure had vanished, leaving behind an emptiness that felt almost more insulting than danger.

The place where they had fought for survival had become a waiting room for bad news.

Liu Feng stood frozen.

Then his grip tightened until his knuckles whitened.

"They sent us back," he said.

Lan rose slowly.

"Yes."

"Not out. Back."

"Yes."

"Does that mean eliminated? Or waiting? Or are they just enjoying watching us guess?"

Lan did not answer immediately.

Because in the first few breaths, he was angry too.

He had not spent three and a half years under Ji Hongbin's pressure and Yin Tianfan's traps to pretend effort did not matter. Liu Feng had broken through fear until his speed became useful. Qian Lei had taken an unreliable martial soul and made thirty seconds decide two monsters' lives. They had hidden beneath a Dragon, jumped into false magma, endured heat, waited through terror, and turned impossible into a path.

If the result was that one teammate ascended while the other two were discarded because of a rule revealed only after success, then Shrek Academy was not testing talent.

It was mocking it.

The thought was satisfying for one breath.

Then it broke under its own weight.

Lan looked up at the empty sky.

Shrek Academy did not need to mock them.

That was the problem.

"Xuanyu," Liu Feng said.

Lan heard something fragile in his voice and turned.

Liu Feng's eyes were red.

He looked furious, ashamed of being hurt, and unable to stop either feeling. For years, people had called him fast and useless in the same breath. Lan's support had given him a road, but a road that ended one step before Shrek because a teammate was too valuable to keep with them would be its own kind of cruelty.

"I'm not resigned," Liu Feng said. "We were so close. I thought... I thought we really had a chance."

Lan placed a hand on his shoulder again.

This time, it was not a signal.

It was support.

"We did have a chance," Lan said.

"Did?"

Lan did not like the word after it left his mouth.

Liu Feng laughed once, harsh and short. "So that's it? Qian Lei gets in, and we go home?"

"Maybe."

"Maybe?"

Lan lowered his eyes.

The ash around his shoes had no footprints except theirs. If Shrek wanted to eliminate them, why send them here instead of ending the simulation? If Qian Lei's decision had to be immediate, why let Lan and Liu wait in the same valley that had already finished its combat function? If the red-door transition had been only a reward, why separate Dorm336 into a visible but unreachable pocket before speaking to Qian?

Separate scoring spaces.

Luo Haoran's silent conclusion behind the partition returned to Lan's mind without needing sound.

Shrek never gives one test when it can give three.

Lan suddenly laughed.

It startled Liu Feng so badly that the White Dragon Spear almost came up again.

"What are you laughing at?"

"Us," Lan said.

"Us?"

"No. Them. A little. Mostly Teacher Yin."

Liu Feng stared at him. "Have you been cooked by the magma?"

"Maybe a little."

"Xuanyu!"

Lan sat down cross-legged on the hot stone.

The motion made Liu Feng even more alarmed.

"Why are you sitting?"

"Because if this is a waiting test, pacing will make me look guilty."

"Guilty of what?"

"Being unable to wait."

Liu Feng opened his mouth. Closed it. Then, in a tone of deep worry, said, "You are definitely cooked."

Lan rubbed the space between his brows.

"Think about it. Shrek has existed for tens of thousands of years. It is not a small academy trying to trick children for fun. It can be cruel. It can be unreasonable. But if it were truly unfair in such a simple way, it would not have become what it is."

"Rules can still be unfair."

"Yes. But this scene is too deliberate."

Liu Feng's anger paused.

Lan continued, "They did not announce our elimination. They did not send us out of Soul World. They separated us from Qian Lei only after placing the choice in front of him. They let Dorm336 see enough to know something happened but not enough to interfere. They placed every team in a different pocket after the red doors. Why?"

Liu Feng slowly lowered the spear.

"To test reactions."

"Most likely."

"Qian Lei's reaction?"

"And ours."

Liu Feng's face changed as understanding arrived unwillingly.

Lan nodded. "If Qian Lei accepts immediately and forgets us, Shrek learns something. If we resent him immediately and curse him for being chosen, Shrek learns something. If we assume the academy is unfair and collapse, Shrek learns something."

"That's too much," Liu Feng said.

"It's Shrek."

"We're twelve."

"That is probably why they think the reaction is honest."

Liu Feng stared at him for a long time.

Then he reached out, seized Lan by both shoulders, and shook him.

"Are you human?"

Lan let himself be shaken.

"Not the first time someone has asked."

"How can you think of all this now?"

"Teacher Yin once shot me in the back during my first simulated warship lesson."

Liu Feng's hands froze.

"He what?"

"Shot me. From behind. With his own warship."

"Why?"

"To teach me that there is no absolute trust in battle and that a commander must protect even against what he thinks is safe."

Liu Feng looked horrified.

Lan added, "Teacher Ji's lessons were less creative, but more painful."

"Your teachers are terrifying."

"Yes."

"That explains too much."

Lan smiled helplessly.

The smile faded quickly.

"I don't know what Qian Lei will choose."

Liu Feng's breathing changed.

"You think he might accept?"

Lan looked toward the empty place where the red door had been.

He did not want to answer carelessly. Qian Lei was their friend. He was greedy, proud, timid, funny, and too easily tempted by things that glittered. He was also the boy who had trusted Lan's hand on his shoulder before every impossible summon. He had risked panic under a Dragon's belly. He had pointed a murderous Emerald Demon Bird at the right eye while shaking hard enough to betray himself and still not backed away.

People were not one quality.

That was why tests hurt.

"Shrek Academy is a dream," Lan said. "Early admission means one of the thirty places. It means his family, his future, everything. If he hesitates, that is normal."

Liu Feng lowered himself to the ground beside Lan.

"And if he chooses it?"

Lan was quiet.

Then he said, "Then we bless him."

Liu Feng's head snapped around.

"Xuanyu—"

"And then we find another path," Lan said.

The words were calm.

They were not painless.

Liu Feng heard both parts.

For a while neither of them spoke.

In the green valley, Qian Lei stood alone before Ling Yiyi.

Alone was the first thing he noticed.

Not the offer.

Not the trees.

Not even Shrek.

Lan Xuanyu and Liu Feng had been there a breath ago. Now the space beside him was empty, and emptiness was far louder than either of his friends had ever been.

Qian Lei looked at the place where Lan's vine had broken against the light.

He had seen Lan reach for him.

That was unfair.

Not because the vine failed.

Because Lan reached.

"Qian Lei," Ling Yiyi said gently.

He turned back.

The smile on her face had not changed much. That made him want to cry and complain and maybe hide behind Liu Feng, except Liu Feng was not there.

"If you are willing," she said, "we can complete the registration now."

A light screen unfolded beside her.

It did not look dramatic.

That was somehow worse.

Name. Martial soul. Academy. Confirmation.

A few lines, and his life would change.

Qian Lei stared at them.

He saw his father's face.

He saw relatives who had once patted his head and said an unreliable martial soul was still special in its own way, which was what adults said when they did not know how to help. He saw himself returning home with Shrek Academy attached to his name. He saw the elite junior class staring. He saw Jin Xiang's mouth open without words. He saw everyone who had laughed when his gate produced something useless.

He saw all of it.

It was beautiful.

It was poisonous.

"If I sign," he said slowly, "Lan Xuanyu and Liu Feng really can't continue?"

Ling Yiyi said, "The following tests are arranged around teams of three. Their current team structure would no longer be complete."

Qian Lei's face twitched.

"That is not an answer."

For the first time, Ling Yiyi's eyes showed a sharper amusement.

"You have been with Lan Xuanyu too long."

"Answering questions with fog is Shrek's specialty, right?"

"Sometimes fog lets people reveal how they choose to walk."

Qian Lei looked at the screen again.

His name waited there.

He had never thought his own name could weigh so much.

He raised one hand.

Stopped.

His fingers shook.

He hated that they shook.

He hated that he wanted to sign.

If the test had offered him treasure, he could have joked. If it offered food, he could have exaggerated. If it offered a title, he could boast and then negotiate.

But this was Shrek.

It was the future.

It was not fair to expect a twelve-year-old boy to turn away from a future that large.

Qian Lei swallowed.

"I am not strong alone," he said.

Ling Yiyi watched him.

"Your martial soul is unique."

"Unique does not mean useful," he said. "Sometimes I open a gate and get nonsense. Sometimes I open a gate and get something that wants to eat us. Even the Emerald Demon Bird... without Xuanyu helping before, I don't know if I would have connected to it at all. And Liu Feng protects me while I stand there pretending to be mysterious."

His voice cracked on the last word.

He tried to laugh.

It failed.

"If I go in alone, what happens after everyone realizes the great Qian Lei is only great when his friends are standing close enough to stop him from dying?"

Ling Yiyi said nothing.

The screen waited.

Qian Lei's eyes reddened.

"I want it," he said.

This time he did cry, though he rubbed the tears away angrily before they could fall properly.

"I really want it. Only a fool wouldn't want it. I am greedy. I admit it. I want my family to know. I want everyone to know. I want to stop being the fatty with an unreliable gate."

He looked at the screen again.

Then at the empty places where his teammates should have been.

"But if I take it like this, then every time someone says I entered Shrek, I will remember that I stepped through a door and let it close on them."

His hand dropped.

"I refuse."

Ling Yiyi's expression did not change.

Qian Lei hated that too.

"Say something," he demanded.

"Are you sure?"

"No!" Qian Lei burst out. "Of course I'm not sure! That's Shrek Academy! Do you think saying no feels good? My heart is being cut into pieces! Ask me if I am choosing. I am choosing. Do not ask if I am sure."

Ling Yiyi's smile finally became real at the edges.

"Then, Qian Lei, your choice is recorded."

Light opened under his feet.

"Wait," he said, panicking immediately. "Recorded good or recorded bad? Sister Ling? Sister Ling! If this was a trick, can you at least say it was a trick? If it wasn't a trick, can you say I can change my mind? Actually don't say that. I might—"

The light took him.

Across the partition, Dorm336's test did not wait politely for Dorm333's pain to finish.

Shrek did not ignore monsters.

It also did not worship them.

The assessment projection facing Yan Shuo, Song Yichen, and Luo Haoran carried Ling Yiyi's outline, but its eyes had the smooth depth of a recording array. It had listened to their route report already: Luo's thermal seam and impact reading, Song's shield anchoring, Yan's restrained Fire Phoenix heat-balance, the controlled true Fire Phoenix pressure that bent back inferior fire and disrupted the Scarlet Dragons' flame rhythm, the decision not to abandon formation for solo scoring when the Dragons weakened, and the final red-door escape when the false geothermal current changed.

Then the projection stopped asking about the route.

It looked directly at Yan.

"Yan Shuo."

Song and Luo both went still.

Yan answered, "Here."

"Your preliminary scan and qualifier route have triggered special observation. Bone age below eleven. Soul Power Rank39. Spiritual Power nine hundred and sixty-two. Three purple soul rings. Fire Phoenix martial soul in a complete awakened state. Ultimate Fire control within safe student output. Perfectly compatible and fully absorbed soul-spirit foundation exceeding ten thousand years in combined age. Fire Phoenix suppression successfully affected inferior fire-dragon targets under second-phase human-priority pressure. Team survival route maintained."

The words fell one after another.

Not praise.

Measurement.

That made them heavier.

Song's mouth opened slightly despite himself.

He knew Yan's numbers. He had stood beside them before. Hearing them recited by Shrek after the qualifier made them sound less like class rumors and more like a verdict.

Luo's eyes sharpened.

Yan did not move.

He had practiced not moving while adults weighed him.

The projection continued, "Based on current data, Shrek Academy can open an individual advance-admission registration path for you. If you choose this path, your personal candidacy may be settled before the public ranking announcement. Candidates who can suppress fire-dragon-type beasts before the age of eleven are not treated as ordinary applicants."

For the first time since entering the assessment pocket, Song forgot to breathe.

Luo looked at Yan.

Even the partition light seemed to quiet.

Yan felt the words strike somewhere old.

An individual path.

A settled candidacy.

Shrek Academy, not as a dream in the distance, but as a door opening directly in front of him.

He had wanted many things in silence because wanting loudly was dangerous. He had wanted strength enough that no one could decide his life for him. He had wanted the Fire Phoenix to be more than a risk hidden under paperwork. He had wanted to stand somewhere so high that the name Yan Shuo could carry everything concealed behind it without collapsing.

This was not small.

This was not token recognition.

This was Shrek seeing him.

Publicly as Yan Shuo.

Dangerously enough.

"If I accept," Yan said, "what happens to Dorm336?"

The projection's answer came without hesitation.

"Song Yichen and Luo Haoran's results remain valid and recorded. Their contributions are part of why your route reached this layer. However, later assessments are arranged by team structures. If you register as an individual in advance, Dorm336's current three-person route ends here. Their following opportunities would be recalculated separately according to available rules and rankings."

Song's face changed.

"Class monitor—"

Yan lifted one hand slightly.

Song stopped, but the words remained in his eyes.

Take it, maybe.

Or don't decide because of us.

Luo said nothing. That was worse. Luo's silence meant he understood all angles and did not like any of them.

The projection added, "This is an individual opportunity earned by your own displayed value. You are not required to preserve your current team route. Shrek Academy selects monsters. A true Fire Phoenix with Ultimate Fire and that foundation can walk very far even alone."

Song flinched.

Luo's gaze went cold.

Yan finally looked away from the projection.

He looked at Song's taped shoulder. At the shield arm that had held stone when the thermal fissure tried to close. At Luo's pale face and the hand that had found the route before heat became death. At the green valley that existed only because all three of them had reached it.

Then he looked through the partition toward the empty space where Lan and Liu had vanished, and where Qian Lei had stood alone a moment longer.

Shrek had opened two doors.

Not identical doors.

Qian Lei's door asked whether a boy who had always been underestimated would abandon the two people who made him possible when recognition finally arrived.

Yan's door asked whether an obvious monster would step over the team that proved he was more than a bright flame.

Different doors.

Same blade.

The Fire Phoenix in him did not like lowering its head.

It also did not like being bought cheaply.

"Dorm336's route was not my route alone," Yan said.

The projection watched.

"My fire suppressed their fire because their bloodline and flame were beneath a true Fire Phoenix. That part is mine. But if Song had not anchored the shield line, their bodies would still have crushed the fissure. If Luo had not found the false seam, my pressure would have had no safe path and the ceiling would have collapsed on all three of us. My control mattered because they gave it shape. The answer that reached this valley was a team answer. If Shrek records only me, it records the wrong answer."

Song stared at him.

Luo's expression softened by one invisible degree.

The projection said, "You may lose certainty."

"Then I lose certainty."

"You may face harder later tests."

"Then we face them."

"You may lower your personal efficiency by remaining attached to the team route."

Yan's mouth curved faintly.

"A Fire Phoenix that cannot protect formation is only a fire."

The projection's eyes held on him.

Yan did not look away.

He was very tired. Too tired, perhaps, to feel the full weight of what he was refusing. That weight would probably find him later, when the room was quiet and the thought of a settled Shrek path returned with teeth.

But the choice itself was clear.

"Record Dorm336," Yan said. "All three."

For a moment, nothing happened.

Then the projection smiled.

This smile was smaller than Ling Yiyi's.

It also felt more dangerous.

"Choice recorded. Dorm336's team route remains intact. Individual special observation remains attached to your file. Final integrated results will be released later."

Song exhaled so hard that it almost became a laugh.

"You could have warned us before refusing Shrek in front of us."

Yan glanced at him. "You were going to tell me to take it."

Song opened his mouth.

Closed it.

"Maybe."

Luo said, "He was absolutely going to tell you to take it. Then he would regret saying it. Then I would have to listen to both of you being noble and miserable. This is more efficient."

Song stared. "Why do you always sound like you are grateful in the most insulting way possible?"

"Practice."

Yan almost laughed.

The almost mattered.

The projection raised one hand.

"Qualifier ending."

Light gathered beneath Dorm336's feet.

Yan looked once more toward Dorm333's empty pocket.

Lan Xuanyu had reached toward Qian Lei when the light separated them.

Yan had noticed.

Of course he had noticed.

A battlefield announced danger with teeth.

Shrek announced it by asking what a person would abandon.

Yan closed his wings.

"We go out as three," he said.

Light swallowed Dorm336.

In the volcanic valley, Lan opened his eyes just before another light appeared.

Liu Feng rose so fast he nearly slipped.

A figure stumbled out of brightness ahead of them.

Qian Lei landed on both feet, took one step, saw them, and immediately sat down hard as if his bones had dissolved.

Then he cried.

Not elegant tears.

Not the dramatic fake sobbing he sometimes used when food was threatened or training schedules looked unreasonable.

Real tears.

Messy, angry, humiliated tears that made his face red and his breathing uneven.

Lan and Liu Feng rushed to him.

Liu Feng reached first.

Relief made him foolish.

"Qian Lei," he blurted, "did you do something terrible and get sent back as punishment?"

Qian Lei's head snapped up.

Tear tracks shone on his face.

For one breath Lan thought Qian would wail louder.

Instead, Qian Lei launched himself forward and grabbed Liu Feng by the front of his uniform.

"Do you have a conscience?"

Liu Feng froze.

Qian Lei shook him with the full strength of a boy who had very little physical strength but enormous emotional momentum.

"Do you know what I gave up? I gave up early admission to Shrek Academy! Shrek! Academy! I gave it up for you two, and the first thing you ask is whether I did something terrible? I did something noble! Painfully noble! My heart is bleeding and you step on it!"

Liu Feng stared at him.

Lan stared too.

They had guessed.

Guessing was not the same as hearing it.

Qian Lei's hands trembled around Liu Feng's lapels.

"I could have registered," he said, voice breaking. "She had the screen ready. My name was there. One of the thirty places. I almost touched it. I wanted to touch it. I wanted it so much I could hear my ancestors preparing drums."

Liu Feng's expression cracked.

"Fatty..."

"Do not Fatty me yet. I am furious."

"All right."

"And heartbroken."

"All right."

"And hungry, possibly from grief."

"That part sounds normal."

Qian Lei shook him again. "This is why I am furious!"

Lan crouched beside them.

"You refused," he said softly.

Qian Lei looked at him.

For a moment all the performance fell away.

"How could I not?" he whispered. "If I went alone, what would I be? Without you, my summons are half luck and half funeral notice. Without Liu Feng, I get eaten while pretending to command. Without both of you, I would never have reached that offer in the first place."

Liu Feng's eyes reddened.

Qian Lei sniffed hard and tried to look dignified while crying.

"Don't misunderstand. I was very reluctant. Extremely reluctant. If someone writes a history of my life, there should be a whole chapter about how reluctant I was. Possibly with illustrations."

Lan's chest hurt.

So he smiled.

"We will remember."

"You had better."

Liu Feng reached up and pulled Qian Lei into a hug.

Qian Lei stiffened, then made a sound suspiciously close to a sob and grabbed him back.

"Good brother," Liu Feng said.

"Of course I am a good brother," Qian Lei muttered into his shoulder. "An expensive one. The price was Shrek early admission."

Lan placed one hand on each of their shoulders.

The volcanic valley was empty around them. No Dragons. No lizards. No audience they could see.

But Lan had never felt more certain that Shrek was watching.

Let them watch.

"We will enter Shrek," Lan said.

Qian Lei and Liu Feng looked at him.

Lan's voice did not rise.

It did not need to.

"The three of us. Together."

Qian Lei wiped his face with his sleeve.

"If we fail after I gave that up, I will haunt both of you."

"Then we don't fail," Liu Feng said.

Qian Lei nodded miserably. "Good plan. Simple. Elegant. Difficult enough to be Xuanyu's fault."

Lan laughed.

The sound surprised him.

A cold electronic voice descended from the sky.

"Qualifier ended. Final survivor routes recorded."

Light opened beneath them.

This time, none of them tried to resist.

When the light faded, the three stood at the entrance space where they had first met Ling Yiyi before entering the qualifier. The air was clean, neither volcanic nor forest-wet. The red-black valley was gone. The green recovery pocket was gone. Ling Yiyi stood ahead of them, hands folded, wearing the same smile that had become more suspicious every time they survived another layer of the test.

To their right, another column of light faded at almost the same moment.

Yan Shuo, Song Yichen, and Luo Haoran stood inside it.

For one breath, both teams simply looked at each other.

Dorm333 was streaked with false-magma residue, exhausted and emotionally battered.

Dorm336 looked no better: Song's shield arm hung stiffly, Luo's face was pale from overused focus, and Yan's copper-red hair had been forced back into public restraint though a tired red-gold glimmer still lived along its edges. Even worn down, Yan's face remained too striking for the plain academy uniform to make ordinary, an age-appropriate phoenix beauty sharpened into the public mask of an unusually handsome boy.

Qian Lei blinked through drying tears.

"Why do they look like they also sacrificed something?"

Song stared back. "Why do you look like you fought your own wallet and lost?"

"It was worse than that," Qian Lei said solemnly.

Luo glanced at Yan. "Similar category, perhaps."

Yan did not answer that.

Lan noticed.

So did Ling Yiyi.

Her smile deepened by the smallest amount.

"Both final survivor routes from your academy group have ended," Ling Yiyi said. "Dorm333. Dorm336. Your choices have been recorded."

Qian Lei stiffened. "Choices plural?"

Song's mouth opened.

Yan said calmly, "Later."

That one word carried the weight of a closed wing.

Qian Lei, surprisingly, understood enough not to pry.

Perhaps pain recognized pain even when it did not know the shape.

Lan scanned the surroundings.

No other students stood nearby.

"Sister Ling," he asked, "where are the others?"

"Most have already completed their qualifier process and returned," Ling Yiyi said. "You six remained in the final hidden assessment layer the longest among your academy's entrants. The official results will calculate all routes together."

Not three.

Six.

Lan heard the correction in the world and accepted it.

Liu Feng asked, "If Qian Lei had accepted the early admission, would Xuanyu and I truly have been unable to continue?"

Ling Yiyi smiled.

It was a sealed door of a smile.

"Different choices lead to different results. You will understand more in the future."

Qian Lei clutched his chest. "If I had accepted, would I really have been admitted? Or was the offer fake?"

Ling Yiyi shook her head lightly.

"The teachers have final authority over admissions. What I can tell you is that your martial soul is truly special, and some teachers have indeed noticed you."

Qian Lei looked wounded all over again.

"That does not heal me."

"It was not meant to."

"Shrek is cruel."

Ling Yiyi's smile brightened. "Shrek is selective."

"That is what cruel people call themselves when they have good uniforms."

Liu Feng elbowed him.

Lan bowed slightly before Qian Lei could continue insulting their dream academy to its representative.

"Sister Ling, our results?"

"The qualifier results across Tian Luo Planet will be published together," Ling Yiyi said. Her gaze moved once, including both teams this time. "The specific rankings and advancement list will be announced later. Rest now."

Yan asked, "Dorm336's team route remains intact for result calculation?"

Ling Yiyi looked at him.

"Yes."

Song's shoulders eased despite his effort to appear unconcerned.

Luo closed his eyes briefly.

Yan bowed. "Understood."

No matter what they asked, Ling Yiyi would not say more.

Lan understood that quickly.

He also understood something else.

Ling Yiyi had not denied the tests.

A denial would have been easy.

Shrek Academy preferred sealed doors.

The Soul World dissolved.

Lan Xuanyu woke inside the simulated cabin with exhaustion waiting for him like a debt collector.

The jelly-like nutrient fluid clung to his skin as the cabin opened. Air touched his face, cool enough that his body almost mistook it for kindness. The moment the pressure released, every thought he had held sharp during the qualifier fell onto him at once.

The Dragon's foot.

The false magma.

The Emerald Demon Bird's green line.

Ling Yiyi's offer.

Qian Lei crying.

Lan sat up too quickly and nearly swayed.

A hand caught the edge of his cabin.

Not touching him.

Close enough in case he fell.

Yan Shuo stood beside the adjacent row, his own cabin lid still open behind him. He had awakened only a few breaths earlier. Nutrient fluid still dampened the ends of his restrained copper-red hair, and fatigue made the red-gold beneath harder to hide before he forced it dim. Song Yichen was dragging himself upright nearby with one shoulder taped by the cabin's automatic medical strip. Luo Haoran leaned against a support rail as if pretending the rail needed supervision.

Dorm336 was out.

Alive.

Not early enough to be dismissed.

Not late enough to steal anyone's door.

The final wave had returned both teams.

Yan's hand withdrew the moment Lan steadied himself.

A public class monitor could help any classmate.

The private self behind that mask could not let worry linger too visibly.

Lan looked at him.

Yan looked back.

Neither said what the partition had already said.

Alive.

Qian Lei's cabin opened with a hiss, and he emerged with the tragic dignity of someone who believed the universe owed him compensation.

Liu Feng climbed out beside him, moving stiffly but steady.

Only then did Lan notice the rest of the room.

The entire Elite Junior Class had not dispersed.

Twenty-one students, some seated, some standing, some too tired to pretend they were not staring, filled the training-ground entrance area. Jin Xiang's face was dark; his team's early elimination had left anger with nowhere useful to go. Several lower-ranked teams looked hollow, still carrying the disbelief of being removed before showing their full strength.

Lu Qianxun stood with Ye Lingtong and Chang Jianyi. They looked tired but calmer than most. Their team had at least fought, adapted, and scored. Lu's gaze moved from Lan to Yan and back again with an expression that admitted something had changed whether he liked it or not.

The teachers were there too.

Mu Zhongtian's relief was visible before he controlled it. Ji Hongbin had already stepped forward, eyes sharp enough to count injuries before words. Yin Tianfan stood behind him with arms crossed and a pleased expression he was trying, badly, to disguise as criticism.

President Zhang was not shouting for once.

That alone made the room feel solemn.

Ji Hongbin reached the final two teams first.

"Reports," he said.

One word.

Not because he lacked questions.

Because too many questions would waste time.

Lan lowered his head respectfully.

"Teacher, we should have a chance to pass, but they did not tell us the ranking. They said the results will be announced together."

Yan answered immediately after him, voice steady despite fatigue. "Dorm336's route was recorded as a team route. They also deferred final ranking."

Ji Hongbin's eyes sharpened.

Team route.

Lan heard it.

So did Yin Tianfan.

So did Qian Lei, whose grief made him unusually sensitive to any sentence shaped like sacrifice.

Mu Zhongtian looked between Dorm333 and Dorm336, then at the closed record panel beside the training ground. His voice was careful.

"Both final teams returned without official ranking disclosure. Until the results come out, no one speculates publicly. You will rest, recover, and wait. Understood?"

"Yes," the students answered, some loudly, some weakly.

Ji Hongbin turned to Dorm336.

"Yan Shuo. Injuries?"

"Simulation strain, heat-load fatigue. Song's shoulder took shield impact. Luo overused sensory focus. No lasting damage reported by the cabin."

Song blinked. "That was very complete for someone who also almost got cooked."

"I had time to prepare the report while you complained about breathing," Yan said.

Luo smiled faintly. "Accurate."

Ji Hongbin's gaze held approval he did not voice.

Then he looked back at Lan.

"You three. Medical check, food, and rest. Detailed report after official results. Do not exaggerate. Do not conceal anything that affects safety. Do not gossip about assessment mechanisms before rankings are published."

Qian Lei opened his mouth.

Lan and Liu Feng both looked at him.

Yan, Song, and Luo also looked at him.

He closed it again with great pain.

Yin Tianfan noticed and smiled.

"Good. At least one lesson entered your skull."

"Teacher," Qian Lei said weakly, "my skull has had a difficult day. Please speak gently to it."

"Impossible."

The room began to move after that. Teachers directed students toward rest. Some teams left quickly, unable to bear the sight of the two groups who had stayed inside longest. Others lingered, curiosity stronger than exhaustion.

Lan stepped down fully from the cabin platform.

His legs felt heavier than they should.

Yan shifted half a step, not enough to draw attention, but enough that if Lan stumbled, the class monitor would again be close enough to help any classmate.

Lan did not stumble.

He did glance sideways.

"Your route?" he asked quietly.

Yan's mouth curved by the smallest amount.

"A crack that lied less than the pool."

Lan almost smiled.

"That sounds dangerous."

"Yours looked worse."

"It was warm."

"So was ours."

The exchange lasted only a few breaths.

It said nothing secret.

It carried more than the words.

Qian Lei inserted himself between them with the solemn desperation of someone who needed witnesses.

"Yan Shuo, if anyone asks, remember that I made a noble sacrifice today."

Yan looked at him.

"A noble sacrifice?"

"Historic. Painful. Very expensive."

Liu Feng said, "He refused an early admission offer."

Several nearby students heard and turned.

Qian Lei immediately looked horrified.

"Liu Feng! Secret! Painful secret!"

Liu Feng paused.

"You announced noble sacrifice first."

"Not the details!"

Yan's expression changed, but not with surprise alone.

Song's head turned slightly toward him.

Luo's eyes sharpened with the unmistakable look of someone filing away future teasing material and serious information at once.

Lan said quietly, "Qian chose the team."

That ended the laughter before it grew too sharp.

Yan looked at Qian Lei again.

This time his gaze held something steadier than amusement.

"Then it was noble," he said.

Qian Lei froze.

Praise from Yan Shuo, class monitor and public monster of the year, was not something he had prepared defenses against.

His ears went red.

"Yes," Qian said, trying to recover. "Exactly. Finally, someone understands the proper tone."

Song gave him a tired thumbs-up. "Historic. Painful. Very expensive."

"See? Dorm336 understands!"

Luo said, "More than you think."

Qian Lei blinked. "What does that mean?"

Yan said, "Later."

The same word as before.

A closed wing.

Lan heard it and did not pry.

He only looked at Yan for half a breath longer.

Qian Lei was not the only one Shrek had tested with a door.

A true Fire Phoenix with Ultimate Fire had not been ignored. A Rank39 child with three purple rings and a fully compatible soul-spirit foundation beyond ten thousand years had no chance of being treated like a normal survivor, especially after forcing inferior fire-dragon beasts to hesitate in their own valley.

Natural, then.

Cruel, but natural.

The strongest students were not spared tests.

They were given sharper ones.

Then Lan felt a gaze from across the room.

Lu Qianxun was walking toward him.

Ye Lingtong followed one step behind, quiet. Chang Jianyi came with them, thoughtful and pale from fatigue.

Lu stopped in front of Lan.

He did not look arrogant now.

He looked like someone who had lost, thought about it, and decided that pride was less useful than answers.

He extended his hand.

"Thanks," Lu Qianxun said.

Lan looked at the offered hand.

Around them, the noisy room seemed to narrow.

The qualifier had ended.

The consequences had not.
