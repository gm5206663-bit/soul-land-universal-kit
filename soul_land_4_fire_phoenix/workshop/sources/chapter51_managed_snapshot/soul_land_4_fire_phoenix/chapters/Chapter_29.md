# Chapter 29: The Defeat He Chose

The pause was a gift.

Lan Xuanyu did not trust gifts in an examination.

Across the empty arena, no challengers appeared. The light that usually gathered before new opponents descended remained quiet, as if the Soul World itself had taken a breath and was deciding whether to apologize for the last four matches.

Qian Lei sat cross-legged behind him, both hands resting on his knees, lips pressed together, face pale enough that his usual complaints would have looked more comforting than the discipline he was forcing on himself now. Spiritual Power did not refill like Soul Power. Restoration light could return the body to fighting condition. It could not make the mind forget the weight of holding a Crocodile Dragon, the knife-bright danger of an Emerald Demon Bird, or the heat and mass of a Red Armored Dragon.

Four wins.

One more would put them across the safest line.

That should have made Lan happy.

It did not.

A number was not a plan. Four victories meant Dorm333 had earned room. It did not mean they could spend carelessly.

Lan looked at Qian Lei's shoulders. They rose and fell slowly, too slowly for a child who usually breathed like his thoughts were racing ahead of his mouth.

If Qian recovered enough, another summon might win another match. Lan already knew the most dependable direction: not chasing the uncertain girl through the Gate, not gambling on a rare answer that did not come when called, but leaning into the strange way his golden-patterned right hand could guide Qian's martial soul toward soul beasts with dragon blood.

Dependable did not mean safe.

The Emerald Demon Bird had proved that the pattern could split open beneath his feet.

Beside him, Liu Feng adjusted his grip on the White Dragon Spear. The tremor in his wrist had faded. The light in his eyes had not.

"If they give us this much time," Liu Feng said quietly, "Qian Lei may recover enough."

"Maybe," Lan said.

Qian Lei did not open his eyes. "I hear optimism. It makes me suspicious."

"Recover."

"I am recovering suspiciously."

Lan almost smiled, then looked again at the empty side of the arena.

The pause had given them rest.

It had also given the others sight.

A tactic seen once could still work if the opponent was frightened enough.

A tactic replayed by teachers became a question the whole room was invited to answer.

Lan flexed his right hand.

The next team would not enter like the fourth.

---

The waiting hall had become quieter after the noise.

At first, the released video had made everyone speak at once. Hands sliced through the air to mark Liu Feng's line. Students argued about whether jumping back, splitting sideways, or releasing defensive skills first would matter. A few tried to copy footwork in the narrow space until Mu Zhongtian's assistant teacher glared hard enough that they remembered this was still an exam hall and not a marketplace.

Then the fifth challenger team was called.

Liang Zheng stood up.

The room noticed.

It would have noticed even before Yan Shuo's public rise, before Dorm333's four plus signs, before Qian Lei's mocked summoning became something no one wanted to face. Liang Zheng had always been the kind of student whose silence took up less space than other people's boasting and somehow weighed more.

He had served as assistant monitor because he did not waste words, because Mu Zhongtian could hand him a small duty and expect it done, and because strength without steadiness made teachers tired. Among ordinary one-ring students, his combat reputation was built on steadiness, defense, and never handing an opponent a free opening.

That old ordinary scale could not measure the current class.

Yan Shuo could already defeat Lu Qianxun even without relying on a full fusion burst. When his Fire Phoenix and soul-spirit fusion route were counted properly, the gap had been large, not narrow. Liang Zheng was further away still. Now Yan Shuo was Rank23, a two-ring Fire Phoenix with deep yellow and deep purple pressure under a boyish public cover. Dorm333 had turned four plus signs and a supposedly weak center into four arena victories. Lu Qianxun had reached Rank20 and still carried Golden Lion King pride like armor. The class no longer had one clean ladder.

Liang Zheng's danger was not that he stood near Yan.

He did not.

His danger was that he was calm, useful, and disciplined enough to punish the one opening the video had shown him.

Wang Lijian rose on his left, fingers already brushing the place where his bow would appear. Chen Yuelin rose on his right, eyes lowered as if he were watching vines grow under the floor.

They did not look excited.

That made them more dangerous.

Liang glanced once at the frozen memory of the fourth-match video, then at his teammates. His voice was too low for the surrounding teams to hear clearly.

"Do not meet the first line. The moment entry begins, retreat. I take Liu Feng. Chen covers the ground. Wang, Qian Lei."

Wang Lijian's mouth tightened. "The summoner?"

"The resting summoner," Liang said.

That was enough.

The public video had not shown the Crocodile Dragon. It had not shown the Emerald Demon Bird. It had not shown the Red Armored Dragon.

It had shown Qian Lei sitting behind Lan and Liu Feng after three unseen wins.

The result board had shown the rest.

Three victories before the speed match. One summoner with SP187. One exhausted boy meditating because Spiritual Power did not obey arena lights.

A weak point did not have to be weak in order to be attacked.

Sometimes it was attacked because it was important.

Liang Zheng's team stepped toward the transfer light.

Across the hall, Yan Shuo watched them go.

Luo Haoran leaned closer and murmured, "Please tell me he is not calm because he has a secret plan."

"He is calm because he has a plan," Yan said.

"That was not the reassurance I ordered."

Song Yichen looked after Liang's team. "He will not charge blindly."

"No," Yan agreed. "The video made charging blindly unforgivable."

A few students nearby turned their heads. Yan's voice had not risen, but the class monitor's quiet words had a habit of becoming public property when everyone was afraid.

Yan kept his gaze on the fading transfer light.

She did not know what would happen inside the arena.

That boundary mattered.

She knew what the room knew: Dorm333 had won four times. The first three wins had happened before any footage was released. The fourth video showed Lan and Liu Feng turning first-move advantage into a blade. Qian Lei, once treated as a joke, had sat behind them in recovery while the board carried three unexplained victories on his back.

She knew Liang Zheng was not stupid.

She knew Wang Lijian had reach.

She knew Chen Yuelin could make ground inconvenient.

She knew her own first answer for Dorm336 had been right in shape: no straight line, no convenient cluster, no waiting for full release if position itself could save the first breath.

That was public knowledge and class knowledge.

It was enough to think with.

It was not enough to steal Lan's truth.

Beneath the dull collar, Yan Shuo'er felt the hidden line of her true body stay steady. Dawnflame Kite stirred warm and pale-gold under the ribs, impatient in the way fire-birds disliked not seeing the sky. Dawn-Iron Phoenix Roc answered with weight: stance first, then wings.

She let both impressions pass through her breath and said only, "Watch the board."

Luo blinked. "Not the door?"

"The door tells us who leaves. The board tells us what the teachers are willing to say."

Song gave a slow nod.

They watched.

---

Light gathered in the arena.

Lan moved.

So did Liu Feng.

They did not wait for the three figures to become fully solid. Waiting would waste the only advantage the defending-champion rule still gave them. Lan stepped into the same line as before, right arm warm, golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass brightening, body turning force from foot to waist to shoulder.

Liu Feng came through the line like a thrown spear.

Lan's palm met his back.

The White Dragon Spear answered.

Liu Feng reached the far side almost before the challengers had faces.

Almost.

The three figures retreated at the same instant.

Not stumbled.

Not panicked.

Retreated.

Their bodies leaped back before their eyes had finished taking in the arena, before ordinary children would have decided whether the floor beneath them was flat. Liu Feng's opening sweep cut the air close enough to lift the edges of their uniforms, but not close enough to end them.

Lan's heart tightened.

They had watched.

Liu Feng did not stop. Surprise could not be allowed to become pause. The White Dragon Spear thrust forward in midair, spear aura bursting out in a concentrated fan as he poured the remaining amplification from Lan's push into pressure. If the instant kill had failed, suppression came next.

The challengers became clear.

Liang Zheng stood in the center, tall for their age, expression steady. Wang Lijian and Chen Yuelin fell behind him at once, not because they were afraid, but because the formation had been decided before entry.

Liang raised both hands.

Two metallic half-moon shapes flashed into being.

They joined.

A shield caught Liu Feng's spear aura.

Ding ding ding ding.

Strike after strike rang against the curved metal. Liang slid back one step, then another, then two more, shoes scraping bright lines across the arena floor. His arms shook. His face did not.

When the spear aura ended, Liang still stood.

Liu Feng's eyes sharpened.

So did Lan's.

A team that survived the opening line became a different problem.

Chen Yuelin moved next. Green ivy spilled from his hands, fast and low, spreading in several directions at once. It did not rush like a single vine trying to bind an ankle. It spread like a question asked of the whole floor: where will you step now?

Wang Lijian lifted a white bow with a beautiful curve.

The first soul ring under his feet brightened.

A yellow arrow formed on the string.

Lan saw its angle and knew the target before the arrow flew.

Qian Lei.

"Liu Feng!" Lan called.

But Liu Feng had already been forced to angle away. Green ivy cut his landing path, and Chen Yuelin was weaving it wider, spider-web lines waiting for a speed specialist whose defense could not afford one mistake. If Liu Feng turned back now, he might save nothing and lose himself.

The bowstring snapped.

The arrow curved.

It did not fly straight toward Qian Lei. A straight arrow could be blocked by a child who understood straight lines. Wang Lijian's arrow bent around the obvious path, guided by soul skill and intent, aiming for the one person in Dorm333 who could not afford disturbance.

Qian Lei's eyes remained closed.

He was deep enough in recovery that even a glancing interruption could scatter the Spiritual Power he was rebuilding. Losing Qian in battle was one thing. Shaking his spiritual sea while it was trying to gather itself was another.

Lan abandoned the attack.

There was no noble feeling in the choice. No time to mourn the lost advantage. He turned because the correct answer had become obvious and because an obvious answer delayed by pride became a mistake.

Behind him, metal sang.

Liang Zheng separated the half-moon blades.

Two arcs of light flew from them.

Lunula Slash.

The blades chased Lan's back while the guided arrow curved toward Qian Lei's head.

Lan's body blurred.

Left.

Right.

A small shift of the waist let one moonlight blade pass where his shoulder had been. He ducked under the second, feeling the edge of it brush cold pressure over his hair. His left hand lifted.

Ice condensed.

Not large.

Large would be slow.

An ice awl formed between his fingers, narrow, hard, and aimed at a point he had to trust before the arrow arrived.

He threw.

Ding.

The sound rang above Qian Lei.

The guided arrow jolted aside and struck the floor, its yellow light scattering into fragments.

Qian Lei's eyes snapped open.

Lan landed between him and Liang Zheng's team.

For one breath, the arena held all possible futures.

Liu Feng stood beyond the ivy, spear ready but path compromised. Chen Yuelin's vines had made the floor hostile. Wang Lijian was already preparing the next shot. Liang Zheng advanced with half-moon blades separated, shield no longer needed now that the first wave had passed.

Lan counted.

Qian Lei could not summon now.

If forced, perhaps he could tear open the Gate, but what would come through? A weak beast? A dangerous beast? Nothing? Something that attacked anyone? Even if the summon succeeded, the disturbance might ruin his recovery for the matches still to come.

Liu Feng was fast, but control vines loved fast opponents who had to cross predictable paths.

Lan could fight.

Lan could protect.

Lan could not be in three places forever.

Four wins.

One more needed.

But not necessarily this one.

A commander who spent the last arrow because the target was in front of him deserved to lose the battle behind it.

Lan raised his voice.

"Wait."

Liang Zheng's blades paused, not lowering, not relaxing.

Lan did not hesitate.

"We admit defeat."

Qian Lei's mouth opened.

Liu Feng turned sharply.

Liang Zheng looked at Lan for half a heartbeat.

Then the tension in his shoulders eased. He brought the half-moon blades down and nodded once.

No taunt.

No laughter.

That made the defeat cleaner.

Lan exhaled.

He did not feel humiliated.

He felt the size of the price he had refused to pay.

Qian Lei finally found his voice. "We could still..."

"Maybe," Lan said. "And maybe you would not recover for the next one."

Qian stopped.

Liu Feng's spear lowered by a fraction.

Lan kept his eyes on Liang Zheng. "Good counter."

Liang's answer was as calm as the rest of him. "Good reason to concede."

The arena registered the result.

White light did not swallow Dorm333 this time like a sudden defeat in combat. Instead, the system's calm announcement marked what Lan had already chosen.

Fifth match: Liang Zheng's team victory.

Dorm333: concession.

The word looked strange.

Lan let it stand.

---

In the observation room, several teachers breathed out at once.

It was not the sound adults liked to make when children lost.

It was relief.

Mu Zhongtian leaned back slowly, then realized he had been gripping the edge of the console. He loosened his hand.

"He admitted defeat," one teacher said, as if the words needed to be checked against reality.

Ji Hongbin's severe face did not soften, but something in his eyes approved. "Correct."

Yin Tianfan laughed once under his breath.

Mu looked at him. "You are pleased?"

"Of course," Yin said. "A child who cannot admit defeat should not touch a command console."

Ji gave him a sideways glance. "You will turn anything into a warship lesson."

"And you will turn anything into a combat lesson. Today both of us are right." Yin pointed at the screen. "He saw the resource problem. Qian Lei is the strategic reserve now, not baggage. Forcing the fight would risk the next matches."

Mu nodded despite himself.

That was the change the class had not yet fully understood. Qian Lei had entered the term as the unreliable summoner people used for jokes. Four matches had turned him into the center of the question: if he recovered, Dorm333 might produce another monster; if he was interrupted, Dorm333's shape changed at once.

Liang Zheng had seen enough from public information.

Lan Xuanyu had seen enough from the first exchange.

Both decisions were good.

That was why the match had lasted so little and mattered so much.

Ji Hongbin replayed the instant Liang's team retreated from the entry light. "The released video worked. They adapted."

"And did not need private footage," Mu said.

"Good," Ji said. "Adaptability is the point. We are not training children to be slaughtered by a rule loophole."

Another teacher glanced toward the waiting-hall screen. "This should help morale."

No one had to ask what he meant.

If Dorm333 had taken five consecutive wins immediately after the fourth-match video, the rest of the Elite Junior Class would have felt the gap like a wall dropping through the room. Four wins already hurt. Five would have made some students wonder whether the exam existed only to prove they were decorations around miracles.

Children could survive losing.

They struggled more with becoming irrelevant.

Mu watched the waiting hall. Several faces lifted toward the board. Some looked relieved. Some looked more afraid because now there was a method, and methods could be blamed on oneself if they failed.

His gaze passed over Dorm316, where Lu Qianxun stood too still and Chang Jianyi looked like he was rewriting several plans at once.

Then it passed over Dorm336.

Yan Shuo stood with Song Yichen and Luo Haoran, neat collar, calm eyes, public boy identity intact. Rank23. Deep yellow and deep purple. Second place in the Maze. A team strong enough that other students kept glancing at it even while Dorm333 filled the screens.

The class was no longer simple.

Mu Zhongtian was tired enough to miss simple things.

He was also teacher enough to know simple things did not make better students.

"Continue recording," he said. "No additional footage unless needed. Results only."

Ji nodded.

Yin Tianfan folded his arms. "Now we see who learned from a concession."

---

The board changed before the waiting hall heard the full announcement.

Dorm333's four bright victory marks remained.

A new line appeared beneath them.

Fifth match: Liang Zheng, Wang Lijian, Chen Yuelin — victory.

Dorm333 conceded.

For a moment, no one spoke.

The word did not fit the shape fear had made.

Conceded.

Not crushed.

Not eliminated.

Not exposed as frauds.

Conceded.

Then the sound came back all at once.

"They lost?"

"Liang Zheng beat them?"

"It says conceded."

"That still counts!"

"Four wins and one loss..."

"Does this mean the video works?"

"Does this mean they are tired?"

"Does this mean Qian Lei cannot summon?"

The last question made several students lower their voices as soon as they said it. Even now, no one wanted to speak too confidently about Qian Lei. The old joke had teeth marks on it.

Luo Haoran stared at the board and let out a breath that sounded personally offended.

"I hate useful information," he said. "It makes me responsible for thinking."

Song's gaze stayed on the concession line. "Liang survived the first move."

"Probably," Yan said.

Luo turned to him. "Probably?"

"We did not see it."

"The board says he won."

"The board says Dorm333 conceded," Yan corrected. "It does not say how much Liang forced and how much Lan chose."

That quieted the immediate circle around them.

Yan did not mean to make a speech, but the distinction mattered too much to leave sloppy.

If Dorm333 had been overwhelmed, that meant one thing.

If Lan Xuanyu had chosen to spend a loss instead of spending Qian Lei, that meant something else.

And Yan knew Lan well enough to distrust easy interpretations.

She knew the boy who had once tried to explain Teacher Nana's catching game with more seriousness than most children used for homework. She knew the boy who had promised to say what he could and then kept boundaries not because he enjoyed distance, but because some doors had adults, teammates, danger, and confusion behind them. She knew Lan's face when he felt ashamed. She knew Lan's face when he had calculated too far ahead to explain it quickly.

A concession did not sound like shame.

It sounded like arithmetic with pain in it.

Song said, "If he chose it, he may be saving Qian Lei."

"Or himself," Luo said, then held up both hands when Yan glanced at him. "Not an insult. If I were him, I would save myself often. I am precious."

"Qian Lei was sitting in the fourth video," Yan said. "The first three victories happened before that. If Liang's team had any long-range way to disturb him, forcing Lan to protect him would break the first-move attack."

Luo's face changed.

"That is exactly what you told us to worry about," he said.

"I told us not to give Liu Feng a straight sweep. Liang's team may have done that. The rest follows from public facts and knowing classmates' martial souls."

A student from another team looked over. "Then what are we supposed to do if even Liang only made them concede?"

Yan looked at him.

The boy regretted asking immediately, but Yan answered anyway.

"Do not make yourself easy."

"That is not a complete plan."

"It is the beginning of every plan."

Song's mouth twitched. Luo looked betrayed that Song had found someone else's dry answer amusing.

Across the hall, Lu Qianxun's expression had sharpened into something cleaner than anger. A loss by Dorm333 did not restore his old world. It made the next layer of the problem visible. Chang Jianyi was already talking to him, one hand making small controlled motions that probably marked distance, angle, and timing. Ye Lingtong listened with a complicated face.

They, too, understood that a concession was not the same as weakness.

Public consequences moved through the room like wind over grass.

Dorm333 was answerable.

Dorm333 was still dangerous.

Liang Zheng's team had become proof that preparation mattered.

Dorm336 remained untested.

Lu Qianxun remained untested.

Fear did not disappear.

It became organized.

Yan preferred organized fear. It could be trained.

Luo leaned close again. "If our turn comes soon, do we copy Liang?"

"No," Yan said.

"Wonderful. I enjoy not copying successful people. Very original."

"We learn from him," Yan said. "Copying gives Lan the second answer. Learning gives us our own."

Song nodded. "Our front is different."

"Our speed is different," Luo added, then frowned. "That sounded too honest. Forget I said it."

"Our risk is different," Yan finished.

His hand touched his collar once, a small public gesture like straightening cloth. Beneath it, Yan Shuo'er felt the pressure of her hidden frame, the Earth/Metal steadiness waiting under fire-light, the deep-purple second ring that could make the room stare if her turn came.

No reveal.

No full wings.

No pretending she was smaller than she had become.

If Dorm336 entered, they would not be Liang Zheng's team. Song was not Liang's shield. Luo was not Wang's bow. Yan was not Chen's vines.

Their answer had to be theirs.

The board held its new line.

Dorm333: four victories, one concession.

Yan looked at those words and thought, not for the first time, that Lan Xuanyu was more dangerous when he refused to be proud.

---

Inside the arena, Qian Lei was still frowning at Lan.

"I had at least one heroic complaint prepared," he said.

"Use it later," Lan answered.

"Complaints expire if not spoken fresh."

"Then write it down."

Liu Feng came back through the last fading strands of green ivy, spear lowered. He did not look happy, but he did not look ashamed either.

"I could not cross cleanly," he said.

"I saw."

"If you had kept attacking, maybe we could have split them."

"Maybe," Lan said.

That word did more work than a longer explanation.

Maybe they could have forced Liang back.

Maybe Liu Feng could have cut past Chen's vines before the web finished forming.

Maybe Lan could have protected Qian and dodged Liang and stop Wang's second arrow and still create an opening.

Maybe Qian could have summoned one more beast and won everything.

Maybe the beast would be useless.

Maybe it would be hungry.

Maybe winning this match would cost the match that mattered more.

Liu Feng looked at Qian Lei, then at Lan. His fingers tightened once on the spear shaft before relaxing.

"You were right," he said.

Qian Lei sighed. "I dislike being the reason sensible people are right."

Lan crouched in front of him. "How much did the arrow disturb you?"

Qian Lei's expression turned serious again. "Not much. You hit it before it hit me. But when it came close, my Spiritual Power shook. If it had landed... I do not know."

"Then recover."

"Cruel, evasive, and correct," Qian muttered, closing his eyes again.

Lan stood.

On the opposite side, Liang Zheng's team had already faded from the arena after receiving the victory. There was no celebration left behind, only the echo of a good answer.

Lan respected that.

He also filed it away.

The released video had changed the exam. The next teams would not politely stand where Dorm333 wanted them. They would aim at Qian. They would control Liu Feng's path. They would make Lan choose between force and protection until he chose wrong.

Unless he chose first.

Four wins.

One concession.

The test was still alive.

Lan looked toward the empty side of the arena and smiled faintly.

Not because losing pleased him.

Because the shape of the fight had become honest.

Somewhere outside, Yan Shuo had probably seen the result by now. Lan did not know what Yan would think. He suspected Yan would not make the easy mistake of treating concession as collapse.

That suspicion warmed something small in him, almost like relief.

Best friends did not need all the secrets to recognize a chosen line.

They only needed enough truth to respect the silence around it.

Lan lifted his right hand once, then let it fall.

"Next one," Liu Feng said.

Lan nodded.

"Next one."

The arena light began to gather again.

This time, no one expected the answer to be simple.
