# Chapter 31: The Match the Board Owed

The academy learned something uncomfortable before the children did.

A result could be true and still be incomplete.

Dorm333 had beaten Lu Qianxun's team. No teacher in the observation room could deny that. The arena system had recorded the elimination. Lan Xuanyu had entered the space alone, swallowed Lu Qianxun, Ye Lingtong, Chang Jianyi, and himself in that impossible color, and left Liu Feng and Qian Lei standing on the field as the only remaining members of the defending team.

Victory.

Fifth match.

Forced pressure survived.

All true.

Mu Zhongtian kept looking at the second line on the roster anyway.

Dorm336.

Yan Shuo. Song Yichen. Luo Haoran.

A line the teachers had moved past but had not defeated.

The observation room no longer had the stunned silence from the moment Lan's light vanished. That first shock had been noisy in its own way: held breath, tightened fingers, teachers exchanging looks, Yin Tianfan's neck still carrying the memory of Ji Hongbin's hand. Now the room had the heavier quiet that came after adults realized children would remember how they were treated.

Mu finally said, "If the report says Dorm333 is simply first overall and the rest is closed, it will be false."

Yin Tianfan leaned back in his chair and made a face as if honesty had a smell. "It will be administratively convenient."

"That is not the same thing."

"It often becomes the same thing if everyone signs quickly."

Ji Hongbin looked at him.

Yin sighed. "Fine. Not quickly."

Mu did not smile. He had already replayed the waiting-hall feed twice. He had seen Dorm336's transfer light start and stop. He had seen Yan Shuo stand when the board briefly returned to his team's name. He had seen Song Yichen rise without theatrics and Luo Haoran stop joking for almost a full breath.

Those small things mattered.

Not because children deserved every answer they wanted.

Because the academy had created this answer by changing the question.

"Dorm333 cannot fight them right now," one teacher said. "Lan Xuanyu is empty. Qian Lei's Spiritual Power was not recovered even before the Lu match. Liu Feng spent heavily."

"Correct," Mu said. "Sending Dorm336 in now would be a punishment disguised as fairness."

Another teacher looked at the roster. "But if we do nothing, Dorm336 is punished instead."

No one argued.

That was the problem.

Yan Shuo's public file was not decorative. Rank23. Spiritual Power 156. Two rings. A first ring whose public paperwork still said one thing while its response had long stopped behaving like a shallow one-hundred-year ornament. A deep-purple second ring from a heavy bird soul spirit that had become something more under perfect absorption. Fire Phoenix pressure that had to be judged by body, ring, soul spirit, team role, and actual stage, not by a single formal number.

Song Yichen and Luo Haoran were not decorative either.

Dorm336 had earned Maze second place because they were a team, not because Yan's name was bright enough to hide empty shadows.

Ji Hongbin tapped the table once.

"Minimum recovery," he said. "Then direct assessment."

Yin's eyebrow lifted. "Against Dorm333?"

"Against Dorm333."

"And the thing he used?"

Ji's eyes narrowed.

They all knew which child he meant. They all knew which thing. No one in the room had a clean name for it yet, and that was part of the danger.

"Prohibited for this assessment," Ji said. "It is unclassified, all-energy consuming, and medically unsafe. It is not a normal repeatable combat tool."

Yin snorted. "You are saying my disciple's best move cannot be used in his match."

"I am saying a child is not allowed to tear himself empty so adults can settle a ranking argument."

For once, Yin Tianfan did not answer immediately.

Mu looked from one to the other. "Then the assessment is restricted direct combat. Recorded separately from Dorm333's forced-pressure victory."

"Say it clearly," Ji said. "Dorm333 keeps the pressure-stage result. Dorm336 receives the field it was owed. The two records measure different things."

"The students will complain anyway," a teacher murmured.

Yin waved a hand. "Students complain when breakfast is too soft. That is not a reason to stop serving it."

Mu looked at him.

"What?" Yin asked.

"That was almost responsible."

"Retract that insult."

Ji ignored them both. "Two days. Lan Xuanyu's team receives medical and Spiritual Power recovery. Qian Lei must be able to summon safely. Liu Feng must pass a movement check. Dorm333 may use ordinary registered skills, command, support, and summoning. The unclassified all-energy technique is prohibited. Dorm336 may use its registered two-ring combat state. Lethal or irreversible attacks remain forbidden as usual."

Mu wrote it down.

"And the result?"

Ji's gaze returned to the screen where Lan's color had once frozen the room.

"The result will tell us what it tells us," he said. "Not more. Not less."

That, Mu thought, might be the most honest thing said in the room all day.

---

The announcement reached the Elite Junior Class the next morning.

Not during breakfast. Mu Zhongtian was not cruel enough to put that much tension into bowls and chopsticks.

He waited until the class assembled, then stood in front of them with the same expression he used for ordinary lectures. That fooled no one. Ordinary lectures did not make Lu Qianxun sit like a drawn blade. Ordinary lectures did not make Qian Lei look awake before being threatened. Ordinary lectures did not make every student glance between Dorm333 and Dorm336 as if the air itself had become a scoreboard.

Mu said, "Yesterday's actual-combat record has been reviewed. Dorm333's victory over Lu Qianxun's team is valid. The forced-pressure match stands."

A ripple went through the room.

Qian Lei straightened.

Liu Feng stepped on his foot under the desk.

Qian Lei's heroic posture collapsed silently into offended restraint.

Mu continued, "However, Dorm336 was the remaining qualified challenger whose match was interrupted by the order change. Dorm336 was not defeated. Their challenge right remains valid."

This time the room did not ripple.

It cracked into whispers.

"So they are fighting?"

"Dorm333 again?"

"But Lan Xuanyu just used that thing."

"Can Yan Shuo beat them?"

"If the strange light is banned, maybe."

"If it is not banned, who can stand there?"

Mu let the whispers show themselves before cutting them off.

"A restricted direct-combat assessment will take place after minimum recovery. The unclassified high-cost technique Lan Xuanyu used yesterday is prohibited for this assessment. This is not a punishment. It is a safety and category rule. The assessment will measure repeatable team combat under academy conditions. Dorm333's forced-pressure achievement remains separately recorded."

The whispers changed flavor.

Some students looked relieved. Some disappointed. Some confused. Some immediately began building arguments that would let their preferred team win in their own heads before the match happened.

Yan Shuo sat still.

Publicly, he was a boy with a neat collar, a calm face, and hands folded on the desk.

Privately, Yan Shuo'er felt two different lives answer inside her.

Dawnflame Kite stirred bright and hot, 480 years of private living maturity pressing against the first ring like a bird against the sky it had not reached yet. Dawn-Iron Phoenix Roc remained heavier and lower, purple structure resting under the spine, under the ribs, under the places that would one day have to bear more than a child's shoulders could admit.

Not awakened.

Not full.

Not yet.

But not small.

Never small.

Song Yichen leaned slightly closer. "Two days."

"Enough to recover," Yan said.

Luo Haoran whispered from Yan's other side, "Enough for Qian Lei to think of twelve speeches and for me to dislike eleven of them."

"Only eleven?" Song asked.

"I am being generous because of historical pressure."

Yan did not smile, but some of the tightness left his jaw.

Across the room, Lan Xuanyu looked at him.

There were too many eyes between them for a long conversation. There were also too many secrets.

Lan gave a small nod.

Yan returned it.

It was not apology.

It was not challenge.

It was both children agreeing that the field had finally been placed where words could no longer carry it alone.

---

Lan Xuanyu found him beside the recovery corridor after afternoon drills.

That, too, was a choice.

Lan could have stayed with Dorm333. Yan could have stayed with Dorm336. They could have let the teachers' announcement become the only bridge between them until the match. Children often did that when friendship became inconvenient.

They were not those children.

Lan stopped beside the wall where sunlight slid down the protective glass in long pale strips. He looked much better than he had after the Lu match. Better did not mean fully recovered. His eyes still carried the careful steadiness of someone holding pieces together because adults expected him to.

Yan waited for him to speak first.

"Teacher Ji banned it," Lan said.

No name.

No explanation.

Enough.

"Good," Yan said.

Lan blinked. "You say that very quickly."

"If using something makes you look like yesterday, it should not decide a normal assessment."

Lan looked down at his hands.

For one moment, Yan saw the child under the cleverness: the boy who wanted to win, wanted to understand himself, wanted to protect his team, wanted adults to stop placing him into corners where the only exit had teeth.

"It is not that I want to hide behind it," Lan said softly.

"I know."

"And it is not that I think Dorm336 should lose because it cannot be used."

"I know that too."

Lan looked up.

Yan's voice stayed even. "If we win, we win the restricted direct-combat assessment. We do not erase your forced-pressure victory. If you win, you win without tearing yourself open. Either result is cleaner than yesterday."

Lan let out a breath that might have become a laugh if he had more energy to spare.

"Teacher Ji said something similar, but less kind."

"Teacher Ji uses kindness like rare medicine."

"And Teacher Yin uses suffering like seasoning."

"Then recover before they invent a recipe."

This time Lan did laugh, quiet and short.

The silence after it was not awkward.

It carried old housing corridors, school mornings, shared warnings, public masks, private worry, and the strange burden of being children who had become too interesting to adults.

Lan said, "I will not ask what you can do now."

Yan gave him a sideways look. "That makes one of us."

"You will ask yourself?"

"My body already is."

Lan understood that more than most would. Perhaps too much.

"Then I will see it on the field," he said.

"What I can show there," Yan corrected.

Lan's eyes sharpened, then softened.

He knew about shown things and unshown things.

"What we can show," he said.

Yan accepted the correction.

They stood a moment longer.

Then Liu Feng called from the far end of the corridor, and Luo's voice answered from somewhere it should not have been able to reach, accusing Dorm333 of trying to steal pre-match atmospheric advantage by using dramatic hallway spacing.

Qian Lei immediately began defending the artistic rights of exhausted geniuses.

The moment broke before it became too heavy.

That was probably mercy.

---

Two days did not make Dorm333 whole.

It made them safe enough.

That distinction was written into the assessment record before the first ring rose.

The training arena used for the deferred match was smaller than the final-exam battlefield but denser in protection. The floor panels had been reinforced. Medical staff stood at both exits. Mu Zhongtian handled the main control platform. Ji Hongbin stood with his arms folded, eyes cold enough to make every student sit straighter. Yin Tianfan had appeared despite claiming he had better things to do; no one believed him.

The whole Elite Junior Class watched.

They had to.

If the problem had been public, the answer could not be hidden entirely in a file.

Mu's voice carried across the arena.

"Deferred direct-combat assessment. Dorm333 versus Dorm336. Assessment category: repeatable team combat under academy restrictions. Dorm333's unclassified high-cost technique from the Lu Qianxun match is prohibited. Any attempt to activate it will stop the match. Lethal, irreversible, or uncontrolled attacks are prohibited for both teams. Victory is determined by incapacitation, ring-out, surrender, or teacher stoppage."

Qian Lei raised his hand.

Mu looked at him.

"Can verbal brilliance count as incapacitation?" Qian asked.

"Only if it incapacitates you first," Liu Feng muttered.

Luo looked delighted. "Finally, a rule I understand."

Song did not move, but his shoulder shifted in the way that meant he was preparing to block either an attack or a conversation.

Yan stood at the center-left of Dorm336's line.

Across from him, Lan Xuanyu stood between Liu Feng and Qian Lei.

Dorm333 did not look weak.

That mattered.

Lan was pale, yes, but his eyes were clear. Liu Feng held the White Dragon Spear with a steadier grip than he had at the start of the semester. Qian Lei had his Summoning Money ready, and behind the exaggeration in his face was a sharpness that had survived too many failed summons to be useless anymore.

Dorm333 was dangerous.

Not because of yesterday's forbidden answer.

Because even without it, Lan could turn fragments into a formation.

Mu's hand lowered.

"Begin."

Qian Lei moved first.

That surprised several students enough that whispers flashed across the viewing seats. Qian Lei was not known for bravery unless the danger had already passed and could be narrated safely. But this was not bravery. It was preparation.

His coin spun.

A Gate of Summoning opened behind Dorm333, low and dark-gold at the rim.

Lan's silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass touched Qian's arm for only a breath. Not wrapped long enough for casual eyes to understand. Not explained. Enough to steady the door.

Yan did not chase the mystery.

He chased the consequence.

"Luo," he said.

Luo was already gone.

The summoned beast emerged with a heavy scrape of claw against arena floor. It was not the Emerald Demon Bird. The teachers had made certain of that. It was not the Crocodile Dragon either, but its bloodline leaned in that direction: a squat armored ground lizard with a broad skull, ridged back, and a mouth full of blunt crushing teeth. A hundred-year beast at least. Perhaps more. Stable. Heavy. Useful.

Qian Lei pointed dramatically. "Behold!"

The beast looked back at him and sneezed.

"Behold with respect!" Qian corrected.

Lan did not waste the opening. Golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass slid toward Liu Feng. At the same time, ice formed under the ground lizard's claws, not to freeze it but to shape its first charge. Dorm333's line became three parts: summoned weight in front, Liu Feng on the side, Lan behind both with water-ice control ready.

Song stepped forward.

His Stoneplate Tortoise pressure spread low and wide. He did not try to meet the lizard like a wall meeting a cart. That would be foolish. Instead, he angled his defense, making the center look open by half a step.

The lizard took the bait because it was a beast, not Lan.

It charged.

Song's first ring lit.

The impact struck the shield-line and slid.

Not harmlessly. Song's boots scraped back. His face tightened. But the charge did not go straight through Dorm336. It veered, forced into a diagonal path that gave Yan a line and Luo a shadow.

Liu Feng moved at that moment.

Fast.

Faster than the watching class expected even after all the exam footage and rumors. Lan's enhancement made his speed sharper, not merely greater. The White Dragon Spear came low, aiming not for Yan first but for Luo's likely route, because Dorm333 had learned that a hidden scout could be more dangerous than a visible fire.

Luo clicked his tongue from a place that was not quite where Liu aimed.

Threadwind Mantis did not beat White Dragon Spear in raw speed.

It did not need to.

Luo had watched Liu Feng for two days with the desperate seriousness of someone who made jokes so fear did not get the whole room. He knew Liu liked clean lines. He knew Lan's pushes made those lines cleaner. So Luo dirtied the line.

A thread of wind cut across the spear path at the wrong angle.

Not enough to stop Liu.

Enough to make his next foot land three fingers narrower than perfect.

Yan moved into that flaw.

Dawnfeather Mantle opened.

Copper-red fire and pale-gold light wrapped Yan's arms, shoulders, upper back, and chest. It was not purple. It was not awakened. It was not a new form pretending to be a miracle.

It was 480 years of living fire-light maturity under a first ring that had grown far beyond its paperwork but had not yet crossed the threshold that would demand a new name.

That was already enough to make the viewing seats go quiet.

The mantle did not flare outward wastefully. It hugged the body. Feather edges lit along the forearms, white-gold where the fire was thinnest and sharpest. Beneath it, Yan's second ring rose deep purple.

Skybone Phoenix Frame.

The change in Yan's stance was small.

The meaning was not.

Feet settled. Hips aligned. Spine took weight. Ribs opened for heat. Shoulders carried the folded pressure where wings were not yet allowed to be wings.

Yan met Liu Feng's spear with his left forearm edge.

Not a block.

A deflection.

The White Dragon Spear screamed along the feather-light boundary. Sparks tore sideways. Liu Feng's eyes widened, because the force did not simply resist him; it returned just enough of his own line to make his wrist correct in the middle of motion.

Lan's ice arrived under Yan's foot.

Good.

Yan had expected Lan to punish any planted stance.

Dawn-Iron Phoenix Roc answered from the second ring. The Skybone frame pulled the slipping force up through the ankle and hip rather than letting the ice steal the ground entirely. Yan slid half a step, turned with the slide, and drove Liu Feng away from Luo instead of chasing him.

"Middle," Yan said.

Song understood.

He released pressure for a breath.

The ground lizard, freed from the angled shield, lunged toward the apparent opening.

Lan's eyes changed.

He saw the trap and tried to recall it with Blue Silver Grass, but the lizard's momentum was already committed.

Yan's right hand opened.

Dawnflame did not explode.

It curved.

A crescent of fire-light swept low across the lizard's front legs, not cutting deep, not burning through, but striking the tendons of balance with heat, light, and timing. The beast stumbled.

Song stepped in and slammed the shield-line down.

The lizard hit the floor hard enough for the arena panels to hum.

Qian Lei made a wounded sound. "My glorious beast!"

"It is not eliminated," Lan said.

He was right.

The lizard rolled, tail sweeping. Song took the tail on the shield and grunted. Liu Feng used that same sweep as cover to re-enter from the side.

Dorm333 did not fall apart because the first exchange failed.

Lan changed the field.

Ice spread in broken patches, not a single sheet. Water beads gathered in the air like suspended rain. Liu Feng's path flickered between them, each step given a little push, each angle hiding the next. Qian Lei stayed back, one hand toward the lizard, coaxing the summoned beast to rise and block again.

Yan's eyes sharpened.

This was why Dorm333 mattered.

Lan did not need overwhelming power to make the floor feel crowded.

"Song, hold the beast. Luo, take the beads."

"Take the what?" Luo demanded from somewhere near the left wall.

The answer came when the suspended water beads turned to ice needles.

Luo swore and moved.

Threadwind cut through the air. Not every needle. Enough. The rest struck Song's shield, Yan's mantle, and empty floor where Lan had wanted feet to be.

One needle grazed Yan's cheek.

Cold bit skin.

Dawnflame answered with a thin hiss under the collarbone.

Private Yan Shuo'er felt the familiar old pressure of Lan's water and ice, the childhood nearness that had never been simple because neither of them was simple. The Fire Phoenix did not hate it. It refused to be smothered by it.

Yan stepped forward.

Lan stepped back.

That, more than any clash, told the class what had changed.

Lan Xuanyu rarely retreated without purpose.

This retreat had purpose.

He needed space.

Yan denied it.

Liu Feng cut across again, boosted by gold-patterned grass. This time he aimed at Yan's shoulder, the exact place where the mantle thickened and the Skybone frame had to carry folded wing-root pressure. A good choice. A cruel choice. Lan's choice.

Yan respected it.

Then he broke it.

The second ring's deep purple light thickened. Skybone Phoenix Frame aligned hard enough that the air around Yan's shoulders seemed to settle into invisible joints. He dropped under the spear line, left foot catching the ice rather than resisting it, and let the slide accelerate him.

Dawnfeather Mantle flared at the elbow.

Yan's elbow struck the spear shaft.

Liu Feng did not lose his weapon. He was too good for that now. But his line opened.

Luo appeared behind him.

Not to stab. Not to eliminate. To place a wind thread across Liu Feng's retreat.

For half a breath, Liu Feng had no clean exit.

Song used that breath to drive the ground lizard back with a shield slam that made Qian Lei yelp as if struck personally.

Yan reached Lan.

The viewing seats stopped whispering.

Lan's left hand lifted with silver-patterned grass and a curve of ice. His right hand still carried the golden-patterned grass linked toward Liu Feng. He had no time to bring both answers together properly. He could choose defense, enhancement, or risk.

For the smallest instant, Yan saw his fingers twitch toward each other.

Not enough for the forbidden technique.

Enough for Ji Hongbin's voice to crack across the arena like a blade.

"Lan Xuanyu."

Lan froze.

Yin Tianfan's hand had already moved toward the emergency stop.

Yan did not wait for adult machinery.

He stopped himself.

Dawnflame Mantle folded inward until the fire-light edge rested a finger-width from Lan's throat. The heat was controlled so precisely that a loose strand of Lan's hair lifted but did not singe. Yan's other hand hovered near Lan's chest, where any real strike would have driven him out of the match.

At the same time, Luo's wind thread held Liu Feng's retreat line without cutting, and Song's shield pinned the ground lizard's front claws to the floor.

Qian Lei raised both hands very slowly.

"For the record," he said, "I surrender before anyone decides my face is a legal target."

No one laughed.

Then Mu Zhongtian's voice announced, "Stop."

The arena released a breath.

Yan lowered his hands at once.

The mantle withdrew, but not completely. A faint copper-red warmth remained over his forearms as if the first ring disliked being dismissed before the body had fully calmed.

Lan looked at him.

For a moment, neither child spoke.

Then Lan smiled faintly.

"You reached me."

Yan's own breath came a little harder than he wanted. "You almost reached for it."

Lan's smile thinned.

"I know."

"Do not."

"I know that too."

It was not a victory sentence.

It was something heavier.

Yan stepped back.

Only then did the class remember how to make sound.

---

The official record took longer than the match.

That was often how adults proved they were adults.

Dorm336 had won the restricted direct-combat assessment. The arena record was clear: Yan reached Lan, Qian surrendered under pinned formation pressure, Liu Feng was controlled by Luo's interruption line, and the summoned beast was neutralized by Song's shield control. Dorm333 had not collapsed easily. They had forced Dorm336 to spend strength, adjust twice, and reveal serious Mantle-plus-Skybone coordination before the decisive moment.

That mattered too.

Mu Zhongtian wrote the categories on the board himself.

Not because children could not read a file.

Because if he did not say the categories aloud, the class would turn one result into a weapon against the other.

"Dorm333," he said, "retains the defending pressure-stage record from the forced Lu Qianxun match. Their ability to survive pressure, create unexpected tactical answers, and overturn disadvantage remains first-class."

Qian Lei nodded solemnly until Liu Feng elbowed him.

Mu continued, "Dorm336 wins the deferred restricted direct-combat assessment. Under repeatable academy combat conditions, with unclassified high-risk techniques prohibited, Dorm336 currently holds the top direct-combat challenger result."

The wording settled over the class like a complicated weather pattern.

Some students wanted a simpler answer. Children loved simple answers when simple answers placed someone else below them.

But no simple answer fit.

Lan Xuanyu could do things no one understood. Qian Lei's summon, when supported, could turn a useless reputation into a battlefield problem. Liu Feng's speed was no longer only running.

Yan Shuo could cross the field against all of that.

Song Yichen could make a beast's charge become someone else's angle.

Luo Haoran could make a fast spear step wrong by the width of three fingers and decide a match with it.

Lu Qianxun had lost to an impossible technique and now had to watch another team prove it had never been below him in the first place.

Liang Zheng watched from two rows back, expression calm and thoughtful. His team had beaten Dorm333 through concession logic before Dorm333's forced miracle. That result still mattered. It just belonged to a different shelf.

The class hierarchy did not become a ladder.

It became a map.

That was harder to brag about and more useful to learn from.

Ji Hongbin spoke next.

"A warning. Do not misunderstand today's match. A prohibited technique is not the same as a nonexistent technique. A restricted result is not the same as an absolute result. If any of you use this match to claim Dorm333 is weak, you are stupid. If any of you use yesterday's match to claim Dorm336 is irrelevant, you are also stupid. If you cannot hold two truths in your head, train until your head improves."

The room became extremely quiet.

Yin Tianfan muttered, "Inspirational as always."

Ji ignored him.

Yan stood with Dorm336 and let the words pass through him.

They were harsh.

They were fair.

Fair did not mean soft.

Soft things often broke first.

---

Lan approached after the class was dismissed.

Not immediately. Qian Lei needed to mourn his summoned beast's dignity. Liu Feng needed to ask Luo how he had read the second retreat angle, and Luo needed to pretend he had done it entirely through genius rather than two days of obsessive observation. Song stood nearby, silent, but his eyes were warm in the way his face rarely admitted.

Yan waited near the edge of the arena, where the protective light still hummed faintly through the floor.

Lan stopped beside him.

"Congratulations," he said.

"For a restricted result," Yan said.

"For reaching me without making it cheap."

That was a better sentence than congratulations.

Yan accepted it with a nod.

"Your formation changed faster than the records suggested," Yan said. "Liu Feng's second line was dangerous. The ice beads too."

Lan's eyes brightened despite the exhaustion. "You noticed the beads?"

"Luo complained at them before they moved. It helped."

"I did not complain," Luo called from across the arena. "I issued an atmospheric objection."

Qian Lei pointed at him. "That is a complaint wearing formal clothes."

"Better dressed than yours."

Liu Feng looked between them and sighed as if the match had been less tiring.

Lan shook his head, smiling despite himself.

Then the smile faded a little.

"Thank you for stopping," he said quietly.

Yan did not pretend not to understand.

"Teacher Ji would have stopped you."

"Maybe."

"Teacher Yin would have pressed the emergency stop."

"Maybe."

"I was closer."

Lan looked at him for a long moment.

There were secrets between them, but not a wall. Walls did not let warmth through. This was a door neither child had the key to yet, and both had learned not to kick it open.

"One day," Lan said, "we should fight without adults making the rules weird."

Yan looked back toward the center of the arena.

"One day, we should fight when both teams know what the rules mean before the match starts."

Lan laughed under his breath. "That would be new."

"It would be efficient."

"You and Teacher Ji should never plan vacations."

"Recover first. Complain after."

"That sounds like you stole Teacher Ji's medicine cabinet."

Yan almost smiled.

Almost was enough.

---

That night, Dorm336 did not celebrate loudly.

Luo wanted to. Song vetoed it by placing one hand on his shoulder and saying nothing. Yan vetoed it by sitting down in the practice room and beginning recovery breathing. Between the two of them, Luo declared the leadership structure oppressive and joined them on the floor.

For a while, there was only breath.

The match replayed in Yan's body more clearly than any screen could show.

Liu Feng's spear line against the forearm mantle.

Lan's ice underfoot.

The ground lizard's weight striking Song's shield.

Luo's threadwind pulling one step out of shape.

The instant at Lan's throat when winning and hurting were separated only by control.

Dawnflame Kite pulsed inside the first ring, hot and restless. It had wanted more when the mantle folded near Lan. It had wanted the clean line, the bright line, the answer that ended resistance.

Yan did not scold it.

A soul spirit was not a dead tool. It wanted according to its nature. Dawnflame was a fire-light bird with a Phoenix seed asleep inside, not a polite classroom ornament. Wanting to rise, strike, guard, and burn did not make it wrong.

It made discipline necessary.

Dawn-Iron Phoenix Roc settled beneath it, weight through bone and breath.

Feather over bone.

Fire-light over frame.

Want over rule.

Rule over harm.

That was the lesson.

Song opened his eyes. "Your left arm?"

Yan looked down.

A faint red mark crossed the forearm where Liu Feng's spear had scraped along the mantle. It would fade by morning.

"Fine."

Luo leaned sideways to see. "That is not fine. That is a souvenir from a fast person."

"It is skin."

"Skin is famously where pain lives."

Song said, "Luo."

"I am expressing medical concern."

"Quietly."

Luo lowered his voice at once. "I am expressing quiet medical concern."

Yan let their words settle around him.

This, too, was growth.

Not a new ring. Not a new number. Not a soul spirit awakening that the future had not earned yet.

A team learning the difference between standing behind Yan and standing with Yan.

A class learning that results could belong to categories without becoming lies.

A fire-bird soul spirit learning that restraint was not fear.

A public boy mask learning to carry victory without exposing the girl beneath it.

Yan breathed in.

Dawnflame answered.

Dawn-Iron held.

Outside the practice room, the academy corridors slowly quieted. Somewhere else, Lan Xuanyu would be pulled between Ji Hongbin's harsh combat lessons and Yin Tianfan's strange abyss of training. Somewhere else, adults would write files careful enough to hide what they feared and clear enough to teach what they could not hide.

Time would move soon.

Not yet.

Tonight, the field owed to Dorm336 had finally been stepped on.

Not fully paid.

Not forever settled.

But no longer erased.

Yan lowered his stance by a finger's width.

The floor remembered.

This time, it remembered a match.
