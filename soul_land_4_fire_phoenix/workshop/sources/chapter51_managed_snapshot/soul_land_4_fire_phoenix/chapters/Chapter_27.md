# Chapter 27: The Door That Chose Twice

The sound beyond the Gate of Summoning became a weight.

It was not loud at first.

It rolled.

Qian Lei's face changed before anyone appeared on the other side of the arena. His earlier wild hope remained, but it was pressed flat by the sudden realization that hope could be heavy enough to crush him.

Soul Power poured out of him.

Spiritual Power followed.

The yellow ring at his feet trembled. The Summoning Money shone until its edges blurred. The gate in front of him no longer looked like an old door waiting to be opened; it looked like something had grabbed the other side and was pulling the world closer.

"Qian Lei," Liu Feng said sharply.

"I know," Qian Lei gasped. "I know. I am not doing it on purpose."

Lan Xuanyu's right hand tightened around the gate frame.

Golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass wrapped across his fingers and wrist. The patterns were hidden from the other side of the arena by the glare of the Gate, but the force running through them was not hidden from him. Something beyond the opening had answered him. Not Qian Lei alone. Him.

That had happened before.

It still made the back of his neck tighten.

Across the circular platform, light gathered into three figures.

Their first challengers appeared.

All three were girls.

The one in the middle stood tallest. Zhang Ruiyan's eyes were narrow and bright, the kind of eyes that made even silence feel like she was counting something. On her left, Shu Xiangyi crouched lightly, petite enough to seem harmless until the body remembered that small blades were also blades. On Zhang Ruiyan's right, Tong Mengru held herself like a hammer waiting for permission.

Then the hammer appeared in her hand.

It was heavy enough that Qian Lei, even while being emptied by his own summoning, found room in his heart for fresh fear.

"Why," he whispered, "does everyone in our class look more reliable than me?"

"Because you are busy summoning reliable things," Lan said.

"That is not comfort."

"It is strategy."

The three girls moved.

Zhang Ruiyan's yellow ring rose first. Light gathered above her head into a bright sphere. It was not sunlight, but it had the clean sharpness of light that had decided to become a weapon. A thin arrow flashed out toward Liu Feng.

Liu Feng shifted.

Lan moved before the arrow reached him.

A layer of ice rose and curved, not to block the light with stubborn thickness but to turn it aside. The arrow struck, slid, and broke into sparks that scattered harmlessly across the arena floor.

At the same time, Shu Xiangyi vanished from where Lan's eyes expected her to be.

Not truly vanished.

That was the dangerous part.

Her body flickered at the edge of sight, not fast in Liu Feng's straight-line way, but uncertain. One heartbeat she was low beside Zhang Ruiyan's left. The next she was farther out, body bent, feet silent, moving along the flank toward Qian Lei.

Illusion Cat.

Lan recognized the martial soul from class lessons. In early stages it did not look as domineering as Golden Lion King or as direct as a heavy hammer, but concealment did not need to roar to be deadly. If Shu Xiangyi reached Qian Lei now, the Gate would be interrupted at the worst possible time.

Liu Feng wanted to go.

Lan could feel it in the angle of the White Dragon Spear. Liu Feng's entire body pointed toward the flank like a line pulled tight.

But Tong Mengru had already charged.

The sledgehammer rose.

The arena seemed to become smaller.

Lan's right hand could not leave the Gate.

Qian Lei had no Soul Power left to protect himself.

The growl sounded again.

This time, it came through.

Shu Xiangyi's claw descended toward Qian Lei's back.

It struck dark-green scale.

The sound was not flesh meeting flesh. It was fingernails striking a metal door.

Shu Xiangyi's face twisted in pain. Even inside the simulator, where pain was lowered enough to protect children from true injury, the impact was honest. She tried to retreat.

Something enormous moved beside her.

A head larger than her entire body swung through the space she needed.

Bang.

Shu Xiangyi became light and vanished.

For a breath, no one moved.

Then the thing that had crawled out of the Gate raised itself fully.

It was a reptilian beast covered in dark green scales, huge-bodied and low with a head that looked as if it had been built to bite through gates rather than pass through them. Its limbs struck the arena floor with thick, confident weight. The mouth alone seemed large enough to end arguments.

A Crocodile Dragon.

Not a true dragon. Not one of the legends children whispered about until the word dragon became larger than sense. But a ground dragon with true dragon blood somewhere in its body, and at their age, somewhere was enough.

Qian Lei stared upward.

His knees nearly forgot they belonged to him.

"Mine?" he asked weakly.

The Crocodile Dragon lowered its head.

Lan Xuanyu did not step back.

The huge beast brought its scaled snout down to his feet and rubbed against him with a carefulness so absurd that even Liu Feng's eyes widened.

Across the arena, Zhang Ruiyan and Tong Mengru froze.

Qian Lei's mouth opened.

Closed.

Opened again.

"I take it back," he said hoarsely. "I am extremely reliable."

Lan's ears warmed, but he did not waste time being embarrassed by a summoned beast treating him like something familiar. This was not the first time Qian Lei's Gate had made the impossible act intimate around him. Surprise could wait. The match would not.

"Attack," Lan said.

The Crocodile Dragon raised its head.

The arena changed.

Before, Dorm333 had been three children under pressure. Now the air around them seemed to crouch under the weight of a seven-meter monster deciding to listen.

Zhang Ruiyan recovered first.

"Eyes!" she shouted.

The light sphere above her brightened. Two arrows shot toward the Crocodile Dragon's face, clean and fast.

Tong Mengru roared and charged straight in.

Her yellow ring flared. Her body snapped forward three meters with sudden explosive force, and the sledgehammer came down with both hands. She did not hesitate because a huge beast had appeared. She did not flee because Shu Xiangyi had vanished in one blow. Her fear, if it existed, had been put behind the hammer.

That courage deserved respect.

It did not change the difference.

The Crocodile Dragon stopped so suddenly that its momentum seemed to fold into the ground. Then its body twisted. Tong Mengru's hammer passed through the space where the beast's head had been and smashed into the arena floor.

The two light arrows struck dark-green scales.

They broke without leaving a mark.

The Crocodile Dragon's tail swept around.

Tong Mengru dragged the sledgehammer up in front of herself. The block was correct. Her timing was good. Her stance was heavy for a child her age.

The tail still took her off the ground.

She flew, struck the arena surface, rolled, and forced herself back onto one knee.

Zhang Ruiyan's face tightened.

She sent arrow after arrow, each aimed at angle, joint, eye, mouth. None of them were careless. None of them were childish panic. The Crocodile Dragon simply carried them through its scales as if light had become rain.

It lunged.

Zhang Ruiyan vanished in white simulation light.

Tong Mengru was the last one left.

She did not surrender.

Lan noticed that.

Even after her leader disappeared, even after Shu Xiangyi had been taken out before the fight truly opened, even with the Crocodile Dragon turning toward her, Tong Mengru lifted the sledgehammer again. Her hands shook. Her breathing was rough. But the hammer rose.

Qian Lei's laughter died in his throat.

Liu Feng lowered his spear slightly.

The Crocodile Dragon charged.

Tong Mengru met it.

For several seconds, the arena belonged to the sound of hammer against scale, feet grinding against the floor, and a child refusing to admit that a difference in strength was the same as permission to give up.

Then the Crocodile Dragon's head slammed down from an angle her hammer could not cover.

Tong Mengru disappeared.

The first match ended.

Light fell over the arena.

The Crocodile Dragon turned its huge head toward Lan once more. For an instant, its fierce eyes softened with the same unreasonable familiarity.

Then the Gate of Summoning dissolved.

The beast dissolved with it.

Qian Lei collapsed backward into a seated position and laughed once, very softly.

"We won," he said.

Lan looked at the empty space where the Crocodile Dragon had stood.

"One match," he said.

Liu Feng tightened his grip on the White Dragon Spear.

The words did not make the victory smaller.

They made the remaining eight larger.

---

In the simulator management room, Mu Zhongtian stared at the observation screen for a moment longer than a teacher should need.

Beside him, the invigilating teachers were quiet.

The Elite Junior Class had already taught them not to underestimate Dorm333. Four plus signs were not decorations. The Maze had not been luck. Lan Xuanyu's SP200 threshold that morning had not been a pretty number written for praise.

Even so, watching a Crocodile Dragon crawl out of Qian Lei's Gate, eliminate Shu Xiangyi instantly, then lower its head to Lan Xuanyu's feet like an overlarge household pet was a separate category of educational difficulty.

One teacher finally said, "That was not normal summoning affinity."

Mu Zhongtian did not answer at once.

Normal had become a weak word around Lan Xuanyu.

"Record it," he said instead. "Do not release match details to the waiting teams. Only match order and result."

"Understood."

Fairness mattered.

The challengers were already facing Dorm333 one after another. If the later teams saw every method in advance while Dorm333 had no equivalent chance to study them, the test would become scouting punishment instead of evaluation. The teachers would review the footage. The children outside would know only that Dorm333 had won.

Mu's gaze returned to the screen as the simulator restored Dorm333's Soul Power.

Soul Power could be returned cleanly.

Spiritual Power could not.

Qian Lei had summoned something far above ordinary expectation, and the price was written in the way he sat down the moment light touched the arena floor.

"Next match," Mu said.

A teacher hesitated. "So quickly?"

"This is the test."

No one argued.

---

The waiting hall did not show the battle.

That made waiting worse.

If the other students could have watched Dorm333 fight, they would at least have known what shape their fear should take. Instead, they had a result board, a row of inactive simulation pods, and their own imaginations.

Dorm304's names lit up.

Then dimmed.

Dorm333 victory.

That was all.

Luo Haoran leaned forward until Song Yichen placed one hand on the back of his collar and pulled him upright.

"The board will not confess if you glare at it," Song said.

"It might," Luo said. "Some boards are weak-hearted."

Yan Shuo stood with them in the Dorm336 line, public posture straight, face calm, collar clasp dull against his throat.

Inside, Yan Shuo'er felt the weight between the blank result and the story hidden behind it.

Dorm333 had won quickly.

That was not unexpected.

It still mattered.

The first match after a target was placed on a team's back was not just a fight. It was a declaration. If Dorm333 had stumbled, every challenger after them would smell blood. Winning first did not save them from exhaustion, but it protected the shape of fear around them.

Luo whispered, "Do you think Qian Lei summoned something useful?"

"Probably," Song said.

"That is unfair. When I say probably, people say I am guessing. When you say it, people call it analysis."

"Because I stop after one word."

Yan's mouth twitched.

Luo noticed and brightened, then lowered his voice again. "Monitor, if we draw them while Qian Lei is tired, what do we do?"

Yan did not answer immediately.

The public answer would have been easy: attack according to formation, test their center, pressure the summoner, force Lan Xuanyu to divide attention, do not chase Liu Feng, do not let Qian Lei stand comfortably behind a Gate.

Every team in the room could say something like that.

The private answer was heavier.

If Dorm336 faced Dorm333, she would have to fight Lan properly.

Not because she disliked him.

Because she respected him.

Because a test that spared a friend out of softness could harm him more than a clean strike. Because Dorm336's own survival and pride were real. Because Song and Luo deserved a leader who did not enter the arena with half a heart and call hesitation kindness.

And because Lan Xuanyu, if their places were reversed, would think until the floor itself became a plan.

"We do not chase what we cannot see," Yan said at last. "If Qian Lei starts a summon, interrupt only if the path is clean. If it is not clean, we do not throw ourselves into Lan's center just because everyone says he is weak in Soul Power."

Song nodded.

Luo looked toward the sealed simulator doors. "That sounds like you expect him to punish people for believing the scoreboard."

Yan remembered Lan under the helmet, pale and clear as the number reached two hundred. He also remembered the small promise carried between trays and academy noise: I will say what I can.

That promise did not make Lan easy to read.

It only made Yan more certain that the parts Lan could not say were real enough to respect.

"He already has."

The next line on the result board brightened.

Dorm333 would fight again.

The challengers' names appeared.

Jin Xiang.

Tang Yuege.

Li Pengbin.

A faint stir passed through the waiting hall.

Luo's eyebrows rose. "Oh. Narrow road."

Song's expression remained steady, but even he looked more attentive.

Yan Shuo's fingers brushed once against his sleeve, not the collar. Publicly, it was only a small adjustment of uniform cloth.

Privately, Yan Shuo'er let the tiny movement remind her of the first rule of the day.

Do not expose what must remain hidden.

Do not pretend hidden means helpless.

And do not look away just because the door has closed.

---

Lan Xuanyu did not sit when the first match ended.

Qian Lei did.

The restoration light had barely faded before Qian Lei folded his legs beneath him, closed his eyes, and began dragging breath back into order as if every second were a coin he might need later.

"How much?" Lan asked.

"Soul Power is back," Qian Lei said, eyes still closed. "Spiritual Power is... not back. It feels like someone borrowed my head and returned only the shell."

"Can you summon again?"

"Yes."

Liu Feng glanced at him.

Qian Lei opened one eye. "Do not look touched. I said yes, not happily."

Lan watched the arena light gather across from them again.

Too soon.

The teachers were not even pretending otherwise. The short rest confirmed the true shape of the test: not whether Dorm333 could win one brilliant match, but whether their brilliance could be rationed without breaking.

The second challengers appeared.

For the first time that afternoon, Lan Xuanyu's expression became strange.

Qian Lei's eyes opened fully.

Liu Feng's mouth pressed into a thin line.

Jin Xiang stood opposite them.

The boy's hair seemed to bristle before his martial soul even appeared. His expression carried the old annoyance of someone who had been embarrassed by Dorm333 before and had been waiting for the world to provide a legal excuse.

Beside him stood Tang Yuege, quieter, with hands already half-raised as if feeling for invisible spear shafts. On the other side, Li Pengbin watched the space around Liu Feng rather than Lan. That made him more dangerous than he looked. People who stared only at Lan were emotional. People who counted Liu Feng had planned.

Jin Xiang grinned.

"Enemies really do meet on narrow roads," he said. "Last time you got away with tricks. This time, try hiding in an arena."

Qian Lei stood and patted dust that did not exist from his trousers. "We do not hide. We conduct tactical distance management."

Jin Xiang stared at him. "What?"

"He means we ran," Liu Feng said.

"Betrayal," Qian Lei whispered.

Lan did not enter the exchange.

He looked at Jin Xiang's shoulders, Tang Yuege's hands, Li Pengbin's feet.

They were not going to test slowly.

"Now," Lan said.

Qian Lei's yellow ring rose again.

Summoning Money flew.

The Gate of Summoning formed, old and strange, light falling into its frame. Lan's right hand reached out and gripped the pillar. Golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass wound around his palm and disappeared partly into the glare.

Jin Xiang moved immediately.

Flaming Bird wings burst from his back in hot, red-gold arcs. Strictly speaking, it was not true flight like a mature flying martial soul could manage. At his current level it was closer to a fierce glide, a burning leap that let him cover air and pressure faster than ordinary footwork.

It was enough.

He drove straight toward Qian Lei.

Tang Yuege's martial soul appeared at the same instant: a pair of short spears shining in his hands. He did not spend time gathering a grander strike. His yellow ring flashed, and one spear cut through the air.

Cleaving Wind.

The spear did not merely fly.

It accelerated.

Lan saw it appear in front of him almost as soon as it left Tang Yuege's hand.

Li Pengbin moved last and still arrived in time.

Green wings spread behind him. Unlike Jin Xiang's flaming glide, Li Pengbin's movement had the lightness of a bird meant to remain in the air. A green shadow rushed out from his body, bird-shaped and sharp-edged, cutting toward Liu Feng's path.

The plan was clean.

Jin Xiang pressure the Gate.

Tang Yuege force Lan to release or defend.

Li Pengbin hold Liu Feng away from rescue.

Break the center.

The thought flashed through Lan's mind without surprise.

They had found the correct target.

That did not mean they had found the answer.

Lan's right hand could not leave the Gate.

The short spear aimed at his face.

He shifted his feet. The spear changed course with him.

A lock-on effect.

Good.

His left hand rose.

Faint ice mist gathered around the palm, thin enough that it looked like breath against winter glass. The spear reached the space before his eyes.

Lan caught it.

The impact drove through his shoulder and down his spine. For a heartbeat, his fingers felt as if they had closed around a piece of wind sharpened into metal. The Cleaving Wind force fought to tear free.

Ice crawled over the spearhead.

It stopped one inch from his face.

Tang Yuege's eyes widened.

Lan Xuanyu's Soul Power rank was low.

Everyone knew that.

The morning strength test had said something else.

Jin Xiang was already above and closing.

"Liu Feng," Lan said.

Liu Feng did not ask why.

He stopped trying to force through the Green Roc Shadow and retreated instead, body blurring backward so fast that for an instant it looked like he had chosen to abandon the block entirely.

Then his back struck Lan Xuanyu's left palm.

The caught short spear vanished from Lan's hand and returned to Tang Yuege's grasp, but Tang had no time to feel relief.

Lan's left hand had changed from gripping to pushing.

Ice mist passed from palm to back.

Liu Feng used it.

The White Dragon Spear rose.

His whole body snapped forward along the force Lan had given him. The spear point entered the Green Roc Shadow with a faint chill wrapped around it.

The shadow broke.

Not slowly.

Not after a contest.

It scattered as if the part of it that should have resisted had been found and cut.

Li Pengbin's face changed.

Jin Xiang's face did not.

He was too close to notice anything except the Gate.

Flaming wings flared wider. Heat rolled from them in a red wave, and his body twisted in the air as he prepared to strike down toward Qian Lei before the summon could emerge.

Qian Lei's eyes went round.

"Any time," he told the Gate. "Truly. No pressure. Except all pressure."

The Gate answered with a cry.

Not a growl.

A bird cry.

Clear.

Sharp.

So fast that sound seemed to arrive behind it.

Something shot from the opening.

Lan saw only a green line.

Liu Feng saw less.

Jin Xiang reacted by instinct. He twisted midair, Flaming Bird wings sweeping forward. A hot wave cut across the line of attack, the kind of response that would have scorched and deflected many small targets.

It did not stop this one.

There was a soft, terrible sound.

Not a roar.

Not an explosion.

Just contact.

Jin Xiang's forward momentum vanished.

For one impossible moment, he hung in the air with his wings still open and his eyes wide, as if the simulator had forgotten how falling worked.

Then he dropped.

The thing that had struck him paused above.

Small.

That was the first word the mind could hold because the rest of the body did not want to believe danger could be that size.

It was a jade-colored bird no larger than a man's palm, with a red beak and eyes like two hard green sparks. Its feathers looked carved from living jade. Its body was delicate enough to sit in a child's hand.

Its gaze was not delicate at all.

The entire arena seemed to tighten around that tiny shape.

Qian Lei stared upward, too exhausted to decide whether to celebrate or hide.

Liu Feng's spear point lowered by a fraction.

Tang Yuege and Li Pengbin stopped breathing for a beat.

Lan Xuanyu's pupils contracted.

This was wrong.

Not wrong as in failed.

Wrong as in outside the pattern he had begun to trust.

When his right hand supported Qian Lei's Gate, the summons that answered usually carried some relationship to dragons. Crocodile Dragon. Dragon blood. Ground dragon pressure. Enough repeated results had given him a faint outline in his heart: golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass, dragon vein, certain beasts drawn through that road.

But the jade bird hovering above Jin Xiang was not a dragon.

It was something else.

Something fiercely, cleanly dangerous.

Lan heard his own voice before he decided to speak.

"Emerald Demon Bird?"

The bird's jade eyes turned slightly.

For a breath, it looked at him.

And the second door chose differently.
