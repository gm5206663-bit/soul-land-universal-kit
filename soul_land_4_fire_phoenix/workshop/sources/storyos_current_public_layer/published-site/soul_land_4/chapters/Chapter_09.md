# Chapter 9: Three Months of Quiet Fire

Ye Lingtong hated crying more than she hated losing.

Crying made the inside of her nose hurt. It made words break apart before they reached the person who needed to hear them properly. It made adults use soft voices, and soft voices were sometimes worse than scolding because they made the tears think they had permission to continue.

She still cried.

"Daddy, I should have won!"

The Soul Guidance communication device trembled in her hands because she was gripping it too tightly. Her bedroom door was closed. Her school uniform jacket had been thrown over a chair instead of hung properly. On another day, that would have earned a reminder from the housekeeper and a stricter reminder from her father when he returned.

Today no one came in.

"I trained," Ye Lingtong said, voice shaking. "I used Celestial Body properly. I didn't rush like before. I didn't let his water fool me. I had him. I really had him. Then his right hand touched me and I couldn't move. It was like... like my whole body stopped listening."

On the other end of the call, Ye Feng did not answer at once.

That silence frightened her more than immediate anger would have.

"Daddy?"

"Your Celestial Body had already reached basic control," Ye Feng said at last. His voice was calm, but not careless. "Even if your martial soul was suppressed, the reaction should not have been so violent once you had guarded yourself. It was fine at first?"

Ye Lingtong wiped her cheek with the back of her hand. "At first it was fine. I touched his water shield. It became ice. He slipped away. I still could move. I kicked him. I was going to win. Then... then he used his right hand."

"The golden-patterned one?"

"I think so. I didn't see clearly." Her face flushed again, this time from humiliation instead of tears. "Everyone started saying I let him win. They said I liked him. I don't! I don't like Lan Xuanyu! I want to beat him!"

Her voice cracked on the last word.

Ye Feng's eyes narrowed where she could not see.

Lan Xuanyu again.

A child with Blue Silver Grass that could restrain his daughter's Celestial Dragon once might be coincidence, mutation, or instinctive bloodline suppression at close range. Twice, after Ye Lingtong had improved control, was not coincidence.

"Did the teachers see what happened?"

"They were confused too. Teacher Gong checked me. I wasn't badly hurt, but I couldn't move at first. Everyone stared. Huang Qifan said..." She swallowed, and anger rushed back in because anger was easier than embarrassment. "He said stupid things."

"And Yan Shuo?" Ye Feng asked.

Ye Lingtong blinked through tears. "Why ask him?"

"He is in your class. He has a Fire Phoenix martial soul and a new yellow ring. He was present."

"He told them to shut up," she muttered.

"What?"

"He told them not to laugh." Somehow that made her face feel even hotter. "I didn't ask him to. I don't need him helping me."

Ye Feng's expression softened for one breath, then returned to a soldier's focus.

A proud child being protected when she wanted to appear strong would feel like another injury. But that was a children's matter. The real problem remained Lan Xuanyu.

"Listen to me," Ye Feng said. "There is nothing wrong with your Celestial Dragon. You did not lose because you were weak. The problem is likely with Lan Xuanyu. Daddy cannot judge carelessly until I see it myself, but when I return, we will look into his ability properly."

"Then teach me how to beat him."

"I will. But do not provoke him blindly before then."

"I won't lose again."

"Not provoking is not admitting defeat. It is storing strength."

Ye Lingtong disliked the sentence because it sounded true and did not make her feel better.

A knock came from Ye Feng's side of the call. The voice of an officer followed. "Commander, everyone has arrived."

Ye Feng's gaze shifted.

"Daddy has a meeting. We will talk after the mission."

"You always have missions," Ye Lingtong said before she could stop herself.

Ye Feng paused.

For a moment the commander disappeared and the father remained. "I know. Train well. Eat. Sleep. Do not let rumors decide who you are."

The call ended.

Ye Lingtong stared at the dark screen until her own red eyes stared back.

Then she picked up her uniform jacket and hung it properly.

Far away from her bedroom, Ye Feng entered a conference room where childhood tears had no place to stand.

Eight officers rose together. The air changed around him the moment he crossed the threshold, becoming colder, cleaner, and sharpened by duty. He wore a military uniform. The tenderness from his daughter's call had already folded itself behind discipline.

"Sit."

Chairs moved.

Files opened.

The Valiant Dragon Special Forces were not mobilized for children's school rumors. They were mobilized because a brutal, organized enemy had entered their operating range with strong anti-reconnaissance ability, because civilian panic had to be prevented, because if one fish escaped the net then more blood might follow later.

"From the moment the operation begins," Ye Feng said, "external Soul Guidance communication devices will maintain radio silence. Internal channels only. Intelligence will provide live prompts. The target is dangerous, prepared, and confident. We end this fast."

"Yes!"

Ye Feng looked down at the mission map.

A father's promise had to wait behind a commander's duty.

In school, waiting became the shape of many things.

Ye Lingtong waited to stop hearing whispers.

Lan Xuanyu waited for people to forget the day her body had gone limp under his hand.

Yan Shuo waited for adults to decide a proper comparison with Lan Xuanyu was safe.

Adults were very skilled at making children wait.

The first week after the combat class was noisy in all the wrong places. Children lowered their voices when Ye Lingtong passed and then forgot that lowered voices traveled better than normal ones. Huang Qifan stopped making the worst joke after Ye Lingtong looked at him once during morning formation. He was brave, but not foolish in that specific way.

Yan Shuo discovered that telling people not to laugh created new problems.

"Do you like Ye Lingtong?" Bao'er asked him during lunch with the solemn cruelty of a child holding a better weapon than a spoon.

Yan Shuo nearly choked. "No!"

"Then why did you defend her?"

"Because laughing at someone who fell weird is bad."

"But she is scary."

"Scary people can still be laughed at wrongly."

Bao'er thought about this. "That sounds like something a teacher says."

Yan Shuo looked wounded. "Take it back."

Across the dining hall, Ye Lingtong sat straighter than usual and did not look at them.

Lan Xuanyu sat with Class B. He smiled when someone spoke to him, but his eyes moved toward Ye Lingtong once and then away. Yan Shuo noticed. He also noticed that Lan Xuanyu's food portions had become stranger. Ordinary cafeteria food still vanished quickly, but Lan Xuanyu ate as if the food were something to finish before returning to a lesson only he could attend.

"Your teacher gives homework after school too?" Yan Shuo asked when they met near the washing area.

"En. Teacher Nana says I have to practice every day."

"My mother says that too. Adults are copying each other."

Lan Xuanyu looked honestly concerned. "Maybe they have a meeting."

"A secret adult meeting about making children tired."

"My Teacher Nana would not make me tired for no reason."

Yan Shuo opened his mouth, remembered Lan Xuanyu's right hand flashing gold, and closed it again.

"Mine says tired is fine if it is the correct tired," he said instead.

Lan Xuanyu nodded. "That sounds like Teacher Nana too."

They both looked offended on behalf of their freedom.

Teacher Gong Yinghao solved Class A's unrest by inventing drills that made gossip difficult because breathing became a more urgent hobby.

"Dawnfeather Mantle is not a festival lantern," he told Yan Shuo after Yan's shoulders brightened too much during a step exercise. "If it shines more than it protects, you are feeding your pride, not your skill. Again."

Yan Shuo wiped sweat from his chin. "Teacher, can pride be trained into skill?"

"Only after it is beaten flat."

"That seems unfriendly."

"Again."

Again became three months.

It was not dramatic most days. Growth rarely was when it had to live around breakfast, school bells, homework, and parents asking whether hands had been washed. Yan Shuo woke, ate, trained, went to school, trained again, studied, complained, ate more, slept, and began again before his body finished deciding whether yesterday had been fair.

Lin Mei gave his home routine a name he hated immediately.

"Wing Root Drill."

"That sounds like planting vegetables."

"Good. Vegetables grow because their roots behave."

"Phoenixes are not vegetables."

"Then stop wobbling like one in the wind."

Yan Ke laughed once and was assigned to timing duty for the rest of the week.

Wing Root Drill was not beautiful. It did not look like wings opening. It did not look like a Fire Phoenix sweeping across a battlefield. It looked like a boy standing with knees bent while his mother tapped his shoulder, ribs, elbow, and ankle with a padded rod whenever the mantle put heat in the wrong place.

Shoulders.

Ribs.

Back.

Ankle.

Step.

Stop.

The first month, Dawnfeather Mantle still flared whenever Yan Shuo grew excited. A compliment made the feather edges brighten. A mistake made them sharpen. Thinking about Lan Xuanyu's footwork made them push his shoulders forward as if chasing an absent friend.

Lin Mei stopped him every time.

"The mantle is part of you," she said. "If your mood drags it by the beak, it will bite."

The Dawnflame Kite pulsed inside his chest at that.

Yan Shuo pressed a hand over his ribs. "It does not have a beak in there."

"Are you sure?"

He was not.

The second month, the mantle began to listen faster. Not always. Never perfectly. But when a vine snapped toward his wrist in class drill, the feather-light no longer surged wildly to burn it. It slid close first, took the pressure, found the angle, then let heat nibble at the vine until the grip loosened. When a wooden training baton hit his shoulder, his body no longer stiffened under pride. His knees softened, heat moved down the back, and the blow became a lesson instead of a bruise asking for revenge.

The hidden work happened before thought.

A breath changed because the ribs needed space.

An ankle corrected because falling last week had left a memory stronger than instruction.

Heat withdrew from his palm when his temper rose, returning to the shoulder roots before the mantle could turn a practice touch into a real burn.

No one named it.

Gong Yinghao called it training sense.

Lin Mei called it finally listening.

Yan Ke called it fewer reports to sign.

Yan Shuo called it unfair that his own body had joined the adults.

The Dawnflame Kite did not speak, but its warm pulses felt suspiciously satisfied.

Money remained less cooperative than training.

Lan Xuanyu's growth became visible even from across the schoolyard. He grew taller, and his skin seemed healthier, as if sunlight had learned to stay under it. He moved with a balance that made running look optional. Rumors said Teacher Nana fed him strange good food. Rumors said his mother had found an expensive tutor. Rumors said he was secretly eating soul beast eggs for breakfast.

Yan Shuo repeated the last one at home and was forbidden from spreading it further.

"Can we buy strange good food?" he asked anyway.

Yan Ke showed him the family budget.

Yan Shuo looked at the neat rows of numbers and felt personally attacked by arithmetic.

"What if I eat only half as much strange good food?"

"Then we still cannot buy it regularly," Yan Ke said. "Rare ingredients are rare because ordinary families cannot treat them like rice."

"Teacher Nana can."

Lin Mei's chopsticks paused for one breath.

Yan Ke's eyes lowered slightly.

They had heard enough neighbor talk to know Nana was not ordinary. They also knew that not ordinary was not the same as safe to discuss.

"Lan Xuanyu has his path," Lin Mei said. "You have yours. Looking at another child's bowl does not fill your stomach."

Yan Shuo looked down at his very full bowl.

"My stomach disagrees. It wants both paths."

"Your stomach can file a complaint after dinner."

So Yan Shuo ate ordinary food made more carefully than before: extra meat when training was heavy, mild fire-attribute herbs Lin Mei could afford through normal channels, warm soups that helped recovery but did not pretend to be dragon-lizard tendon or treasures from a world they could not pay for.

It was enough to grow.

Not enough to become a miracle.

That distinction mattered.

On the tenth day after Lan Xiao left for his exploration assignment, he called home.

Lan Xuanyu pressed close to Nan Cheng's side, asking whether there were aliens, whether wormholes were dizzy, whether Father had eaten, and whether a spacecraft could get lost if it sneezed during a jump.

Lan Xiao answered as many as he could without making Nan Cheng tell him to stop encouraging nonsense. Then his gaze moved past the screen toward Nana, who stood quietly behind them.

"Everything is going well," he said. "After this we will pass through a wormhole to probe the new world. The next call may be in about a month. The whole operation should last half a year."

Lan Xuanyu counted on his fingers and frowned. "That is too many school days."

Lan Xiao smiled. "Then study well and surprise me when I come back."

"I will! Teacher Nana says I am improving."

"Then listen to Teacher Nana and your mother."

After the call ended, Nan Cheng looked at Nana and found, not for the first time, that the silver-haired woman was watching Lan Xuanyu with a tenderness no performance could imitate.

Fear did not disappear.

But trust grew around it like a fence.

Through those three months, Ye Lingtong avoided Lan Xuanyu.

Avoiding someone did not mean forgetting him. Sometimes it made remembering sharper. She trained with her jaw set, corrected her Celestial Body until the white light clung closer to her skin, and refused to look at the spot where she had fallen during combat class. When Class A practiced paired footwork, she chose Huang Qifan twice and threw him once so cleanly that he lay on the mat blinking at the ceiling.

"You did not have to throw me that hard," he said.

"You said Battle Bear was heavy."

"That was confidence, not invitation."

Yan Shuo laughed before he could stop himself.

Ye Lingtong turned.

The laugh died heroically.

"I was laughing at his sentence," Yan Shuo said.

"I don't care."

"Good. I was worried you cared too much."

Her eyes narrowed.

For one dangerous second, it seemed as if she would challenge him in front of everyone. Then Gong Yinghao's gaze arrived like a door closing.

"Training continues," the teacher said.

Ye Lingtong turned away.

Yan Shuo exhaled.

Huang Qifan, still on the mat, whispered, "You are lucky Teacher Gong likes order."

"Teacher Gong likes making us tired," Yan Shuo whispered back. "Order is his excuse."

Gong Yinghao did not turn around. "Yan Shuo, extra stance set after class. Huang Qifan, join him for commenting from the floor."

Huang Qifan looked betrayed by sound itself.

At the end of the third month, the numbers changed.

Lan Xuanyu's change arrived first in the Lan home, where Nan Cheng measured his height and found that her son had grown until the top of his head stood higher against her than it had any right to. He was about 1.3 meters tall now, tall among his classmates, his body more proportionate and firm. The soft healthy luster under his skin made Nan Cheng look toward Nana with gratitude she could no longer hide behind caution.

"Rank 13," Nan Cheng said after the soul-power check, and hugged him before worry could decide whether the number was good or frightening. "You reached Rank 13."

Lan Xuanyu beamed. "Can I tell Yan Shuo?"

Nan Cheng hesitated.

Rank was not a forbidden secret. Many children compared ranks. But every number around her son had begun to carry another shadow.

"You may tell him you improved," she said. "Do not talk about private training details."

"Teacher Nana's private lessons?"

"Yes."

Lan Xuanyu nodded seriously. "I will say Rank 13 and nothing about my body class monitor."

Nan Cheng stared at him.

Nana turned her face slightly away, but her shoulders moved once.

"Body class monitor?"

"The two hot-tempered ones," Lan Xuanyu explained. "I am not supposed to let them fight."

Nan Cheng looked at Nana.

Nana's eyes were innocent in a way that did not help.

Yan Shuo's number changed two days later, in a much less mysterious room.

There was no rare food on the table. No silver-haired teacher. No secret vortex. Only a school safety room, Gong Yinghao with a tablet, Lin Mei standing behind Yan Shuo with crossed arms, and Yan Ke pretending not to look nervous while looking extremely nervous.

"Again," Gong Yinghao said.

Yan Shuo placed his hand on the soul-power apparatus again because apparently machines liked being asked the same question until adults believed them.

The result appeared.

Rank 13.

Yan Shuo tried very hard not to jump. Jumping would prove he had extra energy. Extra energy became extra drills.

His knees still bounced once.

Lin Mei's hand landed lightly on his shoulder.

"Stable," Gong Yinghao said. "Not a fluctuation. Level 13."

Yan Ke exhaled.

"I can hear your relief," Yan Shuo told him.

"Good. It means your ears still work after all the complaining."

Then came the Spiritual Power check.

Yan Shuo sat with the testing cap lowered over his head. He had learned this sensation better than he wanted to. The coolness against his scalp. The urge to push back. The flutter of Dawnflame Kite warmth wanting to know whether this was danger, game, or bath.

Not danger.

Not game.

Hold shape.

Breath low. Shoulders wide. Heat quiet.

Thirty-nine points.

Still Spirit Origin Realm.

Gong Yinghao wrote it down without allowing the room to become too excited. "Yan Shuo. Rank 13 / Level 13. Spiritual Power 39 points, Spirit Origin Realm. First ring stable. Dawnfeather Mantle improved but not mastered. Continue restricted-duration use."

"Restricted," Yan Shuo repeated, because victory had just been insulted.

"Yes."

"But I advanced."

"Your ability to injure yourself has also advanced."

Yan Ke coughed.

Lin Mei nodded as if this were excellent scholarship.

Yan Shuo looked at all three adults. "This is the adult alliance again."

"Correct," Lin Mei said. "We meet weekly."

He stared at her.

She did not blink.

For three seconds, he genuinely wondered if she was joking.

That evening, Lan Xuanyu's message arrived first.

Lan Xuanyu: I reached Rank 13.

Yan Shuo stared at it, then looked at his own test record, then looked at the ceiling as if thanking fate for having a sense of timing.

Yan Shuo: I also reached Rank 13.

Lan Xuanyu: Really? Congratulations!

Yan Shuo: You sound too happy. You are supposed to be challenged.

Lan Xuanyu: I am challenged. But also happy.

Yan Shuo: That is allowed.

Lan Xuanyu: Can we compare now?

Yan Shuo's thumb hovered.

He wanted to type yes so strongly that the word nearly appeared by itself. Then he remembered Ye Lingtong falling limp, Lan Xuanyu looking frightened by his own hand, Gong Yinghao saying restricted, Lin Mei saying shape before pride, Nana saying wait until you know how to hold back.

Yan Shuo: Adults will say no.

Lan Xuanyu: Teacher Nana says wait.

Yan Shuo: My mother says wait. Teacher Gong says wait. My father says forms first. This confirms the adult alliance.

Lan Xuanyu: Then we compare homework?

Yan Shuo: I take back friendship.

Lan Xuanyu sent a smiling face.

Yan Shuo laughed so hard the Dawnflame Kite fluttered awake.

Rank 13 did not make him master of anything.

It did make morning practice more humiliating, because his body now had enough strength to make mistakes faster.

The day Nan Cheng planned to take Lan Xuanyu out to play, Nana trained him in the cultivation room after breakfast.

Lan Xuanyu had grown used to attacking Teacher Nana from the strangest angles he could invent. It had become a private game and a private lesson. This time he pushed off lightly, leaving a small shadow of movement to one side while his body shifted faster toward another. A faint golden glimmer touched his right hand. His left hand gathered water, ready to change direction.

Nana smiled.

Her silver hair moved.

It did not move like hair. It moved like every strand could see. It sealed his path, drank the water he sent toward it, and guided his momentum where he had not planned to go.

Lan Xuanyu tried to spin behind her.

Nana lowered herself, breathed in, and the water vortex he made vanished into that breath as if swallowed by the air.

The next breath pulled him off balance.

"Boop."

Her lips touched his cheek.

Lan Xuanyu wiped his face with great dignity. "Teacher Nana, you're bullying me."

"You attacked first."

"Your soul power is too strong."

"Then grow stronger."

That answer was unfair because it was exactly what he wanted to do.

Nana's expression became more serious. "You have learned the basics of the footwork. Improving further will need more soul power and more practice. Today I will teach you something new."

Lan Xuanyu's eyes lit up. "An attack?"

"An ancient martial art. I think its name is Golden Dragon Shocks the Heavens."

The name made the air in the room seem heavier for one breath.

Lan Xuanyu did not know why.

Nana did not fully know why either.

She closed the defense system of the cultivation room and stood behind him. "This technique uses the power in your blood, your soul power, and the force of the body together. Your current soul power is still low, so you can only learn a weakened way to operate it. It will be painful. Remember the route. In the first ten attempts, I will guide you and protect you. After that, you must try to remember it yourself."

Lan Xuanyu swallowed.

"Will it make me stronger?"

"Yes. It will also strengthen your meridians if practiced correctly."

Meridians were one of the words adults used when trying not to say danger.

Lan Xuanyu nodded.

Nana's hand touched his back.

Gentle soul power and Spiritual Power entered him together.

The room disappeared.

In his consciousness, he saw a body shaped like his own, clear enough to frighten him. Blood flowed through it in red lines. Soul power moved in another path. Then Nana's guidance turned the flow.

Pain arrived like fire with teeth.

Lan Xuanyu wanted to cry out. He could not find the sound. His mind stayed too awake, forced to watch every wrong-way current, every surge of heat, every place where the body protested that this was not how blood should move.

Then the heat rushed into his right hand.

Golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass covered his arm. The skin beneath seemed to swell and harden. Diamond-shaped golden scales appeared along his right hand and forearm, bright and terrifying and beautiful. Something low sounded from his palm, not quite a roar, not something a child should carry.

"Golden Dragon Shocks the Heavens," Nana said behind him.

Power gathered.

A faint golden dragon-head shape shot forward.

The target one meter away shook under the impact. The cultivation room trembled despite its defenses.

Lan Xuanyu's strength vanished.

Nana's power immediately wrapped him, steady and endless, guiding his blood back, calming the two-color vortex, filling the emptiness enough that he did not collapse.

"Remember," she said softly. "Again."

Again became the whole day.

The first ten times, Nana guided the reversal and protected every dangerous edge. Lan Xuanyu learned pain by route and route by pain. He learned that wanting strength was easy before the body paid for it. He learned that Teacher Nana's hand on his back meant the difference between a technique and disaster.

He also learned that attacks had costs.

By evening he had eaten two meals of high-grade food and still fell asleep so deeply that Nana did not ask him to meditate.

In the Yan apartment that same evening, Yan Shuo was discovering a much less glorious cost.

"My legs are broken," he announced from the floor.

"They are not," Lin Mei said.

"They are emotionally broken."

"Then comfort them by standing up properly."

Rank 13 had made his Wing Root Drill heavier. Lin Mei had added a new rule: the mantle could not flare beyond the shoulders for the first five breaths. Only after the root shape stayed stable could fire-light move to the forearms.

This was, in Yan Shuo's private opinion, like giving someone wings and asking them to admire the floor.

He stood anyway.

Dawnfeather Mantle formed slowly. Fire-light feathers lay close across shoulders and upper back. They wanted to rush forward. He wanted to rush forward. The Dawnflame Kite stirred, then settled when his breath did.

Five breaths.

Then forearms.

The feather edges extended, soft and bright.

Not wings.

Not yet.

A memory flashed: Lan Xuanyu in the air, golden right hand rising, Ye Lingtong collapsing.

Yan Shuo's mantle tightened too quickly.

Lin Mei's padded rod tapped his shoulder at once.

"Memory is also pressure," she said.

Yan Shuo looked up sharply.

"I didn't say anything."

"You did with your shoulders."

He hated that she was right.

"Again."

This time, when the golden memory came, his fire did not leap to meet it. It crouched around the center of his chest, not surrendering, not attacking, simply remaining itself. The Dawnflame Kite tucked close, warm and alert. Yan Shuo breathed around the memory until it became something he could carry without chasing.

Six breaths.

Seven.

Eight.

The mantle held.

Lin Mei lowered the rod. "Good."

Yan Shuo did not grin until after he released the skill.

Growth, he was learning, often meant being happy two seconds later than he wanted.

The next morning, Lan Xuanyu climbed out of bed and almost startled himself with the sound his feet made.

"Boom."

He looked down at the floor.

"Did I become heavier?"

His body was sore all over, but it was a warm soreness, as if strength had hidden beneath the ache and was waiting to be found. At breakfast, he ate until Nan Cheng gave up pretending to be surprised. Nana watched him with quiet satisfaction and did not join the outing.

"Teacher Nana, come with us," Lan Xuanyu pleaded.

"Go play with your mother," Nana said, touching his head. "I will think about what to teach you next."

Nan Cheng's happiness at having her son to herself lasted until he asked, from the Spirit Guidance car, whether they could buy something back for Teacher Nana.

"Even now you remember Teacher Nana," Nan Cheng said. "Do you like her more than Mommy?"

Lan Xuanyu glanced at her and used the solemn tone of a small adult. "Mommy, in adult words, you are making trouble without reason."

The car was on autopilot. This was fortunate, because Nan Cheng's hand twitched.

"Where did you learn that?"

Lan Xuanyu deepened his voice in imitation of Lan Xiao. "Nan Cheng, although I spoil you, you should not make trouble without reason. Even soft bone rabbit spirit beasts can bite."

Nan Cheng stared at him, torn between laughter and discipline.

"You eavesdropped!"

"You talked loudly."

"Little brat."

Lan Xuanyu smiled, then leaned closer. "Mommy, do you love me?"

Nan Cheng knew he was acting cute. It worked anyway. She squeezed his cheeks. "Mommy loves you. Mommy loves you the most."

"Mommy is the best."

"Only when Teacher Nana is not here?"

"Mommy."

"Fine, fine."

The car screen lit with Nan Cheng's usual entertainment channel. A concert broadcast had begun. Noble Le stood on a stage in white, blue hair swept back, silver patterning bright beneath the lights. He had become famous in only a few months, a singer who sang one song and somehow made people feel as if one song contained a lifetime.

"Noble Le's Remembrance is on," Nan Cheng said, her eyes brightening despite herself.

Lan Xuanyu looked at her. "Mommy, your eyes are twinkling."

"Watch quietly."

The song began.

It was not a children's song. Lan Xuanyu did not understand most of the words. They spoke of memory, longing, one life and three worlds, someone lost and still present because the heart refused to let go. The melody carried grief gently, the way Teacher Nana sometimes looked out of windows. Noble Le's voice became hoarse near the end, not ugly, but as if the song had reached into him and found an old wound.

Nan Cheng's eyes reddened.

Lan Xuanyu grew quiet.

His chest trembled.

Not like the vortex fighting. Not like Golden Dragon Shocks the Heavens. Softer than both and somehow more difficult to ignore. He did not know Noble Le well. He had met a blue-haired uncle once on Heaven Dou Planet and liked him without knowing why. The song made that feeling stir like a small hand knocking from inside memory.

"Mommy," he asked softly, "what does loving deeply mean?"

Nan Cheng wiped the corner of one eye and immediately pretended she had not. "You are still little. You will know when you grow up."

"Adults always say that when they don't want to explain."

"Then grow up and complain later."

Lan Xuanyu nodded as if storing the plan.

In another part of Zi Luo City, Yan Shuo saw Noble Le on a public screen outside a store while walking back from a weekend supply errand with Yan Ke.

Several adults had stopped to watch. One auntie held a shopping bag in both hands and forgot it was heavy. Even Yan Ke slowed for a moment.

Noble Le sang with eyes that looked empty and full at the same time.

Yan Shuo listened for half a verse and frowned.

"Is he using a charm-type soul skill?"

Yan Ke blinked, then smiled. "No. He is singing."

"Singing can make adults look like that?"

"Some songs can."

Yan Shuo considered this with suspicion. "Then singing is dangerous."

"Many things are, if used well."

The Dawnflame Kite stirred faintly in his chest, not frightened, only curious at the emotion moving through the crowd like warm wind. Yan Shuo did not know why the song made him think of Lan Xuanyu's quiet face after the combat class, or Teacher Nana's name spoken too softly by his friend, or the city alarm no one could explain.

He only knew the music made people look for something they could not see.

"Can we go?" he asked after a while.

"Yes."

They walked on.

Behind them, Noble Le's voice followed through the street until traffic swallowed it.

Ahead of Lan Xuanyu and Nan Cheng, the navigation display marked the route toward the new Horizon Building and its World of Trampolines.

Lan Xuanyu's mood slowly returned as the promise of jumping pushed the song's ache into a corner of his chest. He talked about whether a Spirit Master could bounce higher than an ordinary child, whether Teacher Nana would think trampolines counted as footwork practice, and whether Mommy would jump too.

Nan Cheng laughed and told him she would watch.

At home, Nana sat alone by the window.

She did not hear the car's broadcast. She did not know Noble Le's song had just crossed Lan Xuanyu's heart like a remembered shadow. Yet for no reason she understood, she lifted a hand to her chest and looked toward the distant city roads.

Memory did not return.

Longing did.

At the Yan apartment that night, Yan Shuo recorded his new numbers in the training notebook because Yan Ke said writing them by hand made pride slower.

Rank 13.

Spiritual Power 39.

Spirit Origin Realm.

Dawnflame Kite: original acquisition paperwork exactly 100 years; private actual living age at this point is actual living age of 154 years, yellow tier.

Dawnfeather Mantle: improved, not mastered.

He added one more line when his father was not looking.

Adult alliance: still active.

Then he stared at the page for a long time.

Lan Xuanyu had also reached Rank 13. That should have made the comparison simple. Same rank. Different classes. Different skills. Direct duel when adults finally stopped being unreasonable.

But the more Yan Shuo thought about it, the less simple it became.

Lan Xuanyu's right hand had made Ye Lingtong fall wrong. His footwork moved like water had learned feet. His eyes sometimes held a brightness that was not the same as winning. Teacher Nana waited at his home with lessons no academy teacher seemed able to explain.

Yan Shuo did not know the truth.

He was beginning to understand that not knowing was also something one had to train with.

He closed the notebook.

Inside his chest, Dawnflame Kite settled against him, warm and quiet.

In another home, Lan Xuanyu laughed in a car on the way to play after a day of learning a technique too old and dangerous for his small hands.

One boy grew through rare food, private protection, golden pain, and a song that touched a memory he did not own.

One boy grew through ordinary bowls, strict drills, public records, quiet fire, and the stubborn decision not to chase secrets just because they shone.

Both had reached Rank 13.

Neither had reached safety.

Above Zi Luo City, the sky looked calm.

Below it, the next trouble was already choosing a building.
