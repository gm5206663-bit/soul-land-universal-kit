# Chapter 22 - The Quietest Place

The stone figure came one step at a time.

Bang.

The small lamps in the wall trembled.

Bang.

Dust shook loose from the ceiling and fell in pale lines through the dim light.

Bang.

Qian Lei swallowed.

The stone aspect was only about two meters tall, but in the narrow passage it looked much larger. Its shoulders nearly brushed the walls. Its axe was wider than Qian Lei's chest, and every step made the floor answer as if the Maze itself had a heartbeat.

"Xuanyu," Qian Lei whispered, "should I summon?"

Lan Xuanyu did not look away from the stone aspect. "No."

Qian Lei's expression collapsed. "That fast? You did not even think."

"That was thinking." Lan Xuanyu's eyes tracked the axe, the shoulders, the speed of the feet. "Your summon has uncertainty. We do not know what else is in the Maze, and we do not know how many times we will need you later. Liu Feng, test it first. Do not fight to the end. Just see how it moves."

Liu Feng nodded.

White Dragon Spear appeared in his hand.

The moment the spear settled into his grip, the restless impatience in his eyes found a place to go. He did not wait for the stone aspect to reach them. His body flashed forward, thin and sharp, and the spear point arrived before the rest of him seemed to have crossed the distance.

The stone aspect raised its axe.

Slow.

Not weak, but slow.

Liu Feng's first thrust struck its chest and left a small hole. Stone powder burst outward. The axe came down a breath later, heavy enough that the air seemed to fold around it.

Liu Feng was already gone.

He slid sideways, feet brushing the stone ground, and the White Dragon Spear flicked out again.

Another small hole.

Another axe swing.

Another dodge.

Qian Lei's shoulders relaxed by a little. "It cannot hit him."

"It has not hit him yet," Lan Xuanyu corrected.

Liu Feng circled inside the narrow space with more restraint than he would have used a month ago. He did not chase speed until his steps blurred out of his own control. Each turn stopped before it became reckless. Each retreat left just enough room that the stone axe struck air instead of his ribs.

He had learned the painful lesson well.

Being fast enough to arrive first was useless if he could not still choose after arriving.

The White Dragon Spear left another mark on the stone aspect's shoulder. The damage was small, but not meaningless. The enemy's movements were crude, its rhythm obvious. It relied on defense, weight, and pressure, not intelligence.

"Frenzie," Lan Xuanyu called. "Back."

Liu Feng obeyed at once. He retreated in two light steps and landed beside Lan Xuanyu, spear angled forward.

"Its defense is strong," Liu Feng said quickly, "but not impossible. If I keep stabbing long enough, I can break it. It is slow. The axe is dangerous only if we give it the line."

Lan Xuanyu nodded. "Then we do not give it time to wear us down. Let it come closer."

Qian Lei looked between them. "I thought you said not to fight to the end."

"I said not to waste what we may need later." Lan Xuanyu lifted his right hand. Golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass curled from his palm. "This is not waste if we finish it fast."

The stone aspect continued forward.

The distance shortened.

Ten meters.

Eight.

Six.

The battle axe rose again.

Lan Xuanyu placed his right hand on Liu Feng's shoulder.

Gold spread.

For an instant, Liu Feng felt as if the spear in his hand had awakened from sleep. The White Dragon Spear lit, the dragon eyes opening with a cold gleam. A sharp aura climbed along the shaft and into his arm until even his breathing seemed to have a point.

His eyes brightened.

"I will take the front."

This time, when Liu Feng moved, the narrow passage could hardly hold him.

The spear struck the stone aspect's chest before the axe completed its fall. It did not leave a small hole now. It burst through, carving a cavity in the heavy stone body. Rock fragments scattered across the ground.

Liu Feng used the spear's momentum to swing himself upward.

The axe missed below him.

He landed on the stone aspect's shoulder, twisted, and smashed the spear butt against its head.

Crack.

Half the stone face broke away.

At the same time, Lan Xuanyu rushed in under the axe's outer line. Golden scales surfaced faintly over his fist. He did not try to overpower the entire body. He struck the arm that held the axe, then the shoulder joint that guided it.

The stone aspect's arm jolted.

The axe crashed to the ground.

Liu Feng dropped from above like a white streak. Three spear strikes landed in succession, each one stabbing through cracks opened by the first attack. The stone head shattered completely.

The body froze.

Then it collapsed.

Qian Lei jumped backward as rubble tumbled across the floor. "Hey, do not bury your summoning master!"

Lan Xuanyu retreated in time, dust brushing his sleeves.

Liu Feng landed on the broken stone pile with the White Dragon Spear slanted downward. For a moment, the sharp aura around the spear had not fully faded. He looked taller than usual standing there, as if the speed that normally made him seem thin had finally gained weight.

Then Lan Xuanyu removed his hand from Liu Feng's shoulder, and the spear's light gradually drew back.

Liu Feng looked at Lan Xuanyu.

Lan Xuanyu gave him a thumbs-up.

Liu Feng returned it with a small, rare smile.

Qian Lei looked at the collapsed stone aspect. "It was really not that strong."

Liu Feng glanced at him. "You did not fight it."

"I observed it intellectually."

"From behind us."

"That is the safest place for intellectual observation."

Lan Xuanyu had already crouched beside the rubble. He did not laugh. His gaze moved from the broken stone body to the corridor, then to the dead end behind them.

The Maze was not a straight test of who could hit hardest.

That was the important part.

The stone aspect had guarded the crossroad in front of the dead end. After it moved toward them and collapsed, its remains made a natural screen. From the other side of the passage, a person looking in would see only a dead end and a mound of broken stone.

Lan Xuanyu's expression changed.

Qian Lei knew that look now. It meant his roommate had found a way to make other people suffer later.

"What do we do?" Qian Lei asked, already suspicious.

Lan Xuanyu sat down behind the rubble.

Qian Lei stared. "What are you doing?"

"Waiting."

"Waiting? Here?" Qian Lei lowered his voice as if the Maze itself might report him. "Are we going to skive?"

Lan Xuanyu looked up at him. "Why does it sound so shameful when you say it?"

"Because it is very close to shameful."

"It is called preserving strength and watching the situation." Lan Xuanyu patted the ground beside him. "This is a good hiding place. The chance of being discovered is small. Other teams will want to perform after three months of training, so they will fight when they meet. If we join every battle, our Soul Power will be spent before the end. If we wait, we may meet tired opponents later."

Liu Feng's expression became strange.

The fight with the stone aspect had made his blood warm. He wanted to move. He wanted to test the spear again, to prove that the morning's plus sign was not a piece of paper but a path.

But wanting to fight and needing to fight were not the same.

That was also something he had learned from losing control.

He sat down.

Qian Lei hesitated, then sat too. "I still think it sounds like skiving."

"Then skive quietly," Lan Xuanyu said.

Qian Lei opened his mouth, found no good argument, and leaned against the wall with an injured expression.

Lan Xuanyu reached over, gripped the battle axe by the handle, and dragged it closer. It was heavy, but his strength was enough to move it. The axe scraped against the ground with a low sound.

"A weapon?" Liu Feng asked.

"Maybe useful." Lan Xuanyu settled it beside him. "If the Maze gives us something, there is no reason to leave it for someone else."

Qian Lei looked at the axe, then at the rubble, then at the dead end hiding them.

"Xuanyu," he said sincerely, "you look honest, but your heart is very complicated."

Lan Xuanyu closed his eyes. "Rest."

Qian Lei whispered to Liu Feng, "He did not deny it."

Liu Feng also closed his eyes. "Rest."

The dead end became quiet.

Outside the rubble, the Maze began to grow louder.

In another passage, far away from Dorm 333's hiding place, Yan Shuo raised one hand and stopped his team at the first fork.

Song Yichen halted without a word.

Luo Haoran nearly took one more step, caught himself, and slid back into line with a grimace. "I saw the left turn first. My feet became enthusiastic."

"Your feet do not vote," Song said.

"My feet are oppressed."

Yan Shuo did not smile until his breath settled. The Maze's air was cool and carried old stone dust. The lamps were dim, not dark enough to blind, not bright enough to trust. Three paths opened ahead of them: left, center, and right. All three looked empty.

Empty did not mean safe.

His yellow ring rose beneath his feet.

Dawnfeather Mantle opened in low output.

It did not flare.

Copper-red Phoenix flame folded close around his forearms, shoulders, upper back, and chest. Pale-gold motes ran through the flame like dust caught by dawn. White-gold feather edges formed and settled, narrow and warm, shaping the aura so it did not spill across Song or Luo's lanes.

Inside his spiritual sea, Dawnflame Kite pulsed once.

Not words.

A small bright beat of attention.

Yan Shuo answered with breath instead of force.

Teacher Ji's voice seemed to return with the stone dust: if fire was everywhere, it was nowhere. If power could not tell friend from wall, then it was only a tantrum wearing a martial soul.

"We do not chase the first sound," Yan Shuo said softly.

Luo looked offended. "What if the first sound is an enemy being foolish for our benefit?"

"Then it can remain foolish without us." Yan Shuo pointed to the floor. "Look at the dust."

Luo crouched.

Song lowered his gaze too.

The center path had faint scrapes. Heavy. Straight. Too regular to be from students. The left path had a thin line of disturbed dust along the wall, as if something had dragged a weapon there. The right path had no obvious track, but the lamp nearest it flickered every few breaths.

Luo's grin faded into focus. "Center has a guard. Left may have a patrol. Right has airflow. Maybe another crossroad."

"Right gives us choices," Song said. "Center may force impact."

Yan Shuo nodded. "Right. Song front. Luo, do not outrun our sight."

"I never outrun sight."

Song looked at him.

Luo corrected, "I will not outrun it this time."

They moved.

Song took the front with Stoneplate Tortoise already half-manifested as a heavy shield-line presence around his arms and shoulders. The old Song might have planted himself too early, waiting for pressure to arrive. This Song shifted with each step. Heavy, yes, but not dead weight. His Spiritual Power had crossed the threshold, and it showed in how he listened to the corridor before the corridor struck him.

Luo slipped along the wall, Threadwind Mantis light around his limbs. He did not vanish like Liu Feng. He was not that kind of speed. His gift was interruption: a foot placed in the wrong enemy angle, a cut at the wrist before a blow finished forming, a sudden correction that made a clean attack ugly.

Yan Shuo stayed half a pace behind and between them.

Not because he was weaker.

Because the team needed a center more than it needed a bonfire.

The right path bent twice.

At the second bend, the wall ahead moved.

A stone sentinel unfolded from a crouch beside the corner. It was not exactly the same as the axe-bearing aspect Dorm 333 faced. This one carried a short stone shield in one hand and a blunt hammer in the other. Its shoulders were lower, its steps quicker, and the shield filled half the corridor when it raised its arm.

Luo clicked his tongue. "The wall has opinions."

"Back half step," Yan Shuo said.

Song moved before the hammer fell.

The stone hammer struck his shield-line with a dull boom. Song did not try to stop everything through stubbornness. His knees bent, his shoulders sank, and the impact slid down into his stance. The Stoneplate Tortoise aura around him thickened, not beautifully, but correctly.

Yan Shuo saw the change and felt a strange satisfaction.

A month ago, Song would have received the blow like a door.

Now he received it like a hinge.

"Right knee crack," Luo said.

Yan Shuo saw it at the same time: a thin seam where the stone joint rotated. Pale-gold light at the edge of his Mantle brightened, not outward as a flash, but along his forearm like a line drawn on hot metal.

"Luo."

The Threadwind Mantis moved.

Luo darted low. His blade-hand did not try to cut through the thickest part. It tapped, withdrew, tapped again, each strike marking the joint just before the sentinel shifted weight.

The stone shield came down toward him.

Yan Shuo entered.

Dawnfeather Mantle tightened across his shoulder and ribs. Copper-red warmth pushed against the stone shield, but the flame did not wash over Song's back or chase Luo's retreating line. White-gold feather edges caught the recoil and carried it across his upper back instead of letting it explode from his elbow.

He slammed the shield's rim sideways.

Not enough to destroy it.

Enough to turn it.

Song stepped in at that exact moment and drove his own shield-line into the sentinel's hammer arm. Luo's third cut landed on the knee seam.

Crack.

The sentinel lurched.

Yan Shuo did not shout a technique name. He did not need one. Dawnfeather Mantle was his first soul skill; everything else here was breath, footwork, and the skill's controlled application. He struck the cracked knee with a short, tight punch wrapped in light-lined copper-red fire.

The joint broke.

The sentinel fell sideways.

Song's shield pinned the hammer arm to the wall.

Luo darted past and cut the back of the neck seam.

Yan Shuo placed a burning palm against the chest crack and released one narrow pulse.

Pale-gold motes flickered inside the copper-red flame.

The stone body went still.

Dust slid down the wall.

No roar.

No wide blaze.

No wasted announcement down three corridors.

Luo exhaled. "I would like to say that was easy."

Song looked at the dent across his shield aura. "It was not."

"That is why I only said I would like to say it."

Yan Shuo flexed his fingers. The Mantle's feather edges loosened, then resettled. His soul power had not splashed. His shoulders did not ache with the old sharp recoil. He had spent strength, but the spending felt counted.

That was new enough to matter.

"We leave the body there," he said.

Luo pointed down the passage. "As decoration?"

"As a warning and a lie." Yan Shuo crouched beside the fallen sentinel. He pressed two fingers to the cracked stone and drew a thin, warm line along the floor toward the left side of the next bend. The copper-red heat faded quickly, but the pale-gold edge left just enough warmth for a sensitive opponent to misread if they rushed. "If a team tracks the heat, they think we turned left. We go right again after the next fork."

Song's brows moved slightly. "False trail."

"Small one. It will not last long."

"Long enough?" Luo asked.

Yan Shuo looked into the dim corridor.

From somewhere far away came a crash, followed by children's voices and the sharper ring of soul skills colliding.

"Long enough to avoid being the nearest answer."

They moved again.

This time Luo did not joke.

In the observation room, ten screens divided the Maze into ten pieces of pressure.

Eight teachers stood before them.

Some nodded.

Some frowned.

Some pointed when a student overextended, or when a team finally remembered it had three people instead of three separate prides.

Mu Zhongtian's attention returned to Dorm 333 again and again.

It was not favoritism, though he knew it could look that way. The previous simulation accident had left a weight in his chest. Lan Xuanyu's coma, the checks afterward, the relief when the child returned safely—all of it made Mu Zhongtian watch that screen with a concentration he did not need to pretend was casual.

Then Liu Feng's spear changed.

Mu Zhongtian leaned forward.

The first few strikes against the stone aspect had been normal for Liu Feng: fast, accurate, but lacking enough force to break a defensive target quickly. Then Lan Xuanyu touched him, and Liu Feng's offensive sharpness rose as if a hidden edge had been unsheathed.

The stone aspect collapsed far faster than Mu Zhongtian expected.

"Liu Feng's attack power..." Mu Zhongtian murmured.

The stone aspects in the Maze were not designed to eliminate most teams by themselves. This was a team-coordination test, not a massacre. Even so, clearing one that quickly meant something.

Something had changed.

On another screen, a different teacher spoke. "Yan Shuo's team has cleared their sentinel too. Cleanly. No overburn."

Mu Zhongtian glanced.

Dorm 336's screen showed broken stone, a shifted shield, and three children already leaving the location without rushing toward the nearest noise. Yan Shuo's low-output Mantle had stayed close to his body. The copper-red and pale-gold boundary was much cleaner than it had been when he first entered the Elite Junior Class. Song Yichen's front line had not frozen. Luo Haoran's interruption timing was sharper than his usual playful face suggested.

Good.

Very good.

The morning's first place was not empty.

But Mu Zhongtian's eyes still returned to the screen where Dorm 333 sat down behind rubble.

He did not know whether to laugh or sigh.

"They are really sitting there," one teacher said.

Qian Lei was visible only from an awkward angle, slumped behind broken stone with the expression of someone suffering from strategic boredom. Liu Feng sat with his spear across his knees. Lan Xuanyu had dragged the battle axe close and settled as if the dead end were a classroom chair.

The vice president stood near the center of the observation room, short and round, his smile warm enough that anyone seeing him for the first time might think he was the gentlest teacher in the academy.

The teachers who knew him did not make that mistake.

The vice president looked across the screens and nodded. "Not bad. Three months have not been wasted."

Battles had already begun elsewhere in the Maze. Children who had spent the morning being measured in categories now wanted to prove that a real fight could not be reduced to columns. Two teams had collided near a narrow junction and immediately turned the corridor into a storm of soul power. Another pair of teams had chosen to avoid each other, only for a stone hazard to force them back together. Sparks, water, wind, armor auras, and shouts broke across the screens.

They were young.

They were eager.

They wanted to dazzle.

Ji Hongbin stood beside the vice president with his arms folded.

His face did not show approval easily, which was why the small curve at the corner of his mouth drew Mu Zhongtian's notice.

"That is not the best screen," Ji Hongbin said.

The vice president turned his head. "Oh? Then which is? The monitor's team? Yan Shuo is controlling the fire-light line better than before. His foundation really is good."

"Good," Ji Hongbin said. "But not the one I mean."

He pointed.

The vice president followed the direction of his finger and saw Dorm 333 sitting behind the rubble.

For a moment, his smile deepened.

"Those three? They are resting."

"They are using the test." Ji Hongbin's voice was flat. "The Maze is not a ring platform. It does not say the bravest team wins. It says survive, find opponents, eliminate them, and earn the best result. They found the crux faster than the teams running around to show off."

The vice president watched Lan Xuanyu's still figure.

Behind the rubble, nothing dramatic happened.

That was precisely why it became interesting.

Ji Hongbin continued, "Calm enough not to be dragged by the atmosphere. Practical enough to exploit terrain. They understand that strength is limited and that other people's impatience can become their resource. Intelligent children walk farther than rash children."

The vice president chuckled. "You make laziness sound elegant."

"You recognize it because it resembles you."

The vice president's eyes narrowed. "What are you implying?"

Ji Hongbin looked at him once. "Fat fox."

Several teachers suddenly found the other screens very interesting.

The vice president's warm smile twitched. "Shut up."

Ji Hongbin placed a hand on his shoulder with rare familiarity. "Still unable to let it go?"

The vice president's smile faded for half a breath, leaving something older behind it. "Letting go is easy to say. There are places a person wants to return to even after the gate is closed. I used to think if I trained enough students, found enough talent, made enough noise, perhaps someone would remember to open it."

Ji Hongbin's expression changed.

The vice president waved a hand before the conversation could continue. "Enough. A real man does not spend the day showing old scars to children on screens."

His eyes returned to Lan Xuanyu.

The faint light in them sharpened.

"What is his name?"

Mu Zhongtian answered at once. "Lan Xuanyu."

"After the test, have him come see me."

Ji Hongbin's head snapped toward him. "Do not misguide the child."

The vice president looked wounded. "How could you say that? I am famous for being easy-going."

Ji Hongbin's expression said he had never heard a more shameless sentence.

"You? Easy-going? You—"

A loud collision from one of the screens cut him off.

The Maze was becoming lively.

Dorm 333 remained quiet.

Behind the rubble, Qian Lei had already changed sitting positions four times.

"There is a fight nearby," he whispered. "I can hear it. Maybe two teams. Maybe if we go now, we can take advantage."

Lan Xuanyu did not open his eyes. "Wait."

"If we wait too long, someone else will take them."

"Then those teams will spend strength taking them."

Qian Lei frowned.

Liu Feng listened to the sounds outside. He wanted to move too. The spear in his hands seemed to grow heavier the longer he sat still, as if speed itself disliked patience. But Lan Xuanyu's logic was not wrong.

Footsteps approached.

All three fell silent.

The footsteps slowed near the crossroad outside. Someone muttered that it was a dead end. Another voice said there was only rubble. The footsteps receded.

Qian Lei's eyes widened.

Lan Xuanyu still did not move.

A short while later, another set of hurried steps passed. This time two students came close enough that Liu Feng's grip tightened around his spear. They glanced into the passage, saw broken stone filling most of the view, and turned away after only a breath.

When the sound faded, Qian Lei let out the breath he had been holding.

"They really did not see us."

"That is why we stay," Lan Xuanyu said.

Liu Feng looked at him. "You are very patient."

Lan Xuanyu shook his head. "Not especially. But we are already safe from elimination this term. If we fight just to prove we dare to fight, that is wasting what the morning gave us. If we want first place, we should wait for the best chance."

Qian Lei's expression changed as understanding finally found him.

Protection did not mean they should relax.

It meant they could choose.

He leaned back against the wall and smiled. "So we wait until they are tired, then go out and clean up?"

"If the chance comes," Lan Xuanyu said.

"That is very reasonable." Qian Lei nodded solemnly. "I have always loved reasonable plans where other people do the tiring part first."

Liu Feng closed his eyes again.

"Rest," Lan Xuanyu reminded.

The three of them rested in the quietest place they could steal from the Maze.

Dorm 336 did not have such a place.

Their path did not end in a convenient dead end. It kept bending, offering choices that were not different enough to trust and sounds that were always either too close or too far.

Yan Shuo kept the Mantle low.

The longer they moved, the more grateful he became for the month's restraint training. It was easy to use fire in a corridor and feel powerful. It was harder to use fire in a corridor and not turn the corridor into a warning banner for every team nearby.

The pale-gold edges helped.

Not as holy light.

Not as a second skill.

As boundary.

The flame knew where to stop.

Once, Luo lifted a hand before Yan Shuo could speak. He crouched near a crossing and pointed to a smear on the floor. "Three students. Running. One heavy step dragging. They fought recently."

Song listened. "Voices ahead. More than one team."

The crash that followed proved him right.

Two passages ahead, soul power burst in a messy exchange. A wind blade struck stone. Someone shouted. A shield rang. Then a third voice joined, and the fight became worse.

Luo's eyes brightened. "Three teams?"

"Or two teams and a hazard," Song said.

"Either way, tired people."

Yan Shuo looked down the passage.

The old version of him would have felt heat rise in answer. The morning's first place, the new plus sign, Rank 19 sitting just beneath the second-ring threshold—all of it could have become a reason to charge and prove the numbers were not fragile.

His body did not move.

That was growth too.

"If we enter now, we become the third or fourth knife in a narrow room," Yan Shuo said. "If they hear us behind them, they may all turn."

"We can win," Luo said, not boasting this time. Testing.

"Maybe." Yan Shuo looked at Song. "Can we win without spending what we need for the next fight?"

Song answered honestly. "Unknown. Bad angle."

Luo grimaced. "I hate when unknown is correct."

Yan Shuo touched two fingers to the wall. A thread of warm light-lined fire moved across the stone, thin enough to be mistaken for a lamp reflection. The air drifting from the fighting passage wavered around it. Smoke, dust, and soul-power residue pushed outward in pulses.

"They are blocking each other," he said. "The exit on our side will clog if one retreats. We take the side turn, keep parallel, and listen for the winner. If the winner comes out exhausted and alone, we choose then. If not, we let them spend."

Luo stared at him. "You have become less fun."

"He has become harder to eliminate," Song said.

"That is a kind of fun," Yan Shuo said.

They took the side turn.

Behind them, the fight continued to tear at itself.

On the teachers' screen, Dorm 336's path drew a different kind of mark from Dorm 333's stillness. They were not hiding in one place. They were moving, but not being dragged. They avoided the obvious collision, used a narrow false heat trace to disrupt tracking, and kept the three-person shape intact.

Mu Zhongtian noticed.

So did Ji Hongbin.

Ji's eyes paused on Yan Shuo's screen for a moment.

"Better," he said.

Only one word.

For Ji Hongbin, it was almost praise.

Then his attention returned to the rubble screen.

The vice president watched both and smiled. "Your class has interesting children. The public strongest overall controls himself. The low-total protected team refuses to move. The Golden Lion King is hunting. Good. A test should not have only one answer."

"One answer is enough if it is correct," Ji Hongbin said.

"And if three are correct?"

"Then the Maze will decide which one remains correct under pressure."

The vice president laughed softly.

Elsewhere in the Maze, Lu Qianxun advanced at the front of his team.

He did not need the class-monitor title for people to look at him in combat. The Golden Lion King did that by itself. Even before he released his martial soul, his stride carried pressure: shoulders square, eyes forward, body held as if the corridor should make room because it knew better.

Ye Lingtong moved behind and to one side, light on her feet, the Celestial Dragon route giving her body a clean offensive readiness. She had changed since childhood too. Pride remained, but it had edges now instead of only heat.

Chang Jianyi kept the other side.

He was quieter than the two of them, and many classmates mistook quiet for being carried. Lu Qianxun did not. He had known Chang Jianyi before the Elite Junior Class, and he knew the boy's value was not in shouting. Chang saw timing. He saw when enthusiasm became danger.

They had already eliminated one team.

It had not been difficult.

That should have satisfied Lu Qianxun.

It did not.

The morning still sat behind his teeth.

Thirty-eight and a half points.

A plus sign.

For most students, that result would have been glory enough to sleep on.

For Lu Qianxun, it was second.

Half a point behind Yan Shuo.

And the team that escaped elimination for the term was not his team or Yan Shuo's team, but Dorm 333, the lowest-total team with four plus signs.

Lu Qianxun did not consider Lan Xuanyu's team his equal in ordinary strength. He had seen the numbers. Rank 14. Low total. Qian Lei's body was a joke outside Spiritual Power. Liu Feng was fast, but speed alone had limits.

Yet the academy had looked at them and seen something worth protecting.

The thought made his chest feel tight.

Not fear.

Challenge.

If the morning's rules had given other people strange meanings, then the afternoon would let him make the simplest meaning visible.

Win.

"Qianxun," Chang Jianyi said, "should we slow down?"

Ye Lingtong looked over immediately. "Why? We have not spent much. If we eliminate another team now, our result will be more stable."

Chang Jianyi did not argue with her tone. "We do not know how many teams remain near us. We also do not know whether other teams will join together if they hear us fighting. Everyone in this class is strong for our age. The difference exists, but not so much that we can ignore being surrounded."

Lu Qianxun stopped.

The corridor quieted around them.

Chang Jianyi continued, "We are one of the teams others will fear. Fear can make them avoid us, but it can also make them cooperate. If we keep chasing, we may enter a fight at a bad angle. Better to recover, listen, and choose."

Ye Lingtong frowned. "That sounds like waiting for luck."

"No," Chang Jianyi said. "It is refusing to give luck to someone else."

Lu Qianxun's eyes shifted.

Rational.

That was why he listened.

He wanted to keep moving, to crush one team after another until no one could talk about plus signs without also remembering who ruled the actual combat test. But wanting was not the same as leading. A Golden Lion King that charged into a trap was still trapped.

"We slow down," he decided. "We keep our peak state. First place must be stable."

Ye Lingtong still looked unwilling, but she nodded.

They moved forward more carefully.

After another turn, Ye Lingtong lifted a hand. "There."

Ahead, the passage opened near a crossroad. One side led to a dead end, or appeared to. A pile of broken stone lay in the passage, large enough to block most of the view.

Chang Jianyi studied it. "Rubble. It may be from a Maze guard."

"A dead end with cover," Ye Lingtong said. "We can rest there for a moment. If someone comes from the crossroad, we will hear them first."

Lu Qianxun looked at the stone pile.

It was not a bad place.

"Go," he said.

Behind the rubble, Lan Xuanyu opened his eyes.

Qian Lei's mouth tightened.

Liu Feng's fingers closed around the White Dragon Spear.

The voices outside were clear enough now.

Lu Qianxun.

Ye Lingtong.

Chang Jianyi.

Of all the teams that could have chosen the dead end, the strongest rival-team block had walked directly toward them.

Qian Lei's expression said what his mouth wisely did not: what kind of luck was this?

Liu Feng looked at Lan Xuanyu.

Not with panic.

With the question that mattered.

What now?

Lan Xuanyu inhaled once.

The plan of waiting had not failed. It had simply become a different plan sooner than he wanted. They had preserved strength. They had terrain. They had a weapon. They had surprise for only a moment longer.

That moment had to be spent correctly.

He lifted both hands.

One index finger drew the shape of a door toward Qian Lei.

Qian Lei's face turned bitter at once, but the Summoning Coin was already appearing in his palm.

Lan Xuanyu turned to Liu Feng and nodded.

Gold and silver patterned Blue Silver Grass began to unfurl in the dimness behind the rubble.

Outside, Lu Qianxun's footsteps came closer.

The quietest place in the Maze was about to break.
