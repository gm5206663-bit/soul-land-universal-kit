# Chapter 40: The Scan Before the Roar

Lan Xuanyu discovered that eight hours of sleep could become a trap.

When he had collapsed onto his bed the night before, he had thought eight hours sounded like mercy. Teacher Ji had said the number with a solemnity that made it seem generous. Master Yin had not appeared to steal any of those hours back for warship-control correction. Liu Feng had even kicked Qian Lei once under the table when Qian Lei tried to ask whether Lan wanted to review formation plans before sleeping.

So Lan had trusted sleep.

Sleep betrayed him by becoming too deep.

The first thing he heard was not an alarm.

It was Qian Lei.

"Xuanyu! Xuanyu, wake up! If you sleep through the preliminary test, Teacher Ji will turn you into educational material!"

Lan's eyes opened halfway.

The ceiling above him looked unfamiliar for three full breaths, not because it had changed but because his mind had misplaced the concept of ceiling. His body still believed it was somewhere between a fire-control drill, a warship simulation, and the place inside the pillow where people without preliminary tests were allowed to live.

"What time?" he mumbled.

"The time when normal people are already lined up," Qian Lei said. "Get up!"

Liu Feng's voice came from near the door. "We still have time. Barely."

Lan sat up.

The movement was a mistake.

His head floated, then returned with a small thud inside his skull. Soul power moved more smoothly than before Rank20, but smoothness did not mean endless. The past days had scraped him thin: Ji Hongbin forcing fire and water to switch until his thoughts smelled of steam; Yin Tianfan forcing vector corrections until Lan saw thrust angles in dreams; meditation stitching everything back together just in time to be torn open again.

"Preliminary test," Lan said, remembering at last.

"Yes," Qian Lei said with relief. "That thing whose name contains the word preliminary but whose consequences probably contain the word death."

"Death is later," Liu Feng said. "Today should be measurement."

"That is what they want us to think."

Lan washed quickly, tied his uniform collar with fingers that still wanted to be asleep, and followed them out. The corridor was full of students moving with the strange stiffness of children pretending not to be nervous. No one wanted to say they were afraid of a preliminary test. No one believed Heaven Luo Academy would gather the entire Elite Junior Class for something meaningless.

At the turn toward the training ground, Qian Lei slowed.

"Look," he whispered.

Lan looked.

Yan Shuo was ahead with Song Yichen and Luo Haoran.

Even at a distance, Yan stood out. Publicly, he was still Yan Shuo: class monitor, boy's uniform, controlled posture, clipped copper-red hair kept practical and restrained. Yet after the full Fire Phoenix awakening, restraint had become something people could see working. Morning light caught the hair and made red-gold warmth run along the strands before discipline muted it again. His face was too vivid to be dismissed as ordinary handsome; his eyes held a bright phoenix clarity that made younger students glance once, look away, then sneak a second look when they thought no one noticed.

Lan did not understand the secret underneath that strain.

He only knew that Yan looked awake.

That seemed unfair.

Yan turned as if sensing the stare. His gaze passed over Qian Lei, Liu Feng, and finally Lan.

The corner of his mouth lifted.

"You look alive," Yan said.

"Barely," Lan answered.

Song Yichen studied him with the expression of someone judging whether a shield would need to catch a falling teammate from another dormitory. "Your face says Teacher Ji's mercy was not merciful enough."

"Teacher Ji's mercy comes with homework," Lan said.

Luo Haoran nodded gravely. "Then it is not mercy. It is disguised scheduling."

Qian Lei pointed at him. "See? Dorm336 understands."

Yan's expression softened for only a breath before the monitor mask returned. "Line up quickly. President Zhang is already there."

That woke Lan more effectively than Qian Lei.

The large training ground had been cleared.

All twenty-one students of the Elite Junior Class were present. The teachers stood along one side: Mu Zhongtian, Ji Hongbin, Yin Tianfan, and several others who rarely appeared together unless the academy wanted students to understand that the matter was serious. At the center stood President Zhang Zekai, dignified as always, his expression carrying pride, tension, and something Lan could not yet name.

The line was arranged by current Soul Power ranking.

That, more than any speech, revealed how much the class had changed.

Yan Shuo stood first.

Lu Qianxun stood second.

No one called it strange anymore.

A year earlier, some students would have looked toward Lu automatically whenever the word strongest appeared. Lu still carried the heavy pressure of the Golden Lion King. He was tall, steady, and fierce even when silent. But Yan's emergence from the Purple Flame cocoon and the year of training afterward had rewritten the public center of the class. Rank, wings, aura, combat record, and the academy's own monitor badge all pointed in one direction.

Lu's jaw was firm, not resentful enough to be ugly and not calm enough to be careless. He accepted position the way he accepted opponents: by standing harder.

Lan stood near the end.

Not the very last by future possibility.

By Soul Power, yes.

Rank20, newly crossed, still stood at the bottom edge of the class. He had two yellow rings now. His Spiritual Power was strong. His martial souls had evolved. His teachers had changed their tone. None of that altered the number beside Soul Power.

Qian Lei leaned toward him and whispered, "If the machine measures sleepiness, you will be first."

Lan did not have strength to reply.

President Zhang lifted his hand.

The students quieted.

"Students," he said, "today's preliminary test concerns the next stage of your growth. You need not be nervous. Follow instructions and display your real level."

No one believed the instruction not to be nervous.

A young man stepped out from beside him.

He looked only twenty or so, handsome and composed, wearing a white uniform with green decorative lines. The lines were simple, but on him they carried a clean pride that made the training ground feel suddenly farther from Heaven Luo Academy than its walls suggested. He held a metal sphere in one hand, and his gaze passed over the students with interest rather than disdain.

President Zhang said, "This is Chen Yilei, a disciple from Shrek Academy's inner court. He will conduct your preliminary test."

The words Shrek Academy moved through the students like wind through dry grass.

Some gasped aloud.

Lan blinked slowly.

He had heard the name on Heaven Dou from Teacher Nana. He had guessed some of the direction when she said Dong Qianqiu was preparing for Shrek and when Ji Hongbin's recent training became less like preparation and more like siege warfare. But guessing a mountain was ahead was different from seeing its shadow fall over the schoolyard.

Chen Yilei first turned toward the teachers.

"Teacher Yin. Teacher Ji."

He bowed slightly, and the respect in the movement was real.

Yin Tianfan's round face twitched as if being called teacher by a Shrek inner court disciple made him itch. "No need for that. I am no longer there."

Ji Hongbin accepted the greeting more calmly. "Conduct the test."

The students heard enough to become even quieter.

Chen Yilei smiled. "The preliminary test is simple. I will measure bone age, Soul Power, and Spiritual Power. Those twelve years old and below must have at least Rank20 Soul Power to qualify. Those whose bone age has reached thirteen must have at least Rank30. The standard is harsh because the road after this is harsher. If you fail this preliminary requirement, you will not enter the qualifier."

Simple.

Lan decided that adults used the word simple when they wanted children to panic efficiently.

Chen raised the metal sphere. "Step forward one by one. Hand on the sphere. Release martial soul if asked. The device reports only the categories required for this test. It is not a full medical examination."

That last sentence passed over most students.

It mattered more than they knew.

Yan Shuo stepped forward first.

The training ground seemed to inhale.

Lan watched from the back of the line as Yan placed his right hand on the metal sphere. His posture was correct, neither stiff nor casual. Copper-red hair lay neat against his forehead. The public mask held: boy's name, boy's uniform, controlled voice, restrained aura. If the world wanted paperwork, it would see paperwork.

The sphere lit.

Lines of white light swept across Yan's hand, wrist, arm, chest, and head. They did not linger where they were not meant to linger. They read bone, meridian pressure, Soul Power, Spiritual Power fluctuation, and returned to the sphere.

Chen Yilei looked down.

His smile stopped moving.

For the first time since he arrived, the Shrek inner court disciple looked his age.

Young enough to be surprised.

President Zhang had clearly seen the number before in reports. That did not stop his shoulders from tightening with pride.

Chen read aloud because procedure allowed no hiding.

"Bone age: not yet eleven. Soul Power: Rank39. Spiritual Power: 962 points. Spirit Sea Realm."

Silence fell so completely that Lan heard Qian Lei swallow.

Everyone in the class already knew Yan was terrifying.

They had seen the wings. They had felt Fire Phoenix pressure in training. They knew he stood at the front because strength had placed him there. But a formal Shrek preliminary test turning rumor and academy reports into clean numbers made the knowledge heavier.

Not yet eleven.

Rank39.

SP962.

Chen Yilei lifted his eyes to Yan again.

"Release your martial soul. Controlled output only."

"Yes."

Yan's answer was calm.

Red-gold light unfolded behind him.

He did not flare. He did not open full wings across the training ground or let the Fire Phoenix cry out. The release was deliberately narrow, a disciplined line of heat and noble bird-pressure kept within a few paces. Even so, the air brightened. Copper-red hair caught flame from inside and shone phoenix red-gold for a breath. Three purple rings rose around him, steady and clean, the colour deep enough that several students forgot to breathe.

Song Yichen's jaw tightened with old pride.

Luo Haoran's eyes flicked automatically along the edge of the aura, measuring how far the heat reached and where a teammate could stand safely.

Lan felt the warmth brush his face from all the way at the back and thought of the corridor after his return: Yan asking Rank20, Yan accepting what Lan could not explain, Yan standing in public with a brilliance that attracted eyes and carried burdens no one discussed.

Chen Yilei's gaze sharpened.

A Fire Phoenix with three purple rings before eleven would be enough to make any academy write reports in red ink. But Shrek's visitor did not shout or promise anything. He only looked carefully: at the restraint, the ring colour, the breath, the way Yan folded power back before pride could waste it.

"Passed," Chen said at last. "Special observation mark. You may return."

Yan withdrew the martial soul and stepped back into line.

Only when the red-gold light vanished did the training ground remember how cool morning air was supposed to feel.

Lu Qianxun stepped forward next.

In another world, perhaps he would have been first.

In this one, second position did not make him small.

The sphere lit again.

Chen read, "Bone age: thirteen. Soul Power: Rank33. Spiritual Power: 241 points. Requirement met. Passed."

Lu released the Golden Lion King pressure briefly when asked. Golden light gathered around him, majestic and dense, carrying a roar that remained behind his teeth because he controlled it well. Three rings rose. The result was strong, clean, and worthy of respect.

The class did respect it.

Yan's shadow did not erase Lu.

It simply made everyone understand that there were different kinds of mountains.

One by one, the others stepped forward.

Chang Jianyi passed with the calm of someone whose support skills hid sharp edges. Ye Lingtong passed, chin high, her Celestial body-line giving the scan a bright white steadiness that made Chen glance twice. Jin Xiang and his teammates passed. The middle of the line exhaled as names survived the machine.

Dorm336's remaining two members stepped forward after the higher-ranked core of the class.

Song Yichen placed his hand on the sphere with shoulders squared as if the device were an opponent trying to push him back.

"Soul Power: Rank29. Spiritual Power: 188 points. Passed."

The numbers were not Yan's.

They did not need to be.

Song's Stoneplate Tortoise aura sank into the ground when he released it, defensive and stubborn. Lan suddenly understood why Yan's team did not look like a phoenix with two shadows. Song was a return point. A wall. A place a flyer could trust beneath falling heat.

Luo Haoran was next.

"Soul Power: Rank28. Spiritual Power: 226 points. Passed."

Threadwind Mantis appeared in a faint green line, sharp and quick, the pressure light but difficult to catch. Luo withdrew it almost immediately, eyes already reading the reactions around him instead of admiring his own result.

Qian Lei nudged Lan. "Their support is growing too fast."

"They train too," Lan said.

"I know. It is inconsiderate of them."

Liu Feng's turn came soon after.

He passed.

His White Dragon Spear appeared with a clean flash, and Chen Yilei's attention paused on the spear's unusual quality for a moment before he marked the result. Qian Lei walked forward next, face serious for once. His Spiritual Power result made Chen's brows rise, and when the Summoning Coin appeared, the Shrek disciple's interest sharpened further.

"Passed," Chen said.

Qian Lei returned to Lan with his seriousness already cracking. "Did you see his expression? I am definitely rare."

"Rare does not mean safe," Liu Feng said.

"I choose to hear only the first half."

At last, Lan Xuanyu stood alone at the end.

The line felt much longer when he had to leave it.

He walked forward under twenty sets of classmate eyes and too many adult eyes. Sleep tried to pull at his thoughts again. His body wanted another hour. His pride refused to shuffle, so he forced his steps to be steady.

Chen Yilei watched him approach.

"Are you feeling unwell?"

Lan blinked. "No. I slept too deeply."

Several students near the line tried not to laugh.

Ji Hongbin's expression suggested none of them valued life.

The laughter died quietly.

Lan placed his hand on the sphere.

Cold light swept through him.

Not like Nana's silver light.

Not like soul power.

This was mechanical, indifferent, and very precise. It read bone age, meridian strength, Soul Power, Spiritual Power. It did not care that his chest held a gold-silver vortex. It did not know the difference between a safe story and a truth he had promised not to expose.

The sphere hummed.

Chen looked down.

Then looked again.

"Bone age... not yet eleven. Very close, but not yet." He lifted his head toward President Zhang. "His file says he should be nearly twelve."

The training ground shifted.

Ji Hongbin and Yin Tianfan looked at each other.

Lan's sleepiness thinned.

This question he had expected only in the loose way one expected rain someday. He had not expected the machine to put the cloud directly over his head.

"My parents may have made a mistake when recording," Lan said honestly enough to keep the lie simple. "I am not very clear about it."

He did not add the illness excuse.

No one asked yet.

Chen Yilei studied him for one breath, then returned to the device. Shrek disciples were not naive; neither were they here to pry into every family's protective record on the training ground. Bone age mattered for the requirement. The discrepancy mattered for evaluation. The reason could be checked later if necessary.

"Soul Power: Rank20," Chen said. "Spiritual Power: 499 points."

Qian Lei's mouth opened.

No sound came out.

That was rare enough to deserve its own record.

Lan heard murmurs ripple through the class.

SP499.

One point below 500.

For Lan, who had been mocked by his own slow Soul Power for years, the number felt like someone had placed a different kind of weight in the other side of the scale. Rank20 was low here. Spiritual Power nearly at 500 before eleven was not low.

Chen's eyes narrowed. "You have just reached Rank20?"

"Yes."

"Release your martial soul and soul rings."

Lan lifted his left hand.

Silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass emerged.

It was no longer the fragile grass many classmates remembered from the early years. The strands had grown tougher, rounder, vine-like, with silver patterns winding through them in structured oval nets. Two yellow rings rose from beneath his feet and circled him quietly.

The reaction this time was different from Yan's.

Yan's numbers had struck like a bell: huge, bright, undeniable.

Lan's two yellow rings landed like a question with too many answers.

Everyone remembered his first ring.

White.

A ten-year ring.

Now both were yellow.

Lu Qianxun stared harder than anyone. After the last team battle, he had already learned that Lan Xuanyu's value could not be measured by Soul Power alone. Seeing Rank20, SP499, and two yellow rings before eleven made his earlier conclusion settle into something more dangerous.

Lan Xuanyu could not be judged lazily.

At the front of the line, Yan's expression changed only a little.

Enough for Lan to see relief first.

Then worry.

Then the careful closing of questions.

Yan knew the public facts now. Rank20. SP499. Two yellow rings. Younger than the file.

He did not know why the rings had appeared naturally. He did not know about Nana's warning. He did not know about the vortex or the rainbow point or the self-fusion that had saved Lan from swelling apart.

The ignorance stood between them like a thin glass wall.

Lan was grateful for it and hated it at the same time.

Chen Yilei examined the grass, then marked the result.

"Passed."

The word entered Lan's chest and stayed there.

Passed.

Not safe.

Not chosen.

Not Shrek.

But the first door had opened.

When the last result was recorded, all twenty-one Elite Junior Class students had met the preliminary requirement. President Zhang's smile grew bright enough that some teachers looked embarrassed on his behalf, though none of them could blame him. For a branch academy on Heaven Luo Planet, this result was already an achievement.

Chen Yilei put away the metal sphere.

"Your qualifier will begin in three days," he said. "Rest well. Prepare properly. Do not assume that today's pass means anything more than permission to continue."

Permission to continue.

The phrase sounded light until Lan considered what kind of road needed permission at the first stone.

The students were dismissed, but Lan did not escape.

Ji Hongbin's voice caught him before he could follow Qian Lei and Liu Feng.

"Lan Xuanyu. Stay."

Lan stopped obediently.

Qian Lei sent him a look of sympathy so exaggerated it became useless. Liu Feng pulled Qian away before sympathy became a speech.

Yan paused too, farther ahead.

For a breath, his eyes met Lan's.

Lan gave a tiny shake of his head.

Not now.

Yan understood. His mouth tightened, but he turned away with Song and Luo.

Lan followed Ji Hongbin and Yin Tianfan to the side of the training ground.

Yin Tianfan spoke first. "Your bone age is not even eleven?"

Lan looked at the ground. "I am not sure about the record. Father handled it when I entered school."

Ji Hongbin's eyes were sharp enough to cut paper. "You told the school you were seven when you first enrolled, yes? Illness, late entry."

Lan's shoulders stiffened.

He had forgotten that teachers remembered old files better than children remembered old lies.

"Yes."

Yin Tianfan inhaled slowly, then rubbed his forehead. "So you are a year younger than we thought."

Ji Hongbin's expression changed in a way Lan did not expect.

Not anger.

Calculation.

Then something close to relief.

"That improves your future chances," Ji said. "Shrek's age limits are ruthless. If your bone age is not yet eleven, then even if you fail this year, next year you may still have a chance under the younger threshold."

Lan looked up quickly. "Then should I wait?"

Yin Tianfan snorted. "If you wait, you get one more year of training. You also lose the experience of this attempt and possibly your current team's rhythm. There is no answer that costs nothing."

Ji Hongbin nodded. "I recommend you try. Do not think of failure as waste. Shrek's qualifier itself is a rare tempering opportunity. If you pass, you go forward. If you fail, you learn what Shrek truly tests and prepare next year."

Lan thought of Nana on Heaven Dou.

Work hard to enter Shrek Academy.

He thought of Dong Qianqiu, the dark-blue-haired girl whose anger and talent both pointed toward the same school.

He thought of Yan at the front of the line, Rank39 and not yet eleven, carrying red-gold light under public restraint.

He thought of himself at the back, Rank20 and sleepy, with a vortex no scanner could name.

"I want to try," Lan said.

Ji Hongbin's eyes softened almost invisibly. "Good. Then from now until the qualifier, you recover first. Training continues, but not in a way that empties you before the start."

Yin Tianfan looked at him suspiciously. "You understand that sentence applies to you too, Old Ji?"

"I understand restraint better than you understand moderation."

"Your restraint nearly made him sleep through the test."

"Your moderation launched him into space drills after elemental training."

Lan lowered his head and let the teachers argue.

It was safer when they aimed at each other.

The three days before the qualifier felt shorter than three days should.

Heaven Luo Academy changed its rhythm. Classes continued in name, but everyone knew the Elite Junior Class had entered a different pressure chamber. Teachers lowered ordinary assignments and increased review, rest, tactical discussion, and warnings that somehow made rest feel like another kind of exam.

Lan did sleep more.

Not enough to become lazy.

Enough to remember that thinking required a body.

Ji Hongbin corrected his water-fire switching in shorter sessions now. He emphasized not power, but response: how quickly Lan could sense heat, how little ice he needed to change footing, how to use fire without alarming himself, how to stop thinking of opposing elements as enemies and start treating them as tools that demanded manners.

Yin Tianfan reduced real-warship time but increased mental drills. "If the test is simulated, it will still punish stupidity," he said. "Simulation does not mean fake. It only means you survive the lesson. Maybe."

Qian Lei claimed this was not comforting.

Liu Feng spent most of the time checking routes, spear angles, and how far Lan's golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass support could reach now without direct contact. The new range changed everything. It meant Liu Feng did not have to remain glued to Lan's side to receive amplification. It meant their formation could stretch.

It also meant mistakes could happen at longer distances.

Dorm336 trained nearby more than once.

Yan's team did not use the three days to celebrate the scan.

If anything, the formal numbers made Song and Luo train harder. A teammate at Rank39 could make people forget that formation weight did not vanish just because the center was strong. Song drilled shield-load changes until his Stoneplate Tortoise defense could catch heat from above and pressure from below without collapsing his stance. Luo mapped sensor lanes around Yan's aerial path, calling angles aloud until the words became rhythm.

Yan practiced folding wings halfway, then fully, then not releasing them at all while maintaining Fire Phoenix pressure inside his body. His low-output flame control stayed steady. High output remained restricted. The third ring did not become a toy because a Shrek scanner had admired it.

On the second evening, Lan found Yan alone near the west corridor water dispenser, filling a bottle slowly because the machine had chosen that moment to become dramatic.

For a while, neither spoke.

The corridor lights made Yan's hair look deeper copper, then red-gold where soul power warmed under the skin. The boy cover held because posture, uniform, habit, and years of practice held it. Still, Lan noticed again how many people looked when Yan entered a space now.

He did not know what to do with that noticing.

So he said, "Your number scared Qian Lei."

Yan glanced sideways. "Only Qian Lei?"

"Liu Feng was quiet. That usually means he was scared efficiently."

Yan laughed under his breath.

The sound loosened something in Lan's chest.

Then Yan said, "Your age scared the teachers."

Lan winced. "I didn't mean to."

"I know." Yan watched water crawl into the bottle. "Your family must have had a reason."

Lan looked at him quickly.

Yan's face remained calm.

No demand.

No clever trap.

Only trust pressed into a shape that would not ask him to break a promise in a corridor.

"Probably," Lan said softly. "I was little. I don't remember much."

"Then don't explain what you don't know."

The water dispenser clicked off.

Yan closed the bottle.

Lan wanted to say thank you.

The words felt too large for the hallway and too small for what he meant.

Instead he said, "If Qian Lei finds out I am younger than he thought, he may demand I call him elder brother."

Yan's mouth curved. "Then pass Shrek before he thinks of it."

"That is your solution?"

"It solves many problems."

Lan laughed.

The next morning, Ji Hongbin gathered the Elite Junior Class.

No metal sphere waited this time.

That made it worse.

The students stood in their teams of three. Dorm333: Lan Xuanyu, Liu Feng, Qian Lei. Dorm336: Yan Shuo, Song Yichen, Luo Haoran. Lu Qianxun's team: Lu, Ye Lingtong, Chang Jianyi. Jin Xiang's team and the others stood nearby, each group trying to appear more prepared than they felt.

Ji Hongbin's gaze passed over them.

When he spoke, his voice was not loud.

It did not need to be.

"The goal of the Elite Junior Class has always been one place. The highest ivory tower for Soul Masters in the Federation. Shrek Academy."

This time the name did not rustle.

It struck.

Even after the preliminary test, hearing Ji Hongbin say it plainly made the students' eyes change. Some flushed with excitement. Some stiffened under sudden fear. Some looked toward President Zhang's building as if the entire academy had secretly become a ladder while they were not looking.

Ji continued, "Shrek Academy was founded thirty thousand years ago. It has produced generation after generation of true powerhouses. The Shrek Seven Devils are legends tied to the fate of the continent and the Federation. The Tang Sect and Shrek have shaped history more than any ordinary academy can compare with. You have all heard fragments. Today you should understand why you have suffered these years."

Qian Lei whispered, "I knew suffering had a suspiciously famous smell."

Liu Feng elbowed him.

Ji Hongbin's eyes passed over them.

Qian Lei became a statue.

"Every year," Ji said, "Shrek recruits from across the entire Federation. The number of young people who meet the basic right to compete is enormous. More than a hundred thousand may begin in different ways. The final number admitted is about thirty. Heaven Luo Planet's qualifier is only one of the first filters. Only the top hundred on our planet will continue toward the later stages."

Thirty.

Out of countless geniuses.

The number made Lan's stomach feel suddenly hollow.

He had known Shrek was difficult.

Numbers made difficulty less romantic.

Ji did not soften it. "The academy will reward success. If any of you enter Shrek, the teacher responsible for your team will receive the right to study at Shrek for one year. That is an opportunity even adults dream of. But do not mistake this as only the academy's ambition. Whether you succeed or fail, participating will show you how wide the Federation is. You will learn where you truly stand."

His gaze settled on Lan for half a breath, then moved to Yan, then Lu, then the rest.

"Do not hold anything back."

Lu Qianxun raised his hand.

"Teacher Ji. The assessments are by teams of three. Does that mean if one member fails, the whole team fails?"

The question carried weight because every strong student had weaker partners by some measure.

Yan did not move, but Song's fingers tightened once around his shield strap. Luo's eyes narrowed thoughtfully. In Dorm333, Qian Lei looked suddenly innocent, which was always suspicious.

Ji Hongbin answered, "Usually, yes. Shrek values teams. If you cannot carry, support, adapt to, or coordinate with your teammates, high individual rank may not save you. The three-person structure used by the Elite Junior Class was established for this reason."

A murmur passed through the teams.

Song exhaled slowly.

Yan said quietly, not for the whole class but clearly enough for his team, "A flyer who loses his landing points has already failed. We go together."

Song's grip loosened.

Luo nodded once. "Then I will keep calling the lanes."

Across the field, Lan heard the words and remembered what Ji had said: teams.

Not numbers standing side by side.

Teams.

Ji continued, "Do not assume the assessment will be a simple fight. Shrek assesses speed, strength, Soul Power, Spiritual Power, battle experience, wisdom, temperament, teamwork, resilience, and things I cannot predict. Sometimes unusual ability matters more than a beautiful report. Sometimes one correct decision matters more than defeating ten opponents. Shrek recruits monsters, not children who only know how to be high-ranked."

Lan felt Qian Lei straighten beside him.

Rare did not mean safe.

But rare might matter.

Ji's voice deepened slightly. "And remember this: strength without heart is not Shrek's standard. A Soul Master who cannot stand upright as a person will eventually collapse no matter how high his rank climbs."

The training ground became very quiet.

Lan did not know exactly what future trials those words pointed toward.

He only knew they felt less like encouragement and more like a warning written on a door he had not opened yet.

Ji Hongbin nodded to Mu Zhongtian.

"Enter the simulation pods. The qualifier begins now."

The pods waited in ordered rows.

Lan had entered simulation systems before, but this time the familiar equipment seemed to have grown teeth. He lay down, connected the sensors, and watched the canopy close above him. Darkness fell. Then light.

When his vision returned, he stood in the academy training ground again.

For a moment he thought the system had failed.

Then he saw Ji Hongbin and Mu Zhongtian standing ahead of the students.

Qian Lei looked down at his own hands. "Did we enter the simulation just to come back out? That is efficient in a useless way."

"This is still the simulation," Lan said.

He could feel the difference: a faint smoothness at the edge of perception, the world too responsive to thought and sensor logic.

Mu Zhongtian led them toward the side of the training ground where a counter had appeared. It had not existed in the real academy space. Behind it sat a young woman in green and white, smiling as if twenty-one nervous children were exactly the morning's entertainment she had ordered.

"Welcome," she said. "I am Ling Yiyi. I will guide this stage of your qualifier."

The students looked at her.

She looked back with perfect friendliness.

That made Lan distrust her immediately.

Ling Yiyi lifted her hand.

Light spread under the students' feet before anyone could ask a question.

Ji Hongbin and Mu Zhongtian remained outside the circle.

Qian Lei yelped, "Wait, no briefing?"

Ling Yiyi smiled. "Briefing comes after transfer. Good luck."

The world folded.

After the students vanished, Mu Zhongtian released the breath he had been holding.

"Old Ji," he said, voice low, "which team do you think has the greatest chance?"

Ji Hongbin did not answer immediately.

In canon's shape, perhaps the question would have pointed first toward Lu Qianxun. Golden Lion King, three rings, strong conventional team. It still deserved respect.

But this class was no longer that simple.

"If you ask which team has the clearest raw public strength," Ji said, "Yan Shuo's team. Rank39 before eleven, Spirit Sea, full Fire Phoenix, and two teammates who have learned to support rather than merely follow. Shrek will not ignore that. No sane academy would."

Mu nodded. "And if I ask which team makes you uneasy?"

Ji's mouth twitched. "Lan Xuanyu's."

Mu Zhongtian looked toward the empty transfer circle. "Because of the two yellow rings?"

"Because of too many things that do not line up neatly," Ji said. "Twin-side Blue Silver Grass mutation. Water and fire on one martial soul. SP499 before eleven. Qian Lei's rare summoning. Liu Feng's speed under Lan's amplification. Their team is weak by ordinary rank comparison and troublesome by every other measure."

"What about Lu Qianxun?"

"Excellent," Ji said without hesitation. "But more conventional. Shrek does not reject excellence. It simply becomes harder to stand out when your excellence can be described in one sentence."

Mu Zhongtian smiled faintly. "So we have a phoenix, a lion, and a strange blade of grass."

"No," Ji said. "We have children. Shrek will decide which of them can become monsters without first becoming fools."

The transferred students landed in heat.

Lan's feet struck dry ground. He crouched automatically, one hand touching red-brown dust. The training ground had vanished. Around them stretched a barren valley hemmed in by steep mountains. The earth was cracked in places, dark red beneath the surface, as if old fire lived underground and breathed through stone. Heat shimmered in the air. Far above, the sky looked pale and merciless.

All twenty-one students had arrived together.

Ling Yiyi stood ahead of them as though she had merely walked there.

"This qualifier is a survival mission," she said. "You will act in your groups of three. The valley contains aggressive soul beasts. The longer your group survives, the higher your survival score. Killing soul beasts will also grant points, and different soul beasts are worth different amounts. Once you die, your qualifier ends."

She smiled.

"That is all. I wish everyone good results."

"That's all?" Qian Lei blurted.

Ling Yiyi's smile brightened.

Then she disappeared.

The valley became very large.

For two breaths, no one moved.

Jin Xiang looked around and laughed once, too loudly. "Survival? We have done survival training before. There are twenty-one of us. If we stay together, what soul beast can—"

"No," Yan said.

The single word cut cleanly through the valley heat.

Heads turned toward the class monitor.

Yan's expression had changed. The public beauty and red-gold brightness that drew eyes in the academy now looked sharper under the barren light, but his aura remained restrained. He had not released the Fire Phoenix. He did not need to. His attention was on the mountains, the ground, the empty air between stones.

Lan felt it too.

Wrongness.

Shrek would not place them together because together was safe.

Qian Lei whispered, "Something is off. If the mission is by teams, why drop everyone in one place?"

"Because the first mistake is probably to stay that way," Lan said.

Yan glanced back.

For an instant, their eyes met.

Agreement arrived without discussion.

Yan raised his voice. "Teams spread out. Keep visual contact, but do not cluster. Find cover first. Luo, left high lane. Song, shield ready."

Lu Qianxun did not argue.

He gave his own team a sharp gesture. "Move."

The class broke into motion.

Not fast enough.

The roar came from deeper in the valley.

It was not a normal beast cry. It rolled through stone and heat, low at first and then vast, as if something underground had remembered anger and pushed it up through every crack in the earth. Dust jumped under Lan's feet. Several students froze. A few staggered.

"Cover!" Lan shouted.

"Behind the rocks!" Yan's voice overlapped his.

Liu Feng seized Qian Lei by the collar before Qian could decide whether panic counted as movement. Lan slid behind a jagged boulder, silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass already stirring at his left palm. Across the broken ground, Yan's team vanished behind a ridge of dark stone, Song's shield rising like a small wall while Luo dropped low to read the line of approach. Lu Qianxun's Golden Lion King aura flickered to life near another outcrop.

The roar sounded again.

Closer.

Hotter.

Lan pressed his back to the stone and felt heat tremble through it.

The preliminary scan had named numbers.

The qualifier did not care about names.

Somewhere beyond the dust, claws began to strike the valley floor.
