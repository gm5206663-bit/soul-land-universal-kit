# Chapter 15: The First Coin

The first thing Yan Shuo smelled was dust.

Not hospital antiseptic. Not cafeteria soup. Not the clean metal of Heaven Luo Academy's training field.

Dust.

Old stone dust, sun-warmed earth, distant cooking smoke, and the strange dry smell of streets that had never truly existed but had still been worn smooth by thousands of passing feet.

He opened his eyes.

A narrow street stretched in front of him. Wooden signs hung from tiled eaves. Red lanterns swayed above shopfronts. The sky was too blue, the shadows too sharp, and the robe on his body felt rough against his neck in a way no simple illusion should have managed.

Dawnflame Kite stirred inside the ring channel.

The pulse was not fear.

It was a question.

Yan Shuo flexed his fingers.

His fingers flexed back.

There was weight in them. Heat in the joints. The old ache at his shoulders existed here, faint but precise, as if Soul World had copied not only his strength but the memory of every place that had been strained before.

"Do not stare like a tourist," Song Yichen said.

Yan Shuo turned.

Dorm 336 had arrived together.

Song Yichen stood in front, already frowning at the street as if the ancient buildings had failed a formation inspection. He wore the same beginner robe as Yan Shuo, but somehow made it look like academy uniform. Luo Haoran crouched beside a stone post, tapping it with one finger, then the back of his hand, then his knuckle.

"Feels real," Luo said. "Too real. If I hit my head here, do I wake up wiser?"

"You wake up poorer," Song said. "Pain sensitivity here cannot go below thirty percent. The teacher said carelessness can affect the real body. Treat the ground as ground, walls as walls, and teeth as teeth."

Luo looked offended. "I was going to treat teeth as friendly ornaments. Thank you, front wall."

Yan Shuo rolled one shoulder.

The robe shifted. Beneath it, the invisible wing-root muscles along his back answered a heartbeat earlier than usual. The Soul World data did not give him wings. It did not smooth away limits. It brought his real limits with him and made them honest.

Good, his body seemed to decide.

Honest things could be trained.

A group of students appeared farther down the street. Some laughed as if they had entered a festival. Others looked pale, still adjusting to the too-sharp realism. Dorm 333 stood near the training-ground road: Liu Feng thin and impatient, Qian Lei already talking with both hands, Lan Xuanyu quiet between them in a plain robe that made his face look even calmer.

Yan Shuo did not walk over.

Lan Xuanyu glanced once in his direction.

It was enough.

Not his dorm.

Not his fight.

Still the same class.

Qian Lei pointed grandly toward a building with a sign over the entrance.

Training Ground.

Luo followed Yan Shuo's gaze and grinned. "Your friend looks like he has been adopted by trouble."

"Trouble should be grateful," Yan Shuo said.

Song gave both of them a look. "We are not here to watch Dorm 333. We are here to learn whether Dorm 336 can function for longer than three breaths."

"Three breaths is a cruel standard," Luo muttered.

"Then breathe carefully."

They followed the street toward the training ground, but not close enough to merge with Dorm 333. The building looked small from outside. That lasted only until they stepped through the door.

Inside, space unfolded.

The hall was wide enough to hold counters, waiting teams, and a ceiling that should have broken through the roof. Ten counters stood in a line. Behind each one, a calm attendant waited with the expression of someone who had seen generations of children pay money to be bitten by simulated spirit beasts.

At the counter ahead, Qian Lei produced a shiny soul coin with the tragic reluctance of a person parting from family.

Yan Shuo saw Lan Xuanyu ask something. Liu Feng answered. Qian Lei clutched the coin once before handing it over.

Then light gathered around Dorm 333.

They vanished.

Song stopped at a different counter.

"Junior training scenario," he said.

The attendant smiled. "One soul coin."

Song took out his own coin.

Luo leaned toward Yan Shuo and whispered, "He looks calm because it is his coin. If we lose, he will become a vengeful tortoise ghost."

"I heard that," Song said.

"Excellent hearing is an important front-line trait."

The coin left Song's fingers.

For one breath, Yan Shuo thought of money outside Soul World: his father's careful accounts, his mother's practical silence when something cost too much, the Spirit Pagoda bill that had made the flawed Dawnflame Kite possible because it had been flawed enough to be within reach.

A coin was never only a coin.

The attendant's voice became distant and clear.

"Junior training scenario prepared. Team link confirmed: Dorm 336. Please enter."

The floor disappeared.

For the second time that day, another world opened under Yan Shuo's feet.


Dorm 333 landed in a colosseum.

Lan Xuanyu's first impression was space, noise, and four closed steel gates.

The stands around them were full of muffled cheers that did not belong to real throats. The ground was hard under his thin shoes. Heat waited behind the gates before anything emerged.

Qian Lei spoke quickly, explaining cost, reward, and danger as if speed could make the situation less expensive.

One coin to enter.

Win, and they received the coin back plus one more.

Lose, and the coin was gone.

At least fifty ten-year spirit beasts. Three hundred-year spirit beasts.

Lan Xuanyu looked at the gates.

They opened together.

Low howls poured out first.

Then came the Lava Hounds.

They were not large compared with the beasts Lan Xuanyu had seen in the earlier assessment, but numbers gave small things a different kind of weight. Fire clung to their bodies. Their paws struck sparks from the ground. Heat rose fast enough for the air to shimmer.

Liu Feng's eyes sharpened.

A yellow soul ring rose beneath him. White light flashed into a spear with scale patterns along the shaft and a dragon-headed spear tip. The White Dragon Spear looked beautiful in his hand, fast and eager and a little too hungry for open space.

Qian Lei released his Summoning Money. His eyes shifted toward moss green, and the coin in his palm rose with his first soul ring's light.

The three of them fell into a triangle almost by habit.

"It is time to test my luck," Qian Lei announced.

Lan Xuanyu thought luck sounded like a dangerous teammate.

The Gate of Summoning opened.

The Lava Hounds slowed around it, wary of the strange aura.

"Break one direction first!" Liu Feng shouted.

Then he moved.

For a moment, Lan Xuanyu understood why Liu Feng had been selected. White light flashed, and Liu Feng crossed seven or eight meters almost instantly. A Lava Hound flew upward from the strike of his spear. Liu Feng appeared above it, smashed it down, and used the momentum to rush farther.

Too far.

The colosseum was only so large. Two charges carried him nearly to the wall and far from the formation.

Behind Lan Xuanyu, the Gate of Summoning finished opening.

A tiny white cat stepped out.

It looked left.

It looked right.

It saw the Lava Hounds.

"Meow."

Then it ran back into the gate.

The gate vanished.

Qian Lei caught his returning coin with an expression that tried very hard to become an explanation before Lan Xuanyu could speak.

Lan Xuanyu did not have time to speak.

Most of the pack turned toward him and Qian Lei.


Dorm 336 landed in a stone yard under a copper-colored sky.

Yan Shuo hit the ground with knees bent and one hand down. Grit scraped his palm. The pain came through muted but real enough to make his fingers curl.

Thirty percent, he thought.

His body remembered the number without liking it.

The yard was ringed by broken pillars and four low archways. There were no cheering stands here, only a cracked stone wall and the smell of hot dust. The space was wider than a classroom and smaller than pride wanted.

Song appeared three steps ahead of him.

Luo appeared to the left, already moving before his balance fully settled.

"Hold," Song snapped.

Luo froze with one foot off the ground.

From the archways came a scraping sound.

Small shapes flowed out close to the earth.

Needleback rats.

Dozens of them.

Each was no longer than a child's forearm, but their backs bristled with gray spines and their teeth clicked like thrown pebbles. They moved low and quick, not in a clean line but in ripples that made the eye want to chase the wrong body.

Behind them, three heavier shadows stepped from separate archways.

Ironhorn rams.

Hundred-year class, if Soul World's scent could be trusted. Not large enough to be monsters. Heavy enough to break a child's ribs if he decided bravery meant standing still.

Luo exhaled through his teeth. "Rats and rams. Very poetic."

Song's yellow ring rose.

Stone-gray light thickened over his shoulders and forearms. His back broadened as the Stoneplate Tortoise martial soul settled onto him, not as a full beast shell, but as overlapping plates that made his front half look rooted to the arena.

"Formation wedge," Song ordered. "I anchor. Luo interrupts. Yan Shuo controls lanes. No chasing."

"No chasing," Luo repeated, sounding personally wounded.

Yan Shuo's own yellow ring rose.

Copper-red and pale-gold light moved under his robe, feather-thin at first. Dawnfeather Mantle layered across his arms, shoulders, and upper back in quiet low-output Dawnflame Form. It was not the True combat Dawnflame Fusion that had pulled his whole body into one current against the Stonehide Boar. This was the normal form, the one that lived in breath and tendon and mantle angle, controlled enough for academy training and sharp enough to matter.

The Dawnflame Kite pulsed once.

Yan Shuo let heat slide into shape instead of flame.

"Left lane open," Song said.

"I see it."

The first wave arrived.

Song met it like a door deciding not to open.

Needleback rats struck his stone plates, bounced, and tried to pour around his ankles. Luo's yellow ring flashed as his arms blurred. Mantis-edged wind cut low, not killing every rat, but interrupting the ripple long enough to make gaps.

Yan Shuo breathed into one of those gaps.

Dawnflame brushed the ground in a narrow line.

Not a wall.

A warning.

The rats recoiled from the heat and bent away from Song's exposed side. Two leaped through anyway. Yan Shuo's mantle feathers snapped down over his forearm. One rat bit into living light and scraped his skin beneath. Pain sparked up his arm.

Thirty percent did not mean pretend.

He shoved the rat aside with the mantle's recoil and kicked the second away before its teeth found his ankle.

His body stored the angle before anger reached it.

Bite height.

Spine spread.

Low enemies liked the space pride forgot.

"Good lane," Song said.

Yan Shuo almost grinned.

Then the first Ironhorn ram lowered its head.

The ground trembled.

Song lowered his center. "Behind me."

Luo shifted left.

Too far.

Not as badly as Liu Feng had in another arena, but enough.

The hidden rhythm in Yan Shuo's shoulders tightened. His first instinct was to flare, leap, cross the angle, and smash the ram's eye-line apart before it reached Song. Fire answered the thought eagerly. Dawnflame Kite's pulse sharpened.

True combat Dawnflame Fusion waited under the skin like a second sunrise.

Not yet.

The correction came from everywhere at once: Lin Mei's drills, the Stonehide Boar's charge, Song's planted back, Luo's loose foot, the pain in Yan Shuo's bitten arm, and the simple fact that a team was a shape, not three separate wishes.

Yan Shuo did not burst forward.

He called, "Luo, low right!"

Luo twisted.

The Threadwind Mantis light around his arms snapped down just in time. He did not meet the ram. He cut across the rats flowing behind it, breaking the second wave that would have swallowed Song after impact.

The ram hit Song.

Stone rang.

Song slid backward half a step, then another. His face tightened. The tortoise plates held, but holding did not mean painless.

Yan Shuo moved on the third step.

Not ahead of the shield.

Through its shadow.

He drove Dawnfeather Mantle along his shoulder, pounced from the safe side of Song's plate, and struck the ram's flank with a compact burst of dawnfire. The heat did not explode. It bit, flashed, and folded back before it could wash over Song.

The ram bellowed and staggered sideways.

Luo was already there.

His mantis-light cut across the ram's foreleg. Not deep enough to finish it. Enough to spoil the charge.

Song exhaled.

"Again," he said.

This time it sounded less like command and more like belief.


Lan Xuanyu moved because stopping would mean being eaten.

He caught Qian Lei by the robe and pulled him hard enough that Qian Lei's complaint became a yelp. His left hand flashed. Four ice awls shot toward the nearest Lava Hounds. One hound fell shrieking as ice struck an eye. The rest kept coming.

"Down!"

Qian Lei obeyed without understanding.

Ice spread under him. He slid away over the slick patch, shouting in alarm, while Lan Xuanyu used the freed space to accelerate. His footwork shifted right, then left. Teeth snapped through his torn robe instead of his skin.

Across the arena, Liu Feng was near the wall with seven or eight Lava Hounds chasing him. He was fast enough to stay ahead. His spear was sharp enough to harass. But the hounds he struck kept moving, angry rather than finished.

Speed without formation became a bright thread cut loose from cloth.

Lan Xuanyu ran toward Qian Lei.

More hounds pursued.

Too many.

He raised his left hand. Silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass shot out in more than ten strands.

"Pull me!"

Qian Lei grabbed the grass and pulled with both hands. Lan Xuanyu used the force to change direction, widening the gap just enough to breathe.

Ice awls became ice fog.

The Lava Hounds hated the cold. Their charge slowed, paws skidding and flames hissing where frost touched them.

"Liu Feng, come over!" Lan Xuanyu shouted.

They needed to be together. Even unreliable pieces became useful if placed correctly.

"How long before another summon?" he asked Qian Lei.

Qian Lei looked miserable. "About half an hour. The cat did not take much, but the skill still needs time."

Lan Xuanyu's mouth twitched.

Half an hour was not a plan.

It was an obituary with numbers.

"I'm coming!" Liu Feng shouted.

White light streaked toward them.

Too fast.

"Stop!" Lan Xuanyu yelled.

Liu Feng tried.

Trying was not stopping.

The three boys collided and rolled together across the ground while Lava Hounds closed around them from every side.

Lan Xuanyu pushed himself up with dirt on his hands and a very clear understanding of why Dorm 333 had needed help.

"Qian Lei," he said, forcing his breathing steady, "prepare another summon. I will hold them back."

His left hand struck the ground.

Spirit Power poured out.

Ice thorns erupted around them in a jagged ring, each one tall enough to keep the smallest hounds back for the moment. The effort tore more than half his available Spirit Power away at once.

The Lava Hounds hit the ice and howled. Some were pierced. Others retreated, circling, waiting for heat to do what teeth could not.

For a breath, Dorm 333 was safe.

Then three larger forms stepped from the gates.

Hundred-year Lava Hounds.

Smoke curled from their mouths.

Lan Xuanyu saw the fireballs forming and understood that ice thorns were only a delay.


Dorm 336's second ram did not charge Song.

It charged the gap Yan Shuo had just used.

Smart, Yan Shuo thought, and hated it.

Soul World was not polite enough to make training beasts stupid.

"Switch," Song barked.

Luo moved to cut across the front.

Needleback rats used the moment to surge at Yan Shuo's legs.

He stamped once, not hard enough to waste power, but with Dawnflame folded into the impact. Heat flashed in a crescent. The nearest rats recoiled. One did not. It leaped straight through the low flame, spines flattened, teeth aimed for his knee.

Yan Shuo's mantle dropped.

The rat struck feathers, skidded along copper-red light, and left a line of pain across his thigh before Luo's wind-edge flicked it away.

"Debt," Luo said.

"Keep accounts later."

"I am excellent at accounts while terrified."

The ram came on.

Yan Shuo felt the path open.

A high-output wing-frame burst could clear the ram's horn and put him above its neck. True combat Dawnflame Fusion could pull Fire Phoenix, Dawnflame Kite, first ring, mantle, soul power, Spiritual Power, body, and Phoenix seed into one short, fierce answer. Against a child opponent, it might have ended the exchange at once.

Against a hundred-year ram in a team exercise, it would also empty him faster, scatter Luo's angle, and teach Song that his rear striker could not be trusted to stay in formation.

Power was not the same as permission.

Yan Shuo lowered his shoulder instead.

"Song, two steps left!"

Song moved.

Not much.

Enough.

Stoneplate Tortoise light took the ram on a slant rather than head-on. Yan Shuo's fire struck the ground at the ram's outside hoof. Luo's mantis cut flashed at the inside. The ram stumbled into Song's plate, not through it.

The impact still knocked Song to one knee.

Yan Shuo caught the edge of Song's shoulder plate with both hands and felt the force bite up his arms. Dawnfeather Mantle spread over his palms to keep skin from tearing.

Thirty percent pain filled his bones with useful honesty.

Too high next time.

Lower pull.

Song's plate angle holds better from left hip.

Luo's cut lands half-beat early when frightened.

His hidden foundation did not speak. It never had.

It simply took pain, pressure, timing, and fear, broke them into pieces small enough for a child's body to keep, and tucked them into tomorrow.

"You held," Song said through clenched teeth.

Yan Shuo looked at the third ram turning toward them and at the rats still flowing around the broken pillars.

"We are still paying for the coin."

Luo laughed breathlessly. "Can we return it and apologize?"

The third ram charged.

This time Dorm 336 moved before Song had to order it.

Song planted.

Luo cut the low wave.

Yan Shuo breathed heat into lanes.

Not wild.

Not small.

Controlled.

The Dawnflame Kite beat once in his chest like a hidden wing.


Liu Feng leaped.

His first soul skill, White Dragon Flash, lit his body. He met the first fireball from the side with the White Dragon Spear and changed its path. The fireball flew back toward the pack and burst among the Lava Hounds.

Momentum dragged him toward the second fireball.

This time silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass wrapped around his waist at the exact moment he needed it.

Lan Xuanyu pulled.

Liu Feng twisted just enough to redirect the second fireball too.

Two explosions shook the colosseum. Sparks scattered. Several ten-year Lava Hounds were thrown into confusion.

The grass pulled Liu Feng back.

He landed badly, pale-faced, both hands bleeding around the spear grip. White Dragon Flash had shown why he mattered. It had also shown the price of using speed without enough control.

Qian Lei stared from inside the melting ice-thorn ring.

"Be more reliable," Liu Feng snapped, though his voice shook.

"I am trying to become a miracle," Qian Lei said, almost crying. "Miracles need a little time."

Another hundred-year Lava Hound lowered its head.

Flame gathered in its mouth.

Lan Xuanyu stepped forward.

His Spirit Power was low. His teammates were damaged. The ice was melting. The pack had not stopped being a pack.

But his eyes were still calm.

Not because he had an easy answer.

Because panic would not become one.

"Again," he said.

The next fireball began to fly.


The third Ironhorn ram made Dorm 336 honest.

It did not fall for the same slant twice.

It feinted toward Song, twisted toward Luo, then drove through the fire lane Yan Shuo had left too neat and too predictable.

Yan Shuo saw the mistake one heartbeat before it punished him.

Predictable fire became a road.

The ram used it.

"Back!" Song shouted.

Luo jumped, but Needleback rats swarmed under his landing place. Yan Shuo's body moved before thought, but this time it did not leap into full fusion. Low-output Dawnflame Form tightened across shoulders, spine, and knees. Dawnfeather Mantle flared broad over his left side.

He slammed into Luo midair.

Both boys crashed down behind Song's shield line.

Pain tore across Yan Shuo's shoulder where the mantle scraped stone. Luo's elbow hit his ribs. A rat spine caught his sleeve and nicked skin beneath.

The ram struck the place Luo would have landed.

Stone cracked.

Luo stopped laughing.

For once, that was the most alarming sound in the arena.

Yan Shuo shoved him upright. "Move."

Luo moved.

Not away from the team this time.

Back into the gap.

Song's shield plates shifted, one overlapping another. His breathing was rough, but his eyes were steady.

"Yan Shuo," he said, "short burst. I'll open it."

The words hit differently from permission given by pride.

It was not someone chaining his fire down. It was a shield user creating a door and trusting him to take it.

This was formation.

This was timing.

Yan Shuo inhaled.

True combat Dawnflame Fusion rose close enough to light the edge of the world.

For one heartbeat, Fire Phoenix, Dawnflame Kite, first ring, Dawnfeather Mantle, soul power, Spiritual Power, body, and Phoenix seed pulled toward one current. Not overdrive. Not full release. A controlled bite from the trump card, held only as far as the shared opening could carry without burning through the team.

Song struck his stone-plated forearm against the ground.

The ram flinched toward the sound.

"Now!"

Yan Shuo moved.

A short wing-frame burst snapped across his back. It did not lift him into flight. It drove him forward and sideways, one fierce angle inside the shield's shadow. His mantle struck the ram's horn from below while dawnfire bit into the joint behind its jaw.

Luo's mantis-light followed half a breath later, cutting the leg already spoiled by the turn.

The ram fell to one knee.

Yan Shuo landed hard.

The burst vanished almost immediately, leaving hunger and heat trembling in its wake. His shoulder throbbed. His lungs wanted twice the air they had earned.

Not free.

Good.

Free power taught nothing.

The ram dissolved into light.

Only one of the hundred-year beasts was down. The first still limped. The second had recovered. Needleback rats still circled, fewer than before but not gone.

Song looked at Yan Shuo once.

No praise.

No awe.

Just a nod that said he had used the right amount at the right time.

Yan Shuo discovered he liked that more than applause.

"Again?" Luo asked, voice thin.

Yan Shuo swallowed against the dry burn in his throat.

"Not like that. Not yet."

Song lifted his shielded arms.

"Then we do it properly."

The remaining beasts came on.

Dorm 336 met them as a shape.

Not a perfect one.

A living one.


Outside the pods, Mu Zhongtian stood before the monitoring array with his hands clasped behind his back.

Thirty screens carried thirty children into trouble.

Some teams failed almost immediately. Some survived by running. Some relied on one strong student until the scenario taught them how quickly one strong student could be surrounded.

Dorm 333's screen showed ice thorns melting, Liu Feng pale and bleeding, Qian Lei trying to force another miracle out of a coin, and Lan Xuanyu still standing in the place where a team should have had a plan.

Dorm 336's screen showed stone plates, mantis-light, controlled dawnfire, and three children slowly learning that a shield, a blade, and a flame were not automatically a formation.

Mu Zhongtian's expression did not soften.

But his pen moved.

Not for scores alone.

Scores were simple.

What mattered was what a child did the moment the first plan failed.

On one screen, Lan Xuanyu raised his arms toward another fireball and bought time for a teammate whose skill could not be trusted.

On another, Yan Shuo held back a stronger burst until Song's shield line made it part of the team's shape.

Different children.

Different fires.

Both still inside the first coin.


Yan Shuo did not know what was happening in Dorm 333's arena.

He knew only the stone yard, Song's breathing, Luo's footwork, the rats testing their ankles, the two remaining rams, and the exact place in his shoulder where the Soul World had written pain in clean numbers.

That was enough.

The Dawnflame Kite pulsed again.

This time, the question had changed.

Not can we burn?

Where?

Yan Shuo smiled despite the hunger beginning under his ribs.

"Left lane," he said.

Song shifted.

Luo's hands came up.

Yan Shuo lowered his center and let the fire wait for the gap the team made together.

The first coin had not bought victory.

It had bought an answer none of them could get alone.
