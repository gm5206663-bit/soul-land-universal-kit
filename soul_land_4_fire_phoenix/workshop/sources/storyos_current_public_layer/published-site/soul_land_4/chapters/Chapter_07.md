# Chapter 7: What Came Home

Nana left a note because modern life expected explanations.

It was a small note, written with quiet strokes and placed where Yun Yan would find it only after worry had already begun. The room in the Heaven Dou Planet Scientific Research Institute was clean. The bed was made. The air held no sign of struggle, no broken furniture, no spilled water, no trace of panic.

That made it worse.

Yun Yan stood in the doorway with the spare keycard still in her hand and stared at the emptiness as if Nana might reappear because being stared at was rude.

"Nana?"

No one answered.

She searched once. Then again, faster. Closet, bathroom, corners, window seals, the little places where people did not fit but panic insisted they might. Nana had never liked wandering outside alone. Nana disliked crowds, disliked being examined, disliked being told what she should remember. But she had also been gentle in a distant, drifting way, like a person who had forgotten most things except how not to hurt others.

Her Soul Guidance communication device rang busy.

The tracking function was gone.

Yun Yan's face lost color.

Modern people did not turn off their devices like that. Children did not. Workers did not. Even fugitives hesitated, because a silent device was a louder alarm than a shouting one. Nana had done it with calm hands and left a calm note that said she had found something she wanted to do.

Maybe she would return.

Maybe she would not.

Yun Yan read that line twice, and sweat gathered at her temples.

Then she made the call she did not want to make.

Far beyond the institute, beyond Heaven Dou Planet's atmosphere, Spacecraft 7703 had already entered the long road home.

The return journey felt different from the trip outward. On the way to Heaven Dou, Lan Xuanyu had looked out with his whole face pressed toward the future. On the way back, the stars outside the protective cover looked the same, but his heart kept turning toward the silver-haired teacher who had promised to come.

His father and mother had settled into the weary silence adults wore after travel, shopping, worry, and too many things they did not know how to explain. Lan Xiao closed his eyes. Nan Cheng rested with one hand near her son even while half-asleep, as if motherhood had its own alarm system.

Lan Xuanyu sat properly because he had been told to sit properly. Then he looked out at the dark.

Space was very big.

It made children his age feel both small and free. The stars did not scold. The stars did not ask why Blue Silver Grass could be too heavy for a dragon-line girl, or why a silver-haired auntie made his chest warm in a way he could not name.

He remembered Teacher Nana saying she would be his teacher.

He remembered Yan Shuo's message too.

I got my first ring. It has wings but not for flying. I will show you when you come back. Do not be jealous unless you want to.

Lan Xuanyu had read it three times before the spacecraft safety system asked him to stop moving his arm so much. He wanted to answer at once, but he did not know what to say. Congratulations was correct. I am jealous was funny. I also met a teacher who makes water dragons was not something Father would like him to type carelessly.

So he saved the message and told himself he would answer after meditating.

He began the Mysterious Heaven Skill.

The flow came faster than before. That still surprised him. After Uncle Ye's terrifying pressure, after the strange dream and the change in his water control, his thoughts settled more easily when he cultivated. He did not understand the numbers his parents hid from him. He did not know that adults had measured Spiritual Power and made frightened decisions. He only knew that the dark behind his eyes had more tiny lights than before.

Silver lights gathered most clearly.

Then a gentle voice called, "Xuanyu."

Lan Xuanyu's eyes opened at once. He sat up and bumped lightly against the protective cover.

"Teacher Nana?"

"Sit properly. Don't move."

He froze because Teacher Nana's voice was soft, and because it was very hard not to listen to it.

"Are you on the spacecraft?" he asked quickly. "Are you in a better seat? Daddy said those seats cost a lot. Can you lie down there?"

For a moment, Nana did not answer.

Then her voice warmed. "Yes. You could sit somewhere better in the future too."

Lan Xuanyu accepted this because adults often answered only the part of a question they liked.

"Can I come find you?"

"The rules of the spacecraft would not allow that. But if you want to see me, close your eyes and meditate. I can come to you another way."

That was much better than a better seat.

Lan Xuanyu closed his eyes so quickly that his lashes trembled.

The dark space changed.

It was still his own consciousness, though he did not understand that word yet. It had been a vague black place with scattered lights a breath ago. Now light entered it like morning entering a room. A figure appeared in front of him, silver hair unmasked, purple eyes gentle, face so beautiful that even a child's heart understood it was something rare.

"Teacher Nana!"

He tried to run to her and discovered that he could move as if the dark had become ground.

"Where is this?"

"In your heart," Nana said, then thought about how to make it easier. "Or in your consciousness. When your Spiritual Power grows stronger, you may understand it more. For now, I can guide you here."

"Is it Spiritual Power?"

"I think so."

Lan Xuanyu smiled widely. "Then can I always meet you here?"

"Only when the distance is not too far. And for now, only I can come find you."

That was still wonderful.

Nana asked what he wanted to learn.

Lan Xuanyu thought very seriously. He thought about Ye Lingtong, who always looked angry at him even when he was not doing anything. He thought about Yan Shuo, who rushed toward trouble faster than adults preferred. He thought about his mother, who said not to bully girls, and his father, who said some things must be hidden, and the strange helplessness that came when someone wanted to fight and he did not.

"Teacher Nana," he said, "is fighting bad?"

Nana's eyes lost focus for one instant.

Somewhere far behind her missing memories, the word protect opened a door that would not fully open.

"It depends why you fight," she said. "If it is to guard the people you love, then fighting is not bad. If your mother were bullied and you could not protect her, what would you do?"

Lan Xuanyu's small face tightened.

"I would want to protect Mommy."

"Then cultivation can be for protection."

He nodded slowly, as if a complicated school problem had suddenly become one toy placed in the correct box.

"Then... can you teach me how not to get hit? I don't really dare to hit people first."

Nana smiled.

The smile made the dark space gentler.

"Then try to catch me."

"Catch you?"

"If you catch me, I will reward you. If you cannot catch me, I will punish you."

Lan Xuanyu looked suspicious. "What reward?"

"If you catch me, I will let you kiss me. If you cannot, I will kiss you."

He accepted the terms immediately. They were very fair terms.

He rushed forward.

Nana took one step.

It was only one step. She did not move fast. She did not blur. She did not vanish. But Lan Xuanyu's fingers closed on empty air.

He stared at his hand, offended by space.

Then he tried again.

And again.

Nana's feet followed a pattern too strange for him to read. Each time he thought he had found the line, she slid away from it. Each time he jumped too hard, she was no longer where his body insisted she must be. There was no martial soul here for him to release, no water to gather, no silver grass to wrap around his palm. There was only his body, his eyes, his thoughts, and Teacher Nana smiling as if failure were not a wall but a game.

He grew tired.

Even in the space of consciousness, children became tired when Spiritual Power was used too much.

"Teacher," he panted, "I can't catch you."

"Then you owe me one," Nana said.

Lan Xuanyu lifted his face at once.

She laughed softly. "Not now. Continue meditating. When your Spiritual Power recovers, I will come again. Watch my footwork next time. Don't only chase."

The lesson became the journey.

Outside, Spacecraft 7703 crossed its long route between planets. Inside, Lan Xiao and Nan Cheng saw their son meditate for longer and longer periods, stop only to eat, then return to stillness with bright eyes. They thought he was unusually obedient after travel. They thought Heaven Dou's trip had helped his mood.

They did not know that every time Lan Xuanyu's Spiritual Power recovered enough, Nana returned to the dark space and let him chase her again.

Sometimes he grew impatient.

Sometimes he nearly cried from missing by a finger's width.

Sometimes Nana slowed too much and allowed his small hands to catch her sleeve, because children who never touched success might stop reaching for it.

He learned without knowing he was learning.

His feet stopped rushing straight. His shoulders stopped telling the whole story before his body moved. When Nana shifted left, his eyes no longer dragged his body after her too late. Once, for a single step, his foot landed where her next opening almost was.

Nana's smile deepened.

"Again," she said.

In Zi Luo City, Yan Shuo was also learning the word again.

He had begun to dislike it.

"Again," Lin Mei said.

Yan Shuo stood in the family practice room with sweat darkening the hair at his temples. A yellow ring moved beneath his feet, steady and real. Dawnfeather Mantle covered his arms, shoulders, upper back, and chest in close fire-light feathers that looked much more obedient than they were.

The mantle was not armor. Yan Shuo had learned that on the second day, when he tried to block a padded staff and discovered that a feather aura could soften impact but not erase the lesson of pain.

It was not wings either. He had learned that on the third day, when a flare from his back made him hop forward like a startled frog and nearly headbutt the wall.

Yan Ke had added a second safety mat after that.

Yan Shuo pretended not to notice.

"Hold it at the shoulders," Lin Mei said. "Not the hands. If you pour everything into your fists, you are only teaching the mantle to punch."

"Punching is useful."

"So is not breaking your wrist."

The Dawnflame Kite stirred inside him with a small warm pulse that felt suspiciously like agreement.

"You are supposed to be on my side," Yan Shuo muttered.

"It is on your side," Lin Mei said. "That is why it is not flattering you."

He shut his mouth because mothers had an unfair way of winning arguments without raising their voices.

He breathed in.

The breath changed before he decided how to change it. Heat that had thickened around his palms drew back through forearms and shoulders, spreading into the upper back. The mantle's feather edges stopped fraying. A line of dawn-gold ran down his ribs and vanished. His knees bent a little, not because he thought bend now, but because his body remembered what falling had taught it.

Lin Mei saw it.

She always saw the useful parts.

"Good. Now step."

Yan Shuo stepped.

The mantle pushed.

He did not fall this time. His right foot landed too hard, but his left corrected the angle. Heat slid down toward the ankle that had taken too much weight. His shoulder lowered. The little bird warmth inside his chest fluttered and then settled.

"Six breaths," Lin Mei said.

Yan Shuo grinned despite himself. "Yesterday was five."

"Today your face is more annoying, so we will call it practice and not victory."

Yan Ke, sitting at the side with a tablet full of school notices, coughed into his fist.

Yan Shuo's grin widened. The mantle brightened with it and immediately wobbled.

"Emotion," Lin Mei warned.

He swallowed the grin. The mantle steadied again, though not before a feather of light popped from his shoulder and vanished like a guilty spark.

Yan Ke looked down at the tablet. "The academy sent the follow-up notice. They want his holiday training record before the new term resumes."

"They want to know whether his yellow ring is stable," Lin Mei said.

"They used more polite words."

"Academies often do."

Yan Shuo tried not to listen and failed. "Do they want me to demonstrate?"

"They want you to be alive, uninjured, and not on fire in the wrong places," Yan Ke said.

"I can do all three."

Lin Mei looked at the half-formed mantle.

Yan Shuo corrected himself. "Most of the time."

The report that followed him now was heavier than the ring itself. Rank twelve. Spiritual Power thirty-six points, still Spirit Origin Realm. Fire Phoenix. Yellow first ring. Dawnflame Kite. First soul skill. Children did not understand how quickly adult words traveled, but they understood when other children looked too long.

Yan Shuo had not returned to school yet, but the news had reached the edges of school life before he did. One classmate sent a message asking whether the ring was really yellow. Another asked whether he could fly. An attack-type boy he barely liked asked whether Fire Phoenix burned homework.

Yan Shuo replied only to the last one.

Not yet.

Lin Mei took the tablet away after that.

"Records are not toys," she said.

"Messages are not records."

"They become records when a foolish child writes foolish things."

Yan Shuo considered this and decided adults had made the world less fun than necessary.

At night, when training was done and his hunger had become large enough to deserve its own chair at the table, he checked his device again.

Lan Xuanyu still had not answered.

Yan Shuo told himself spacecraft messages could be slow. Then he told himself Lan Xuanyu might be sleeping. Then he told himself maybe his friend had read the message and fainted from jealousy.

That one made him feel better for almost three seconds.

The Dawnflame Kite shifted drowsily inside him.

Yan Shuo pressed a hand over his chest. "He will answer."

The warmth answered with a sleepy pulse.

It was not speech. It was not words. It was only a small living agreement, or perhaps only his own feeling passing through the bond and returning softer.

That was enough.

On the seventh day, Spacecraft 7703 shook through Heaven Luo Planet's atmosphere.

Adults exhaled. Children made noises. The long half-lying seats released them back into gravity and impatience. Lan Xuanyu stepped out with bright eyes and legs that knew slightly more than they had known before.

He looked back more than once.

"Who are you looking for?" Nan Cheng asked.

Lan Xuanyu almost answered.

Then Nana's voice sounded in his mind.

"Stop looking for me. I will go to your home. Go with your parents first."

"Okay," he said at once.

Nan Cheng turned. "Okay what?"

Lan Xuanyu smiled with the innocent face of a child holding a secret too big for his cheeks. "Nothing."

"Xuanyu."

"This is a secret."

Nan Cheng narrowed her eyes. "Then I will confiscate your battle armor kit."

His secret collapsed instantly.

"Mommy! My good, beautiful, best Mommy!"

Lan Xiao laughed despite travel exhaustion. The family took the transfer route back to Zi Luo City. They did not stop long in Heaven Luo City. Home pulled at them too strongly, and even Spirit Masters became tired after interstellar travel.

It was evening by the time they reached their apartment.

Lan Xiao wanted a shower, food, and no more surprises for at least one night.

Fate had never cared very much about his schedule.

Lan Xuanyu remembered Nana's instruction and meditated before sleep. In the space of consciousness, the silver-haired teacher appeared again, and the catching game continued until his thoughts blurred softly into dreams.

The next morning, he woke feeling different.

Not taller in a way that could be seen by standing against a wall. Not stronger like Ye Lingtong, whose confidence entered rooms before she did. Different inside, as if the path his soul power followed had become a little less tangled.

"Mom!" he shouted, running toward the kitchen. "I think my soul power improved."

Nan Cheng, frying omelettes, almost smiled before worry caught up with her. "Then we will test it after breakfast."

"Before breakfast?"

"After."

"But if I improved before breakfast, shouldn't we know before breakfast?"

"If you argue, the omelette improves into mine."

He stopped arguing.

The Spirit Power Test Apparatus showed Rank 12.

Lan Xuanyu bounced in place.

For another child, reaching Rank 12 after already having a ring would be simple good news. For Lan Xuanyu, whose parents knew he had awakened at full innate soul power and then crawled forward too slowly for what that should mean, it was both relief and worry. Lan Xiao looked at the number and did not say everything behind his eyes.

Nan Cheng hugged her son too tightly.

Then the doorbell rang.

Lan Xuanyu ran so fast he nearly skidded.

"Ask who it is first!" Lan Xiao called.

Lan Xuanyu did not.

When the door opened, Nana stood outside with silver hair hidden partly beneath a black mask, eyes gentle above it.

"Teacher Nana!"

Lan Xuanyu threw himself into her arms.

Nana knelt as if the whole journey between planets had led to this exact height. When his arms closed around her neck, the loneliness from a night wandering Zi Luo City's streets disappeared from her face.

Lan Xiao came to the door and stopped.

"Esteemed One," he said carefully. "You truly came."

Nana looked up. "May I come in?"

What answer could he give?

She entered. Nan Cheng appeared from the kitchen, saw her, and needed half a breath to become polite instead of shocked. They invited her to breakfast because that was what families did when someone stood at the door in the morning and changed the air inside the home.

Nana ate little. Lan Xuanyu ate enough for three children and looked happier with every bite.

Nan Cheng could not hold back the question. "Esteemed One, he has been eating more and more lately. Is this normal?"

"It is normal," Nana said. "His body needs nutrients and the spiritual energy within food to grow. Ordinary food contains limited spiritual energy. Better ingredients would help him more."

Lan Xiao understood at once and wished he did not. "Medicinal cuisine and rare ingredients?"

Nana nodded.

Rare food was not merely expensive. It belonged to a world of high-ranked academies, mother planet resources, soul beast planets, strict Federation control, and transport costs that made ordinary middle-class families feel the distance between love and ability. Lan Xiao asked. Nana listened. Nan Cheng's mouth tightened.

No parent enjoyed hearing that a child needed something love could not simply buy.

"I can teach him," Nana said.

They had discussed this on Heaven Dou Planet. Hearing it in their home made it heavier.

"Esteemed One," Nan Cheng said, "we truly cannot afford your classes."

"I do not want money. A place to stay is enough. It does not need to be spacious."

Lan Xiao and Nan Cheng exchanged a look.

A Titled Douluo-level figure asking for lodging instead of payment did not become less frightening because the price was low. It became more frightening because no ordinary scale could weigh it.

But Nana looked at Lan Xuanyu, and Lan Xuanyu looked back with trust so complete it made refusal feel like pushing away shelter during rain.

Lan Xiao made the decision because someone had to.

"Then we will trouble you."

They gave Nana the guest room, not the cultivation room. Nan Cheng replaced the bedding herself and asked, with the caution of a mother who would stand between her son and the sun if necessary, whether she could observe the lessons.

"Yes," Nana said.

That yes made Nan Cheng breathe easier.

Only a little.

In the Yan apartment, Yan Shuo finally received Lan Xuanyu's reply.

I'm back. Teacher Nana came too. I learned a catching game. Congratulations on your ring. I am jealous if you want me to be.

Yan Shuo stared at the message.

"A catching game?" he said aloud.

Yan Ke looked over from the table. "From Lan Xuanyu?"

"He says he learned a catching game. He also admits jealousy."

"Did he write those exact words?"

"Enough of them."

Lin Mei put another plate in front of Yan Shuo because his hunger had become a household event. "Eat first. Compete later."

"It is not competing."

Both parents looked at him.

Yan Shuo picked up his chopsticks. "It is friendly comparing."

"Eat," Lin Mei said.

He ate.

His body made use of food with embarrassing seriousness. Warmth spread from stomach to limbs, not as flame but as repair. The Dawnflame Kite rested more quietly after meals. The mantle held steadier in the afternoon if breakfast had been large enough. If he skipped rest, the feather edges frayed sooner. If he grew angry, the shoulders overbrightened. If he laughed, the chest lines jumped.

Everything taught him.

That was unfair too. A person should be able to make mistakes without his own body collecting evidence.

After breakfast, Yan Ke showed him the school notice again. "Your training summary must include duration, control, recovery, and any discomfort. No exaggeration."

"What if I accidentally sound impressive?"

"Then your mother will correct the accident."

Lin Mei did not smile. "Also, you will not visit Xuanyu's home today unless invited. They just returned from another planet."

"But he is back."

"Back is not the same as rested."

"He has a teacher now."

"Then he is busy too."

Yan Shuo frowned at his bowl.

The Dawnflame Kite shifted, a tiny restless warmth beneath his ribs. It liked Lan Xuanyu's presence, or perhaps it liked the way Yan Shuo's feelings became clear around his friend. It also remembered pressure, though memory in a soul spirit was not like memory in a child. Around Lan Xuanyu, Yan Shuo's inner fire sometimes crouched instead of leaping. Not fear exactly. Not submission. More like a young bird lowering itself when deep weather passed overhead.

Yan Shuo did not know how to explain that.

So he said, "When he is done being busy, I will show him five breaths."

"Six," Yan Ke said.

Yan Shuo brightened.

Lin Mei glanced at her husband.

Yan Ke pretended to study the tablet.

In the Lan family's cultivation room, Nana stood between mother and son.

"Pressure-resistant exercises," she said.

Lan Xuanyu tilted his head. "What are those?"

"I will give you some pressure. You will use your soul power to resist it. It will help your soul power and body. Suitable pressure can draw out potential."

Nan Cheng remembered Ye Feng's pressure and the way her son had lain unconscious afterward. Her heart tightened immediately.

Nana seemed to feel it. "I will control it. You may try with him."

Before Nan Cheng could answer, the room blurred.

She found herself standing in a different position without feeling the movement. Nana was now between her and Lan Xuanyu.

Nan Cheng released her martial soul at once. Cold filled the cultivation room. Four yellow rings and two purple rings rose beneath her feet, proof of a Spirit Emperor's cultivation and of a mother who had never thought she would feel so small inside her own home.

Pressure arrived.

It was not too strong at first. That was part of the shock. Nana's pressure did not crush blindly. It touched, measured, adjusted. Nan Cheng felt one kind of force around herself and saw, from the corner of her eye, a different kind around Lan Xuanyu.

Two people. Two pressures. Two exact balances.

Nana watched only Lan Xuanyu.

"Run your soul power as usual," she told him. "Feel the change. Do not let it run randomly. You may release your martial soul."

Lan Xuanyu raised his left hand.

Silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass emerged and wrapped from fingers toward arm, its leaves turning as if answering a pressure older than thought. Water elements gathered around him and formed a shield. At first, the pressure came from every side. Then it changed, stronger in one place, weaker in another.

Lan Xuanyu's face tightened. He moved the water shield by instinct and effort, patching the stronger side, thinning the weaker side, losing shape, finding it again.

Nana smiled slightly.

Nan Cheng was sweating before she realized it.

The pressure on her increased.

"Esteemed One, I..."

"Continue," Nana's voice sounded beside her ear. "You protect yourself too much. Without outside pressure, you will not touch your limit."

Nan Cheng wanted to say she was a researcher, not a battle maniac. She wanted to say limits were overrated when one had a child to raise. She wanted to say many things.

Instead, she clenched her teeth.

Lan Xuanyu was also reaching his limit.

His small face paled. His water shield trembled. Soul power ran faster and faster, but it was being consumed just as quickly. His mouth opened, but no complaint came out. Nana's eyes encouraged him, and because Nana was looking at him that way, he puffed out his chest even while afraid.

Then his right hand lit.

Golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass appeared.

Warmth spread from his right hand through his body. The pressure seemed to lighten. His limbs felt stronger. Confidence rose so suddenly that he did not know whether it was his own or something lent to him by the grass.

The left-hand silver grass trembled.

The two forces answered each other.

Nana's expression changed.

The pressure vanished. Before Nan Cheng could breathe, Nana was already beside Lan Xuanyu, one hand pressed against his chest.

Gold and silver rushed inward from both arms.

They met near his heart like two stubborn children who did not know the body between them could break.

Lan Xuanyu's face changed. Silver lines spread over one side, golden lines over the other. One eye shone silver, the other gold. His small body swelled slightly, soul power fluctuating with frightening instability.

"Esteemed One!" Nan Cheng cried.

Nana's brows drew together. Her power entered gently but urgently, forming a spinning separation before the two forces could collide fully.

"Two different energies," she said softly, and the softness made it more frightening. "Both powerful. Both stubborn. Too dangerous if they strike each other. Why are they familiar?"

She tried to remember.

Pain flashed behind her eyes.

She stopped trying.

"What do we do?" Nan Cheng's voice shook.

Nana sat cross-legged, drew Lan Xuanyu into her arms, and placed her hand over his chest.

"I will think of a way. Do not disturb me."

Three days became a room holding its breath.

Lan Xiao requested leave and did not explain enough for anyone to ask useful questions. Nan Cheng barely slept. Nana sat with Lan Xuanyu in her arms, her hand steady over his chest, guiding two forces that wanted to devour or reject each other into a shape that could survive.

Lan Xuanyu dreamed.

Gold and silver clashed in the dark. An invisible force pulled them into motion, faster and faster, thinning them into strands. At first, every touch made them rebound like shocked wires. Then the spinning changed the meeting. They braided without merging, circled without swallowing, close enough to be one pattern and separate enough not to destroy the child at the center.

Outside the Lan home, ordinary life continued because ordinary life was often ignorant of the rooms beside it.

In the Yan home, Yan Shuo sent two messages and received no answer.

The first said, Are you still playing catching game?

The second said, If your teacher catches you too much, you should demand payment.

Neither was read.

Yan Shuo told himself he was not worried.

His training disagreed.

Dawnfeather Mantle flared too quickly that afternoon. It brightened along the shoulders, thinned at the chest, and snapped out around his forearms before he was ready. Lin Mei stopped the exercise at once.

"Again, but calmer."

"I am calm."

"Your mantle is shouting."

Yan Shuo looked down at the fading feather-light as if it had betrayed state secrets.

Inside him, the Dawnflame Kite was restless. Not afraid. Not exactly. It ruffled at nothing, then tucked itself close. Yan Shuo's breath kept wanting to grow hot and sharp. His body kept pulling that heat back toward the center before it reached his hands.

Hold shape, something in him seemed to remember.

He did not know from where.

Lin Mei watched him longer than usual. "You are thinking about Xuanyu."

"He did not answer."

"Families are busy after travel."

"For three days?"

Yan Ke looked up from the table. Concern crossed his face, then caution followed it. The Lan family had always carried quiet around them. Yan Ke respected quiet. Quiet was sometimes the wall that kept other people's lives whole.

"We can ask a simple question through normal channels," he said. "Not push. Not pry."

Yan Shuo looked at him quickly.

"I will message Uncle Lan," Yan Ke said. "Only to ask if they returned safely."

Lan Xiao's reply came after a long delay.

Returned safely. Xuanyu is resting after training. Thank you for asking.

It was polite. It was ordinary. It was also a closed door.

Yan Shuo read the words and hated how adult they were.

"He is resting," Lin Mei said.

"After training," Yan Shuo muttered.

"Then you will train too. Calmly."

He wanted to argue. Instead, he stood again.

His body had already begun correcting. Breath lower. Shoulders down. Heat wide, not sharp. The Dawnflame Kite settled a little when he settled. The mantle formed more slowly this time, feathers close to the body, not reaching for a fight that was not in the room.

Seven breaths.

He did not cheer.

That helped it last to eight.

On the third day, the golden and silver lines vanished from Lan Xuanyu's face.

Nana opened her eyes.

The exhaustion in Lan Xiao and Nan Cheng's faces was plain, but Lan Xuanyu's breathing had become stable. Nana carried him to his room and tucked him in with such care that Nan Cheng's fear loosened for one breath before returning as questions.

When the door closed, Nan Cheng could no longer hold them back.

"Esteemed One, Xuanyu, he..."

"His condition is unique," Nana said.

They sat in the living room. Nana explained only what she understood: two powerful energies, one golden and one silver, nearly equal, both stubborn, both without consciousness, both capable of helping greatly if alone and endangering him together. She had woven them into a spinning equilibrium, keeping contact limited, using motion to prevent collision.

Lan Xiao listened with the face of a researcher hearing the world speak in a language his instruments had not learned.

"Will he be in danger if you are here?" he asked.

"Not for now. The danger should appear during major breakthroughs or as his body grows. His cultivation may be slower because the two energies counteract each other. When he approaches Rank 20, we may see more clearly."

Rank 20.

The number entered the room and stayed there.

Nan Cheng's hand tightened in her lap.

"What can we do?" Lan Xiao asked.

"Strengthen his body," Nana said. "Especially his meridians. If his body is strong enough, he can endure danger longer and we will have more time. The energies also nourish him, especially the golden one. I will guide him every day."

Lan Xiao bowed his head.

There were many things he still did not know. Where Nana came from. Why she cared. What his son truly was. Why fate kept opening doors inside their ordinary home.

But for now, his son breathed steadily in the next room.

That was enough to choose the next step.

Yan Shuo saw Lan Xuanyu on the fourth evening.

Not at school. Not in a training room. In the courtyard between the housing blocks, where the fountain changed colors when children ran around it and parents pretended not to listen from benches.

Lan Xuanyu came down with Nan Cheng watching from a distance. He looked a little pale, but his eyes were bright in a new way. Yan Shuo had prepared three different greetings. One scolding. One dramatic. One casual.

All three disappeared when he actually saw his friend.

"You didn't answer," Yan Shuo said.

So much for casual.

Lan Xuanyu lowered his head. "I was sleeping. And training. And sleeping."

"For three days?"

"I think so."

Yan Shuo stared at him. "You think so?"

Lan Xuanyu looked honestly uncertain. "It was a lot of sleeping."

That was such a Lan Xuanyu answer that Yan Shuo's anger lost its grip.

"Fine," he said. "But if you learn a secret catching game, you have to show me."

Lan Xuanyu brightened. "I can show a little. Teacher Nana taught me in consciousness."

Yan Shuo opened his mouth.

Closed it.

Opened it again. "That sentence needs adult permission before I understand it."

Lan Xuanyu nodded as if this were reasonable. "Mommy also looked like that."

"Is Teacher Nana the silver-haired auntie from your trip?"

"Yes. She came to my house. She is my teacher now."

Yan Shuo glanced toward Nan Cheng. She was watching carefully, but she did not stop the conversation. That meant the surface was allowed. Only the surface.

He understood enough about adult secrets to know where not to kick.

"Then watch," he said, recovering pride because pride was easier than worry. "I have a real soul ring now."

He did not release full power. Lin Mei stood near the bench, and he valued survival. But a yellow ring rose beneath his feet, clear and bright, and Dawnfeather Mantle formed over his arms and shoulders in a close layer of fire-light feathers.

Lan Xuanyu's eyes widened.

"It's pretty."

Yan Shuo almost objected that powerful was a better word. Then he saw the way Lan Xuanyu meant it and decided pretty could live.

"It is not for flying yet," Yan Shuo said quickly.

"Yet?"

"Yet."

"Can I touch it?"

"No," Lin Mei and Nan Cheng said at the same time.

Both boys jumped.

Yan Shuo coughed. "Later, when adults stop having opinions."

"Adults always have opinions," Lan Xuanyu said.

"Then much later."

Lan Xuanyu laughed.

Yan Shuo stepped forward and let the mantle push just a little. Not a leap. Not a charge. A controlled half-step with feather-light tightening across his shoulders. He stopped before the heat could run ahead of his feet.

Lan Xuanyu moved without thinking.

One small shift. A turn of the foot. His body slid aside along a rhythm Yan Shuo did not recognize.

Yan Shuo froze.

"Do that again."

Lan Xuanyu blinked. "Do what?"

"The not-being-there thing."

"Oh. Teacher Nana's game."

Yan Shuo looked at his own mantle, then at Lan Xuanyu's feet.

"I got a ring," he said slowly. "You got weird feet."

Lan Xuanyu considered this comparison and smiled. "Your ring is better."

"Your feet are annoying."

"Thank you?"

"It was not praise."

"It sounded a little like praise."

Yan Shuo tried to glare. It did not work very well because he was relieved.

Then, for one quiet breath, the air around Lan Xuanyu felt different.

Not visibly. Not enough for the fountain to change or the adults to react. It was only a familiar heaviness inside Yan Shuo's chest, deeper than before, layered strangely beneath Lan Xuanyu's ordinary smile. The Fire Phoenix within him crouched. The Dawnflame Kite tucked close. Heat that wanted to flare moved inward instead, forming a steady center under his ribs.

Yan Shuo breathed before he knew he needed to.

In. Low. Hold shape.

The heaviness passed.

Lan Xuanyu tilted his head. "Are you tired?"

"No," Yan Shuo said, too quickly. Then, because Lin Mei was near and lies had short legs, he added, "Maybe a little. Mantle practice uses a lot."

"My training uses a lot too," Lan Xuanyu said.

There were things behind those words neither boy could explain.

One had a soul spirit sleeping like a small sunlit bird inside his chest. One had gold and silver forces braided near his heart by a teacher who did not know why she knew how. One wore a yellow ring openly. One hid white rings and a future no child should have to carry. One wanted wings. One wanted to protect without hurting first.

They stood in the courtyard between ordinary apartment blocks while the fountain turned blue, then green, then warm orange under the evening lights.

Yan Shuo lowered the mantle before it failed on its own.

"When school starts," he said, "we compare properly."

Lan Xuanyu nodded. "Okay."

"Not fighting. Comparing."

"What's the difference?"

Yan Shuo looked at the adults.

"Rules," he said wisely.

Lan Xuanyu accepted that.

From the bench, Lin Mei watched Yan Shuo's shoulders settle after the strange breath. She said nothing. From the other side of the courtyard, Nan Cheng watched Lan Xuanyu laugh like a normal child after three days of terror. She said nothing either.

Adults kept many silences.

Some protected children.

Some waited to become mistakes.

For now, the two boys argued over whether a catching game counted as martial arts if no one got hit, and the evening let them be children a little longer.

Above them, no wings opened.

Inside them, everything that would one day become wings kept learning where to grow.
