# Chapter 48 — The Line in the Stars

"Back then, I was determined to enter Shrek's inner court."

Yin Tianfan said it without his usual laugh.

The testing device still showed 505 beside Lan Xuanyu. The number should have filled the room with excitement, but Yin's voice made the office feel older than the night outside.

"I was in Shrek's Space Command System," Yin continued. "I had good results, good teachers, and too much confidence. I liked surprise attacks, ugly angles, tricks inside tricks. Some teachers admired it. Some disliked it. I thought the ones who disliked it were too rigid. Results mattered most. If a tactic won, why should I care whether it looked clean?"

Lan listened carefully.

He had just used an impossible battlefield answer to become champion. He could not pretend the question did not touch him.

Yin looked at him. "The final exam of the sixth semester was a space battle. Three students formed one group. Each of us commanded a fleet, and the three fleets became a joint fleet. I was the strongest commander in my group. My two teammates were weaker. At the time, I thought that only meant I had to carry them."

His fingers tapped once on the table.

"Three opposing groups formed a coalition to suppress me. Nine fleets against our three. They feared my score would block their inner-court chance. I fought them with every method I knew: harassment, interference, false weakness, provocation, ambush. I made them chase shadows and distrust each other."

Lan could imagine it too easily: stars, fleets, signals, traps, a net tightening and then fraying under the hand of someone who saw the weak joints.

"My teammates obeyed," Yin said. "They acted as decoys when I needed decoys. They held lanes they could barely hold. They lost ships. They never refused."

Lan's hands tightened on his knees.

"Eventually the coalition could not continue and asked to negotiate. They wanted me to let them keep enough score to avoid a miserable ending. They promised to cooperate afterward."

"Did you believe them?" Lan asked.

Yin smiled thinly. "No. But negotiation changes formation. Weapons cool. Routes open. People look for agreement instead of danger. So I agreed. Then, while the alliance was forming, I attacked and destroyed them."

Lan did not speak.

"My teammates were angry," Yin said. "They asked why I broke my word. I told them war never has too much deception. I told them strength proved everything. They said things I did not want to hear. I was proud. I could stay calm against enemies, but I could not bear my own teammates accusing me."

Yin's voice became very quiet.

"So I destroyed their fleets too. They were already damaged from obeying my orders. They could not stop me. In a moment, the world was quiet."

Lan felt cold.

Not because the tactic against enemies was clever.

Because the line had been crossed after the battle was already nearly won.

"I regretted it," Yin said. "But not enough at first. I still thought: I showed such talent, so what if I was rash? The exam ended. I ranked first. Undisputed first. Then Shrek expelled me."

The room seemed to hold its breath.

"I was furious," Yin continued. "It was a simulated battlefield. No real soldiers died. The enemy had surrounded me first. But my favorite teacher told me something I did not accept then. Shrek could accept sly tactics, sudden ambushes, and ugly methods against enemies. War is not clean. But a commander who cannot forgive allies, who destroys teammates because they object, is not fit to command a fleet. Today it was simulation. Tomorrow it could be soldiers."

Lan lowered his eyes.

A commander always used people.

The difference was whether he remembered they were people.

Yin saw the thought arrive and nodded slightly.

"I lost Shrek. My honor was erased. Advanced Space Command academies would not take me. For two years I lived inside anger and nightmares. Later, after I understood more, I found those teammates and apologized. One forgave me. One did not. Both had the right."

He leaned back.

"Then I joined the military from the bottom. Ordinary soldier. No Shrek certificate. No halo. It took me twenty years to rise, become a space warship officer, command a small fleet, and reach major general. Then my record blocked the road again. I could go no farther. People called me the Elusive Fox. They thought it was praise."

His smile was tired.

"So I came here."

Lan looked at him. "Teacher, do you still think Shrek was wrong?"

Yin was silent for a while.

"Sometimes," he said honestly. "But less than before. If I had continued with that character, maybe I really would have become someone dangerous. That is why I teach you tricks, Xuanyu, but I also tell you the wound. Principles without ability can only watch disaster happen. Ability without principles becomes disaster."

Lan bowed his head. "I will remember."

"Good." Yin's eyes sharpened with sudden life. "Then remember one more thing. You have a real chance to enter Shrek. Your command talent is rare. Space Command is not a side road. A fleet commander can decide the fate of countless lives across the stars. Are you willing to accomplish your teacher's dream and choose the Space Command faculty?"

The words struck exactly where Yin aimed them.

Lan saw stars for a moment. Warships. Routes. Signals. Lives moving because a commander's mind found a path.

He was tempted.

Then another memory rose: pirates, helplessness, his own body too weak when real danger arrived, Teacher Ji's insistence on foundation, Teacher Nana's bloodline warnings, the gold and silver vortex inside him.

Before Lan could answer, the door opened hard.

"Silly boy," Ji Hongbin said from the doorway, face dark. "This was a routine. He was waiting for you at the end."

Yin's expression stiffened. "Old Ji, what do you mean routine? I spoke from the heart."

"Your heart has hooks," Ji said. "You lured me away, told the wound, arranged the lesson, and placed Space Command at the end while his emotions were soft. Where is the fair competition?"

"Everything I said was true."

"True is not the same as complete."

Lan looked from one teacher to the other and understood more than he had a moment before.

Yin's story was true.

Yin's pain was true.

Yin's lesson was true.

And Yin had still formed them into a fleet aimed at Lan's choice.

That did not make the lesson false.

It made the teacher still Yin Tianfan.

Ji turned to Lan. "Your world is not only the sea of stars. Individual cultivation, mecha, and battle armor matter. A commander who cannot survive the enemy reaching him will not command long."

Yin snorted. "And a hero who can only punch one battlefield cannot save a star system."

"No one asked him to decide tonight."

"I asked him to think."

"You asked him to agree."

Lan lowered his head slightly. "Teachers, I understand both sides. I will decide after I earn the right to enter Shrek."

For once, both old men stopped.

Then Ji nodded. "Good."

Yin coughed. "Acceptable. But think more about warships."

"Think more about surviving them," Ji said.

The argument might have continued for another hour if Mu Zhongtian had not arrived with perfect timing.

"It is time to draw the prize," he said. "Soul World simulation ground."

Qian Lei, who had apparently been waiting outside with Liu Feng and pretending not to listen, appeared at once. "Prize? Now? I am emotionally prepared."

Liu Feng said, "You have been emotionally prepared since the word reward appeared."

"Preparation is a virtue."

Mu ignored them. "Simulation pods."


Ling Yiyi was waiting inside Soul World with the same gentle smile that had once offered Qian Lei early admission and made him suffer for refusing it.

Qian saw her and became polite in the way a person becomes polite around a dangerous treasure box.

"Sister Ling," he said carefully.

Ling Yiyi's smile deepened. "Qian Lei, why do you look as if I owe you something?"

"Not owe. I am merely hoping destiny has a good memory."

"Then perhaps you should hope your hands have discipline."

Liu Feng murmured, "Good advice."

Ling turned to Dorm333. "Congratulations. Your team obtained first place in the Heaven Luo Planet qualifier with an extremely outstanding result. According to the information I have, your consolidated score should also be first across the federation qualifier stage. Usually, students from the Mother Planet occupy the best positions. This time, Heaven Luo Planet has gained great honor because of you."

Mu Zhongtian's chest rose slightly.

First across the federation.

Even after seeing the score, the phrase still landed heavily.

Lan bowed politely. "Thank you, Sister Ling."

Then he looked up with clean, hopeful eyes.

"Since it is first across the federation, is there any additional reward? For example, an extra Lucky Wheel spin?"

Qian's eyes lit like lamps.

Ling Yiyi's expression froze for half a breath.

This child, she thought, had just reported a battlefield calculation that made adults wary, and now he was using his face to bargain.

"No," she said. "For fairness, everyone is treated equally."

Lan blinked. "But receiving a first-place reward is already unequal compared with teams that did not place first. If unequal rewards based on result are allowed, then federation first—"

"Then you may choose not to draw," Ling said, smiling dangerously. "Would that be fairer?"

Lan immediately shook his head. "That would be unfair to us, because other first-place teams would draw."

Qian whispered, "Excellent defense."

Ling looked at him.

Qian became silent.

Lan sighed softly, accepting defeat. "Then one draw."

Ling Yiyi pressed her hand into the air.

Pink light unfolded into a doorway.

Pretty.

Soft.

Suspicious.

Beyond it, the Lucky Wheel waited.

This was Dorm333's reward scene. Dorm336's second-place result remained outside it, in ranking screens, academy support schedules, teacher debriefs, and the pressure of the next round. Bringing them into this doorway would not make their result more real. It would only make the rules feel fake.

Lan understood that.

So did the teachers.

Qian swallowed. "Captain, if luck has formation positions, put me in the center."

"The center of discipline," Liu said.

"A cruel formation."

Lan stepped toward the doorway.

Yin's lesson still echoed inside him.

Use every tool.

Do not discard the people holding the tools.

He looked at Qian and Liu.

"Together," he said.

The three entered the pink light.
