# Chapter 6: The Right Wings

Lan Xuanyu left Zi Luo City with a promise in his pocket.

It was not an important promise by adult standards. No contract stamped by the Federation. No academy record. No soul guidance seal. Only a promise made under the school gate while holiday notices still shone on the classroom boards and parents called children toward waiting cars.

"Pictures," Yan Shuo had said.

Lan Xuanyu nodded with the solemnity of a child accepting a mission. "Pictures."

"Of the spacecraft."

"Yes."

"Of the stars."

"If the window lets me."

"Of anything with wings."

Lan Xuanyu tilted his head. "Spacecraft do not have wings."

"Then anything that should have wings."

That required thought. Lan Xuanyu thought about it so carefully that Yan Shuo almost regretted the instruction. Then Lan Xuanyu brightened. "I will look."

Yan Shuo wanted to say more. Good luck. Do not get scared. Bring back something better than your face. Do not let Ye Lingtong bother you from another planet. Do not forget that I reached the wall too.

The last thought stayed in his throat.

He had reached rank ten before final exam week, but a bottleneck was not a ring. It was only a door, and his side of the door had no handle yet.

So he bumped his shoulder against Lan Xuanyu's shoulder and said, "Do not come back boring."

Lan Xuanyu looked offended. "I am never boring."

"Sometimes you are politely boring."

"That is different."

Yan Shuo laughed first. Lan Xuanyu followed half a breath later.

Then Nan Cheng called, Lan Xiao waved, and the Lan family's holiday became real.

The next morning, Lan Xuanyu sat in a soul guidance flying car and asked for the sixth time whether they would soon board the spacecraft.

Nan Cheng answered with patience sharpened by love. Lan Xiao smiled from the front seat. Zi Luo City's tunnels carried their car toward the aviation base in ordered streams of metal and light. From there they would take a Spirit Guidance plane to Heaven Luo City, then pass through the space center that only the capital could support, then climb beyond Heaven Luo Planet toward the first migration planet in human history.

Heaven Dou Planet.

Lan Xuanyu pressed his face close to the window without touching it because Nan Cheng had already warned him about smudges.

The trip had been booked months before the final exam. It was meant to be a reward, a family holiday, a way to let their son see a wider world before secrets pressed his childhood too flat. It was also expensive enough that Lan Xiao pretended not to calculate the remaining savings where Nan Cheng could see him.

At the Heaven Luo City space center, Lan Xuanyu forgot every number that adults cared about.

A wall-sized screen showed the starry dark and the silver lines of Federation ships. A galactic battle group moved across the image like a city of blades. The largest vessel looked less like a ship and more like a fortress that had decided planets were optional.

"Battleships," Lan Xuanyu breathed.

Nan Cheng put a finger to her lips. "Lower voice. Public manners."

Lan Xuanyu lowered his voice and somehow kept all the excitement in it. "Battleships."

Lan Xiao explained what he could: galactic fleets, migration protection, the difficulty of becoming a battleship pilot, the long road through studies and specialized academies. Lan Xuanyu listened with the kind of shining attention that made a father's heart become foolish.

"I want to pilot one," he said.

Lan Xiao patted his head. "Then study hard."

That answer satisfied Lan Xuanyu for exactly three seconds before he saw the spacecraft through the glass.

It stood upright on the launch ground like a pale artillery shell aimed at the sky. Propulsion units held its main body, ready to detach and return after launch. The adults saw engineering, cost, scheduling, safety risks, and the long irritation of travel procedures. Lan Xuanyu saw a road into space.

He almost forgot to take a picture.

Almost.

The image he sent to Yan Shuo arrived later than he expected. The message was short because Lan Xuanyu was too excited to type properly.

Real spacecraft. Bigger than school. No wings but should count.

Yan Shuo received it while sitting on the floor of the Spirit Pagoda branch waiting hall with his knees pulled up and his palms pressed together to stop them from warming the seat.

He stared at the picture for a long time.

"It does not have wings," he said.

Yan Ke, who had been filling out a form with the despair of a man asked to summarize his child's entire future in six boxes, leaned over. "That is what you noticed?"

"It should have wings."

"If you write that to the spacecraft engineers, please do not use my office address."

Lin Mei sat on Yan Shuo's other side, reading the Spirit Pagoda consultation notice again. She had read it before leaving home, once in the transit lift, twice at the entrance, and once more while the receptionist confirmed their appointment. Each reading made her expression calmer and Yan Ke's less calm.

The notice said what every rank ten child eventually learned: to step into the next realm, a soul master needed a soul ring. In the current era, the legal and ordinary route was a soul spirit supplied through recognized channels. Compatibility mattered. Quality mattered. Cost mattered. Family status mattered more than polite brochures admitted.

Yan Shuo had known the words before.

Today they had weight.

Around him, the Spirit Pagoda branch looked nothing like the academy. The ceilings were higher, the light cooler, the walls carved with stylized images of spirit beasts, spirit souls, and famous soul masters whose names meant more to adults than children. Screens showed rotating options: plant types, beast types, elemental imprints, ten-year, hundred-year, premium packages, safety guidelines, payment plans.

Payment plans made Yan Ke's jaw tight.

Yan Shuo's body noticed everything. The cold floor under his shoes. The faint dryness of filtered air. The way his heat rose when a screen flashed a flame-type soul spirit and settled when Lin Mei's fingers brushed his shoulder. The bottleneck inside him did not sleep. It waited beneath his ribs, a clear wall touched by every breath.

Lan Xuanyu was not beside him.

That absence mattered more than Yan Shuo wanted to admit.

For years, his friend's presence had been part of the weather of his life: gentle voice, strange heaviness, laughter, hidden pressure that his body had learned like a familiar hill on a daily road. Now the air around Yan Shuo felt lighter and less complete. Without that weight, his Fire Phoenix rose too easily, eager to push at the wall.

His body corrected before pride could become action. Heat widened from his chest into his back, then folded down into his belly. Breath slowed. Fingers unclenched.

Lin Mei saw the unclenching. "Good."

"I did not do anything."

"You did not do the foolish thing. That is something."

A consultant called their number.

The first list of soul spirits was impressive in the way shop windows were impressive. A Flame-Tailed Fox with clean output and quick-footed enhancement. A Blazing Thorn Vine suitable for binding fire along the ground. A Young Ember Hawk with simple fire compatibility and predictable first-skill paths. All legal. All safe. All expensive once the compatibility refinement fees appeared.

The consultant smiled as if numbers were not weapons. "For a Fire Phoenix martial soul, we recommend stable fire-bird alignment. Of course, because the martial soul grade appears high, you may also consider saving for a stronger hundred-year option. However, reservation fees rise quickly."

Yan Ke looked at one price and then at another, perhaps hoping the first had been a printing error.

It had not.

Lin Mei asked questions the consultant respected: output instability, spiritual burden, long-term growth, whether the soul spirit's pattern would limit avian movement, whether too much raw fire would worsen a child's emotional flares. The consultant's smile became less decorative and more professional.

Yan Shuo listened.

He liked the Ember Hawk at first because it looked like what everyone expected him to want. Fire. Bird. Straight path. The projection flared red, lifted its wings, and scattered sparks in a neat circle.

It was beautiful.

It also felt finished.

His Fire Phoenix stirred, interested but not hungry.

They left without choosing.

That, Yan Shuo discovered, was worse than failing a test. Failing ended. Waiting continued.

In space, waiting became Lan Xuanyu's enemy too.

The spacecraft was called 7703. Its announcements were detailed enough to satisfy any adult and exciting enough to delight Lan Xuanyu for exactly one day. Automated belts fastened around chest, waist, and legs. Protective covers rose around child seats. The safety broadcast explained oxygen, pressure control, turbulence, wormhole procedures, and rescue capsules that sounded much less comforting once Lan Xuanyu understood they floated in the cosmos until someone found them.

At launch, excitement became fear.

The pressure pushed him into the seat. The vibration filled his bones. His breathing turned difficult, and for a moment space was not a dream but a force trying to prove he was small.

Then warmth spread from his right arm.

It moved through his body like someone placing a hand against his back. The fear loosened. The pressure remained, but it no longer filled the whole world. When the worst of the shaking passed and the clouds slid by outside, Lan Xuanyu let out a long breath.

"Mommy," he whispered later, after the protective cover opened, "the spacecraft is a little scary when it takes off."

Nan Cheng hugged him. Lan Xiao teased him about becoming a pilot. Lan Xuanyu straightened immediately and said he still wanted it.

For a day, he explored everything his cabin allowed.

By the second day, space became smaller.

Ordinary cabin seats did not care about a child's dreams. Meals tasted like mashed potatoes given responsibilities beyond their ability. Passengers could not simply wander into higher-class cabins. The window showed beauty, yes, but beauty did not answer questions after the hundredth look.

"I am bored," Lan Xuanyu announced.

"Meditate," Nan Cheng said, far too quickly.

So he did.

Under the protective cover, following the Mysterious Heaven Skill, he entered stillness faster than before. His Spiritual Power had changed after Ye Feng's pressure; he did not understand the numbers, but he felt the result. Thoughts settled. Soul power moved more smoothly. The darkness behind his eyes filled with lights.

Blue lights he knew.

Silver lights he did not.

They drifted around him like little fish in a river he could feel but not command. One brighter silver point seemed to lead the others. When he woke after six hours, Nan Cheng told him those were space elements.

"Space elements?" Lan Xuanyu repeated.

"Yes. Spirit masters with space affinity often like cultivating during space travel. Feeling space well can help with battleship piloting."

Lan Xuanyu's eyes lit so brightly that Nan Cheng knew exactly what she had done.

"Then I can be a pilot."

"You still have to study."

"I will."

Far away, Tang Le revealed his face to the Federation.

The press conference played on the spacecraft's entertainment feed while Lan Xiao watched news from the mother planet. Nan Cheng, who had just finished saying she had no interest in pretty male stars, leaned over with casual disdain that lasted only until the starlight around Tang Le disappeared.

The man on the screen had blue hair, calm eyes, and a melancholy that made even noise seem to soften around him. Reporters fell into stunned silence, then erupted all at once. Bei Tianhong of Xing Tang Media looked like a man who already knew he had found a treasure. Tang Le introduced himself with only a few words, yet the whole room bent toward him.

Nan Cheng's eyes shone.

Lan Xiao turned slowly to look at his wife.

"Embroidered pillow?" he asked.

Nan Cheng did not look away from the screen. "I do not remember saying that."

"Mommy, you did," Lan Xuanyu said helpfully.

Nan Cheng betrayed no shame. "Then Mommy was wrong."

Lan Xiao turned off the screen.

This did not save him.

The spacecraft reached Heaven Dou Planet after seven days. Lan Xuanyu stepped out feeling slightly floaty and very ready to claim that battleships were still better than passenger travel. The city beyond the space center was vast, green and metal woven together, old by interstellar standards and new compared with the mother planet's ancient weight. Nan Cheng's itinerary waited like a gentle ambush.

Museums. Parks. Shopping. More shopping if Lan Xiao failed to defend the budget.

Yan Shuo studied a different kind of sky.

The Spirit Pagoda sent a longer list the next day, then another after Lin Mei requested nonstandard fire-bird compatibility options and Yan Ke requested something that did not require selling their furniture, their future, or possibly Yan Ke himself.

The third list contained a warning mark.

Dawnflame Kite.

Late ten-year grade, recorded age ninety-nine years. Fire-and-light avian soul spirit. Strong bloodline response for its grade. Repeated failed 99-to-100-year stabilization. Fire-light split risk. Not recommended for ordinary fire martial souls. Not recommended for ordinary light martial souls. Discounted evaluation category pending reassignment.

Yan Shuo read the name twice.

The projection did not flare like the Ember Hawk.

At first it looked dim.

A slender bird shape perched inside the light cage, wings folded too tight. Its feathers should have been red. Or gold. They were both, and neither, fire bleeding toward light and light thinning the flame until the outline trembled. When it lifted its head, the glow around it brightened beautifully for one heartbeat.

Then it collapsed inward.

The consultant cleared his throat. "This one is usually skipped. Its quality is not low, but the evaluation risk is high. Every time its imprint approaches the exactly 100-year result, the fire and light patterns interfere. For a child's first ring, stability is generally more important."

Lin Mei did not answer at once.

Yan Ke did. "Why show it to us, then?"

The consultant looked toward Lin Mei. "Madam Yan requested nonstandard fire-bird possibilities with growth potential and strict budget limits. This fits the search terms. I am obligated to state again that standard choices are safer."

Yan Shuo leaned closer to the projection.

The Dawnflame Kite's head turned.

It was not alive like a person was alive. It did not speak. It did not understand forms, school, promises, or fathers pretending not to panic about money. But something in that small, bright, collapsing shape recognized heat. Or wanted it. Or feared it.

Yan Shuo's bottleneck pulsed.

His Fire Phoenix did not surge to crush the projection. It went still, deep inside him, as if a larger bird had seen a smaller one trapped in bad wind.

"It's stuck," Yan Shuo said.

"Yes," the consultant replied. "That is the problem."

Yan Shuo shook his head slowly. "No. That is not all of it."

Lin Mei's gaze shifted to him.

He did not have the words. He only had the feeling of a wall beneath his ribs and a bird one step from becoming more than it could manage alone. It was not perfect. It was not safe. It was not the brightest option on the screen.

It was trying.

That night, before they made any decision, Lan Xuanyu's second message arrived from Heaven Dou Planet.

The picture showed a museum display of Blue Silver Grass behind glass.

Lan Xuanyu's message was longer this time, probably because Lan Xiao or Nan Cheng had helped him spell.

The guide said Blue Silver Grass helped make Heaven Dou Planet livable because it survives well and cleans the air. It is not useless. It helped a planet.

Yan Shuo stared at the words.

He knew Lan Xuanyu's Blue Silver Grass was not ordinary, no matter what public records said. But the display did not know that. The museum was not praising hidden gold and silver mysteries. It praised the ordinary grass because it endured, spread, changed air, and gave later life a place to stand.

Something inside Yan Shuo softened around the Dawnflame Kite's image.

"Not useless," he murmured.

Yan Ke, reading over his shoulder, sniffed in a way that pretended not to be emotional. "Your friend sends educational guilt from another planet."

"It is not guilt."

"It is very good guilt. Museum-grade."

Lin Mei took the device and read the message. Then she looked at her child. "Do not choose a soul spirit because you pity it."

"I don't."

"Do not choose it because it looks like a story."

"I know."

"Then why?"

Yan Shuo looked down at his hands. When he breathed, the bottleneck answered. The wall was still there. The Fire Phoenix behind it was warm and watchful.

"Because I think it can become right with me," he said. "Not because it is already right. Because it is trying to be."

Lin Mei was silent for a long time.

Yan Ke stopped making jokes.

At last Lin Mei said, "Then we test again. If the numbers reject you, the answer is no. If your body rejects it, the answer is no. If I judge the risk too high, the answer is no. You may choose seriously, but you do not choose alone."

Yan Shuo nodded at once.

That was fair.

Earlier that same day on Heaven Dou Planet, Lan Xuanyu had met silver hair.

The interstellar migration museum had many things adults cared about: relics from the first exploration teams, old tools, historical records, models of Heaven Dou Planet's strange geography, and explanations of how humans and spirit beasts had entered an age where expansion became survival. Lan Xuanyu listened politely, but the thing that made him stand straighter was still the Blue Silver Grass.

His own martial soul's public name stood inside a museum as part of history.

He had just told his parents how important it was when a soft voice behind them repeated the words as if waking from a dream.

Blue Silver Grass.

The woman wore a black mask and had silver hair long enough to touch the ground. Beside her stood a golden-haired companion. Even before the mask came off, her eyes made the museum seem quieter.

Lan Xuanyu told her proudly that his martial soul was Blue Silver Grass.

Nana looked at him.

For one instant, the world inside her broke its stillness.

Pain struck through her head. Familiarity without memory flooded up so sharply that she stepped back and knelt, hands at her temples. Yun Yan panicked. Lan Xiao and Nan Cheng moved in alarm.

Lan Xuanyu did not understand any of that.

He saw only silver hair touching the floor.

"Auntie, your hair will get dirty," he said, and gathered it carefully.

The moment his hands touched her hair, Nana's pain eased.

The child rubbed the hair against his cheek because it was soft and because he was still young enough to trust wonder before rules. Nan Cheng hurried to correct him. Nana did not mind. She looked at him as if she had lost something beyond time and found only a sound of it in a child's voice.

When Lan Xuanyu asked why she wore a mask, she removed it.

The museum light seemed to lose an argument.

Lan Xuanyu told her she was beautiful.

Nana told him he was beautiful too.

Neither understood what the words had opened.

On Heaven Luo Planet, the Spirit Pagoda testing room opened a different door.

Yan Shuo stood inside a circular chamber with Lin Mei behind the safety line, Yan Ke beside her with both hands clasped so tightly his knuckles paled, and a senior evaluator watching from a console. The Dawnflame Kite's imprint hovered in a controlled light sphere at the center.

"Last warning," the evaluator said. "The compatibility response is unusually high, but the instability risk remains real. The soul spirit is stuck at ninety-nine years for a reason. If the child cannot stabilize the fire-light split quickly, we abort."

"We abort," Lin Mei agreed.

Yan Ke swallowed. "Aborting is good. I like aborting if needed. Very responsible word."

Yan Shuo barely heard him.

The small bird inside the light sphere trembled.

His own body trembled back, not from fear alone. Every ordinary thing he had lived through seemed to wake at once: falling as a toddler and learning where heat should not go; running in artificial air and teaching breath to change; standing beside Lan Xuanyu's hidden heaviness and staying himself; burning gloves, eating too much, sleeping too hot, listening to his mother's warnings, stopping before pride could leap.

None of it felt like a special power.

It felt like being Yan Shuo.

"Begin," the evaluator said.

The light sphere opened.

The Dawnflame Kite entered him as heat and brightness and panic.

For one heartbeat, it was beautiful.

Then it split.

Fire rushed upward, wild and red-gold, trying to become a wing by burning everything that held it. Light pulled thin and sharp in the opposite direction, trying to refine the flame until nothing warm remained. Between them, Yan Shuo's chest became the sky of a storm too large for a child's body.

He gasped.

Outside, the monitors screamed warnings.

Lin Mei took one step forward.

The evaluator lifted a hand over the abort command.

Yan Shuo heard none of it clearly.

Inside him, the Fire Phoenix opened its eyes.

It did not roar. It did not devour the smaller bird. It spread itself as a shape: breast, spine, wing-root, feather-line, beak pointed toward dawn. A place where fire could rise without scattering. A place where light could shine without thinning into nothing.

The Dawnflame Kite fought it at first.

Not because it hated him.

Because it was afraid of failing in the same place again.

Its fire had always burned away its light. Its light had always weakened its fire. Every attempt to cross the last step had ended in collapse. The memory of that failure lived in the imprint like a cage.

Yan Shuo's hands curled.

"Breathe," Lin Mei said from somewhere far away.

He breathed.

His body had been doing that for years: changing breath before thought, moving heat away from damage, letting pain become information rather than command. Now every small habit answered together. His ribs widened. His meridians loosened where the fire tried to scrape. His spiritual sea warmed like a nest built from sunrise. The wall of rank ten did not shatter; it opened a narrow path and demanded that everything pass in the correct shape.

The fire tried to fly upward.

Yan Shuo gave it a circle.

The light tried to flee outward.

Yan Shuo gave it a center.

The Fire Phoenix lowered its head, and from its breast a single seed of deeper flame fell into the Dawnflame Kite's split core.

The small bird froze.

Then it cried.

The sound never reached the room as sound, but every monitor flashed. Red and gold turned dawn-bright. Light no longer thinned the fire; it lined each flame like the edge of a feather. Fire no longer scattered the light; it gave it warmth and direction. The Dawnflame Kite's trembling outline stretched, not into a phoenix, not into something complete, but into a bird that finally knew which way the sky was.

Ninety-nine became one hundred.

A ring rose around Yan Shuo.

For the first breath it was so pale that Yan Ke's heart stopped.

Then yellow settled over it, clean and steady.

Lin Mei exhaled.

The evaluator stared at the data.

Yan Shuo opened his eyes.

A mantle of feather-shaped fire and light clung to his arms, shoulders, upper back, and chest. It did not become full wings. It did not lift him from the floor. It was close, living, warm, and strange. Each feather-line knew where his skin ended and flame began. When he moved his fingers, the mantle rippled like dawn caught in a bird's breath.

The Dawnflame Kite was there too.

Not outside. Not speaking. Not a tool in his hand.

Inside the warmth behind his ribs, a small presence tucked itself into the nest his body had made. Frightened. Exhausted. Proud in a new and fragile way.

Yan Shuo smiled before he had the strength for it.

"Hello," he whispered.

The mantle flickered.

Then his stomach growled so loudly that Yan Ke made a sound halfway between a sob and a laugh.

The evaluator looked at the records again. "Threshold correction confirmed. Hundred-year stabilization confirmed. First ring yellow. Skill manifestation stable for initial release. This is... rare."

Lin Mei's voice became careful. "Rare how?"

"Rare compatibility," the evaluator said, choosing the safest category. "The soul spirit's original flaw appears to have stabilized during fusion. We will mark the age correction as ninety-nine years to exactly one hundred years under high compatibility. Further observation recommended."

Yan Ke recovered enough to ask the most important question. "Further observation with extra fees?"

The evaluator paused.

Lin Mei closed her eyes briefly.

Yan Shuo sat down on the floor because his legs had voted without consulting him.

The mantle vanished. The yellow ring faded. The warmth remained.

He was asleep before the adults finished arguing about recovery protocol.

The days passed quickly.

Nana did not pass through them.

She remained behind the edges of Lan Xuanyu's thoughts after the museum, and he remained inside hers. In her quiet room, where she had once enjoyed empty thought, his eyes appeared again and again. His voice. His small hands lifting her hair. The ache in her head, the warmth when he touched her, the sense that something impossible had recognized her before memory could.

Yun Yan asked whether she had remembered something.

Nana said she did not know.

That was true.

It was also not enough.

At the hotel, Lan Xuanyu practiced water control in the bathroom while Nan Cheng supervised. Since Ye Feng's pressure, the silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass on his left hand had answered differently. Water gathered faster. Ice formed cleanly. A whirlpool became a funnel, then an ice spear, then scattered obediently back into droplets.

Nan Cheng watched her son use water and ice with a freedom she herself, an ice-element Spirit Emperor, did not possess.

Pride and worry stood side by side inside her.

Later, when Lan Xuanyu slept, Lan Xiao spoke of the exploration assignment waiting for him after they returned: a new planet, likely life forms, high allowance, a chance for promotion, money enough to find their son a proper tutor. Nan Cheng feared the distance and the danger. Lan Xiao softened the danger with logistics words. Neither of them fully won the argument.

They both knew only one thing clearly.

Lan Xuanyu needed guidance they could not fully give.

In Zi Luo City, Yan Shuo woke after the absorption with hunger large enough to frighten Yan Ke's grocery plan.

The Dawnflame Kite slept inside him.

He knew that without knowing how he knew. When he closed his eyes, there was a small warmth tucked near the Fire Phoenix, feathered and tired. It did not speak. It sent impressions: high air, split light, fear of falling, relief so fierce it almost hurt.

Yan Shuo spent the first day doing almost nothing.

This was torture.

"Can I release the ring once?"

"No," Lin Mei said.

"Half once?"

"No."

"Can I think about it?"

"You are already doing that loudly."

Yan Ke brought soup, roasted grain buns, two protein bars, fruit, and a small bowl of something Lin Mei said would support recovery and Yan Shuo said tasted like punished grass.

He ate all of it.

His body used the food like dry earth taking rain. Heat moved while he rested: from stomach to chest, from chest to shoulders, from shoulders into the faint new channels where the mantle had appeared. Every nap left the lines clearer. Every waking stretch taught him one more boundary between skin and feather-flame. Passive change did not stop because the dramatic moment had ended. If anything, it became busier.

The second day, Lin Mei allowed one release under strict supervision.

A yellow ring rose at Yan Shuo's feet.

The sight stole his breath even though he knew it was coming.

Dawnfeather Mantle formed close over his shoulders and arms. It was smaller than the first time. Softer. The feather-lines trembled if he became excited. When he tried to make them bigger, the mantle thinned and the Dawnflame Kite's presence flinched.

Yan Shuo stopped at once.

"Sorry," he whispered.

Lin Mei heard him but did not ask who he was apologizing to.

"Again," she said gently. "Smaller. Hold, do not pull."

He tried again.

This time the mantle settled.

Not mastered. Not strong enough to boast. But real.

On Heaven Dou Planet, Lan Xuanyu lost himself in a shop of battle armor kits.

He had already fallen in love with the golden Dragon Emperor Douluo model, a miniature of legendary battle armor that adults explained as history and children understood as glory. On the last shopping day, while Nan Cheng prepared to pay for other purchases, Lan Xuanyu drifted toward another display and found its pair: a silver figure with long hair, purple eyes, wings, and a spear.

Silver Dragon Emperor Douluo.

He wanted it immediately.

It was limited, expensive, and the last one in the store.

Then Nana appeared and said she would buy it for him.

Lan Xuanyu refused because his mother had taught him not to accept gifts from strangers. Nana offered a child's logic: she had touched his cheeks, so perhaps that counted as payment. The salesperson laughed and called him a cheeky little monster.

Nana's eyes turned cold.

For one instant, the air around the salesperson seemed to freeze.

Lan Xuanyu pulled Nana's hand, confused, and Nana restrained herself. Lan Xiao arrived before the moment could become worse. He recognized the silver-haired woman from the museum and insisted on paying for the kit himself. Nana allowed it because Lan Xuanyu's joy mattered more to her than winning a small argument about money.

After the Lan father and son left, Nana remained in place with the softness of the child's hand still living against her palm.

She followed them.

Not like a threat.

Like someone whose heart had gone ahead without permission and needed the body to catch up.

Yan Shuo's first public record did not include the word miracle.

It included safer words.

Fire Phoenix martial soul. Soul power: rank twelve. Spiritual Power: thirty-six points, Spirit Origin Realm. First ring yellow. Soul spirit: Dawnflame Kite, recorded at ninety-nine years before absorption and corrected to exactly one hundred years during fusion. First soul skill: Dawnfeather Mantle. Compatibility: excellent. Follow-up observation advised. Family notified of growth and recovery requirements.

The evaluator explained the number twice because Yan Ke asked twice. Rank ten had been the wall. The first ring opened it. The cultivation Yan Shuo had continued to press into that wall before the absorption had not disappeared, and the Dawnflame Kite's exact one-year correction had not been an empty color change. Most of the force had gone into stabilizing the new bond, but enough remained to settle him clearly at rank twelve.

Yan Ke read the report at home and tapped the phrase ninety-nine-to-one-hundred corrected. "This is a very polite way to say everyone panicked."

"It is a very useful way to say nothing too loudly," Lin Mei replied.

Yan Shuo sat cross-legged on the mat with the Dawnflame Kite's warmth dozing inside him and tried to make the mantle appear only around his forearms. It formed around his shoulders too.

"Too much," Lin Mei said.

He pulled back. The mantle vanished completely.

"Too little."

Yan Shuo opened one eye. "Maybe the mantle disagrees with homework."

"The mantle is not the problem."

The Dawnflame Kite sent a tiny flicker of agreement.

Yan Shuo felt betrayed by his own soul spirit.

He also felt ridiculously happy.

The happiness mattered. The bond answered trust better than force. When he treated the soul spirit like a weapon to swing, the feather-lines stiffened. When he treated it like a living presence learning alongside him, the warmth softened and the yellow ring stabilized faster. His body noticed. His breath changed. The new channels became less foreign by the hour.

That night, Yan Shuo sent Lan Xuanyu a message.

I got my first ring. It has wings but not for flying. I will show you when you come back. Do not be jealous unless you want to.

He almost added that the soul spirit felt like a small bird sleeping in his chest.

He erased that part.

Some things were too new to put into a message.

In Heaven Dou Shopping Mall, Nana put her new wish into words.

"I can be his teacher," she said.

Lan Xiao and Nan Cheng did not know what to do with a stranger so beautiful she seemed unreal, a woman who liked their son with frightening sincerity, who claimed she could teach him, who carried credentials from the Heaven Dou science-and-technology side, and who seemed to have appeared twice by coincidence after coincidence until coincidence became a very thin blanket.

Lan Xiao checked her communication device because he loved his son more than politeness. The record said Nana, twenty-five, ancient martial arts teacher, Heaven Dou branch, tier-three job ranking. Simple. Legal. Too simple for comfort, but not false.

He raised practical problems: they lived on Heaven Luo Planet, could not pay a tutor from Heaven Dou Planet, and could not ask her to abandon her job.

Nana said she needed no money.

That made the offer more frightening, not less.

So she proved ability.

From an upper corridor in the mall, behind a tall glass railing, she looked down at the great fountain pool on the lower level. No obvious soul-power fluctuation rose from her. Lan Xiao and Nan Cheng could not sense how she moved power at all.

Lan Xuanyu felt warmth in his left arm and wanted to step toward her.

Nan Cheng held him back.

Below, the fountain changed.

Water that had been flat as a mirror began to turn. A vortex lifted from the pool. Then a dragon rose from it, water forming whiskers, claws, scales, and eyes with impossible delicacy. Another followed. Then another. Nine water dragons circled through the open heart of the mall around a suspended blue droplet, grand enough that shoppers on every level stopped, gasped, recorded, and believed perhaps the mall had arranged a surprise show.

Nan Cheng could barely breathe.

She was a Spirit Emperor. She knew enough about water and ice to understand that this was not merely strong. It was control beyond the shape of her own understanding.

"Titled Douluo?" she asked.

Nana hesitated, then accepted the word because it seemed to help them place her somewhere their world could understand.

When the water returned to the fountain without a splash, Lan Xiao's remaining objections lost most of their strength.

How did one refuse a Titled Douluo who asked softly and looked at one's child as if guarding him were the only thing she remembered how to do?

Still, the spacecraft home waited. Seats had been booked. Rules existed. Nana did not appear in the waiting room the next morning, and Lan Xuanyu pouted until Lan Xiao told him that fate would decide whether they met again.

Lan Xiao hoped fate might be busy elsewhere.

He was wrong, but not yet.

On the same morning, Yan Shuo stood in the practice room with his yellow ring rising steady at his feet.

"Again," Lin Mei said.

He released Dawnfeather Mantle.

The feather-light formed over arms, shoulders, upper back, and chest. It was close-fitting and warm, not a wing, not armor exactly, not simple fire. When he punched the padded target, the mantle carried the force along his forearm and bled recoil back through his shoulder instead of letting it jar his wrist. When he stepped forward, a small flare from the upper back almost pushed him too far.

He stumbled.

The Dawnflame Kite startled inside him.

His body corrected on the second step, knees bending, breath spreading the heat wider.

Lin Mei's eyes sharpened. "What did you feel?"

"Too much from the back," Yan Shuo said, rubbing his shoulder. "It wanted to jump."

"And you?"

"I wanted to let it."

"Wrong answer?"

"Honest answer."

Lin Mei looked at him for one long moment, then nodded. "Good. Honest answers can be trained. Hidden pride burns houses."

Yan Ke, watching from the doorway, lifted a fire-suppression canister slightly.

"Put that down," Lin Mei said without looking.

He put it down.

Yan Shuo laughed. The mantle rippled with him, dawn-gold lines brightening along the feather edges. Inside his chest, the Dawnflame Kite answered with a small, proud flicker.

This was a change that belonged wholly to him.

Not a stolen destiny. Not a secret from an egg. Not a dragon's pressure or a silver-haired woman's memory. A flawed little soul spirit, a Fire Phoenix still young, a family that had chosen caution before pride, and a boy who had reached the right door early enough to learn that the right key mattered.

Far above Heaven Dou Planet, Spacecraft 7703 prepared to return.

Back in Zi Luo City, Yan Shuo tried again.

The mantle held for three breaths.

Then four.

Then five.

Not flight.

Not yet.

But wings did not begin by carrying the sky.

They began by learning where the body ended and air began.
