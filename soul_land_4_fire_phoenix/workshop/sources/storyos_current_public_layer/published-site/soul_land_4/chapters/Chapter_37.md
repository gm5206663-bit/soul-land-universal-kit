# Chapter 37: The Dragon Outside the Window

Space did not sound like the simulation pod.

In the simulation pod, every explosion came with carefully measured feedback. Every alarm had a practice-room edge to it. Even when the scenario killed him, the world eventually turned black and returned Lan Xuanyu to a cabin that smelled faintly of metal, medicine, and Yin Tianfan's unreasonable expectations.

Real space had no reset.

The moment the small warship shot out from the lower deck of the civilian spacecraft, Lan Xuanyu felt as if every pore of his body had opened at once.

His Spiritual Power poured into the control system.

The warship answered.

Not like a tool.

Not exactly like a body either.

More like a second set of bones made from metal, response arrays, thruster lines, and weapons that would not forgive the pilot for being twelve.

The instruments flared around him. The helmet pressed close. His hands found the buttons and levers by memory before his eyes finished reading them. Simulation had carved the route into his muscles. Reality added weight.

No pain reduction.

No teacher's voice pausing the battle.

No second attempt.

That was why this was not only a battle scene.

It was a profession test with no examiner kind enough to announce the score.

A soul skill could flare because blood was hot. A martial soul could answer because danger shouted loudly enough. But a space-warship pilot was built from dull, repeated, unforgiving things: traffic routes, ship-class diagrams, emergency launch procedure, inertial mathematics, docking failures, simulator deaths, Soul Guidance vehicle control, hostile-lock alarms, and Yin Tianfan's voice asking whether Lan Xuanyu intended to kill himself before or after wasting the aircraft.

For three years, Interplanet Command had not been a decoration beside his cultivation. It had been a second growth route. While other numbers crawled slowly on his Soul Power report, his hands learned panels, his Spiritual Power learned control links, and his mind learned that a profession was not what one liked. It was what still functioned when fear removed every comfortable thought.

Now the academy's secondary-profession ledger, Yin Tianfan's brutal lessons, and every failed simulation pressed into the same cockpit.

Lan inhaled slowly.

Spiritual Power was the first requirement of a space warship pilot. Not Soul Power. Not physical strength. Spiritual Power. Ordinary humans could become mecha masters and even pilots through training, but the better the spiritual control, the more precisely soul guidance technology could be made to answer.

The basic real-piloting requirement was fifty points, Spirit Connection Realm.

Lan Xuanyu had long passed that.

Now his school record already sat at a level that made teachers whisper: Spirit Sea, over eight hundred.

The machine did not care that his Soul Power was still Rank19.

The machine cared that his mind could stretch without tearing.

It stretched now.

The battlefield opened.

Twelve allied mechas — 12 in total — had already launched from the civilian spacecraft: eight purple mechas from Young Noble Le's bodyguards and four yellow mechas from the ship's own security. Opposite them, twenty pirate mechas — 20 dark-yellow machines — rushed out from the five small pirate ships, dark shapes breaking away from their carriers like insects from a nest.

The first exchange filled the void with light.

Lan's breath caught.

For one dangerous instant, he looked too far.

The warship trembled under his hands.

Focus.

He pulled his senses back from the whole battlefield into the part that could kill him first.

Four pirate mechas peeled away from the main clash and came straight for him.

That was correct.

In space, a warship had speed and firepower a mecha could not match if it reached proper momentum. Four mechas sent to crush him before he became dangerous was not arrogance.

It was the right answer.

Lan Xuanyu's fingers moved.

Do not fight before reaching speed.

Yin Tianfan had said it so many times that the sentence had become less like teaching and more like a curse.

A space warship that slowed down before it possessed momentum was not cautious.

It was prey.

Lan turned away.

On the monitor, the four mechas adjusted immediately and pursued. Heavy Soul Guidance cannons locked direction. Their pilots probably thought the warship was fleeing in panic.

Good.

Let them think that.

The little warship carved a shallow curve away from the battlefield. Lan did not look at the civilian spacecraft behind him except through the corner of his awareness. His parents were there. Nan Cheng's hands might already be clenched around the safety belt. Lan Xiao would be calculating what no parent could calculate: whether there was anything he could do when his child was outside the ship in a warship he had never truly flown before.

Lan could not think about that.

He pressed the thruster lever forward.

Acceleration slammed him into the seat.

The equalizer swallowed most of the g-force. Most was not all. His ribs tightened. His vision narrowed at the edges. The warship's rear thruster spat red-hot radiance and the small craft shot forward like a stone flung by a giant hand.

The four pirate mechas followed.

Lan's breathing steadied.

Ten seconds.

A little more.

Terminal velocity.

His hands moved together.

The warship drew a large arc and began to turn back.

The pursuing mechas saw it and spread.

Four angles.

Four cannon mouths.

Four beam rays lanced toward him.

Lan did not slow.

Reducing speed to evade was the habit of unqualified pilots.

A space warship that lost momentum had already lost half the battle.

The beams arrived.

Lan's hands blurred.

The warship flipped belly-up in a high-speed vertical turn. Two beams passed beneath, vanishing into the dark. He rolled left without letting the flight route drift loose. Another beam missed. The last came where a normal roll should have carried him.

Lan reversed the roll.

The warship snapped against inertia so cleanly that even he felt the structure complain.

The fourth beam slid past.

For the first time since entering space, Lan Xuanyu forgot fear.

The warship was still on route.

The four mechas were closer now.

Close enough.

The cannon charge finished.

By the time he entered the enemy's range, they had also entered his.

A warship could have fired from farther away. Lan did not. Distance gave mechas time to dodge. Close range gave the pilot one cruel instant.

He waited until the pirate beams swept past the side of his craft, lighting the warship's shell in hostile brilliance.

Then he fired through their light.

The charged Soul Guidance cannon released.

A white beam cut through the glare and struck the moment the nearest mecha's shield thinned from its own attack output.

The mecha exploded.

Sparks scattered into space.

Lan's heart hammered once.

Hit.

Not luck.

Timing.

The other three mechas hesitated.

That hesitation was another opening.

Lan's warship shot past them at high speed, not stopping to celebrate, not turning into their formation. He flew toward the five pirate ships where the larger danger waited.

Accumulate.

Angle.

Fire.

A second pirate mecha near the carriers vanished in a burst of light.

Inside the civilian spacecraft, Captain Sun Weiping stared at the monitor with his mouth half open.

The warship's movement did not belong to a frightened passenger.

It did not even look like a basic pilot fighting for survival.

"Ace pilot?" someone behind him whispered.

Sun Weiping did not answer.

He wanted the answer to be yes too badly.

On the pirate side, the answer arrived faster.

The little warship became the center of their attention.

One of the pirate ships turned.

Cannon lines locked.

Lan saw it and felt his excitement sharpen into warning.

Mecha cannons could damage him. A spaceship cannon could end him in one hit.

A true man did not stand beside a collapsing wall.

Yin Tianfan's words returned with a sneer built into them.

Lan rolled.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

Three horizontal rolls — triple horizontal rolls — were not impossible by themselves. The impossible part was making each roll carry a slight directional change without bleeding away speed or losing control. To observers, the warship seemed briefly out of control. To Lan, each rotation was a line drawn exactly where he needed it.

The pirate ship fired.

Lan was already gone.

He cut diagonally away before the cannon beams crossed the place he had occupied.

Patience.

Sometimes patience was more important than courage.

He circled, looking for the next killing angle.

And that was where he made the mistake.

Two destroyed mechas.

A successful high-speed evasion.

A real battlefield answering his simulator training.

Confidence rose too quickly, hot and sweet. For one fraction of a second, he thought more about what he had done than what he had not seen.

The instruments screamed.

A numbness struck through the warship's control link and into his body.

Lan's fingers stiffened.

Electric current crawled across the cockpit systems. His Spiritual Power connection became thick, slowed, dragged down by interference.

A vast silver net opened in space ahead.

Lan's pupils contracted.

Space hunting net.

Impossible.

The visible pirate ships were meteorite-class surveillance ships. A space hunting net required at least a meteor-class attack spaceship. The five small ships should not have had one.

Unless there was another ship.

Hidden.

The net closed before thought became movement.

The little warship flew into it like a moth darting into fire.

Systems locked.

Thrusters choked.

The control feedback became sludge inside Lan's skull.

He fought the levers with both hands, but the warship only trembled in the net.

The remaining three mechas came around.

Their cannon mouths pointed at him.

Lan's mind went blank.

Careless.

If Master were here, he would have changed direction again. Twice. Three times. Never believing the visible battlefield was the whole battlefield.

The cannons fired.

Golden light appeared.

It was not the glow of a shield.

It was too vast for that.

A giant golden dragon appeared between the warship and death.

Sound could not travel in vacuum, but rage did not need sound to be understood. The dragon opened its mouth in a silent roar, and the three beams struck its body, spreading only ripples of gold.

The three pirate mechas froze.

Then they turned golden.

Then they melted.

Mecha shell and pirate inside vanished like snow thrown into sunlight.

Lan Xuanyu stared.

The dragon turned one wing.

The space hunting net disappeared.

The warship was free.

Lan was not.

Heat surged through his body without permission. His meridians throbbed, swelling with a pressure that felt as if something inside him had recognized something outside him before he did. His blood wanted to move. His martial souls wanted to answer. The cockpit suddenly felt too small for a body that was still only a child's.

The giant golden dragon turned its head.

It looked at him.

Then it nodded.

Human-like.

Gentle, almost.

Before Lan could breathe, the dragon turned and vanished from its place, reappearing among the pirate mechas on the other side of the battlefield.

Now he saw it clearly.

Over a hundred meters long — over 100m, far larger than the little warship.

Far larger than the little warship.

Golden wings opened in the dark, and the battlefield became smaller around them.

The pirate mechas near it stopped as if the universe had ordered them to wait.

Then they melted.

Lan thought of the technique Nana had taught him.

Golden Dragon Soar.

He had practiced it again and again, had imitated the shape, the burst, the intention. He had never truly understood what made it soar.

Now, watching that enormous golden figure move through space, he understood a little.

Not movement.

Dominion over the line movement wished to take.

The golden dragon swept toward one of the small pirate ships.

The meteorite-class surveillance ship was about seventy meters long, a weapon that could terrify a civilian craft.

Beside the dragon, it looked fragile.

Gold passed.

The ship exploded.

The blast shoved the other four pirate ships outward in flame and debris.

Inside first-class, Tang Le stood by the window with long blue hair floating behind him like a banner in a wind only he could feel.

Le Qingling stood near him, stunned beyond fear.

When the golden dragon had appeared, Tang Le's eyes had gone distant. Confusion. Pain. Something trying to wake and failing.

He did not know why the dragon answered him.

He only knew the child outside the window could not die.

Then Lan saw the hidden ship.

It appeared from the dark like a bad answer to a question he had not asked quickly enough.

Over a hundred and fifty meters long — over 150m, with the pressure of a real meteor-class attack spaceship.

Dark green halo around its body.

Meteor-class attack spaceship.

Invisible until now.

Its main cannon had already fired.

The beam was not aimed at Lan alone.

It was aimed at the civilian spacecraft.

Lan was simply in the way.

There was no evasion route.

No speed left to regain.

No clever roll large enough to avoid a line of death meant for a ship full of passengers.

The beam reached him.

Then stopped.

Everything stopped.

The alarms.

The screaming.

The mechas.

The golden dragon.

The drifting flames.

Even the light of the cannon froze in front of Lan's warship, bright enough to fill his cockpit with the color of ending.

Lan could not move.

He could think.

Somewhere in first-class, Le Qingling could think too.

Most others could not even do that.

A figure appeared in the beam.

Human.

Small compared to the attack.

Impossible compared to what he did next.

Blue hair streamed behind him. He walked forward in space as if space were a hallway and a meteor-class main cannon were only poor manners blocking the path.

With each step, the beam retreated.

Not dispersed.

Not blocked.

Retreated.

As if the moment of firing had been persuaded to go backward.

Lan stared until his eyes hurt.

The figure reached the source.

The beam vanished into the cannon's mouth.

Then Tang Le turned.

Across the impossible distance, Lan saw his face clearly.

Azure-blue eyes.

Handsome features.

The uncle who had once carried him while singing Protect Time, Protect You.

Why is he here?

Tang Le stepped onto the bow of the meteor-class attack spaceship.

Eight golden dragons appeared around the ship.

They crashed inward.

The meteor-class attack spaceship exploded into a fireball so vast it swallowed its own arrogance.

Lan Xuanyu forgot how to breathe.

One person.

One person had walked through space, reversed a cannon beam, and destroyed a warship with bare hands and golden dragons.

Was that still a human being?

Was that what a Soul Master could become?

A tapping sound came through the cockpit window.

Dong. Dong. Dong.

Lan turned mechanically.

Tang Le floated outside the warship, blue hair quiet now, expression gentle.

He pointed at Lan.

Then at himself.

His lips moved.

A voice sounded directly in Lan Xuanyu's ears.

"This will be our secret."

Then Tang Le waved.

Golden light flashed.

He disappeared.

Time returned.

The universe remembered how to move.

Far away, explosions bloomed one after another as the giant golden dragon destroyed the remaining pirate ships. Light filled the dark in brief, violent flowers. Allied mechas hung stunned in space. The civilian spacecraft survived.

Lan Xuanyu could move again.

His body did not want to.

His hands remained on the controls, but the battle inside him had moved somewhere deeper than the cockpit.

He had destroyed two pirate mechas.

He had piloted a real warship.

He had also been trapped, helpless, and saved by something beyond everything Yin Tianfan had told him about modern warfare.

Spaceships were the Federation's strongest weapons.

That had been true in his world yesterday.

Today, a man had walked a cannon beam backward.

The warship returned to the lower deck in a silence that felt louder than cheering.

Then the cheering arrived anyway.

The crew gathered when the cockpit opened. Over ten people stood waiting, faces pale with fear and bright with survival. Captain Sun Weiping himself came forward.

"Welcome back, esteemed pilot. I am the captain of this spacecraft, Sun Weiping. On behalf of the entire crew, thank you for..."

His voice stopped.

Lan climbed out of the cockpit.

The captain looked down.

Then farther down.

The pilot was a child.

A handsome child with a pale face, bright eyes still full of the space outside, and a height two heads shorter than the captain.

"You," Sun Weiping said slowly, "are the pilot?"

Lan blinked once.

"Yes."

Sun Weiping looked at the flight attendant who had brought him.

The attendant nodded weakly. "It was him. He said he had learned space warship piloting. I was panicking, so I brought him down."

For a moment, all the praise Sun Weiping had prepared became useless.

What did one say to a child who had just flown into a space battle and destroyed two mechas?

"Cough," Sun Weiping managed. "Kid, you were... terrific. On behalf of everyone, thank you. You truly are a newborn calf that does not fear the tiger."

Lan's mind was still partly outside the ship, watching gold reverse light.

Then one practical thought returned with terrifying force.

His parents.

"Captain uncle," Lan said, suddenly looking pitiful in a way that made several crew members' faces twitch, "can I ask for something?"

Sun Weiping felt an unreasonable fear. "What is it?"

"Can you not tell my parents what I did? Just say I went to the toilet and got stuck there."

The crew went very still.

Someone made a sound suspiciously like a swallowed laugh.

Sun Weiping stared at Lan.

This was the same person who had performed high-speed rolls under fire.

This was also a child afraid of being scolded.

"Kid," he said helplessly, "it is not that uncle does not want to help you. This is a major incident. Heaven Dou Planet authorities will investigate everything. Your contribution cannot be hidden. It will be important for your future."

Lan's shoulders sank.

He had survived space pirates.

He might not survive Nan Cheng.

The spacecraft resumed its route after emergency checks. The captain announced that the pirates had been destroyed, that passengers were safe, that food would be distributed soon, and that everyone should remain calm.

Calm was impossible.

Alive was enough.

In first-class, Le Qingling stood like a statue in front of Tang Le.

Then she reached up and touched his face.

Warm.

Real.

Human.

For a woman who had just watched him walk outside a spacecraft and destroy pirates like a myth with blue hair, this seemed important to confirm.

Tang Le leaned back slightly. "What?"

"What exactly is going on with you?" she asked.

Tang Le looked toward the window where the little warship was being recovered.

"If I said I do not know, would you believe me?"

Le Qingling did not answer immediately.

Tang Le's voice was quiet. "When you saved me from the sea, I had already lost my memories. The golden dragon may be a past companion, or perhaps the kind of Spirit Soul you told me about. It has fallen into deep sleep. I cannot awaken it on purpose. I only know that sometimes, when something forces me, abilities appear even I do not understand."

"Did everyone see?"

"No," Tang Le said. "I shrouded their senses when I acted. Only you and that child saw enough."

Le Qingling exhaled, but the relief did not erase fear.

"Then remember this. You are Young Noble Le. The singer. The man who only knows how to sing. Not some monster for people to drag away and study. Do not reveal this unless you have no choice."

Tang Le gave a faint smile. "Monster?"

"A beautiful monster," she said, then immediately looked serious again. "I am not joking."

"I know."

He looked tired now, in a way he had not before the battle.

"It was not as easy as it looked. I used what power I could and detonated the Soul Guidance cores. My past self may have known how to do more. I do not. Most of it was instinct."

Le Qingling studied him.

The man before her had once seemed lonely because he remembered nothing.

Now he seemed lonely because something inside him remembered too much without telling him what it meant.

She raised her hand toward his face again.

Tang Le stepped back. "That is enough. Do not take advantage of me."

Le Qingling laughed despite the tears in her eyes.

A few minutes later, Captain Sun Weiping personally brought Lan Xuanyu back to economy class.

Lan Xiao and Nan Cheng had already been through fear once when the pirates attacked. Seeing the captain arrive with their son produced a different kind of fear: the kind that meant the child had done something no parent had approved.

Sun Weiping explained.

Lan Xiao listened until his expression forgot which emotion to choose.

Nan Cheng chose first.

She pulled Lan over and smacked his butt several times.

Lan accepted it honestly.

Then she hugged him so tightly he could barely breathe.

"Do you know how dangerous that was?" she cried.

Lan had many possible answers.

None would help.

So he said the only thing that was true enough to be forgiven later.

"Daddy, Mommy, I just wanted to protect you."

Nan Cheng cried harder.

Lan Xiao turned away for a moment, then turned back with eyes that had changed. His son had left his seat as a child and returned with a shadow of adulthood no parent asked for early.

The passengers learned enough to call him the little hero of economy class.

Sun Weiping offered to upgrade the Lan family to a better cabin. Lan Xiao declined politely. After everything, the three of them preferred to remain together in the place they had chosen.

For the rest of the trip, Lan Xuanyu often sat in a daze.

The stars outside the window were still stars.

They no longer meant the same thing.

"Mommy," he asked once, "can human strength fight a spaceship?"

Nan Cheng looked at him for a long moment.

"Mommy does not know. In legends, very powerful Battle Armor Masters may be able to deal with miniature spaceships. If you cultivate to that level in the future, you will naturally understand."

Lan nodded.

But what he had seen was not a simple answer.

He had believed warships and fleets were the strongest power in the universe. That belief had not died.

It had cracked.

Behind the crack stood a blue-haired man, a golden dragon, and a cannon beam walking backward.

When the unlucky spacecraft finally reached Heaven Dou Planet, police cars and emergency crews already surrounded the landing area.

The passengers were questioned one by one. Reports had been sent ahead. A civilian spacecraft attacked by pirates, a child pilot destroying two mechas, mysterious golden-dragon phenomena, missing pirate signatures, and a destroyed meteor-class attack spaceship were not things authorities could ignore.

Tang Le and Le Qingling were questioned first due to status and pressure from the company behind the superstar.

Lan's family waited near the second questioning line.

Lan was still trying to look like someone who had not caused any paperwork when Tang Le walked out, masked and hooded among bodyguards.

He stopped.

The bodyguards halted immediately.

Tang Le walked toward Lan and lowered his mask.

Lan's eyes brightened.

"Uncle!"

Lan Xiao and Nan Cheng stared.

Only now did they realize that Young Noble Le had been on the same spacecraft.

Tang Le placed a hand lightly on Lan's head.

"We meet again. Your name is Lan Xuanyu, right?"

Lan nodded quickly. "Yes, uncle."

"I heard what you did," Tang Le said gently. "Amazing, little hero. Uncle has to go now. If fate allows, we will meet again."

He lifted his hand.

A softer voice entered Lan's ear, separate from the public words.

"Remember our promise."

Lan had a thousand questions.

Why are you here?

Was that dragon yours?

How did you stop time?

Are you a Soul Master?

Are humans really able to become like that?

Tang Le did not stay long enough for any of them.

He walked away with Le Qingling and the bodyguards.

Le Qingling glanced back once. "Why does that child look familiar?"

Tang Le said, "At a concert years ago, I carried a child while singing Protect Time, Protect You. That was him."

"No wonder." Le Qingling's eyes narrowed with curiosity. "He looks a little like you. If his hair and eyes were different, I might think you were related."

Tang Le stopped for a fraction of a step.

"Do we really look alike?"

"A little. Different aura, but yes. He is very good-looking too. Maybe our talent scouts should ask him."

Tang Le shook his head. "He knows how to pilot a space warship at his age. He will not be interested in becoming a singer."

Le Qingling sighed. "True. Destroying two mechas before twelve is slightly better than signing autographs."

They moved toward the exit.

Among the people waiting outside the space center, two figures drew attention even through masks.

One older.

One younger.

The older woman had long silver hair that nearly touched the ground and purple eyes so calm they made the surrounding noise seem rude. She stood quietly, as if waiting were something the world did around her rather than something she suffered.

Beside her, the younger girl had long dark-blue hair down to her waist and bright dark-blue eyes. Her lashes moved when she blinked, making each impatient glance look sharper than words.

"Teacher Nana," Dong Qianqiu said, looking up again, "why are they not out yet? We have been waiting so long."

Nana's gaze remained on the exit. "Their spacecraft encountered space pirates. There will be questioning. They should come out soon."

At that moment, the bodyguards escorted Young Noble Le and Le Qingling from another side.

Nana turned her head.

Tang Le, walking in the center of the group, turned too.

Neither knew why.

Between them were bodyguards, masks, movement, voices, and ten meters of ordinary distance pretending to be enough.

Tang Le's line of sight was blocked by Le Qingling shifting half a step. Nana's view was broken by the bodyguards passing in formation.

The two groups moved.

Closer.

Past.

Away.

They did not see each other.

Something in the air tightened and then let go without explanation.

Nana's purple eyes lingered for one breath after Tang Le's group had passed.

Dong Qianqiu tugged her sleeve. "Teacher Nana?"

"Nothing," Nana said softly.

Tang Le looked back once after the exit swallowed part of the crowd.

Le Qingling followed his gaze. "What is it?"

"Nothing," he said.

Neither answer was true.

Neither person knew why.

Twenty minutes later, Lan Xuanyu finally walked out with Lan Xiao and Nan Cheng.

Nana saw him.

Lan saw her.

For the first time since the alarm had screamed in space, his face looked entirely like a child again.

On Heaven Luo Planet, far away from the space center and its crossed paths, Yan Shuo did not spend Lan's journey standing still.

That was the mistake adults made when they read only battle reports.

If a child was not on the ship, some report-writers forgot the child still had a route.

Heaven Luo Academy did not allow that kind of laziness.

Vacation had begun, but the professional halls beneath the western practice field remained lit. Most students had gone home with assignments packed into terminals and threats from Teacher Ji packed into memory. A few special-use rooms stayed sealed for those whose routes could not be left to ordinary homework.

Yan Shuo's name sat on one of the protected screens.

Secondary profession: Duo Mecha Master / Battle-Armor Interface Foundation.

Status: Fire Phoenix adaptation route.

Authorization: training frame only. No battle armor completion. No combat deployment.

Below that, in Ji Hongbin's handwriting, someone had added:

Do not let the word foundation become an excuse for stupidity.

Yan stared at the sentence for a moment.

"Teacher Ji insults even screens," Luo said.

Song Yichen checked the floor marks instead of laughing. "He is efficient."

"He is haunting us through administration."

"Stand on your line."

Luo sighed dramatically and stood on his line.

The room smelled of cooling metal, heat-dulling oil, and new padding that had already learned to fear them. In Chapter20, Yan had worn a half-frame rig that punished sloppy posture by dragging limbs a finger-width out of place. After the Fire Phoenix awakening, the academy had rebuilt the frame twice.

The first rebuild had failed because it treated wings as accessories.

Yan still remembered the instructor's face when red-gold wing pressure bent the shoulder rail outward and made three warning lights die at once.

The second rebuild had failed because it treated Ultimate Fire as ordinary heat.

The third did not pretend to solve him.

It admitted him as a problem.

The training frame wrapped shoulders, spine, waist, knees, wrists, and wing-root space without clamping the wings themselves. Fine guide rings floated a palm-width from his back, measuring pressure instead of forcing it. Heat channels ran along both sides but stopped before the living wing line. A dull grey alloy plate sat over the chest interface, not to defend, but to ask whether Yan could pass force through metal without making the metal lie.

Battle armor would one day need far more than this.

A true armor route for a Fire Phoenix body could not simply bolt shining plates onto a normal child and call the result heroic. It would need to leave room for living wings, vent Ultimate Fire without wasting it, protect the hidden identity line without deforming posture, and obey a soul-spirit foundation that had become part of Yan's body rather than equipment hanging outside it.

That was why there was no armor yet.

Not because the profession did not matter.

Because it mattered too much to fake.

Yan lifted both arms.

Copper-red warmth moved under the uniform. Pale-gold motes gathered along the shoulder line. Red-gold wing pressure rose and was caught by the floating guide rings as numbers, not shackles.

The frame chimed once.

Acceptable.

"Route one," Song called.

Song's own growth had never looked as brilliant on a screen as Yan's fire, which was exactly why Yan refused to ignore it. Stoneplate Tortoise was not a martial soul that asked for applause. It asked for correct weight, correct angle, correct survival. Over the past year, Song had learned shield-load diagrams, recoil maps, ground-return points, and enough close-body mecha foundation to know when a teammate's landing would break an ankle even if the teammate insisted it felt fine.

He was Rank29 now, SP188, still slower than Luo, less dazzling than Yan, and more necessary than most spectators understood.

His shield mark on the floor was not decoration.

It was where flight came home.

Yan launched.

Not high.

The room ceiling was too low for pride.

He used a short Wingbeat Burst, crossed the first line, folded the right wing before the guide ring shouted, and dropped into Song's mark. Skybone Phoenix Frame carried the impact down through knees, waist, and spine. Dawnfeather Phoenix Mantle wanted to flare wider to soften the landing.

Yan kept it narrow.

The frame chimed twice.

Acceptable.

"Too clean," Luo said.

Yan looked at him.

"That is a complaint?"

"That is suspicion. Nobody becomes that reasonable without hiding a disaster."

"Route two," Song said before Yan could answer.

Luo's growth looked different again. Threadwind Mantis did not want to be a shield or a flame. It wanted to cut timing. The academy had pushed him into sensor-lane drills, aerial pursuit calls, interruption windows, and agility/mecha-control basics until he stopped thinking of speed as merely arriving first.

He was Rank28 now, SP226, and he had learned to read the tiny moment before Yan's right wing lied.

"East lane false," Luo called. "Left return. Small. If you make it beautiful, I will complain to maintenance."

Yan's mouth twitched.

He launched again.

Left return. Small.

The Purple Flame Eidolon Wing did not manifest. It did not need to. The right side of Yan's body still remembered the third soul spirit as a shadow-glint beneath red-gold, and even that memory could make a movement too sharp if he let pride pull on it.

He did not.

He crossed the false lane, let Luo's interruption marker pass before his shoulder, folded, dropped, and touched down inside Song's mark with half a step to spare.

The frame chimed green.

Luo stared.

"Annoying."

"Good," Song corrected.

"I said what I meant."

The professional-course instructor behind the glass wrote something on a tablet. Mu Zhongtian stood beside him, arms folded. The teacher did not smile, but his eyes paused on each line of data: heat leakage, wing-root recoil, ally-safe return, frame compatibility, emergency halt.

Those lines would not make a crowd gasp.

They were not a new ring. They were not a new soul skill. They were not a finished battle armor set.

They were growth.

The kind that kept a future genius from burning through his own equipment, blinding his teammate, or discovering too late that a spectacular flight path had no safe place to land.

On another academy board, Lan Xuanyu's profession line was already marked off-world leave: Interplanet Command System, warship-piloting focus, special supervision by Yin Tianfan.

Lan's Soul Power remained Rank19 peak, but nobody who had watched him in simulators believed that single number described him. Qian Lei's file carried Rank25 and SP600+ with a summoning route that still made instructors argue over whether luck should be billed as a material cost. Liu Feng's Rank26 line was full of speed-control notes and close-body-mecha agility corrections. Lu Qianxun's three-ring file still mattered. Ye Lingtong and Chang Jianyi still mattered.

The Elite Junior Class was not a single shining name surrounded by shadows.

It was twenty-one remaining students being pulled into different professions, different costs, different futures.

Yan knew only his own room tonight.

That was enough.

A message arrived from Yan Ke while the instructor reset the frame.

Yan opened it.

It was a photo of a materials quotation with three red circles, two question marks, and one line from his father:

Do battle-armor foundations require selling a kidney, or is that only the advanced class?

Yan stared at it.

Then Lin Mei's message arrived beneath it.

Do not let your father bargain with anyone about your wing-root interface. Cheap metal near living fire is expensive later.

Yan wrote back:

No armor yet. Only frame compatibility.

Yan Ke answered immediately.

That is what the quotation said before it attacked me.

Luo leaned over. "Family support?"

"Family accounting," Yan said.

Song, who had grown up practical enough to respect numbers, nodded solemnly. "Important profession."

Yan almost laughed.

The frame chimed ready again.

His terminal remained open on the bench beside the family messages.

No new message from Lan.

That was normal. Lan was traveling through space. Messages could lag, fail, or wait for docking. Normal did not mean comfortable.

Yan looked at the blank line longer than he meant to.

He did not know about the golden dragon outside the window.

He did not know about the cannon beam stopping.

He did not know that the profession line beside Lan's name — Interplanet Command, warship pilot — had just become the difference between helplessness and action.

And Lan did not know that on Heaven Luo Planet, while he was learning what kind of person could walk a cannon beam backward, Yan Shuo was learning what kind of metal frame could survive being asked to carry a Fire Phoenix without lying.

That was how their paths had always grown.

Parallel.

Firewalled.

Not lesser because they were separate.

Song lifted his shield into the ground-return mark.

Luo raised two fingers. "West lane. No drama."

Yan wiped sweat from his jaw and tightened the restraint clasp holding copper-red hair in place. Soul power stirred, and the red-gold inside the strands brightened for one breath before the clasp dulled it back into public restraint.

"Again," he said.

Red-gold wings opened under the Heaven Luo training lights.

Lan Xuanyu ran toward Nana under Heaven Dou's space-center sky.

Between them lay stars, silence, professions still growing, teammates still working, and promises not yet old enough to explain themselves.
