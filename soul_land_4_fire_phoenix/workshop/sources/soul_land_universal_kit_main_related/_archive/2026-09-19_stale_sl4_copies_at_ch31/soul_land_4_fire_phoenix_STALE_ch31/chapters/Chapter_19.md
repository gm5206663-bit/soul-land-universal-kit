# Chapter 19: The Question No Teacher Should Hear

Qian Lei's face slowly discovered all the reasons a mouth should remain shut.

Unfortunately, his mouth had already worked faster than his fear.

Teacher Ji stood at the front of the classroom and looked at him.

No martial soul appeared. No soul power pressed down on the rows of desks. No visible threat moved through the air. The tall, lean man's sideburns were only touched with gray, his clothes were neat, and his expression remained so calm that it made panic feel childish.

That was worse.

Qian Lei stood there with sweat on his forehead, trapped by the silence of the entire Elite Junior Class.

"This..." he began.

Ji Hongbin did not speak.

Qian Lei swallowed. "I said this is Teacher Ji, and he is a demon."

The class did not gasp aloud.

That was the first proof of Teacher Ji's reputation.

Dozens of children heard a classmate call a teacher a demon to his face, and not one of them dared to waste breath reacting.

Yan Shuo sat beside Luo Haoran and felt Luo's elbow tighten against the desk. On his other side, Song Yichen's shoulders became square enough to be measured by a ruler.

Across the room, Jin Xiang's face had lost the last smug trace from his argument with Qian Lei.

Lan Xuanyu looked curious.

That, Yan Shuo thought, was a dangerous kind of courage. Or a dangerous kind of not knowing enough yet.

Teacher Ji finally spoke.

"Sit down."

Qian Lei blinked.

He sat so quickly that the chair almost protested.

For one precious breath, relief crossed his face. He had survived. The heavens had taken pity on him. The demon had shown mercy.

Then Ji Hongbin looked over the whole class.

"Qian Lei is correct," he said evenly. "To all of you, I am a demon."

The relief died in Qian Lei's eyes.

The shadow in the room grew a spine.

Ji Hongbin continued, "This afternoon's classes are mine. The first half will be teaching. The second half will be assessment. After you pass, you may go to lunch. If you do not pass, you will retake the assessment until you do."

A groan rose at once.

It was not loud.

Even despair remembered manners in Teacher Ji's room.

Yan Shuo's stomach, which had been perfectly loyal until that moment, gave a small and traitorous warning. Luo Haoran heard it and turned his head by half an inch.

"If you make hungry noises during a demon's class," Luo whispered, "do I have to pretend not to know you?"

"You were louder," Yan Shuo whispered back.

"Mine was tactical fear."

Song Yichen did not look away from the front. "Both of you are still alive. Try to keep it that way."

Teacher Ji's eyes moved across the room.

All three became model students.

Lan Xuanyu leaned slightly toward Qian Lei and asked in a low voice, "What does Teacher Ji teach?"

Qian Lei's lips twitched as if the answer itself hurt. "Basic knowledge of spirit beasts. We all thought it would be useless at first. We only meet spirit beasts in simulation pods anyway, right? Then we learned how strict he is. He covers too much, remembers everything, tests after every lesson, and the punishments..."

He stopped and looked toward the front.

Ji Hongbin had not turned around.

Qian Lei still lowered his voice further. "They are terrifying. He is also the vice president. People say he is the strongest person in Heaven Luo Academy."

"Basic knowledge of spirit beasts?" Lan Xuanyu repeated softly.

His tense shoulders relaxed by a measure no one else would have noticed.

For other students, spirit-beast knowledge was a book, a lesson, a punishment, a list of dead names and ancient ranks. For Lan Xuanyu, it had been the language of home long before he knew it was a class. Lan Xiao and Nan Cheng worked with ancient spirit-beast research. Dinner talk, bedtime explanations, workroom smells, museum records, harmless arguments over classification, old pictures, repaired data files, and small toys with carefully explained names had built a path in his memory before he knew he was walking it.

He did not know everything.

But compared with classmates who had learned spirit beasts as exam material, he had grown up breathing the dust of their histories.

Teacher Ji turned to the board.

"Today," he said, "we discuss spirit-beast ranks and the characteristics of top spirit beasts."

The lesson began like a blade being drawn slowly enough for everyone to see its edge.

"Spirit beasts have traditionally been classified by years of cultivation. Ten years. One hundred years. One thousand years. Ten thousand years. One hundred thousand years. Their corresponding ring colors are white, yellow, purple, black, and red."

White.

Yellow.

Purple.

Black.

Red.

Simple colors.

No one in the room treated them as simple.

A white ring could still mean the beginning of a child's path. A yellow ring could mean the first true step past childhood awakening. Purple, black, red--each color was a height most of them could only imagine from the bottom.

Yan Shuo copied the list without looking down for longer than a blink.

White, yellow, purple, black, red.

His pen paused after yellow.

Dawnflame Kite had crossed that boundary by one exact year when it fused with him. That was what the paperwork knew. That was what the old record said. A clean legal acquisition, a yellow first ring, a proper beginning.

But a living soul spirit did not stop being alive because a form had been filed.

Numbers mattered. Teacher Ji's voice made that obvious. Colors mattered. Records mattered. History mattered.

And yet, somewhere beneath Yan Shuo's skin, Dawnflame Kite pulsed with warmth and a small line of pale brightness, as if reminding him that a record was a doorplate, not the whole room.

He did not write that thought down.

He wrote what Teacher Ji said.

Ji Hongbin moved on.

"There are exceptions. The most common special color in recorded history is orange-gold. Spirit beasts above one hundred thousand years, especially those that reach two hundred thousand years and above, are called Ferocious Beasts. Their rings are not only powerful. They carry the marks of calamities."

His chalk tapped once.

"Technology can imitate many things. The Spirit Pagoda can simulate even extremely high-level rings to a degree. But calamity is not a color painted onto energy. An orange-gold ring is not merely a brighter red ring. It represents survival through pressure that simulation cannot truly reproduce."

Several students wrote so fast their pens scratched.

Yan Shuo underlined the last sentence.

Pressure that could not be copied by color alone.

His shoulders remembered Soul World teeth. His back remembered the recoil of a wing-frame that was not yet a wing. His palms remembered how a bright edge could hold flame in place without making it bigger.

Teacher Ji spoke of red rings giving two soul skills. He spoke of orange-gold rings that might contain more. He spoke of soul spirits in the modern era and the way abilities could distribute through different ranks and channels.

Then the lesson climbed further, out of the reach of ordinary childhood.

Million-year rings entered the room as legend rather than fact.

Some records said gold. Some said white. Some said white-gold. Some argued that different million-year rings might show different colors altogether. No conclusion had ended the debate.

Sea God Tang San and the Deep Sea Demonic Whale became a name and a shadow on the board.

Spirit Ice Douluo Huo Yuhao and the Daydream Ice Worm followed.

Then Dragon Emperor Douluo Tang Wulin appeared in Teacher Ji's voice as a figure closer to the present age, leader of a new era, envoy of peace between humans and spirit beasts, the legend who changed the Mother Planet.

"Green-gold," Ji Hongbin said. "One recorded ring of that color. Related to life force, according to later recollections. The details remain Federal secret. Remember this: special colors have special meaning. If you do not understand the meaning, do not pretend the color is decoration."

Yan Shuo's pen stopped again.

Not decoration.

Luo Haoran leaned close enough to whisper without moving his head. "If he asks me about green-gold, I am dead. Tell my family I was handsome."

Yan Shuo whispered, "Your family knows what you look like."

"Cruel."

"Alive," Song Yichen murmured.

Teacher Ji's chalk clicked.

Luo sat straight.

After ring colors came names that sounded too large for a classroom.

Beast God Di Tian.

The last Golden Eye Black Dragon.

Ferocious Beasts that had walked through eras like disasters with souls. Some were dead. Some were uncertain. Some had become soul spirits. Some had changed history by choosing a human, fighting a human, losing to a human, or surviving humanity longer than anyone expected.

Teacher Ji spoke quickly.

No story was allowed to remain only a story. Every legend had a rank. Every rank had a characteristic. Every characteristic implied a danger. Every danger could become a test question.

Half an hour passed as if someone had tied time to a speeding soul guide car.

"Ten-minute break," Ji Hongbin said at last. "Assessment begins after the break."

Then he walked out.

The class exhaled as one body.

No one stood up.

That was the second proof of Teacher Ji's reputation.

Usually, a break released children like birds from a cage. This time, even Jin Xiang stayed seated. His face was thoughtful in the way a person became thoughtful after remembering pain.

Lan Xuanyu turned to Qian Lei. "Will the test be very difficult?"

Qian Lei smiled bitterly. "The test is bad. The punishment is worse. Do you know why Jin Xiang fears Teacher Ji? He once failed and said this class was useless. Teacher Ji grabbed him, opened the window, and threw him out."

Lan Xuanyu glanced toward the windows. "From this floor? For a Soul Master, that should not be too dangerous."

Qian Lei's mouth twitched. "Not out. Up. A few hundred meters. Then he caught him and threw him again. More than ten times."

Lan Xuanyu stared.

Across the room, Yan Shuo heard only broken pieces because Qian Lei kept his voice low, but Luo Haoran had clearly heard enough through the rumor network before today.

"Weightlessness training," Luo whispered with a face that said the words were cursed. "Teacher Ji said everyone has to learn flight eventually. Mecha, battle armor, own ability. Tossing Jin Xiang was educational."

Yan Shuo looked toward the window.

His back tightened.

Wing-frame was not flight.

He knew that now more clearly than when he had first felt fire draw feather shapes across his shoulders. Dawnfeather Mantle could lighten a landing, correct an angle, brake a pounce, and make a body remember where the air pushed back. It could not give him the sky. Not yet. Not safely.

A mecha could lift a person before their martial soul could.

Battle armor could teach a body where force belonged.

Teacher Ji's punishment was monstrous.

Teacher Ji's logic was worse, because Yan Shuo could not completely call it wrong.

The classroom door opened again.

Every spine straightened.

Ji Hongbin returned to the platform and swept the room with one calm look.

"Assessment begins," he said. "Who is Lan Xuanyu? You are new. You will start."

Lan Xuanyu stood.

"Hello, Teacher Ji."

"Explain the characteristics of the Ferocious Beast Snow Woman that I just covered."

A thin tremor moved through the room.

Not because Snow Woman was the hardest subject in the lesson.

Because it had been only one part of a rushing flood, and Teacher Ji expected a child to pluck it back out intact.

Lan Xuanyu opened his mouth.

The answer came smoothly.

The Snow Woman, ranked among the top Ferocious Beasts. The Extreme North of the Mother Planet. The Ice Emperor title. The relationship with Spirit Ice Douluo Huo Yuhao. Cultivation, ice control, spirit-soul transformation, and the Ice Jade Emperor Scorpion standing beside that legend in old records.

He did not rush.

He did not stumble.

He did not merely repeat one memorized line and hope mercy would fill the rest.

He spoke for nearly five minutes.

The classroom listened first in fear, then surprise, then something close to envy.

Yan Shuo listened too.

He knew the sound of a person answering from a lesson.

This was different.

Lan Xuanyu was answering from rooms Yan Shuo had never entered: from his parents' work, from quiet family explanations, from shelves and diagrams and childhood questions that had been welcomed rather than silenced. He had always known Lan Xiao and Nan Cheng worked with ancient spirit-beast research. He had seen the way Uncle Lan could make an old bone fragment sound patient and alive. He had laughed at Lan Xuanyu for knowing strange names that no one had asked him to know.

Now those names became a key in Teacher Ji's room.

When Lan Xuanyu finished, Ji Hongbin nodded.

"Not bad. You listened well. Or do you already have extensive interest in spirit beasts?"

"I have been interested since I was young," Lan Xuanyu said.

Ji Hongbin's expression did not soften, but approval entered the space around him.

"You pass. You may leave class early."

For a moment, Lan Xuanyu looked as if passing had frightened him more than failing.

"Ah?"

"Go."

Lan Xuanyu gathered his things under the envious eyes of the class.

Qian Lei's gaze followed him with betrayal strong enough to become a third martial soul.

Liu Feng's expression stayed quiet, but even he looked tempted to sigh.

Yan Shuo caught Lan Xuanyu's eye for one brief instant as he passed the row.

He did not ask anything.

Lan Xuanyu gave him a small, almost apologetic smile and left.

The door closed.

Teacher Ji looked at the rest of them.

The room remembered hunger.

"Next."


Lan Xuanyu discovered that being released early felt wonderful for exactly as long as it took to reach the corridor.

After that, freedom became a question.

Lunch had not begun for him yet. Returning to the dorm would only leave him staring at walls and thinking too much about Gate of Summoning, Teacher Nana, Dong Qianqiu, and why so many impossible things had begun to gather around Dorm 333 after he arrived.

The training ground was open.

Soul World waited.

Qian Lei had given him five soul coins.

The thought came naturally: what if he entered the Lava Hound test alone?

With Qian Lei and Liu Feng, he had spent most of the scenario keeping the team from splitting apart. Qian Lei's summoning was unpredictable. Liu Feng's speed outran support. Lan Xuanyu had learned to coordinate, stabilize, and drag three people across a finish line that kept moving.

Alone, there would be no one to protect.

Alone, there would be no one to fail because of him.

Alone, he could test what his body, his footwork, his water, his ice, and his strange right hand could really do.

He entered the simulation pod.

Light swallowed the academy.

The Soul World training ground took shape around him with familiar clarity. He paid the soul coin and selected the junior Lava Hound scenario.

The colosseum appeared.

Four gates waited in the arena walls.

Lan Xuanyu did not stand in the center.

He ran.

The lesson had not left his mind. Spirit beasts were not targets wearing names. They had habits, bodies, instincts, strengths, weak points. Even a simulated Lava Hound carried the shape of a real creature's logic.

Dog-type and wolf-type beasts had hard heads, strong jaws, and powerful shoulders.

Their waists were not the same.

The nearest gate opened.

Heat rolled out. Ten-year Lava Hounds rushed forward in a snarling flood.

Lan Xuanyu dropped low and pressed his left hand to the ground.

Cold spread.

Ice spikes stabbed upward beneath the first line of hounds. The front bodies broke apart into Soul World light before their claws properly touched the arena floor. The second row crashed over them, instincts too simple to stop the press from behind.

Lan Xuanyu's right hand curled.

Gold-patterned Blue Silver Grass flashed around his fingers. Scales appeared along the skin with a faint gleam. He jumped, drove his right hand into the stone wall, and hung there as if the wall had become soft clay beneath his grip.

From above, his left hand kept firing ice awls.

One.

Another.

Another.

The first gate became chaos.

More than ten Lava Hounds fell before the other three gates emptied their packs into the arena.

Lan Xuanyu did not feel proud.

Pride used breath.

He needed breath.

The three hundred or so ten-year Lava Hounds flooded inward from different directions, and behind them the heavier heat of hundred-year Lava Hounds began to emerge. The arena filled with red bodies, bad breath, and the scrape of claws.

If he had enough soul power to continue firing ice awls, he might have turned the test into arithmetic.

He did not.

He was still only rank fourteen.

His soul power was precious, and the hounds were many.

He dropped from the wall onto the back of the last hound below him. His right fist struck down. Bone gave way at the waist. The hound collapsed. Lan Xuanyu pushed off its body and rushed along the outer wall before the pack could close.

A hundred-year Lava Hound appeared from one gate.

Then another.

Then the third.

They were larger, hotter, and not as stupid as the ten-year beasts.

But Lan Xuanyu had seen their first move before.

Fireballs gathered in three throats.

Lan Xuanyu turned toward the pack behind him and ran directly into the place any frightened child would have avoided.

The fireballs came.

He slipped between two ten-year hounds at the last breath.

The first fireball struck the packed bodies behind him and burst. Heat and noise filled the arena. Hounds howled. The second fireball landed where a cluster had tried to turn. The third exploded against the ground, scattering claws, light, and virtual dust.

Lan Xuanyu did not look back.

He could not afford to enjoy success.

He cut through the wounded edge of the pack and aimed for the nearest hundred-year Lava Hound.

Water gathered in his left palm.

A light-blue vortex spun into the air.

The Lava Hound pounced through it. Steam erupted around its head and shoulders as the water struck the heat of its body. The vortex was not strong enough to stop its charge. That had never been the point.

The steam stole its sight.

Lan Xuanyu borrowed the force of the release to move sideways. The Lava Hound's jaws snapped through empty air. His right fist drove into its waist at the exact moment its body stretched past him.

The impact traveled up Lan Xuanyu's arm.

The hound howled.

Its waist buckled.

Lan Xuanyu's feet hit the ground, turned, and chased before the beast could recover. An ice awl formed from his left hand and punched into the damaged area. He detonated it without hesitation.

The hundred-year Lava Hound rolled, organs torn by ice and force inside the simulation's merciless imitation of life.

It broke into light.

One down.

Two remained.

Lan Xuanyu's chest heaved.

His right hand felt strong, stronger than he sometimes believed it should. The rare food he had eaten, Teacher Nana's training, the strange golden strength under his skin, and the pressure of previous battles all lived in that arm now.

But strength was not the same as endless soul power.

The other hundred-year Lava Hounds were closing.

If they grouped together, he would lose.

He ran at the second one.

A fireball shot past him close enough for heat to slap his skin. He rolled to the side and came up low, left hand brushing the ground, feet pushing hard.

Again, he formed the water vortex.

Again, steam began to rise.

But this Lava Hound stopped.

It did not crash blindly through the same trick. Its claw struck the vortex, scattering water into hissing mist without fully blinding itself.

Lan Xuanyu's heart tightened.

Even a low-intelligence simulation could adjust if the pattern was too obvious.

Good.

Then he would change the pattern too.

He ran as if aiming for the waist.

The hound turned to protect it. Its head snapped down. One hind leg kicked, trying to keep him from slipping beneath its body.

Lan Xuanyu dropped suddenly.

The bite passed over his head. The kick swept where his ribs had been. His legs compressed under him, then reversed his movement with all the strength he could gather. Instead of sliding toward the waist, he sprang back toward the lower jaw.

Ice formed in his left hand.

He passed it to his right.

Gold-patterned Blue Silver Grass brightened around his fingers. The scaled right hand clenched the ice awl until it looked like part of his own bone.

He thrust upward.

The awl pierced the soft skin beneath the Lava Hound's jaw and drove toward the brain.

Speed.

Accuracy.

Aggression.

For one sharp instant, everything Teacher Ji had said about knowing a spirit beast's body became action instead of words.

The second hundred-year Lava Hound convulsed and fell.

Lan Xuanyu rolled free under its collapsing weight.

Then his heart went cold.

The remaining hounds were almost on him.

Dozens of ten-year Lava Hounds closed the space, and the last hundred-year Lava Hound's throat burned with a fireball already formed. Lan Xuanyu's soul power was nearly empty. His stamina had fallen until even breathing felt like something he had to order his body to do.

He could not use Golden Dragon Soar.

He did not even try to summon the memory of it. His body remembered the cost too well.

At least, he thought, I killed two of the hundred-year ones.

The fireball came.

Lan Xuanyu raised both arms by instinct.

Right in front.

Left behind.

Gold-patterned Blue Silver Grass curled around his right hand.

Silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass curled around his left.

His hands touched.

Time slowed.

The fireball seemed to hang before him in a sphere of heat and death. He saw his right hand recoil. He saw it press against his left. He felt two familiar powers that had always lived apart suddenly forced into the same breath.

Gold met silver.

A rainbow glow bloomed between his hands.

Lan Xuanyu's single white soul ring trembled.

For a fragment of time, it seemed to split into two shadows, then fold together again, no longer white but carrying a color he could not name as one thing. Rainbow light flared around the ring.

Inside his chest, the sealed gold-and-silver vortex turned violently.

The two powers that had always pushed against each other became one impossible current.

Lan Xuanyu's mind went blank.

A sphere of rainbow light erupted outward from him.

The fireball vanished into it.

The nearest ten-year Lava Hounds disappeared before they could howl. Thirty or more bodies broke into motes of Soul World light as if erased. The last hundred-year Lava Hound, farther away, stiffened under an invisible pressure. Its legs gave out. It collapsed, then turned into light as well.

For less than a second, the rainbow sphere filled the arena.

Then it faded.

A notification sounded.

Lan Xuanyu had passed.

He stood alone in the dissolving colosseum with both hands still raised.

He did not understand.

The Soul World returned him to the training ground.

He looked at the extra soul coin reward and knew the system had recorded victory.

Victory did not feel like victory.

What was that rainbow light?

Why had his ring changed?

Why did the vortex in his chest feel weaker?

He checked himself before leaving the pod, then again after the pod opened. The heat and pain were gone. The wounds were not real. The exhaustion should have faded with the scenario's end.

But something in his body had not returned.

Lan Xuanyu walked back to Dorm 333 faster than he meant to.

Qian Lei and Liu Feng had not returned from Teacher Ji's test yet.

The room was quiet.

Too quiet.

He sat on his bed and tried to meditate.

His soul power circulated.

Then his eyes opened.

No.

He tried again.

The result stayed the same.

His soul power had recovered to its current fullness, but the fullness itself had become smaller.

Cold spread through him more frightening than any Ice element he could summon.

Dorm 333 had a simple soul-power test apparatus, able to measure up to rank thirty. Lan Xuanyu took it out with hands that wanted not to tremble and fed soul power into it.

The number appeared.

Rank 13.

He stared.

That was wrong.

It had to be wrong.

He tested again.

Rank 13.

A third time.

Rank 13.

The bed under him felt suddenly far away.

He had finally cultivated to rank fourteen. His soul power progress was already slow compared with the others in Elite Junior Class. He was the weakest in soul power rank among them, no matter how strange his control became. Now one moment of rainbow light inside a simulation had made him fall back by an entire rank.

How could Soul World do that?

Hadn't they said only experience came back?

Why had his real cultivation changed?

The answer had to be in the contact between gold-patterned and silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass. It had to be the collision between his two hands. The rainbow burst, the changed ring, the vortex turning inside his chest--all of it belonged to the secret he already could not explain to ordinary teachers.

Could he ask Mu Zhongtian?

Could he ask Teacher Ji?

Lan Xuanyu imagined explaining that his two Blue Silver Grass variants touched, his white ring became rainbow, an explosion killed the Lava Hounds, and his rank dropped afterward.

Then he imagined the teachers' faces.

Suspicion.

Tests.

Questions.

Maybe removal from Elite Junior Class.

Maybe worse.

His breathing became shallow.

Teacher Nana.

The thought arrived like a hand on his shoulder.

Nana had helped him seal the vortex. Nana knew more about the gold and silver in his body than anyone else he could reach. Nana had spoken through Qian Lei's Gate. Dong Qianqiu could come through. There had to be a way to ask.

Not aloud.

Not where a teacher might hear.

Lan Xuanyu closed his eyes and sank his attention carefully inward.

The vortex still existed.

Gold and silver still circled.

But deep in the place where the two colors had always resisted each other, something had changed by a hair's breadth. The repulsion was not gone. It was still there, strong enough to make him afraid. But it had softened at one tiny point, as if two enemies had been forced to share a road for one blink and remembered the road afterward.

He did not know whether that was good.

He did not know whether losing a rank could ever be called good.

But he knew the question could not be asked in the wrong place.


Yan Shuo did not know a rainbow had bloomed in the Soul World while the rest of the class was still trapped under Teacher Ji's calm eyes.

He was busy trying not to fail.

After Lan Xuanyu left, Teacher Ji's assessment became exactly as cruel as the rumors promised and much quieter than Yan Shuo expected. He did not shout when someone answered badly. He did not need to. A wrong answer was corrected with such precise coldness that the student looked smaller by the time he sat down.

Two students failed and were told to review.

One boy forgot the difference between a hundred-thousand-year ring and a Ferocious Beast. Teacher Ji's pause afterward made the window seem very close.

Jin Xiang answered his first question with the caution of someone who had already seen the sky from the wrong direction.

Qian Lei tried to look invisible.

It did not work.

Liu Feng's answers were short but accurate when asked about speed-type spirit-beast movement habits. His voice was still hoarse from yesterday's exhaustion, but his focus held.

Song Yichen passed by building every answer like a wall: slow, plain, correct.

Luo Haoran nearly tripped over the million-year ring-color uncertainty, recovered by admitting there was no confirmed single color, and sat down afterward as if he had outrun death.

Then Teacher Ji looked at Yan Shuo.

"Yan Shuo."

He stood.

"Normal ring colors, in order. Then explain why an orange-gold ring cannot be treated as only a stronger red ring."

The question was not as narrow as Lan Xuanyu's Snow Woman question.

It was not as broad as the whole lesson either.

It was exactly the kind of question that punished children who memorized sounds without meaning.

Yan Shuo breathed in.

White-gold did not appear on his shoulders. He was in class, not a drill room. Still, the memory of Dawnfeather Mantle's bright edge settled under his skin, drawing a boundary around panic before it could flare.

"Ten-year spirit beasts correspond to white rings," he said. "One hundred years to yellow. One thousand to purple. Ten thousand to black. One hundred thousand to red."

Teacher Ji watched him.

"Orange-gold appears with Ferocious Beast level existences, especially above two hundred thousand years. It is connected to the spirit beast passing calamities, not only accumulating a larger number. A red ring can already carry more than ordinary ring value, and a hundred-thousand-year red ring gives two soul skills. Orange-gold is special because the existence behind it has been changed by surviving pressure that technology cannot simply simulate. So the color is not decoration and not just stronger red."

Teacher Ji's expression did not change.

"Especially?"

Yan Shuo's fingers tightened once.

"It means the usual example is two hundred thousand years and above, Teacher, but the principle is the Ferocious Beast level and calamity history rather than a child's shorthand."

"Do not use shorthand when precision is required."

"Yes, Teacher."

"Passed. Sit."

Yan Shuo sat.

His stomach immediately remembered it was empty.

Luo Haoran leaned over by the smallest possible distance. "Passed sounds nicer when he says it to someone else."

Song Yichen made a mark in his notes. "You corrected fast."

Yan Shuo looked at his own page.

His handwriting near the word calamity had pressed too hard into the paper.

"I almost made it smaller than it was," he whispered.

Song's eyes moved once toward him, then back to the front. "Then you heard yourself."

That was all.

No praise large enough to embarrass him. No command. Just a stone placed where a path bent.

Teacher Ji continued calling names.

Lunch moved farther away.

By the time Yan Shuo finally earned the right to leave the classroom, Lan Xuanyu had been gone long enough to become a legend among the hungry.

Qian Lei slumped over his desk and muttered, "He is eating. I can feel it. My soul is connected to lost food."

Liu Feng dragged him upright by the sleeve.

"Review first. Eat after passing."

"You have become cruel," Qian Lei said weakly. "Heroic rebirth has ruined you."

Yan Shuo passed their row on the way out and paused only long enough to grin.

"If your soul can sense food, use it on the assessment."

Qian Lei opened one eye. "Betrayal from outside Dorm 333. Noted."

Lan Xuanyu was not there to smile between them.

Yan Shuo noticed that.

He did not chase the reason.

Some questions belonged to the person carrying them.


Dorm 336's study room smelled of cold food, paper, and the faint heated dust of overworked lights.

Song Yichen had copied the ring-color ladder three times.

Luo Haoran had drawn a small stick figure being thrown upward beside the words weightlessness training until Song silently turned the page over.

Yan Shuo sat with his elbows on the table, staring at the phrase special colors have special meaning.

"If Teacher Ji tests us again tomorrow," Luo said, "I plan to develop a sudden illness."

"He will test you on the illness," Song said.

"Then I will choose an illness with no historical records."

Yan Shuo snorted before he could stop himself.

The room loosened by one breath.

Then his eyes returned to his notes.

Orange-gold was not a brighter red.

Green-gold was not decoration.

White-gold in million-year legends could not be guessed into certainty.

Colors mattered because history lived inside them.

His own pale-gold motes were not a separate legend. They were not a million-year ring, not a divine sign, not a special color that belonged in Teacher Ji's lecture. They were much smaller than that.

But smaller did not mean meaningless.

Dawnflame Kite had been fire and light before Yan Shuo ever touched it. The light had thinned the fire. The fire had scattered the light. A flawed bird had stopped below a boundary until his Fire Phoenix gave it a body that could hold both.

If he treated the pale-gold edge as decoration, he was making Teacher Ji's mistake for him.

No.

Teacher Ji would not make that mistake.

Yan Shuo was making it himself.

"Training room?" he asked.

Luo stared. "After Teacher Ji? Are you possessed by academic guilt?"

"Low-output only," Yan Shuo said. "Ten minutes. I want to test something from the lesson."

Song closed his notes. "Weak points or flight?"

Yan Shuo thought of the window, Jin Xiang's pale face, and the future shape of battle armor around shoulders that were still learning not to become wings too early.

"Boundaries," he said.

That answer was strange enough that Luo followed out of curiosity.

The small training room assigned to Dorm 336 did not contain real spirit beasts. It had target rods, pressure pads, rubber projectiles, adjustable light, and a practice dummy with interchangeable plates marking common attack zones. The academy did not trust children with dangerous freedom, but it did give them enough tools to expose careless habits.

Yan Shuo set the dummy to a four-legged beast frame.

Luo saw the shape and grinned. "If this starts breathing fire, I am leaving."

"It will not," Song said.

"You do not know that. Teacher Ji teaches here. The furniture may have punishments."

Yan Shuo stepped onto the grid.

Low-output only.

No true fusion.

No wing-frame burst.

No extra power just because his pride disliked feeling small beside Lan Xuanyu's effortless answer.

He breathed.

Dawnfeather Mantle answered close to the skin.

Copper-red flame formed first along his forearms and shoulders, no larger than necessary. Then pale-gold motes surfaced inside it, gathering in feather-rib lines. White-gold edges traced each faint feather shape across his upper back and down his arms. The room brightened warmly, not enough to blind, only enough to draw outlines from shadow.

Fire carried force.

Light drew the boundary.

Yan Shuo lifted one hand.

A narrow dawnfire line slid toward the dummy's marked waist plate. Yesterday, he might have scorched the entire side to make sure the hit counted. Today, the light-lined edge touched only the plate's border before the copper-red center pressed in.

The target registered contact.

Not heavy damage.

Accurate contact.

Yan Shuo withdrew before heat spilled past the mark.

Again.

This time to the lower jaw plate.

The line flickered wider when his thought jumped ahead of his hand. Pale-gold motes scattered, then returned as his shoulders corrected. He breathed out and let the white-gold edges settle before releasing the second touch.

The target registered a clean hit.

Luo's joking expression faded into interest.

"It is less dramatic," he said.

"Good," Yan Shuo answered.

"I did not say better."

"I did."

Song walked around the edge of the grid, watching the floor rather than Yan Shuo's face. "Less heat outside the lane. If Luo crosses near the flank, he has more room."

Luo lifted both hands. "I appreciate not being cooked in the name of friendship."

Yan Shuo held the form for one more pass.

The dummy shifted suddenly, a spring-loaded movement imitating a pounce. Its front rose; the marked waist angle changed. Yan Shuo's first instinct wanted to flare and catch the whole body.

His body remembered Teacher Ji's voice.

Meaning, not decoration.

His shoulders settled.

The pale-gold edge pulled the flame narrower before it left his hand. The dawnfire line curved, kissed the new angle at the waist plate, and broke away without washing over the dummy's head.

The recoil came back through his arm.

It did not kick wildly.

Feather-light channels spread it across his shoulder and upper back. Warmth sank into muscle instead of snapping at his wrist.

Yan Shuo lowered his hand.

No new number appeared.

The training room did not care that he had understood something.

It only recorded: cleaner boundary, lower waste, better friendly-lane safety.

That was enough.

Dawnflame Kite pulsed once inside him, copper warmth and pale brightness together.

Yan Shuo smiled before he could decide to.

"Ten minutes," Song said.

Yan Shuo released the form. "It was not ten yet."

"Close. Stop while the edge is clean."

There was no command in it, only the same practical steadiness Song gave to his shield line.

This time Yan Shuo listened because the advice fit, not because anyone owned his path.

The copper-red flame withdrew.

The white-gold feather edges lingered a breath longer, then folded inward.

Luo stretched. "So Teacher Ji taught Lan Xuanyu enough to escape, trapped the rest of us, stole lunch, and somehow made you more annoying. Truly a demon."

Yan Shuo laughed softly.

Then he looked toward the dorm corridor, where Dorm 333's door sat beyond turns and walls he had no reason to cross.

He wondered whether Lan Xuanyu had enjoyed his early freedom.

He wondered why his friend had looked apologetic when leaving.

He wondered many things.

Wondering was allowed.

Pushing was not.

Behind another door, Lan Xuanyu sat with a rank thirteen reading and a question too dangerous for the wrong teacher.

In Dorm 336's small training room, Yan Shuo copied one line from Teacher Ji's lesson into the part of his body that remembered fire.

Special colors had special meaning.

Small colors did too.

The midterm exams were coming.

So were questions none of them were ready to answer aloud.
