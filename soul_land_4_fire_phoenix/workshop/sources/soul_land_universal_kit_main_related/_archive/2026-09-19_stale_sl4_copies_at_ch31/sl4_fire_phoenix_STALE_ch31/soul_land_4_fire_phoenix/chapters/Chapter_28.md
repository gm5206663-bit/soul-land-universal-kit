# Chapter 28: The Match They Were Allowed to See

The jade bird looked too small to make the arena afraid.

That was the first lie.

Lan Xuanyu knew it before the thought finished forming.

Some dangers announced themselves with size. The Crocodile Dragon had done that. Its scales had carried weight. Its head had changed the shape of the floor. Even before it attacked, the body understood that being struck by it would be like being corrected by a wall.

The Emerald Demon Bird did not carry weight like that.

It carried absence.

One heartbeat, the air was whole.

The next, something had already crossed it.

Jin Xiang was still falling when Lan recognized what had happened. A tiny dark point had opened in the center of the boy's forehead. The simulator protected life, not pride; before pain or disbelief could become a scream, Jin Xiang dissolved into white light.

His Flaming Bird wings never finished closing.

Qian Lei made a sound that was not quite a cheer and not quite a sob.

"It... it worked?"

"Do not call it yet," Lan said.

Because the Emerald Demon Bird had not stopped.

Li Pengbin had been higher than Jin Xiang, green wings open, Green Roc line still written through the air from his first skill. A Green Roc martial soul should have owned distance better than most children in the class. Long flight, wide air, patient movement — those were advantages against ordinary opponents.

This was not ordinary.

The jade bird flickered.

It did not chase like a hawk.

It appeared.

Li Pengbin twisted, too late. The green wings that had looked graceful a moment earlier became decorations attached to a body that had no time to use them. The Emerald Demon Bird struck once.

White light swallowed him.

Tang Yuege's hand flashed toward his waist. A small soul guidance handgun appeared, the kind of last-resort tool that made sense for someone whose main martial soul attacked from distance. His eyes were wide but not empty. He had realized the correct problem: the bird was too fast to fight after it moved, so he needed to fire where it would be.

His thought was good.

His body was not fast enough to finish it.

The Emerald Demon Bird crossed the space between them in a green thread.

Tang Yuege vanished.

Two breaths.

Three opponents.

Silence.

Lan Xuanyu felt cold sweat slide down his back.

The bird hung in the air again, palm-sized and flawless, jade feathers bright under arena light, red beak clean, eyes fierce enough to make its beauty worse. It looked like a treasure a child might want to hold if the child had never learned that treasures could bite through skulls.

A hundred-year soul beast, Lan thought.

The estimate came from color and body, from lessons and memory, from the way the energy around it felt. A hundred years. Not thousand. Not impossible by age.

Impossible by bloodline.

He understood in that instant something Teacher Ji had said many times and that children often pretended to understand because the words were easy: age was not the whole of strength. A weak thousand-year beast and a terrifying hundred-year beast were both real possibilities. Species mattered. Bloodline mattered. Instinct mattered.

The Emerald Demon Bird's instinct was murder sharpened until it became flight.

Its head turned.

The jade eyes looked at Dorm333.

Qian Lei's expression collapsed.

"No," he said.

Lan's right hand tightened. Liu Feng lifted the White Dragon Spear.

The bird moved.

Qian Lei cut the summoning.

The Gate of Summoning vanished so fast that the old door seemed to fold out of existence rather than close. The Emerald Demon Bird disappeared with it. For one fraction of a breath, the place where it had been still felt pierced.

Then the arena light returned to ordinary brightness.

No opponent remained.

Dorm333 had won the second match.

None of them celebrated.

Qian Lei stared at the empty air. "It wanted to eat us."

"It wanted to attack," Lan corrected automatically, then decided the correction was useless.

"No," Qian Lei said, voice shaking with offended certainty. "It looked at my head like my head had become a steamed bun."

Liu Feng exhaled hard. "Can you control it?"

Qian Lei turned toward him slowly. "Does my face look like I controlled it?"

"Then why did it attack them first?"

"Because I told it they were enemies before it decided everyone was food." Qian Lei swallowed. "The thought touched it and caught fire. I have never felt anything like that. With the Crocodile Dragon, it was heavy, but it listened. This one listened like a knife listens to the hand throwing it. After that, the knife wanted to keep cutting."

Lan looked at the place where Jin Xiang had disappeared.

He had wanted the second match to test whether the right-hand Gate continued to follow the dragon-related pattern.

The answer was no.

Or not only.

That made the victory larger and less comforting.

Liu Feng's grip on the White Dragon Spear relaxed by one line. "We won."

"Two matches," Lan said.

Qian Lei sat down before anyone told him to. "If the third door sends out something that wants to eat my head, I am retiring from being important."

"You cannot retire during an exam."

"Cruel rules."

The restoration light descended.

Soul Power returned like water poured into a cup.

Spiritual Power did not.

Qian Lei closed his eyes at once, pulling his breath thin and careful. He had won two matches for Dorm333. He had also spent more than their plan could afford.

Plans, Lan thought, watching the empty side of the arena begin to brighten again, did not break because people made them badly.

Sometimes they broke because success cost more than expected.

---

The waiting hall learned only the cleanest part.

Dorm333 victory.

Jin Xiang. Tang Yuege. Li Pengbin. Eliminated.

Second match over.

The result board did not show the hole in Jin Xiang's forehead. It did not show a jade bird hanging in the air with eyes too fierce for its size. It did not show Qian Lei cutting the Gate before his own summon turned on them.

That absence changed the room in its own way.

A visible threat could be hated.

An invisible one had to be imagined.

The second result landed harder than the first. Dorm304 had been a middle-ranked girls' team; Dorm333 beating them quickly was impressive but not enough to silence everyone. Jin Xiang's group was different. They were loud enough to be remembered, coordinated enough to be ranked higher, and personally tangled with Dorm333's earlier conflicts. If any early team had been expected to bruise Dorm333's confidence, it had been them.

Their names dimmed almost as quickly as Dorm304's had.

Luo Haoran stopped joking.

That alone made Song Yichen look at him.

"What?" Luo said, defensive at once. "I am capable of seriousness. It is rare, so appreciate it."

Song looked back at the board. "Two quick wins."

"No footage," Luo said. "No explanation. No screaming. I dislike all three."

Several students nearby had begun whispering anyway.

"Qian Lei again?"

"It has to be. He is the summoner."

"But Jin Xiang can fly."

"Glide. Li Pengbin flies better."

"Then what did they summon?"

"How would I know? Ask the board."

No one did.

The board kept its secrets with adult cruelty.

Yan Shuo stood between Song and Luo, public face calm enough that a classmate glancing over would see only the monitor thinking. Inside, Yan Shuo'er felt the shape of the room change around the hidden result.

Before the arena test, public pressure had three obvious peaks.

Lu Qianxun, Rank20, Golden Lion King, two yellow rings.

Yan Shuo, Rank23, Fire Phoenix, deep yellow and deep purple.

Dorm333, strange first-place team, four plus signs, Lan Xuanyu SP200, Qian Lei SP187, Liu Feng's speed.

The class had tried to arrange those facts like stones on a table. Which was heavier? Which was sharper? Which could be ignored until later?

Dorm333's first two wins had kicked the table.

Yan did not know how they had won.

That was the point.

She knew Lan had promised to say what he could. She knew Teacher Yin's extra training had a simulator and dizziness and hunger. She knew Teacher Nana had once taught him a catching game and then left a space behind that still showed when her name entered his eyes. She knew Lan was not lying when he could not explain everything.

But knowing a friend was not the same as knowing his team's method.

And in an exam, friendship did not entitle her to his teammates' secrets.

So she did not guess beyond what the room could support.

"Qian Lei is not a joke anymore," Yan said.

Luo glanced at him. "Was he ever?"

"To the class, yes."

Song nodded. "Unreliable summoner. High Spiritual Power but bad result control."

"That description is dead," Yan said.

The words were quiet.

They still reached the boys standing nearest them.

One of them swallowed.

Natural consequences did not need speeches. Sometimes one accurate sentence was enough to move fear from one shelf to another.

Across the waiting hall, Lu Qianxun stood with his arms folded. His face did not show anxiety, because Lu Qianxun's pride had never enjoyed helping other people's rumors. But Chang Jianyi's eyes had narrowed, and Ye Lingtong's fingers tapped once against her sleeve.

They were counting too.

Everyone was.

Dorm333 had won twice.

The match order had not reached Dorm336 or Dorm316.

The room's center of gravity kept shifting before anyone was allowed to touch the floor.

---

In the observation room, the teachers had no such mercy.

They saw everything.

Mu Zhongtian replayed the Emerald Demon Bird's movement twice, then stopped because the third time would not make it less unreasonable.

"A hundred-year beast," one teacher said, still not quite believing his own words.

"Species quality," Ji Hongbin said. "Do not teach children that years are the whole answer. This is why."

Yin Tianfan stood nearby, round face thoughtful and eyes not lazy at all.

He had agreed to the arena format quickly because he wanted to see what pressure had made of his disciple. Warship simulations taught reaction, endurance, judgment under chaos, and the ability to continue thinking while the body objected. But a child who could endure space was not automatically useful in a team fight.

Lan Xuanyu, unfortunately for everyone who wanted simple categories, kept refusing to be one thing.

"Is that martial soul fusion?" Yin asked.

Ji Hongbin did not answer with the speed of irritation. That meant he was truly considering it.

"Not at the formal level," Ji said at last. "They are too young. Their rings and cultivation cannot support a true martial soul fusion technique. But the effect is not ordinary assistance either."

Mu Zhongtian looked from the replay of Qian Lei's Gate to the notes from the second match. "Qian Lei's summons become reliable when Lan Xuanyu is involved. Not safe, apparently, but reliable enough to defeat teams immediately."

"Reliable in target selection is already terrifying for that martial soul," another teacher murmured.

Ji pointed at a different clip: Liu Feng retreating into Lan's palm, White Dragon Spear piercing Green Roc Shadow with a faint chill. "Not only Qian Lei. Look here. Liu Feng's martial soul gains an ice-property response when Lan makes contact. The White Dragon Spear is not Qian Lei's Gate. Completely different route. Yet Lan Xuanyu can affect both."

The room quieted.

Teachers liked categories because categories helped keep children alive long enough to become exceptions. Lan Xuanyu was becoming the kind of exception that made categories useful and embarrassing at the same time.

"Control-type," Mu said slowly. "Twin martial souls. Strong body. Middle Spirit Connection at this age. Can enhance summoning and spear routes. But Soul Power Rank16."

"That is the problem," Ji said. "His martial soul may be too unusual, too strong, or too costly. Slow Soul Power growth cannot be ignored. A beautiful future is still a future; if the foundation cannot climb, talent becomes a locked room."

Yin Tianfan snorted. "You combat teachers always think the only door is punching something until it opens."

Ji turned his head. "And you think every door should become a battleship hatch."

"A fleet hatch," Yin corrected. "Think larger."

Mu pretended not to hear. He had learned that interrupting two senior teachers too early only made them include you in the argument.

Yin clasped his hands behind his back and looked at Lan Xuanyu on the screen. "His Spiritual Power has reached two hundred already. His pressure resistance is excellent. His judgment keeps improving. If this continues, Spirit Sea at twelve is possible. A qualified captain needs more than Soul Power. In a fleet, one correct command can beat a hundred strong fists."

Ji smiled rarely, which made the expression more dangerous. "Go upstairs and say that strength does not matter. See how many people refute you."

Yin's mouth twitched. "Under normal conditions."

"Children who become legends are never normal conditions."

"You are trying to steal my disciple again."

"I am trying to prevent you from wasting a child who may lead an elite squad."

"He calls me master."

"Because I did not move fast enough," Ji said, then looked annoyed that honesty had escaped.

Yin's smile became unbearably friendly.

Mu Zhongtian cleared his throat before the two of them turned the finals into a custody dispute.

"There are still strong teams waiting," he said. "Dorm316. Dorm336. Several others."

That brought the conversation back to the class.

On another screen, the waiting hall showed students staring at the result board. Yan Shuo stood very still beside Song and Luo. Lu Qianxun's group stood apart. The room had become a map of questions.

"Do we show the match recordings?" one teacher asked.

Mu considered it.

If they showed the first two matches now, they would reveal too much of Qian Lei's summoning behavior and Lan's connection to it. Later teams would gain more preparation than earlier ones had possessed. That would punish Dorm333 for succeeding spectacularly. It would also turn the test into a lesson in copying answers from footage.

But hiding everything forever would not be fair either if a later structural problem appeared.

"Not yet," Mu said. "Continue. Record everything. Release only results unless the test itself becomes unfair."

Ji nodded once.

Yin laughed softly. "You sound like someone trying to hold a lid on boiling water."

Mu looked at the screen as new light gathered in the arena.

"Then we will see what spills first."

---

The third match was shorter than Qian Lei wanted and longer than his Spiritual Power could afford.

The Gate opened again.

This time the answer came with heat.

A Red Armored Dragon stomped into the arena, smaller than the Crocodile Dragon but still large enough that the opposing lower-ranked team lost its formation before any technique struck them. Its plates glowed like banked coals. When it breathed out, red fire rolled low and wide, not elegant, not clever, simply too much for children who had entered expecting to find three tired classmates.

Lan controlled the line.

Liu Feng guarded the gap.

Qian Lei held the Gate with a face that suggested his soul had begun negotiating with the floor.

The opposing team tried to split around the flame. One succeeded for two steps before Liu Feng's spear redirected him. Another raised a defensive skill and discovered that blocking heat did not also block Lan Xuanyu's ice edge from the side. The third never found a clean path through the Red Armored Dragon's breath.

White light.

White light.

White light.

Third victory.

Qian Lei dropped into meditation the instant the Gate vanished.

This time no one joked.

Lan crouched beside him. "Can you summon again?"

Qian Lei did not open his eyes. "If you want me to summon a headache, yes."

"Useful headaches?"

"Mine rarely attack the enemy."

Liu Feng looked toward the far side of the arena. "Then we switch."

Lan nodded.

They had hoped Qian Lei could carry the first several matches with summons, leaving Lan and Liu Feng fresher for later pressure. The Crocodile Dragon had cost much. The Emerald Demon Bird had cost more because controlling something that did not want to be controlled was like holding a blade by its edge. The Red Armored Dragon had finished the plan's first stage.

Three wins.

That was a foundation.

It was not safety.

They needed at least five wins to protect next term's plus-sign privileges from the penalty Mu had announced. Three victories meant the cliff was closer. It did not mean they had crossed it.

The arena brightened again.

Lan and Liu Feng moved before the three new figures fully appeared.

Defending champion advantage.

The phrase sounded polite in a teacher's mouth. In the arena, it meant the team already standing could turn decision into motion while the challengers were still becoming real.

Lan stepped forward with his right foot.

The force gathered from calf to waist to shoulder. Golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass wrapped around his right arm, and heat ran through the meridians beneath it like a door opening in the body.

Liu Feng came from behind him, a white line drawn with breath.

Lan's palm struck Liu Feng's back.

Push.

The White Dragon Spear changed.

The dragon-head patterns near the spear tip brightened, not with gentle light but with sharpened gold. Liu Feng's whole body seemed to become part of the spear's forward line. He crossed the arena before the challengers' eyes finished focusing.

They had no time to hate it.

The spear swept.

All three were lifted.

For a heartbeat, three children hung above the arena with their martial souls half-called and their plans still trapped behind surprise.

Liu Feng's spear paused in the air, then fell.

The center opponent vanished first.

Lan had already reached them.

He jumped, shoulder brushing Liu Feng's side. His right hand pushed again, changing Liu Feng's direction toward the opponent on the right, while the recoil sent Lan toward the left.

The left opponent managed to release a large net.

It spread beautifully.

That was unfortunate for it, because beauty did not matter if timing was wrong.

Lan's left hand pressed down. Water gathered into a spinning vortex that caught the net's center and dragged its pattern out of shape. His right fist fell through the opening his own movement had made.

The fist carried body force, meridian heat, and the momentum of a child who had learned from walls, warship turns, and too many moments where being Rank16 should have meant losing.

The net collapsed.

Ice thorns finished the answer.

On the other side, Liu Feng's spear light wrapped the last opponent before a proper defense appeared.

White light flashed again.

The fourth match ended.

It had been faster than the summons.

Lan landed and breathed hard.

Liu Feng landed beside him, chest rising and falling, eyes bright with the dangerous joy of a speed specialist who had been given a straight road and a storm behind him.

Qian Lei opened one eye from meditation. "Did we win without me?"

"Yes," Liu Feng said.

Qian Lei closed the eye again. "Good. Continue respecting me while I am absent."

Lan almost smiled.

Then no new opponents appeared.

The far side of the arena remained empty.

Lan looked toward the observation window that was not truly a window. "They stopped it."

"Why?" Liu Feng asked.

Lan thought of the three challengers who had vanished before their martial souls could matter.

"Because that was too fast."

---

Mu Zhongtian rubbed his forehead.

He was proud.

He was also a teacher responsible for an exam that had just revealed a hole in its own fairness.

"If this continues," he said, "later teams may not even release martial souls before Liu Feng reaches them. The arena is thirty meters. With Lan Xuanyu's push and Liu Feng's speed, the defender's first-move advantage is too large."

No one argued.

The fourth team had not been weak enough to deserve vanishing before the match became a match. They had simply entered into a rule shape that Dorm333 had solved faster than expected.

Changing the arena size would punish Dorm333 after they had found a valid tactic.

Starting both sides after a delay would rewrite the defending-champion format.

Showing all prior footage would overcorrect and hand out too much information about Qian Lei's summons.

Ji Hongbin looked at the replay once.

Then he said, "Show the untested teams this match. Only this one."

Mu's eyes sharpened.

Ji continued, "The problem is not that Dorm333 is strong. The problem is that challengers are entering blind into an immediate first-move kill zone. Let them see the public tactic that created the fairness issue. Then adapting becomes part of their examination. Do not show the summoning matches. Those are separate and revealing them now would distort the earlier sequence."

Yin Tianfan's smile widened. "Old Ji can be reasonable when he is not trying to steal children."

"Silence helps you look wiser," Ji said.

"It has never suited me."

Mu ignored them both with the discipline of a man preserving his own sanity.

"Show the fourth-match recording to the teams who have not yet challenged," he ordered. "Brief review only. Then continue."

The teacher at the console nodded.

The waiting hall screens lit.

---

At first, the students thought the board had made a mistake.

The result display changed, but instead of only names and victory marks, a large screen unfolded above the hall. The recording opened from the arena's overhead angle.

Dorm333 stood ready.

Three challengers appeared.

Lan Xuanyu and Liu Feng moved before the opponents fully settled.

The hall went quiet.

Not the silence from earlier, when missing information had made everyone imagine monsters.

This was worse in a cleaner way.

Now they could see exactly enough to be frightened properly.

Liu Feng became a white line.

Lan's hand touched his back.

The White Dragon Spear brightened.

Three opponents rose into the air before one of them finished releasing a martial soul.

A boy near the front muttered, "Again."

No one asked what he meant. Everyone felt it. Dorm333 had once been the weak team with an unreliable summoner, a fragile spear user, and a Rank16 center whose numbers made people comfortable. That version of them had been dying for months. The video finished killing it.

The recording continued.

Lan redirected Liu Feng in midair, then struck the net opponent himself. Water vortex. Fist. Ice thorns. Liu Feng's spear finishing the right side.

End.

The screen replayed once at slower speed.

This time the room watched Lan's hand.

Not everyone understood what they saw.

They did not need to.

Public effects did not require private explanations to matter.

Yan Shuo watched without blinking.

The first time, his body had followed Liu Feng's speed because speed demanded attention.

The second time, Yan Shuo'er watched Lan.

The video did not show the hidden places inside Lan's body. It did not explain gold and silver. It did not show a vortex, a dragon line, Teacher Nana's deeper lessons, Teacher Yin's warship pressure, or anything Lan himself might not yet understand.

It showed enough.

Lan did not merely stand behind Liu Feng.

He chose the timing. He lent force. He changed the angle. He caught recoil and turned it into the next direction. Liu Feng's speed was still Liu Feng's. The spear was still Liu Feng's. But around Lan, the line became sharper than it had any right to be.

Yan felt Dawnflame Kite stir softly beneath the ribs.

Not fear.

Recognition of a pattern: feather over motion, body behind force, timing making power more than itself.

The Dawn-Iron Phoenix Roc answered with a heavier stillness: do not confuse seeing a line with owning it.

Yan breathed once, slow.

Public fact.

Not private truth.

Useful enough.

Luo's voice was very quiet. "If we enter like that, I am going to be airborne before I finish complaining."

Song answered just as quietly. "Then complain earlier."

A laugh moved through Luo's throat and died before becoming sound.

Yan said, "No straight line."

Song looked at him.

Yan kept his eyes on the screen as it froze on Lan's palm against Liu Feng's back. "If our turn comes, do not give them all three bodies in one sweep. Song anchors low and early. Luo splits off before the entry light finishes. I do not stand where Lan can make Liu Feng choose everyone at once."

"Can we release in time?" Luo asked.

"Maybe not fully," Yan said. "So do not make full release the first answer. First answer is position. Second is not being convenient."

Song nodded slowly. "If Qian Lei has recovered by then?"

The room around them still whispered about Liu Feng. About Lan. About how a Rank16 boy could do that.

Yan remembered the first two hidden wins and the third result that had appeared before the video. Three victories before this one. Qian Lei sitting behind Dorm333 with SP187 and a summoning martial soul people had mocked because it was easier to mock the unreliable thing than admit unreliable did not mean weak.

"Then we respect him," Yan said. "Not the old joke. The current person."

Luo looked at the board again.

Four victories.

Dorm333 had nearly reached the line that would protect their next-term privileges.

Across the hall, Chang Jianyi watched the same replay with a face like a closed notebook. Ye Lingtong's expression had become complicated in a way anger could not simplify. Lu Qianxun's jaw was set.

Yan did not know what they would plan.

He knew they would plan.

That was another butterfly effect the old canon could not carry unchanged: no serious team in this room was now planning against a joke. They were planning against a team that could win unseen with summons and seen with speed.

And they were planning while aware that Dorm336 still waited too.

A boy from another dorm glanced toward Yan Shuo's collar, then quickly looked away when Luo noticed.

"What?" Luo asked him pleasantly. "Does our monitor's button have tactical value?"

The boy turned red and faced the screen again.

Yan did not scold Luo.

The collar sat dull against his throat. Beneath it, Yan Shuo'er felt the private line of her true body remain hidden, not weakened, not erased. The public room saw Yan Shuo: Rank23, two rings, monitor, future opponent. That was enough to move their eyes even while Dorm333 owned the screen.

This was what natural pressure felt like.

Not stealing the center.

Not vanishing from the room.

Existing honestly enough that other people's calculations had to make space.

The replay ended.

Mu Zhongtian's voice came through the hall speaker.

"Untested teams will be given a short preparation interval. The recording you saw is the only match footage being released at this time. Use it to adjust. The examination will continue shortly."

The hall erupted.

Whispers became plans. Plans became arguments. Arguments became half-formed formations marked in the air by fingers and nervous hands. Some teams moved away from each other to practice entry spacing. Others stared at Dorm316 or Dorm336 as if hoping stronger teams would explain fear for them.

Song stepped half a pace closer to Yan, not shielding him, simply joining the center of Dorm336's own triangle.

Luo rolled his shoulders and tried to grin. "So. We are not convenient. Good first plan. Do we have a second plan?"

Yan finally smiled.

It was small and sharp and public enough to belong to Yan Shuo.

"Yes," he said. "If they make us fly, we choose where to land."

Luo considered that.

"I prefer plans where I do not fly."

"Then move before Liu Feng teaches you."

Song said, "That was almost a joke."

"Do not spread rumors," Yan said.

The ordinary words steadied the team more than a speech would have.

On the screen, the final frozen image remained for a few seconds longer before fading: Lan Xuanyu's hand on Liu Feng's back, White Dragon Spear turning into a line, three opponents only just realizing they had already lost.

Yan watched it disappear.

Lan had said, I will say what I can.

This, perhaps, was what the arena said when Lan could not.

Not the secret.

Not the whole truth.

But enough of the shape to respect.

Enough to answer properly if the draw finally called Dorm336.

---

Inside the arena, Lan Xuanyu could not hear the waiting hall.

He saw only the empty opposite side and felt the pause stretch.

Qian Lei was still meditating, face pale and serious in a way that would have shocked anyone who knew only his ordinary mouth. Liu Feng stood with spear lowered, breathing almost steady again, though Lan could see the fine tremor in his wrist from spending speed too sharply.

"Are they giving us rest?" Qian Lei asked without opening his eyes.

"Maybe," Lan said.

"I love mysterious kindness."

"It is probably not kindness."

"Then I love not knowing until later."

Lan looked toward the far side of the arena.

Four wins.

They had needed victories.

They had them.

But the longer the pause lasted, the more certain he became that the next teams would not enter the same way.

A good tactic used once was a blade.

Used twice in front of teachers, it became a lesson for everyone else.

Lan flexed his right hand once.

Somewhere outside, someone was probably watching what he had done.

He thought of Yan Shuo's serious face, of the brief lunch conversation, of the promise he had made because best friends deserved more than polite silence even when secrets remained real.

I will say what I can.

The arena had said something for him now.

Lan did not know whether that was good or bad.

He only knew that if Yan saw the shape of the tactic, Yan would not pretend not to see it.

That thought made him smile faintly despite exhaustion.

"What?" Liu Feng asked.

"Nothing," Lan said.

Qian Lei's eyes opened a slit. "Nothing is never nothing when you say it like that."

"Recover."

"Cruel and evasive."

Lan looked back to the empty side of the arena.

The test was not over.

Now the others had been allowed to see.

That meant the next answer would be real.
