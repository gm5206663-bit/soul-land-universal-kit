# Chapter 50 — Two Maps of the Forest

No one stood behind Lan Xuanyu because he had asked them to.

Lu Qianxun simply arrived with Ye Lingtong and Chang Jianyi, then chose the space behind Dorm333 as the six qualifying teams gathered outside the simulation hall. The decision changed the room more than an announcement would have. Jin Xiang, farther back with his own team, noticed it. So did everyone who had spent the last two days trying to understand how three children had earned a score that made ordinary explanations look foolish.

Twenty thousand three hundred and forty-one did that to people.

It made them remember the Ground Fire Scarlet Dragons.

It made them look at Lan’s sleepy face and see the child who had found a way through a battlefield that should not have contained one.

Ji Hongbin let the silence settle before speaking.

“The next qualifier begins now. Show what you can do. If cooperation becomes possible, Lan Xuanyu will take overall command.”

A stir passed through the teams.

Lan looked as if he had been handed a chore rather than a title.

Across the hall, Song Yichen leaned slightly toward Yan Shuo.

“If cooperation becomes possible,” he said under his breath.

Yan’s eyes stayed on Ji Hongbin.

“That is not the same as pretending it exists before we find anyone.”

Luo Haoran folded his arms. “Useful distinction. I dislike receiving commands through trees.”

Song glanced between them. “If we do find them?”

“Lan sees rules quickly,” Yan said. “We use that. If we do not find them, we keep responsibility for our own line.”

It was not defiance.

It was not surrender.

Song nodded once.

An exit for all three.

The rule had become quiet enough to live inside a sentence.

Mu Zhongtian opened his hand toward the prepared pods.

“Enter.”

Eighteen pods unfolded.

Qian Lei stopped beside his own and looked back at Liu Feng.

“If there are Dragons again, I would like my objection recorded before the test begins.”

“No one has asked,” Liu said.

“History will understand me.”

Lan lay back inside the pod before Qian could explain which part of history he expected to take his side.

The lid closed.

Soul World took them.

A pleasant voice filled a sky none of them could see.

“Welcome to the Heaven Luo Planet selection round. Teams will be randomly transferred to separate areas of the simulated Star Dou Forest. Hunt soul beasts. Go deeper. Higher-level prey carries greater value. Each team will receive an independent score. Final kills receive the points.”

Lan listened without moving.

“Killing between teams is permitted. Killing a member of another team transfers half that team’s accumulated score. The eliminated team retains the other half. The selection lasts seven days.”

Random transfer.

Separate scores.

Final kills.

Half-point transfer.

Seven days.

Every rule had a tooth.

Teacher Ji had told them to cooperate if possible. Shrek had built the field so possible would be difficult. A group that found another group might become stronger together. It might also become a larger target. A team with points might be worth hunting. A team that helped with a kill might watch someone else claim it at the final moment.

The countdown ended.

Light fell.

The world spun.

Fresh air struck Lan’s face.

He crouched before his feet fully felt the ground.

Forest enclosed them.

The trunks were huge—old enough that their roots rose from the soil like low walls. Dense vines stitched the undergrowth together. Layers of leaves swallowed the sky until the light became a green, shifting thing. There was no trail. No human mark. No obvious direction that looked more correct than another.

Qian Lei appeared beside him, blinking hard.

Liu Feng had already put one hand near the White Dragon Spear.

Lan pulled Qian into the space between himself and Liu, then backed them against the roots of the largest tree nearby.

“Do not rush,” he said.

Qian glanced at the wall of green. “I had no intention of rushing into a forest that looks eager to grow over my bones.”

“Good.”

Lan kept his voice low.

“First priority is surviving seven days. Going deeper should mean stronger soul beasts and higher points. But if another team kills us after we collect points, they gain half our work. The rules did not say a soul beast takes points from us the same way.”

Qian’s joking expression thinned.

“In the worst case,” Lan continued, “being eliminated by a soul beast is better than giving another team half our score.”

Liu nodded once.

“So we survive, learn the ground, hunt carefully, and do not become a prize.”

“Yes.”

Lan looked through the trees.

“The ancient Star Dou Forest had layers. Peripheral Region. Hybrid Region. Core Region. At the center, the Territory of Great Beasts. Shrek is unlikely to throw us into the Core immediately, but we cannot assume we are safe. We learn what lives here before we decide what deeper means.”

Liu released the White Dragon Spear.

“I scout.”

“Three hundred meters. No farther.”

Qian touched the Coin of Summoning at his waist.

“I prepare?”

“Prepare. If Liu brings something back, we do not start thinking after it has reached us.”

Liu moved.

He stepped onto a vine, then a trunk, then nothing Lan’s eyes could follow cleanly. The forest absorbed him.

Lan released silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass around Qian. Cold gathered in narrow ice awls at the roots, angled outward through leaves and moss. Qian closed his eyes, his spiritual sense spreading in the way it did when fear stopped pretending to be laziness.

“No dangerous aura nearby,” he whispered.

Lan did not remove the ice.

A forest could be quiet because nothing was there.

It could also be quiet because something had decided to wait.

Liu returned quickly.

“No soul beasts within range,” he said. “But we do not have a map. How do we know where deeper is?”

“By what answers us,” Lan said. “Ten-year and hundred-year beasts should be common farther out. Thousand-year beasts mean caution. If the strength rises, we are likely moving inward. If it falls away, we may be moving outward.”

Qian gave him a thumb-up. “We follow you.”

“Then summon.”

Qian’s thumb stopped in the air.

“Now?”

“Now. A summon can open the path, take the first attack, and give us information. You still have Replicate as reserve.”

“My reserve is a murderous bird that does not respect conversation.”

“It respects a target,” Liu said.

“That is not better.”

Lan waited.

Qian sighed with the resignation of a person who had discovered that being a trusted teammate usually required labor.

The Coin of Summoning spun from his hand.

Two yellow Soul Rings rose under his feet.

They were darker than they had been before the Lucky Wheel. Still yellow. Still not the thousand-year purple Qian wanted badly enough to daydream about it. But the color held more weight now, a deeper tone that made the Gate of Summoning look steadier as it opened.

Golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass wrapped the edge of the gate.

Qian glanced at it.

“Why gold today? Silver-patterned grass helped with Dong Qianqiu before.”

Lan paused.

“I offended her.”

Qian’s face sharpened with immediate interest.

Liu’s did not.

Lan continued before either could create a larger problem.

“She is likely in another planet’s qualifier. The selections happen at the same time so no one can pass information between worlds. She has trained with Teacher Nana longer than I have. I am not worried about her.”

Qian accepted the answer because the Gate was already roaring.

A five-meter creature emerged through the light.

Red scales. A heavy head. Spines along its back. Smoke escaping from its mouth as it planted broad feet in the leaf litter.

Qian looked at it with offended familiarity.

“Of course. Another relative.”

“Ground Fire Dragon Lizard,” Lan said. “An evolved Ground Fire Lizard. Ground Dragon line. Not as strong as the Scarlet Dragon, but stronger than an ordinary lizard. Good range. Fireballs. Hard head scales.”

The lizard snorted fire across the ferns.

Qian waved.

“Forward. Make us a road.”

The summoned beast charged.

Liu took the front. Qian stayed in the middle, controlling the lizard. Lan covered their rear, golden grass curled around Liu’s waist and ready to amplify the first movement that needed it.

The lizard tore through vines and low branches. For a little more than a minute, it made the forest admit that paths could exist.

Then a thick vine ahead snapped upright.

It wrapped around the lizard’s head.

“Plant-type soul beast,” Lan said.

The Ground Fire Dragon Lizard’s scales flashed a metallic red. Heat surged through the vine. The plant yellowed, withered, and released a bitter scent as its grip weakened.

Fire restrained wood.

The surrounding vines recoiled.

“Move!” Lan called.

They used every remaining second of the summon’s duration. When the Ground Fire Dragon Lizard blurred back through the Gate, Dorm333 had crossed roughly a kilometer of tangled forest.

Lan chose a small rise between old roots and stopped them there.

Qian sat down before anyone had officially told him to. Liu watched the paths behind and ahead. Lan counted the information they had gained.

No strong beasts at first.

A weak plant-type threat after movement.

Fire opened a useful lane.

The direction was possible, not proven.

Qian looked at the Gate of Summoning with naked longing.

“If I entered the Platform soon and pushed my first ring to a thousand years, maybe I could summon continuously until I ran out of Soul Power. Ground Dragons one after another. I would become the strongest person in the class.”

Liu sat on a root with the spear across his knees.

“If wanting made it happen, you would already rule Heaven Luo.”

“My imagination is being punished for excellence.”

Lan looked at Qian’s rings, then at the gate.

“It is not impossible in principle,” he said. “Your summon speed depends on more than one thing. Soul Power rank. Spiritual Power. Soul Ring strength. The better each foundation becomes, the less the Gate has to fight you.”

Qian became quiet.

A route had appeared.

Unfortunately, it contained training.


Yan Shuo landed on one knee in a place the forest had folded inward.

There was no open sky above him.

Roots crossed overhead from giant trees growing on both sides of a narrow basin. They locked together high above like ribs. Long curtains of moss hung between them. The floor was black soil mixed with pale stone, broken by thin veins of warm vapor. The air smelled of rain, sap, and something mineral beneath both.

Song landed to Yan’s right, shield first.

Luo landed behind them with one hand already against the ground.

For one breath, no one spoke.

Then Yan’s wings stirred under his skin.

The instinct to rise was immediate. Air meant sight. Sight meant position. Position meant control.

“Hold,” Song said.

Yan stopped before the first ember could reach his shoulders.

Luo’s head tilted.

“Upper space ends twenty meters up,” he said. “Root ceiling. Dense enough that a full wing spread will catch. There is a drop beyond the left wall, but not a clean one. The warm veins run down, not up.”

Song looked toward the vapor line. “If we burn through?”

“The ceiling comes down,” Luo said. “Maybe not all of it. Enough.”

Yan let the Fire Phoenix pressure settle back into his chest.

The restraint did not feel like weakness.

It felt like holding a door shut while something on the other side wanted to run.

The rules of the round still echoed in his mind.

Separate transfer.

Separate scores.

Final kills.

Kill another team and claim half their work.

A forest full of children had just been told that other children could become prey.

Song planted the bottom edge of his shield against the ground.

“Then we start by not making ourselves a lantern.”

Yan glanced at the root ceiling.

A low Fire Phoenix flame might clear a route. A full flare would turn the basin red-gold, dry the moss, and announce exactly what had landed here to anyone nearby with eyes or heat sense.

“Agreed.”

Luo’s fingers moved over the stone.

“There is a slope deeper on the right. The vapor travels outward along it. That usually means the higher ground is behind us or to the left, but the root density is wrong. I need closer reading.”

Song looked at the narrow floor ahead.

“Exit?”

Luo pointed back at the transfer clearing, then at a stone shelf beneath the right-hand wall.

“Two, if the shelf holds. One, if it does not.”

Yan watched the shelf.

A thick root arched over it, its underside swollen with old amber-colored resin. One wrong heat pulse would make the resin run. One heavy impact might drop the root across the only clear path.

It was not an enemy.

That did not make it harmless.

“Song,” Yan said, “anchor the shelf. Luo, show me the smallest cut.”

Luo looked at him. “Not burn?”

“Not burn.”

Song moved first, shield braced beneath the arch. His stance went wide. The Stoneplate Tortoise Martial Soul rose behind him in a dense gray-green shape, more weight than spectacle.

“Hold,” Song said.

Yan raised one hand.

A red-gold thread emerged from his fingertip and touched the resin knot Luo had indicated. It did not flare. It warmed one narrow seam, softening the root just enough that the tension inside it released with a dry crack.

The arch shifted.

Song’s shield took the weight.

Luo’s eyes flicked from the right wall to the ground under Yan’s boots.

“Cut,” he said.

Yan withdrew the flame before the resin could melt farther.

The root settled onto Song’s shield instead of snapping through the shelf.

For a breath, it held.

Then the far end of the shelf splintered.

“Out,” Luo snapped.

Song did not try to save the position. He drove the shield forward, using its weight to throw the root toward the collapsing edge. Yan caught Luo at the elbow and pulled him across the remaining stone. All three landed against the right wall as the shelf broke away beneath them.

The fall vanished into vapor and dark roots below.

Song looked at the empty space where he had been standing.

Then at Yan.

“That was not a clean first step.”

“No,” Yan said.

Luo crouched beside the wall, breathing harder now.

“But we knew the exit before it broke,” he said.

Song’s expression changed a little.

Not satisfaction.

Recognition.

Yan looked into the deeper slope where the vapor thinned between roots.

His first instinct still wanted height. His second wanted speed. Both would leave a bright line through a forest that had not yet told them what watched it.

Song lifted the shield.

“Ground route?”

“Ground route,” Yan said.

Luo pointed toward the lower right.

“The warmer current runs out. We move against it until the plants or beasts tell us otherwise. If I lose the line, we stop.”

“And if something finds us?” Song asked.

Yan’s mouth moved by a fraction.

“Then we make sure it finds all three of us in formation.”

They went deeper beneath the root ceiling.

No bright wings opened above them.

No score marked their path.

Only the vapor shifted around their feet, and the forest began to decide what it would ask of them.


At the higher ground, Qian finished recovering enough Soul Power to look impatient again.

Lan watched the trees.

Somewhere else, six teams from Heaven Luo had landed under the same rules. Far beyond them, other teams on other planets were learning what Shrek had put in their own forests.

Lan could not command people he could not reach.

He could not make cooperation exist because Teacher Ji had wanted it.

But he could keep his own two teammates alive long enough to find out whether the forest had left any honest road between strangers.

He rose.

Liu rose with him.

Qian groaned, then stood too.

They moved deeper.

Under the roots of another part of the forest, Yan walked at Song and Luo’s pace.

The two teams did not know where the other had landed.

Between them lay roots, distance, and rules that could turn strangers into enemies. Each kept moving with the only line it had made for itself.
