# Chapter 50 — Two Maps of the Forest

The rule voice carried no age, only indifference to whether anyone understood it in time.

“Teams will enter separately. Each team’s points are its own. The final blow on a soul beast grants the corresponding score. Eliminating another participant grants half of that participant’s accumulated score. The remaining half stays with the eliminated participant’s team. The assessment lasts seven days.”

The light above the simulation field brightened.

“Go deeper. Survive. Choose.”

Lan Xuanyu listened without moving.

Around him, five other Heaven Luo teams shifted their feet. The assessment rules gave them one comfort and took it away in the same breath: they would not begin by fighting one another, because they would not begin together.

Lu Qianxun stood behind Lan with Ye Lingtong and Chang Jianyi. He met Lan’s eyes once and inclined his head.

If they met later, that gesture might matter.

If they did not, it was only a gesture.

Ji Hongbin stepped in front of the six teams.

“Cooperation is possible if you earn it,” he said. “No one is ordered to surrender their score or their judgment. Lan Xuanyu may take overall command if the situation allows it. Until then, every team survives on its own ability.”

His gaze stopped briefly on Dorm336.

Yan Shuo’s face did not change. Song Yichen’s hand rested against the rim of his shield. Luo Haoran had already begun watching the floor, as though a pattern might be hidden in the light beneath their shoes.

Ji looked back at everyone.

“Do not confuse the last round’s result with safety.”

The countdown began.

Lan found Qian’s sleeve. Liu found the other side of the formation without being told.

Three.

Two.

One.

The world turned white.


Lan landed in a crouch.

Forest closed around him before his eyes had finished adjusting. Old trunks climbed out of the ground in every direction. Vines crossed between them at shoulder height. The canopy was so dense that daylight arrived green and broken.

Qian breathed out beside him.

“Please say this is the easy part.”

Liu had already released his White Dragon Spear.

“It is not.”

Lan pulled them behind the nearest tree. Qian went in the middle automatically. Liu held the outer angle. The formation was small enough that it did not need a speech.

“First, we learn where we are,” Lan said.

“No map?” Qian asked.

“No map. No score display. No promise that the nearest danger is the one we can see.”

Liu glanced up into the canopy.

“Scout radius?”

“Three hundred meters. Do not chase anything. If you hear a fight, return before you decide what it means.”

Liu nodded once and vanished through the roots.

Qian looked at the Coin in his palm.

“Summon?”

“Not until we know whether something is already watching us.”

Qian’s rings rose when he focused. Both were yellow. Both were darker than they had been yesterday, their color carrying the uncomfortable reminder that a reward did not make the forest kinder.

Lan released a few silver-patterned strands into the undergrowth. Ice awls formed low around the tree, not as a wall, only enough to punish anything that rushed them straight.

His new Spirit Sea spread farther than it had before, offering a scattered map of damp roots, small lives under bark, and wind moving over leaves.

Nothing had found them yet.

Liu returned quickly.

“Nothing large close. The ground rises north. There is a broken vine line east. I found tracks but not fresh enough to tell team or beast.”

Lan considered the light. The forest felt old rather than empty.

“The rules said go deeper,” he said. “The stronger beasts will tell us whether we are moving correctly. We do not rush the first opening we see.”

Qian grinned faintly.

“Which means we rush the second?”

“It means we make the first opening.”

He nodded toward the Coin.

Qian understood.

The Gate of Summoning opened with a circular pull in the air. Smoke rolled across the roots. A heavy red head pushed through.

The Ground Fire Dragon Lizard hit the forest floor with both forefeet and shook itself hard enough to send wet leaves flying.

Qian pointed toward the north.

“Path. Low path. Do not decide you are a tree.”

The lizard did not understand the words. It understood the gesture and the thicket ahead. It pushed forward, scales brushing vines aside. When a thick plant whip lifted toward its face, the lizard opened its mouth and sent a fireball through the leaves.

The vine blackened and recoiled.

“Fire against wood,” Qian said, pleased.

“For a minute and a half,” Lan reminded him.

“Every good thing has a critic.”

Liu moved ahead of the lizard without losing sight of it. Lan held the rear. The dragon lizard made a rough, hot corridor through the green, and for a few breaths the team covered ground quickly enough that the forest seemed to permit it.

Then the lizard slowed.

Its head tilted.

Qian’s smile vanished.

“What?”

The lizard’s scales lifted along its back.

Lan raised one hand.

No one moved.

A sound reached them from somewhere beyond the trees. Not a roar. Not a branch breaking.

A person shouting once, too far away to identify the words.

The lizard turned its head toward it.

Liu looked at Lan.

“Human?”

“Maybe.”

The summon-light around the lizard had begun to thin. Lan watched the narrow path it had cut, then the untouched growth to either side.

“North,” he said. “We do not enter someone else’s fight because it gives us a direction.”

The lizard moved again.

They followed.


Yan landed badly.

Not hard enough to fall. Hard enough that the ground under his right foot sank to the ankle in warm gray mud.

Heat pushed up through the sole of his shoe.

Song caught the back of his sleeve before he could put more weight on it.

“Still.”

Yan froze.

Steam slid through the roots around them in white, quiet threads. The trees here grew thinner and farther apart than the forest above. Their trunks were dark with moisture. A low basin stretched in front of them, its floor broken by shallow pools and black stone ridges.

The air tasted like wet metal.

Luo crouched at the edge of the nearest pool. He did not touch it. He watched the surface tremble.

“Not water,” he said.

Song looked at the mud swallowing Yan’s boot.

“That helps.”

“Hot spring below it. The crust is holding in patches.”

Yan eased his foot backward exactly as slowly as he had put it down. Steam shifted around his ankle. The mud made a soft sucking sound and let him go.

A bubble broke in the nearest pool.

The sound was small.

Then another bubble broke twenty paces farther in, and a strip of gray earth between them sagged.

Song raised the shield.

“Back.”

They retreated to the root line together. Yan’s wings pressed flat beneath his shoulders. One clean lift could carry him to the far ridge in seconds.

It could also tell anyone above the basin precisely what had landed here.

Luo followed the roots with his eyes.

“The basin is lower on the other side. There is a stone lip there. If we can reach it, the ground will hold.”

Song looked at the path between them and the lip.

“Can we?”

“Not three abreast.”

Yan studied the black ridges. Some rose high enough to step on. Some only looked that way because steam hid where they ended.

“How long?” he asked.

Luo shook his head.

“I cannot give you time. I can give you stones that have not moved.”

“That is better,” Song said. “It is not enough.”

A low scrape came from the basin’s far edge.

All three of them turned.

A section of mud had broken open near a stone ridge. Beneath it, something moved slowly through the steam. Not close enough to identify. Large enough that the crust bowed before it.

Song’s shield came up another inch.

“We do not wait for it to arrive.”

Yan looked at the dark stones.

“I can cross first. Put a wing line back—”

“No.” Song said it before the thought finished. “You cross, the thing chooses between us and you. Then we are standing where it wants us.”

Yan’s jaw set.

Luo pointed at a half-buried trunk lying across the nearest hot pool.

“Use that. The wood is soaked. It will not burn quickly.”

“Burn?” Yan asked.

“Not broad fire. A breath under it.”

Song understood first.

“Dry the top. Not the roots.”

Yan stepped to the trunk. He lowered one hand until heat gathered beneath the bark in a thin, controlled line. Steam hissed. The trunk did not blaze. Its upper surface darkened, then hardened enough that Song could set the shield across it without sinking.

The scrape came closer.

“Order,” Song said.

“Luo,” Yan answered.

“No. Song. The shield keeps the trunk from rolling.”

Song looked ready to argue.

The mud behind them bulged.

He did not.

Song went first. Shield low, he put his weight onto the trunk. It bowed. Yan held the heat steady. Luo pointed without lifting his hand.

“Left foot. Then the black stone. Do not use the pale one.”

Song crossed the first pool.

A pressure wave passed under the mud behind Yan.

The ground where he had landed split open with a wet crack.

“Yan,” Luo said.

Yan moved.

He did not flare his wings. He did not rise.

He ran across the trunk while Song held it level and Luo called the stones. The heat beneath Yan’s feet was just enough to keep the wet bark from throwing him sideways.

The thing beneath the mud surfaced behind them.

A blunt gray back broke steam, ridged with stone and algae. It did not chase. It turned toward the warmth Yan had left on the trunk and struck once.

The trunk jumped.

Song dropped his shield edge onto it. The impact drove him to one knee.

“Now,” he said.

Luo crossed last.

The pale stone under his second step sank before he touched it. He changed his weight in the air, caught Song’s shield rim, and landed on the black ridge hard enough to scrape both palms.

Yan seized his wrist and pulled him clear.

The trunk rolled into the hot pool behind them.

Steam climbed high.

For an instant, the white column rose above the canopy.

Then wind pushed it north.

All three of them watched it go.

Song looked at Yan.

“Was that visible?”

Yan listened to the distant forest.

“Yes.”

Luo wiped mud from his palms.

“Then the far ridge is not ours anymore.”

No one argued.

The basin had given them a route.

It had also given the forest a marker.

They moved west beneath the stone lip before whatever had seen the steam could decide what it meant.
