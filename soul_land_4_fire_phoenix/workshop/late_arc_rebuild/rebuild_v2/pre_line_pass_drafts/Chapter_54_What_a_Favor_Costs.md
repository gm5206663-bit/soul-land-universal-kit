# Chapter 54 — What a Favor Costs

The Gold Silk Ape did not travel with them.

It traveled ahead of them.

That distinction mattered.

It never moved far enough to vanish completely from Qian Lei’s sight, but it never slowed enough for one of them to touch it. Its injured arm stayed close to its side. Its gaze returned again and again to the grass-woven pouch at Lan Xuanyu’s waist, where the Purple Zoysia rested beneath damp leaves.

The agreement had survived the clearing.

It had not become trust.

Liu Feng watched the branches overhead.

“It is taking us somewhere.”

“It is walking somewhere,” Qian said. “We are the ones following.”

Lan’s eyes stayed on the gold shape moving through the trees.

“Both can be true.”

The ground climbed. Roots knotted above a shallow rain-cut channel. The ape stopped at its edge so abruptly that Lan’s grass around Qian’s waist drew tight.

Nothing moved in the channel.

That was what made Qian’s neck prickle.

The ape looked back at him.

Not through him. Not into the part of his mind that wanted the Coin to do impossible things.

At him.

Qian understood the invitation only because the animal waited.

He lifted the Coin but did not open the Gate.

“Can I ask?” he said.

Lan nodded.

“One question. No pushing.”

Qian sent a thin, intentional thread of thought toward the ape. Not images he had not been given. Not a search through an animal’s fear. Only the shape of a question.

**Danger?**

The reply struck quickly.

Spotted fur above the channel. Gold eyes. Three separate points of pressure. A gold-furred shape smaller than the ape lying still beneath leaves on the far bank.

Then the contact snapped closed.

Qian staggered back half a step. Lan caught his elbow.

“That is all,” Lan said.

Qian swallowed the ache behind his eyes.

“It knew they were there.”

Across the channel, three lean shapes appeared among the branches.

Gold-eyed Leopards.

They were not as large as the Palm Tree Bear. They did not need to be. Their paws held wet bark without sound. Their bodies flowed around the tree limbs as if the trees had grown specifically to carry them. Whenever pale light crossed the leaves, their eyes flashed gold.

Lan followed the Gold Silk Ape’s refusal to meet that light directly.

“Do not stare into their eyes,” he said. “Their gaze can lock a body for a breath.”

Qian kept his own eyes low.

Liu shifted his spear into a two-handed grip.

The ape hit the trunk beside it with one fist.

Bark jumped loose.

It did not need a Coin for any of them to understand.

“It wants help,” Qian said.

“It wants them dead,” Liu said.

“Yes.”

The word did not make the forest simpler.

Lan watched the three leopards measure the wounded ape. A human team could leave. The agreement did not include every fight the animal found. The Zoysia did not oblige them to become the ape’s weapon.

But a smaller gold-furred body lay across the channel, visible now through a gap in the leaves.

The leopards had chosen ground, height, and numbers. The ape had brought them there because alone it would be torn apart.

“We help it live through the first exchange,” Lan said. “We do not promise its revenge.”

Qian looked at the animal’s injured shoulder.

“It will not hear the difference.”

“It does not have to hear it. We do.”

Liu said nothing. He only set his feet.

The Gold Silk Ape climbed.

It showed its wounded side as it crossed a ladder of roots, making itself look slower than it was. One leopard dropped lower. A second flowed along the branch above it. The third remained still, gold eyes fixed on the ape’s next movement.

“Qian,” Lan said. “Gate on the ground. Short.”

Qian held the Coin out.

His rings rose, yellow darkened by the Lucky Wheel’s gift but still yellow. The Gate opened at the channel floor with a pull of air. The Ground Fire Dragon Lizard forced its heavy head through, red scales scraping root and stone as it found its footing.

“Stay low,” Qian murmured. “Do not become a tree.”

The lizard did not know language.

It knew the scent of three predators above it.

A leopard moved.

Its eyes flashed.

The Gold Silk Ape’s body locked in midair.

Qian felt the jolt of it—not a new link, only the visible horror of watching a living thing stop answering itself. The ape’s limbs opened. Its feet lost the branch. The other two leopards came from either side, claws already aimed for its throat.

Lan’s golden grass snapped upward.

It caught the ape’s ankle and shifted the fall backward by the smallest possible measure.

That was enough.

The first leopard landed on bark. The second crossed empty air. The third twisted away from its own packmate, but the branch cracked under all the altered weight.

The Ground Fire Dragon Lizard roared.

A fireball crossed the channel and burst against the branch in a short red flare. It did not set the canopy alight. It did make every leopard move.

The ape hit the far bank, rolled, and came up on one knee.

For a moment it looked at the grass around its ankle.

Then it looked at Lan.

Lan released the vine.

The ape leaped again.

This time it was not bait.

Gold hair shivered from its back in a hard bright sheet.

“Down,” Lan said.

Qian dropped behind the lizard’s shoulder as fine golden strands shredded leaves overhead. One leopard cried out. Another fell through branches and struck the channel floor hard, its foreleg bent wrong beneath it.

It saw Qian.

The lizard was too slow to turn.

Liu was not.

The White Dragon Spear crossed the space in one clean line. Liu did not try to meet the whole leap. He struck the leopard’s shoulder and redirected it into the root beside Qian. Claws scored wet wood close enough to send splinters across Qian’s sleeve.

“Gate,” Liu said.

“I am doing it.”

The Ground Fire Dragon Lizard lowered its head and charged. A burst of flame hit the mud ahead of the wounded leopard, forcing it away from the tree line instead of toward it.

The Gold Silk Ape arrived at the far wall first.

It caught the leopard at the shoulder. Both bodies hit mud. The ape’s injured arm shook once. Its good hand found the animal’s throat.

Qian looked away before the movement ended.

A cold line passed through the roots near his cheek.

Lan’s grass tightened around his waist and pulled him behind the trunk. The Gold-eyed Stare struck the bark where he had been standing. Frost-white pressure crawled along one shallow groove in the wood.

“That was aimed for close,” Lan said.

The remaining leopards had separated.

One held to the branches, careful now that fire had made the lower routes dangerous. The other circled on the far side of the channel, watching Lan instead of the ape.

It had learned.

Lan sent silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass across the open strip between it and the lizard. Uneven ice awls rose from the mud, not a wall, only ground that would punish a straight leap.

The leopard chose the trees.

The ape chose the same trunk.

Their fight became glimpses: gold, spotted fur, bark struck hard enough to shake wet leaves loose. A claw reopened the ape’s shoulder. The ape drove the leopard into the trunk and held it there with both arms.

The third leopard came down from the opposite side.

“Spear line,” Liu said.

Lan’s grass struck the trunk beneath its paws. Its landing changed by half a step. Liu met it low with the spear, throwing its balance sideways without calling on Silver Edge again.

The Ground Fire Dragon Lizard’s head slammed into it a heartbeat later.

The summon-light around the lizard had thinned almost to nothing. It held long enough to pin the leopard against the roots with one smoking foreclaw.

Then the Gate took it back.

The leopard was still moving.

The Gold Silk Ape crossed the channel before anyone else could.

It put itself between the boys and the last enemy. Its hair was sparse. Its breath had become ragged. But when the leopard lunged free of the roots, the ape met it with both arms.

The struggle ended under the branches where none of them could see all of it.

When the forest went quiet, the ape crouched beside three still bodies.

No one approached immediately.

Qian could not hear its thoughts. He did not try to. He could see grief in the way its shoulders rose and fell, the way one long hand passed once over the smaller gold-furred shape on the far bank before returning to its own wound.

The favor had done what Lan promised.

It had kept the ape alive through the first exchange.

It had not made the cost small.

The ape drew something from the fur near one leopard’s head.

A small skull rested in its palm, bright gold beneath mud. Thin golden lenses filled the places where eyes should have been.

Lan’s expression changed.

“A skull Soul Bone.”

Liu’s grip tightened around the spear.

“For us?”

The ape looked from the bone to Lan.

Qian did not reach for the Coin. The question did not need translating. The animal had found something strange inside an enemy it hated. It knew the humans had helped it survive. It did not know what the object meant to them.

Lan did not reach for it.

“This is Soul World,” he said. “We cannot take it outside.”

“It is still valuable here,” Qian said.

“To it,” Lan answered.

He looked at the ape’s thinning fur, its reopened shoulder, the tremor running through its legs now that battle no longer held it upright.

“Let it choose.”

The Gold Silk Ape touched the small skull to its brow.

Gold light spread in a thin, uncertain halo.

Its knees folded.

Lan’s grass caught only the fall, lowering the ape beneath the roots without binding it. The bone seemed to sink into the fur at its forehead. The light flickered, faded, returned.

“Is it sleeping?” Qian asked.

“Fusing,” Lan said. “Or trying to.”

The phrase did not make the sight less unsettling.

The ape had asked for help and paid with what remained of its strength. Now it held an unknown piece of the forest inside its head, too weak to move and too proud to let them turn that weakness into ownership.

Liu looked down the path they had taken.

“We cannot wait here for the rest of the agreement.”

“No,” Lan said.

They built shelter, not a cage. Liu placed fallen branches so rain would break around the roots without blocking the animal’s way out. Lan cleaned the fresh blood from its shoulder with a water sphere and drew his grass back afterward. Qian left a single deliberate message at the edge of the Coin’s reach.

Alive.

The plant remains.

The forest has not stopped.

The answer was not a sentence or a promise.

Warmth around a dark-purple shape. Pain beneath the unsteady gold at its brow. Then an instinctive refusal to die under roots while the world went on without it.

That was all.

At first gray light, the Gold Silk Ape had not moved beyond the shallow rise and fall of its chest. The Purple Zoysia remained in Lan’s keeping.

“We do not make its sleep a chain,” Lan said.

Liu nodded.

“And we do not spend the herb to buy an answer it cannot give.”

Qian put the Coin away last.

They left the roots open behind them.
