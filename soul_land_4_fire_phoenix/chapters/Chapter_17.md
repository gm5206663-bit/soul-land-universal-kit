# Chapter 17: The Question at the Table

Lan Xuanyu had always been quiet when he decided something.

That was what made it troublesome.

Loud people announced disaster before they ran into it. Qian Lei did it with both hands and three unnecessary explanations. Jin Xiang did it with his chin. Luo Haoran did it by leaning forward as if his feet had signed a contract without consulting the rest of his body.

Lan Xuanyu simply finished eating, stood up, and walked toward the place where everyone else had already noticed he should not walk.

The cafeteria was warm with dinner steam and children's voices. Plates clicked. Chairs scraped. Someone laughed too loudly near the soup counter. The academy chefs were still bringing out new dishes, and every time they did, at least three students looked over as if good food could fix the exhaustion Soul World had left under their skin.

Lan Xuanyu had already eaten enough food to make Qian Lei stare at him with religious concern.

He still looked pale.

Yan Shuo watched him from Dorm 336's table with one hand wrapped around a cup of warm water. His own stomach was working through dinner in deep, steady pulls. The Soul World had returned his body from the simulation without real wounds, but it had not erased the lesson. His thigh still remembered Needleback rat teeth. His shoulder still remembered stone grit. His ribs still remembered the moment he had laughed too soon after the Ironhorn ram fell.

The pain was reduced.

The memory was not.

That was the part adults had warned them about and the part no warning had really explained. Soul World did not just let a child win a coin. It gave the body a copy of consequences and then asked whether the child was smart enough to keep it.

Yan Shuo shifted his left foot under the table.

His heel found the floor more quietly than before.

Dawnflame Kite stirred beneath his breath, warm and alert. Not urging. Not speaking. It never spoke. It only carried tiny changes through his pulse: the remembered angle of Song Yichen's shield, the narrow path Luo Haoran had cut through rats, the place where Yan Shuo's fire had almost widened because anger wanted a larger shape than discipline did.

He could feel the difference now.

Before yesterday, restraint had meant holding fire back.

After yesterday, restraint also meant giving fire a road.

Lan Xuanyu reached Ye Lingtong's table.

The noise in the cafeteria bent around him.

Ye Lingtong sat with Lu Qianxun and her other teammates. She looked up first with suspicion, then with the complicated expression she always wore around Lan Xuanyu now: pride defending itself from guilt, anger refusing to admit it was also worry.

Two of her teammates lifted their heads.

Lu Qianxun set down his chopsticks.

Lan Xuanyu said, "Ye Lingtong, I want to speak with you alone."

A few nearby students went silent at once.

Yan Shuo closed his fingers around the cup before the heat in his palm could rise into anything visible.

Not his table.

Still public.

Still dangerous in a way that did not involve claws.

Lu Qianxun's brows drew together. "Talk here."

Lan Xuanyu looked at him. His voice stayed mild. "I want to speak to Ye Lingtong. It is a secret between us."

The cafeteria breathed in.

Then children became children.

"Ooooh."

Someone whistled.

A girl covered her mouth and laughed.

At another table, Jin Xiang pushed his chair back with the delighted expression of someone who had found a new way to be annoying.

"A private secret?" Jin Xiang said. "Class Monitor, I am not looking for trouble. Really. I am very peaceful now. But Lan Xuanyu wants to steal someone from your team on the first day after beating me. I could not bear it if I were you."

A thread of warmth climbed Yan Shuo's wrist.

It would have been easy to flare.

A little pressure. A little Phoenix heat. Enough to make Jin Xiang remember that fire-bird jokes had more than one direction now.

His breath changed before the thought finished.

Cooler in the throat.

Lower in the chest.

Yesterday's stone yard returned: rats waiting in blind spots, the ram lowering its head, Song's shield not stopping Yan Shuo's power but giving it a place to arrive.

This was not a fire lane.

This was Ye Lingtong's voice to use.

Ye Lingtong stood abruptly.

"Jin Xiang, why are you shouting?"

Jin Xiang's grin paused.

Ye Lingtong did not wait for him to recover. She grabbed Lan Xuanyu by the sleeve and pulled him toward the cafeteria exit herself.

The whispers changed shape, less certain now that she had acted before anyone else could define the scene for her.

Yan Shuo let out the breath he had not realized he was holding.

Across from him, Luo Haoran leaned forward. "Are we pretending that was not interesting?"

"We are eating," Song Yichen said.

"Eating can include observation."

"Observation can include silence."

Luo looked wounded. "You are becoming crueler after one Soul World coin."

Song's face did not change. "Better timing."

Yan Shuo almost laughed, then kept it smaller so his ribs would not complain.

Lu Qianxun had not missed the exchange. His eyes passed once over Jin Xiang, once over the door Ye Lingtong had used, and then, briefly, over Yan Shuo.

Two Rank 18 public pressure points in the same class was a fact that did not sit quietly anymore. Lu Qianxun's rank carried Golden Lion King pressure and a top-team expectation. Yan Shuo's rank carried the monitor's weight, Fire Phoenix pressure, a separate Dorm 336 result, and the kind of public record adults tried to discuss without sounding nervous.

Yan Shuo understood none of Lu Qianxun's full calculation.

He understood enough to know the air had tightened.

His fingers loosened around the cup.

He did not chase Lan Xuanyu.

A month ago, that might have felt like abandoning a friend.

Now it felt different.

Lan Xuanyu had Qian Lei and Liu Feng. Ye Lingtong had her own pride. Lu Qianxun had his team. Yan Shuo had Dorm 336's table, his aching ribs, and a fire that was learning not to leap at every spark.

Growth did not always move forward.

Sometimes it stayed seated.


Outside the cafeteria, the corridor air was cooler.

Ye Lingtong released Lan Xuanyu's sleeve and turned on him at once.

"What is it?"

Lan Xuanyu looked back toward the closed door, then at his own left hand. He still did not understand why silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass could strengthen the girl from the gate, or why golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass could wake Liu Feng's White Dragon Spear. He only knew that Ye Lingtong's martial soul had failed around him before.

That failure had hurt her.

It had hurt more than her body.

"Do you remember," he said carefully, "how your martial soul lost effectiveness when we sparred before?"

Ye Lingtong's face darkened.

"You called me out to mock me?"

"No." Lan Xuanyu shook his head immediately. "I was thinking. Back then we were opponents. Maybe contact with my martial soul suppressed yours because we were fighting. If we were on the same side, maybe it would be different. Maybe it could help instead of suppress—"

The cafeteria door opened.

Lu Qianxun stepped out with one of Ye Lingtong's teammates beside him.

His expression was polite in the way a drawn line was polite.

"Lan Xuanyu," he said, "when eating, do not converse; when in bed, do not speak. If you need to talk, wait until class resumes tomorrow. Do not disturb our team's meal."

Ye Lingtong frowned. "Why are you pulling me?"

Lu Qianxun glanced at her.

The glance held team position, class politics, and displeasure that Lan Xuanyu had created a public scene at their table. Ye Lingtong's anger did not vanish, but it changed direction. She looked back at Lan Xuanyu.

"We will talk in class," she said.

Then Lu Qianxun guided her back inside.

Lan Xuanyu stood alone in the corridor for two breaths.

Then hurried footsteps came after him.

Qian Lei arrived first because panic gave him speed he never seemed to possess in battle. Liu Feng followed a half step behind, sharper-eyed and ready for trouble.

Qian Lei looked from Lan Xuanyu to the closing cafeteria door.

"What happened? Did you lose love?"

Lan Xuanyu's face darkened.

"What love? How old are we? Do not talk nonsense."

"That reaction means emotional damage," Qian Lei said with expertise he did not have.

Liu Feng ignored him. "What did you ask her?"

Lan Xuanyu lowered his voice. "I wanted to know whether her martial soul only loses effectiveness against me when we are opponents. If we are allies, maybe my martial soul can enhance hers too."

Qian Lei blinked.

Then he stared as if Lan Xuanyu had dropped a Soul coin into a drain.

"You went to ask another team whether you can make them stronger? In front of their team? In front of Lu Qianxun?"

Lan Xuanyu hesitated.

Qian Lei put a hand over his heart. "Xuanyu, your looks are very good, your combat sense is good, your appetite is terrifying, but your social sense is a disaster beast."

Liu Feng's mouth twitched.

Lan Xuanyu frowned. "I only wanted to understand whether the effect is assistance or suppression."

"Understanding is excellent," Qian Lei said. "Giving a weapon to another team is less excellent. Ye Lingtong is not in Dorm 333. She has teammates. If you can enhance her, why would you do it during competition?"

Liu Feng nodded. "We need to test what you can do with us first. If your enhancement is real, keep it secret. We are at the bottom."

Lan Xuanyu understood then.

Not completely. Not because he suddenly cared about ranking the way Qian Lei did. But he saw the shape of the problem: a skill that could change Qian Lei's gate and Liu Feng's spear was not just curiosity. It was a team secret, a survival chance, and perhaps a burden.

"All right," he said. "I will not ask her now."

Qian Lei sighed in relief. "Good. Let us protect our fragile future. Preferably with more food."

"You already ate," Liu Feng said.

"Emotion consumes energy."

Lan Xuanyu looked back at the cafeteria door, where Ye Lingtong had disappeared behind voices and steam.

He still wanted to know.

He also knew wanting was not always enough reason.


Yan Shuo did not hear the explanation.

He saw only the public part: Lan Xuanyu leaving, Lu Qianxun following, Qian Lei and Liu Feng rushing out, and all four boys returning later with faces that were trying to hide different thoughts.

Qian Lei looked relieved and offended.

Liu Feng looked sharper.

Lan Xuanyu looked as if a question had been folded and put away instead of answered.

Yan Shuo did not ask.

That restraint lasted until they passed close enough for Qian Lei to make restraint difficult.

"I have returned from emotional danger," Qian Lei announced.

"You were outside for less than three minutes," Yan Shuo said.

"Danger may be dense."

Luo lifted a hand from Dorm 336's table. "Dense is a good word for you."

Qian Lei pointed at him. "Mantis boy, I sense hostility."

"You sense dinner."

"Also true."

Lan Xuanyu looked at Yan Shuo and gave a small shake of his head.

Not now.

Yan Shuo answered with a shrug that meant he had understood more than he wanted to admit and less than he would need to be useful.

Then Lan Xuanyu went back to Dorm 333's table.

Song watched Yan Shuo for a moment.

"You did not follow."

"I have legs," Yan Shuo said. "They are not an excuse."

Luo's chopsticks paused. "That sounded like something an adult says before assigning homework."

Yan Shuo looked offended. "Take that back."

Song's mouth moved, not quite a smile. "It sounded trained."

That, unfortunately, was true.

After dinner, the academy issued their evening recovery window. Soul World training did not excuse tomorrow's lessons. If anything, it gave the teachers new reasons to watch how children walked when they thought no one was watching.

Dorm 336 returned to their room with three full stomachs, one Soul coin recorded to their team ledger, and enough soreness to make stairs feel philosophical.

Luo groaned as he dropped onto his bed.

"I have decided that rats are worse than Lava Hounds. Lava Hounds at least sound impressive. If I complain about rats, people laugh."

"You were bitten by six of them," Song said.

"Seven. One was emotionally invisible."

Yan Shuo sat cross-legged on his own bed and opened the message his mother had left.

The screen showed Lin Mei in the family apartment kitchen, hair tied back, sleeves rolled, a pan cooling behind her. Yan Ke's shoulder appeared at the edge of the frame; he was pretending not to listen while very obviously listening.

"Your teacher's note said first Soul World entry ended safely," Lin Mei said. "Safely does not mean lightly. Eat. Sleep. Do not practice high-output fusion tonight."

Yan Shuo straightened. "I was not going to."

Yan Ke leaned into view. "He says in the tone of someone who considered it."

"Father."

"Record-keeping includes tone."

Lin Mei ignored both of them with the skill of long practice. "Tell me one thing you learned that is not a score."

Yan Shuo opened his mouth.

His first answer was going to be easy. Teamwork. Fire restraint. Rats were evil.

Then his body answered before his pride did.

His right shoulder lowered half a finger-width.

His breath settled behind the ribs instead of rushing to the throat.

His palms remembered the difference between heat that filled space and heat that shaped space.

"I wasted too much heat when I got irritated," he said slowly. "Not a huge amount. Just enough that the rats used the bright edge. When I made the fire narrower, Luo could cut beside it and Song did not have to brace against my pressure too."

Lin Mei's expression softened only a little.

That was how Yan Shuo knew he had answered correctly.

"Good. A fight that gives only a coin is a wasted fight. A real fight changes the next movement."

The words landed harder than praise.

Yan Shuo looked down at his hands.

Dawnflame Kite pulsed once, bright and small.

A real fight changes the next movement.

Yan Ke cleared his throat. "And a real student submits the recovery form before sleeping."

"Father."

"Forms are also movements."

Luo lifted his head from the other bed. "Uncle Yan is wise. I hate it."

Yan Ke looked pleased. "Unknown student, you have excellent judgment."

Song said from his desk, "He does not."

"Betrayal," Luo whispered into his pillow.

Lin Mei's gaze shifted past the screen, as if she could see all three boys clearly. "No one here is asking you to become smaller so your teammates can stand. If your fire is strong, make it accurate enough that they can stand closer. That is harder than burning alone."

Yan Shuo did not answer quickly.

The hidden warmth in his chest spread to his shoulders, his elbows, his knees. It did not feel like a new power. It felt like yesterday's mistakes finding places to live.

"I know," he said.

Then, because honesty had already started and stopping halfway would be cowardly, he added, "I am learning."

Lin Mei nodded.

"Then sleep after review. Low output only if your instructor assigned it. No wing-frame. No true combat fusion."

"I know."

Yan Ke raised one finger. "And no comparing your coin to Lan Xuanyu's coin. Coins become expenses. Trust me."

"Dorm 333 has Qian Lei," Yan Shuo said. "Their coins are doomed."

From across the room, Luo said, "That is the truest thing spoken tonight."

The call ended with home feeling both far and close.

Yan Shuo sat still for a moment after the screen dimmed.

No new rank flashed in his body.

No new ring answered him.

No voice named what was happening.

But when he rose for the assigned low-output review drill, he moved differently.


The academy review chamber for Dorm 336 was small and plain, with a floor grid, three projection pillars, and a wall screen that displayed their stone-yard scenario from above.

No teacher stood over them. The first review was self-guided unless the system flagged danger. That made Luo relax until the screen began playing his overextension from four angles.

"This machine has no mercy," Luo said.

Song folded his arms. "It has accuracy."

"Same thing."

The first replay showed the moment Luo had chased two Needleback rats too far from Song's shield, creating a gap the Ironhorn ram nearly used. The projection froze at the instant Yan Shuo's fire widened to cover that mistake.

A red arc appeared.

Wasted output: moderate.

Friendly pressure overlap: high.

Yan Shuo stared at the line.

It was not a rank test. It was not a soul-spirit age test. It did not know the living truth inside Dawnflame Kite. It was just a training-system judgment of what his visible technique had done.

It still irritated him.

Because it was correct.

"Run that again," he said.

Luo turned. "You want to watch my humiliation twice?"

"I want to see the gap before I fill it wrong."

Song looked at him, then tapped the replay control.

The scene restarted.

This time Yan Shuo did not watch himself first.

He watched the rats.

One leaped high to catch attention. Two cut low behind Luo's heel. The ram lowered its head one breath later. Song's shield angle shifted toward the ram because it had to. Luo's right foot crossed too far. Yan Shuo's projected fire spread wide because his old instinct had read the whole gap as his responsibility.

Wrong.

Not all gaps were his to fill.

Some gaps were his to shape.

"Freeze," Yan Shuo said.

The replay stopped.

He stepped onto the grid and faced the projected positions. Low-output Dawnflame Form answered within one breath, close to his skin: warm feather-light over shoulders and arms, not a blazing cloak. The first ring did not brighten beyond academy-safe limits. Dawnfeather Mantle formed in thin layers, each featherlike line tucked close instead of flaring outward.

Luo's eyes sharpened despite his jokes.

Song unfolded his arms.

Yan Shuo lifted one hand.

A narrow crescent of dawnfire slid along the floor grid.

It stopped short of Song's projected shield position.

A second thread curved behind the low rat path, not touching Luo's projected route.

The shape looked smaller than the original red arc.

It was not weaker.

The heat did not leak into useless brightness. It waited low, exactly where the rats would have had to put their paws.

The system marked the new line in pale gold.

Projected friendly pressure overlap: low.

Projected interception chance: improved.

Luo sat up fully.

"That is unfair. You became smarter while I was complaining."

"You were complaining loudly," Yan Shuo said. "It gave me time."

Song stepped onto the grid next. "Again. This time I shift later."

He did not command.

He offered a change.

Yan Shuo nodded.

The replay ran. Song altered the shield timing by half a beat. Luo stayed closer. Yan Shuo's first line almost arrived too soon, brightening at the edge because pride liked visible answers.

His lungs adjusted before the flare escaped.

Heat sank.

The line thinned.

The projected rat struck the boundary and turned into Luo's cut.

The system chimed.

Yan Shuo felt sweat at the back of his neck.

Low output still cost something when done carefully. Maybe more in the mind than a reckless flare did. Reckless power spent soul power and called the spending strength. Careful power asked every part of the body to listen.

Dawnflame Kite's warmth shifted along his spine.

Not more.

Cleaner.

Again.

They worked until the review chamber's recovery timer warned them to stop.

By then, Luo had failed the same footwork pattern only twice instead of five times. Song's shield angles no longer made Yan Shuo's fire feel trapped. Yan Shuo could lay three narrow fire lanes without the Mantle fraying at the shoulders.

No one called it a breakthrough.

No one measured a new number.

When Yan Shuo released Dawnflame Form, the warmth withdrew smoothly instead of snapping back like an overused string.

That was growth too.

He felt it in the absence of pain.

He felt it in how quickly his breath returned.

He felt it in the way Luo, for once, did not joke immediately after the drill ended.

Song checked the timer. "Sleep."

Luo groaned. "Now you sound like my grandmother."

"Your grandmother is probably right."

"She is. It is her worst quality."

Yan Shuo laughed softly.

This time his ribs did not protest as much.


On Heaven Dou Planet, Dong Qianqiu did not sleep early.

She sat in a quiet training room with her white skirt folded neatly around her knees and cold mist drifting faintly between her fingers. The room's lights were soft. Outside, the academy grounds held the kind of order that made distance feel ordinary.

Distance had stopped feeling ordinary.

Nana stood near the window.

She had asked Dong Qianqiu to remain close for the time being. Not as a command that needed fear, but as a request that carried weight because Nana rarely asked without reason.

"If the gate answers again," Nana said, "do not force the connection. Observe first. Ask clearly."

Dong Qianqiu looked up. "You really think the boy may be your student?"

Nana's eyes lowered slightly.

Lan Xuanyu's name had not left her thoughts since Dong Qianqiu said it. A handsome boy with black hair and dark eyes. Silver-veined grass. Heaven Luo Planet. An aura that could pull across an impossible distance.

Too many pieces could be coincidence.

Too many coincidences became a path.

"He may be," Nana said.

"Then why not go to him?"

The question was direct because Dong Qianqiu was direct. She had seen enough of Nana's strength to know distance was not always a wall to her teacher.

Nana was quiet for a long moment.

There were people watching her because of the destroyed pirate warship. There were rules around her, questions she did not want to answer, memories still broken inside her own mind. She could leave if she truly chose to leave.

But leaving carelessly would not end with only her.

Lan Xuanyu had a home. Parents. A life she had promised herself not to crush under the weight of mysteries he had not chosen yet.

"If I leave now," Nana said, "others may look for the reason. They may look at his family first."

Dong Qianqiu understood enough not to press.

Nana turned from the window.

"So we wait for the gate. If it is really him, this connection may let me guide him without bringing danger to his door."

Dong Qianqiu's cold mist curled tighter around her fingers.

"And if it summons me into Lava Hounds again?"

A faint smile touched Nana's mouth.

"Then leave some alive long enough to ask your question."

Dong Qianqiu considered this.

"The fat boy will talk."

"Probably."

"A lot."

"Very probably."

For some reason, Nana's smile deepened by a fraction.

Dong Qianqiu decided that if the gate opened again, she would first confirm the black-haired boy's name, then decide whether freezing the talkative one counted as forcing the connection.


The next day did not care that several children had touched things larger than their understanding.

It began with lessons.

History. Soul beast categories. Basic mecha structure. Physical conditioning. Meditation theory. Lunch. More lessons. Extra training.

Heaven Luo Academy's Elite Junior Class did not slow down for mysteries. It wrapped them in schedules and expected children either to grow or fall behind.

Yan Shuo liked that more than he admitted.

A schedule could not tell him what Lan Xuanyu's secret was. It could tell him when to eat, when to stretch, when to stop overusing his shoulders, and when to put his fire through a line so narrow that even Luo stopped making jokes for three whole breaths.

During afternoon movement drills, Instructor Ji set Dorm 336 against a shifting obstacle wall that spat rubber pellets at irregular angles. The task was simple: Song shielded the front, Luo cut or redirected the side shots, and Yan Shuo used low-output fire lines to indicate safe lanes without burning through the targets.

Simple tasks were where yesterday showed itself.

The first volley came low.

Yan Shuo's left shoulder wanted to flare.

It did not.

His breath dropped. Dawnfeather Mantle formed close, one thin layer over the arm, another across the upper back. The fire line went out at ankle height, curved, and stopped just before Song's shield edge.

A pellet bounced off Song's Stoneplate Tortoise armor into the exact gap Luo had prepared.

Luo cut it aside.

The second volley came from above.

Yesterday, Yan Shuo would have looked up first and answered with brightness.

Today, the memory of the Needleback rats made his eyes stay level.

The upper shot was bait.

A side pellet came half a breath later.

Yan Shuo shifted his heel, left the upper shot to Song's shield, and drew a narrow dawnfire line across the side lane. Luo's mantis light snapped through the signaled space and struck the pellet before it crossed the line.

Instructor Ji made a note on his tablet.

No praise.

No new number.

But he did not stop them.

That was praise in academy language.

Yan Shuo felt heat coil through his body and settle instead of spill. The current formal number remained Spiritual Power 72. The current soul power remained Rank 18. Dawnflame Kite and the first ring remained actual living age 248 years, yellow tier in the private truth of his living bond, above normal for that age because the Fire Phoenix path fit them too well to behave like paper. Official records still lagged at the original one-hundred-year acquisition.

None of that changed on the tablet.

His movement changed.

That mattered.

Across the hall, Lan Xuanyu passed between classes with Qian Lei and Liu Feng.

Qian Lei looked impatient enough to vibrate.

Liu Feng looked as if he had been waiting all day with a spear inside his bones.

Lan Xuanyu looked calmer after a night of meditation, but Yan Shuo could see the lingering hunger in the way his eyes moved toward the cafeteria doors even when it was not mealtime.

Their gazes met for a moment.

Yan Shuo lifted two fingers in greeting.

Lan Xuanyu returned the gesture.

Neither boy crossed the hall.

That, too, was a kind of training.


By the time Dorm 333 returned to the training ground for their last task of the day, Qian Lei could barely stand still.

"Let's go," he said. "Training ground. Immediately. Before fate changes its mind."

Liu Feng was already at the pod row.

Lan Xuanyu looked from one roommate to the other. The past two days had changed them. Qian Lei's hope had become almost painful to watch. Liu Feng's silence had sharpened into anticipation.

Dorm 333 had been the bottom team for too long.

Lan Xuanyu had not joined them to become a miracle.

But something had opened when he arrived.

"Do you think she will come if we summon her?" Qian Lei asked as they prepared the entry. For once, worry pushed through the greed in his voice. "Sometimes what comes through my gate just leaves. I cannot force it to fight."

"Try," Lan Xuanyu said. "If I can strengthen Liu Feng, maybe the three of us can pass even without her."

Qian Lei's face twisted between fear and business calculation.

Lan Xuanyu asked, "What is the passing rate for this assessment in class?"

"Before yesterday? Nine teams," Qian Lei said.

"How many teams are there?"

"Ten." Qian Lei looked at him as if the answer were obvious, then coughed. "After we passed, all ten have passed. Which means we are no longer the only tragedy. We are an improving investment."

Liu Feng said, "Stop talking and enter."

The light took them.

The colosseum formed around Dorm 333 again: cracked ground, hot air, gates waiting in the walls like mouths that remembered them. The cost of one Soul coin had already been paid. Qian Lei held his Summoning Money with both hands.

Lan Xuanyu released silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass from his left hand.

Gold-patterned Blue Silver Grass waited at his right.

This time he knew where both hands needed to be.

Qian Lei began the summon.

The Gate of Summoning opened, wavering at first, its frame unstable and bright. Lan Xuanyu stepped forward and caught it with silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass.

At the same time, his right hand gripped Liu Feng's arm.

Gold-patterned strands wrapped around Liu Feng's wrist.

Liu Feng sucked in a breath.

"I feel it," he said, and the excitement in his voice broke through his usual restraint. The White Dragon Spear appeared more solid in his hand, white light gathering around the dragon-headed tip as if something sleeping inside it had opened its eyes.

Qian Lei's attention snapped back to the gate.

"It's here. It's here. Teacher, the summoning is here!"

The cold answered.

Dong Qianqiu stepped out.

Today she wore white again, clean and sharp against the heat of the arena, dark-blue hair falling behind her shoulders. She did not waste a moment looking at the Lava Hounds.

Her eyes fixed on Lan Xuanyu.

"Hey," she said urgently. "Are you Lan Xuanyu?"

Lan Xuanyu froze.

For an instant, the colosseum, the hounds, Qian Lei's gate, and Liu Feng's awakened spear all moved farther away.

"How do you know?"

Dong Qianqiu's expression changed.

She turned toward the gate before the first Lava Hound finished emerging.

"Teacher, can you hear me? He really is called Lan Xuanyu!"

A voice came through the Gate of Summoning.

Not Qian Lei's.

Not Dong Qianqiu's.

Familiar enough that Lan Xuanyu's heart seemed to stop before it leaped.

"Xuanyu," the voice said. "It's me."

Lan Xuanyu's eyes widened.

"Teacher Nana?"

Joy struck so suddenly that it hurt. Surprise followed it. Then longing, so fierce that for a moment he was not in a colosseum facing Lava Hounds. He was smaller again, standing in a room where a silver-haired teacher made impossible things feel safe because she explained them as if he could learn.

Qian Lei stared at the gate.

Liu Feng stared at Lan Xuanyu.

Dong Qianqiu watched all three while frost gathered around her hand.

Nana's voice came again, soft and unmistakable through the unstable light.

"It really is you. What a coincidence that we can communicate this way."

"Teacher Nana, where are you?" Lan Xuanyu asked.

"Heaven Dou Planet," Nana said. "Qianqiu is my student. I teach here now."

Lan Xuanyu's throat tightened.

He had known she left. He had told himself many times that she must have her reasons, that adults moved through worlds children could not follow. But hearing her voice through a gate made distance feel suddenly unfair.

"Then can you come through?" he asked before he could stop himself. "Can you come here? I miss you."

On the other side of the gate, Nana fell silent.

The Lava Hounds charged.

Dong Qianqiu moved first. Ice gathered into a dark-blue spear, and the ground before the first hound flashed white with frost.

Nana's answer came through the unstable light, gentle and heavy.

"I cannot. This space gate is too unstable. If I crossed through it, it could cause spatial chaos. That would be very troublesome."

Lan Xuanyu held the gate with his left hand, Liu Feng's enhancement with his right, and the ache of missing her somewhere deeper than both.

The fire beasts roared.

The silver grass tightened.

Far from that private arena, Yan Shuo stood in a different academy drill room with low dawnfire curved at his feet, listening to Song's shield strike and Luo's mantis step, knowing nothing of the voice that had just crossed planets.

His own lesson was smaller than a miracle.

It was also his.

He breathed once, and the fire line held.
