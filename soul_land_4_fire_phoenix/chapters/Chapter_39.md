# Chapter 39: A Vortex Learns to Hold

Nana did not make the cultivation room darker.

She did not draw runes, summon rare equipment, or fill the room with the dramatic pressure Lan Xuanyu had secretly imagined a protected breakthrough might require. She only closed the door, checked the air once with quiet silver eyes, and placed herself where she could reach him before any mistake became permanent.

That calm frightened him more than preparation would have.

If Nana was relaxed, it meant she had confidence.

If Nana stood so close anyway, it meant confidence was not the same thing as safety.

"Begin," she said.

Lan Xuanyu nodded.

He sat cross-legged and let his breathing sink.

The Mysterious Heaven Method had accompanied him for years now. At first, it had been mysterious because the name sounded large and old. Later, it became mysterious because it was the thing he returned to when his own body did not make sense. It entered meridians, circled, gathered, refined, and returned. It was not fast. It had never been fast for him. But its rhythm was trustworthy.

One cycle.

Two cycles.

Three.

Soul power moved through his body with a heaviness that had become familiar at Rank19. The bottleneck lay ahead like a translucent wall. He had struck it before, rubbed against it before, and dreamed of hearing it crack. Every time, the wall had remained: thin enough to see the other side through, strong enough to mock him.

Now, with Nana's aura behind him, the wall felt different.

Not weaker.

Ready.

Lan did not rush.

He had learned patience from failure more than from teachers. He pulled soul power through the Mysterious Heaven Method again and again, smoothing every small ripple before letting the next wave rise.

His chest warmed.

Then cooled.

The gold and silver vortex revolved there, separated by the spiral Nana had once given him. It was usually quiet when he cultivated. Not asleep. Never asleep. More like two beasts circling on opposite sides of a river, watching the water level.

Today, the river began to rise.

Lan's fingers twitched.

Nana's voice came softly behind him. "Steady."

He held steady.

The Mysterious Heaven Method completed another circuit.

Soul power pressed against the Rank19 shell.

For one breath, nothing happened.

Then something inside him cracked.

It was not loud.

No one outside the room could have heard it.

Lan Xuanyu felt it through his entire body.

The shell that had trapped him for so long finally split open, and soul power surged forward like water released from a dam. His meridians filled. His bones seemed to become lighter. Even his skin felt as if invisible dust had been brushed away.

Rank20.

Joy almost broke his concentration.

He caught it at the edge.

Nana had not told him to stop.

That saved him.

Because the next breath was pain.

Gold and silver erupted from the vortex in his chest. They did not trickle out. They rushed, threads of two different wills entering meridians, bones, flesh, and organs as if the breakthrough had opened gates they had been waiting behind for years.

The silver was clear and cold, affable to elements, soft only on the surface.

The gold was dense and hot, powerful, domineering, carrying a pressure that made his bones ring.

They should have moved around each other.

They did.

For a moment.

Then the rush became too thick.

A gold current brushed silver. A silver current scraped gold. Sparks of pain exploded inside him. It was as if burning iron and freezing needles had decided the same meridian belonged to both of them.

Lan's shoulders shook.

He gritted his teeth.

The room blurred.

His body had always been strong for his rank. That strength now became a crueler cage. Because it did not collapse immediately, it could hold more pain before failing.

Gold and silver filled him too quickly.

His meridians swelled.

His chest felt as if someone had placed a star and a moon in the same small bowl and told them not to touch.

"Teacher Nana..."

He did not know whether the words came out.

Nana did not panic.

One hand rested between his shoulder blades, light as falling snow.

"Vortex," she said.

Only one word.

Lan seized it.

Vortex.

Not meridians.

Not pain.

Not the shell that had broken.

Vortex.

His attention dove into his chest.

The gold and silver spiral was spinning faster than he had ever seen. The two colors stretched, pulled, and trembled along their separate paths. In the center, the tiny rainbow point glimmered like a grain of light beneath deep water.

He dragged the Mysterious Heaven Method's current toward that center.

At first, nothing obeyed.

Pain made every command rough.

Lan breathed in.

Vortex.

He imagined the two bloodline powers as streams that did not need to fight if they circled. He did not command them to merge. He did not dare. He only guided the soul power to follow the spiral, entering from the outside, dividing along gold and silver edges, and returning without collision.

One thread obeyed.

Then another.

The pain lessened by the thickness of a hair.

That was enough to prove the path existed.

Lan held it with everything he had.

The vortex drew in soul power. Gold and silver no longer crashed as wildly. The burning in his bones dulled. The freezing in his meridians withdrew into channels. For several breaths, the breakthrough that had nearly broken him settled.

He almost felt proud.

Then the vortex grew.

The breakthrough had not finished feeding it.

The gold and silver streams continued pouring into the spiral. The vortex in his chest swelled, larger and larger, as if Rank20 had widened not only his meridians but also the mouth of the storm itself. Soul power entered it and was immediately dragged around. Bloodline power followed. The spiral became smoother, yes, but also heavier.

Too heavy.

Lan's body began to swell.

Not visibly enough for an observer to call it monstrous. Internally, it felt as if every part of him had been filled past capacity. His skin tightened. His meridians stretched. Breath caught in his throat.

The shell had broken.

Now the thing behind the shell was trying to become too large for him.

Nana's eyes sharpened.

A cool line entered from the top of Lan's head, flowing through him like moonlight. It did not suppress the vortex. It gave his body one more breath of space.

"Xuanyu," Nana said, voice still gentle but suddenly urgent. "Merge your martial souls."

Lan's eyes opened.

For an instant, fear made the words impossible.

Merge?

Now?

Every use of the self martial soul fusion technique had made his soul power fall backward. Every use had cost him effort he could not afford. He had come to Heaven Dou to reach Rank20. If he used it now, would Rank20 vanish the moment it appeared?

His body swelled again.

The answer arrived without words.

If he did not use it now, there might not be a Rank20 to lose.

Nana's hand pressed lightly. "I am here."

Lan trusted that more than he trusted his own fear.

He lifted both hands.

Blue Silver Grass rose from his palms.

Silver-patterned from the left.

Golden-patterned from the right.

They were unstable before they even touched. The silver grass trembled with pale light; the golden grass pulsed as if something inside it wanted to roar. The two powers in his chest responded at once, racing toward his arms.

Lan brought his hands together.

Gold met silver.

The room disappeared in rainbow light.

It did not feel like the earlier fusion technique.

There was no clear spear, no shaped strike, no outward battle intent.

There was explosion.

Not sound first.

Color.

Gold and silver collided through the fragile bridge of his own martial souls. The tiny rainbow point at the vortex center flared, and the overfed bloodline storm found a mouth. Energy poured through that point, unfolded into seven-coloured brilliance, and burst outward.

Lan saw Nana's silver eyes widen.

Then white swallowed everything.

After white came darkness.

He floated in it.

No pain.

No weight.

No meridians swelling.

No Rank19 wall.

No Rank20 joy.

Only a vast emptiness and the fading echo of colors he could not count.

For a while, he wondered whether this was failure.

Then his chest pulsed.

Gold.

Silver.

Rainbow.

He fell back into himself with a breath that felt like surfacing from deep water.

The cultivation room returned.

He was still sitting cross-legged.

Nana was still there.

Her hair drifted slightly though there was no wind, and faint silver light withdrew from the walls as if the room had been placed somewhere else for the single instant when his body could not contain itself.

Lan did not understand that.

He only understood that he was alive.

Then he understood something better.

The vortex in his chest had changed.

It was larger by a full measure, perhaps a third again as wide as before. Yet it was no longer swollen wildly. It rotated steadily. Gold and silver flowed around each other with clearer boundaries and purer colour, and at the center the rainbow point was brighter, no longer only a speck of dust. It was still small, still fragile, but it shone with a firmness it had not possessed before.

His soul power settled.

Not at Rank21.

Rank20.

Exactly Rank20.

Lan opened his eyes slowly.

"Teacher Nana... did I succeed?"

Nana's expression softened.

"You succeeded. You are a Soul Grandmaster now."

Lan stared at her.

Then his face lit up.

Rank20.

After all the waiting, after being left behind by numbers, after watching others gain rings while he told himself foundation mattered, he had crossed.

He wanted to jump.

His body told him jumping was foolish.

So he smiled instead, a smile so bright and relieved that Nana reached out and rubbed his head.

"Do not be too happy yet," she said. "Feel carefully."

Lan obeyed.

He felt the vortex, the meridians, the strange emptiness after the rainbow eruption.

"I didn't lose the breakthrough," he whispered.

"No. But you used the fusion at the dangerous moment. The third bloodline helped draw away and harmonize what your body could not bear. Remember this. When you reach future thresholds, you must leave yourself enough strength to perform the fusion. If the bloodline power grows beyond what your body can hold and you cannot fuse at that moment, the danger will be far greater than today."

Lan's earlier happiness sobered.

"So every breakthrough after this..."

"Will require care," Nana said. "Between Rank20 and Rank30, use your self martial soul fusion technique ten times. Let the gold and silver powers mix little by little. Do not avoid the cost simply because your cultivation is slow. The cost is part of your safety."

Lan wanted to argue.

He did not.

His body remembered the swelling too clearly.

Nana looked at him for a long moment. For one instant, her gaze narrowed not in alarm, but in recognition of something she could not name.

During the rainbow burst, she had seen his eyes change.

Left silver, clear and sharp enough to cut space into details.

Right gold, fierce with a wildness that made even her heart tighten.

Then both had vanished.

She tried to chase the memory within herself and met only dust-covered pain.

Lan noticed the shadow crossing her expression. "Teacher Nana?"

"It is nothing." Nana shook her head. "Release your martial soul. Let us see what changed."

Lan immediately became excited again.

His left hand opened.

Silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass rose from his palm.

At first glance, it was still Blue Silver Grass.

At second glance, it was almost not grass at all.

The old blades had been thin, fragile, pretty only in the way weak things could be pretty. Now each strand was tougher, rounder, more vine-like, a cylindrical body with a sharper tip. The silver patterns no longer wandered messily along the surface. They spiraled upward in a structured net, oval openings repeating with a strange elegance.

Lan touched one strand with his right fingertip.

It resisted him.

Not much compared with a weapon.

A world compared with before.

"It became stronger," he said, unable to keep wonder out of his voice.

Then his soul rings rose.

Two yellow rings floated from beneath him.

Lan froze.

Yellow.

Both yellow.

Not one white, one yellow.

Two yellow.

His first ring had been white.

A ten-year ring.

Everyone knew that.

But the ring before him was no longer white at all.

"Teacher Nana," he said blankly. "My first soul ring..."

"Has evolved," Nana said.

"But I didn't go to the Spirit Pagoda. I didn't enhance a Spirit Soul. I didn't even get a Spirit Soul for the second ring. Why did it appear by itself? Why are both hundred-year?"

Nana's face became serious enough that Lan straightened unconsciously.

"Xuanyu, listen carefully. Do not tell others that your soul rings appeared naturally. Do not tell others you do not know your Spirit Soul or that you did not attach one. If someone asks, use the explanation your family and teachers already know. Your situation cannot be exposed casually."

Lan swallowed.

"Is it very serious?"

"Very." Nana's voice remained soft. "Not everyone in this world is kind."

Lan nodded quickly.

He had seen pirates.

He had seen cannons aimed at civilian spacecraft.

He did not need a second warning.

"Right hand," Nana said.

Lan released the golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass.

Gold flared, faint at first and then deeper. The change mirrored the silver grass in structure: tougher body, clearer spiral, a net that looked less oval and more diamond-edged, as if every pattern had learned how to brace against force. Golden scales rose faintly along his right arm and then settled.

Lan's grin returned despite the warning.

Both martial souls had evolved.

He did not even need to fight to know his strength had risen sharply.

Nana watched the gold and silver patterns together, and her body trembled once.

Something stirred in her mind.

A name?

A battlefield?

A pair of colours circling in another era?

The memory dissolved when she reached for it, leaving only closeness, ache, and the certainty that the child before her was not a stranger to whatever she had lost.

"Teacher?"

"Try the left hand's first soul ring," Nana said.

Lan activated it.

The silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass turned translucent, the interior filling with a faint ice-blue brilliance. Water elements in the room answered him immediately. Before, he could feel them if he concentrated. Now they came like streams running downhill, eager and clear.

The grass no longer merely wrapped his arm as a conduit. It stored water element inside itself. When he waved it lightly, cold air trailed behind. The vine cracked softly through the room, flexible and sharp, like an ice whip that had grown out of his own palm.

"This is much stronger," Lan breathed.

"Second soul ring," Nana said.

Lan activated it.

The chill vanished.

The first soul ring withdrew by itself.

Red light surged through the silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass.

Lan stared as the crystal-blue interior transformed into bright ruby fire. Golden-red light flickered along the vine. Heat rose so quickly that the air in the cultivation room seemed to inhale. A small flame circled the grass, and fire elements rushed toward his hand.

Lan's mouth opened.

No sound came out.

Then, finally, a sound did.

"Fire?"

The silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass, the hand that controlled water and ice, the martial soul he had expected to deepen into coldness, had given him fire.

Not stronger ice.

Not better water.

Fire.

He looked at Nana with visible distress. "Teacher Nana, water and fire don't get along! How can the same martial soul have water and fire? When the second ring appeared, the first one went away. They aren't helping each other at all. Isn't this bad?"

He was eleven or nearly twelve by paperwork, still a child, and the cultivation pressure he had carried for years made disappointment hurt more sharply than logic could soothe. He had waited too long for a second skill. He had imagined finally catching up. Now his own martial soul seemed to have chosen contradiction.

Nana raised her right hand.

A flame appeared above her palm.

Lan's fire element weakened at once, drawn toward her as if recognizing a greater source.

He stared.

"Teacher Nana, you can control fire too?"

Nana looked at the flame, then at Lan.

"I suspected part of your bloodline might be similar to mine. This confirms more of that suspicion. I do not remember my own origin, nor the source of my strength, but the closeness I felt toward you may come from this. If our bloodlines are alike, then no one is more suited to guide you than I am."

Lan's distress shifted toward hope.

"Then what should I do?"

"First, do not assume opposition means uselessness," Nana said. "Water and fire restrain each other, yes. But restraint can also create power. A weak amount of water meeting sufficient flame may intensify the flame. A strong body of water receiving fire may produce steam and transformation. The question is not whether they oppose each other. The question is whether you understand how they oppose each other."

She flicked a small droplet of water into the flame.

The fire flared.

Lan's eyes widened.

"If used clumsily, they cancel," Nana said. "If used correctly, they change."

She stepped forward and placed a hand on his shoulder.

Silver light folded around them.

The cultivation room vanished.

Cold wind struck Lan's face.

He gasped.

They stood high above clouds, under a dark vault pricked with starlight. The ground was gone. The city was gone. Below them lay a sea of cloud, pale and endless beneath their feet, though some invisible force held him steady.

"Teacher Nana..."

"Watch."

A water ball appeared above Nana's left palm.

It began to rotate.

Slowly at first.

Then faster.

The center hollowed into a tiny whirlpool. The water compressed inward while spinning, every movement deliberate enough that Lan could follow the lesson with his Spiritual Power.

Nana's right hand lifted.

A fireball appeared.

It rotated in the opposite direction, shrinking instead of hollowing. Red became deep red, then darker, denser, fiercer.

Lan held his breath.

Nana guided the fireball into the hollow of the water.

They did not slam together.

They revolved.

Opposite forces nested in one shape, heating, pressing, refusing crude collision. Steam appeared inside the water sphere. The surface trembled.

Nana flicked her hand.

The dual-coloured ball flew outward.

Ten meters away, it detonated.

Red and blue light burst through the air. Fire expanded violently, carrying a dense saturated force that made Lan's scalp prickle even from behind Nana's protection. The explosion was not as large as a warship's cannon, but it was born from two small balls in one person's hands.

It was not size that frightened him.

It was possibility.

Nana brought them back to the cultivation room with another fold of silver light.

Lan staggered once when solid ground returned beneath him.

"Was that space element?" he asked.

"Yes. If your bloodline continues along this route, each future soul ring may bring another element. One day, you may touch space as well."

"How many elements can Teacher Nana control?"

Nana smiled.

One by one, balls of light appeared before her.

Blue water.

Red fire.

Green wind.

Yellow earth.

Silver space.

Gold light.

Deep purple darkness.

Seven colours hung in the room without touching, each obedient, each distinct.

Lan stared until his neck felt stiff.

The gap between himself and Nana, which had always seemed vast, became wider and more beautiful than space.

"Water and fire fusion is very difficult," Nana said. "Among elemental pairings, it is second only to light and darkness in difficulty. There is no simple technique I can hand you. You must become familiar with the elements, comprehend their nature, and find your own way. If you only copy my understanding, you will walk behind me forever."

Lan nodded slowly.

He almost asked the question rising from the space battle.

Can a person destroy a spaceship with bare hands?

Can you?

The image of Young Noble Le stood outside a cockpit window in his mind, blue hair drifting where no air should have moved, a golden dragon tearing through pirate ships, a meteor-class attack spaceship dying in the distance.

Then Lan remembered his promise.

He had promised not to tell.

He swallowed the question.

Nana seemed to sense the movement of thought without hearing its content. She brushed silver hair behind her ear and said, "If your bloodline continues evolving, there are many things you may do in the future that you cannot imagine now. But your path must be yours. For the next two days, I will teach you some ways to use the elements and guide the next step of the footwork I taught you before. The rest will depend on your effort."

Lan's happiness dimmed with reluctance.

"Only two days? Teacher Nana, when will you come back to Heaven Luo Planet? I want to keep learning from you."

He took her hand without thinking and swung it lightly, the old child's plea returning before pride could stop it.

Nana's eyes softened.

"I am temporarily unable to leave Heaven Dou freely. I promised certain things, and I should keep them. But if you need me, you can contact me through Qianqiu when she can be summoned."

The name made Lan wince.

"Will she even answer next time?"

Nana's smile became teasing. "Qianqiu is working hard for Shrek. She has strong talent and is already touching the essence of ice. If she fought you seriously, even your martial soul fusion technique might not be enough. You should not underestimate her."

Lan's pride rose automatically. "That's because her Soul Power is much higher. If we were the same rank, I would definitely win."

"Soul Power is not everything," Nana said. "Every person has their own talent. Yours is special. Hers is too. When she fought you, she still held back because she did not truly want to hurt you. Didn't you say you wanted to be her boyfriend? Then you must work hard."

Lan's face turned red so fast it almost felt like his new fire element had betrayed him.

"Teacher Nana, we should practice."

Nana laughed.

The next two days gave him no time to be embarrassed for long.

Nana's guidance did not feel like Ji Hongbin's lessons or Yin Tianfan's drills. Ji Hongbin stacked knowledge into structures until a student understood why a thing must be so. Yin Tianfan forced instinct through pressure until hesitation died. Nana placed a finger beside a door inside Lan's perception and asked him to open it himself.

Water could carry.

Ice could pause.

Fire could drive.

Steam could obscure or burst.

A step could become lighter not by moving faster, but by giving the ground less argument.

Lan failed often.

Nana never seemed disappointed.

He did not see Dong Qianqiu again during those days. Whether she was truly busy preparing for Shrek or simply unwilling to see him, he did not know. Each time her name appeared in his thoughts, he remembered the plaza, the cheek, the shout, and the tears in her lashes afterward.

He had apologized once.

It was not enough.

He would apologize properly again if he got the chance.

But he would not confuse that guilt with promises his mouth had borrowed too easily.

There were other half-promises in his life. Ones without names yet. Ones tied to a boy on Heaven Luo whose eyes could make silence feel like a question.

On the final day, Nana personally escorted the Lan family to the space flight hub.

Lan hugged her with red eyes.

"Teacher Nana, when will I see you again?"

"When you have time, come to Heaven Dou to visit me," Nana said. "Or, when you become strong enough..."

She did not finish.

Lan heard only what he could understand.

"I will work hard. I will become strong."

"Then work hard to enter Shrek Academy." Nana's smile deepened. "Teacher will agree if you become Qianqiu's boyfriend."

Lan almost choked.

Nan Cheng heard enough to raise an eyebrow.

Lan hurriedly said, "I haven't seen her these days. Teacher Nana, please help me apologize to her. That day was my fault."

"Being brave enough to take responsibility is good," Nana said. "Go. Your flight is waiting."

Lan jumped up and kissed Nana on the cheek as he had when he was smaller, then ran after his parents.

Nana remained after the family disappeared into the space flight hub.

For a long time, she did not move.

A figure came to stand beside her.

Yun Yan's voice was gentle. "You want to go with them?"

"En."

Nana did not deny it.

The feeling had become stronger after she sensed Lan's bloodline. The child drew closeness out of her that logic could not explain. If she truly wished to leave, who could stop her?

But Lan had parents.

A home.

A life still protected by ordinariness, though cracks were already showing.

Nana would not tear that apart because of her own longing.

Yun Yan sighed and changed the subject carefully. "The higher-ups asked again. They want to invite you to Douluo Planet, to Shrek Academy, for testing. They say the equipment there is the best in the Federation. They have already communicated with Shrek."

Nana's expression cooled.

"I am not going."

"This time is different."

"No." Nana turned away. "I only want a peaceful life. Do not disturb me with this again."

Yun Yan watched the silver-haired woman leave and felt the same complicated helplessness she had felt for years.

The Federation had not forced Nana.

Partly because law still mattered.

Partly because Nana had accepted citizenship, work, routine, and restraint without harming anyone.

And partly because every serious assessment returned with the same quiet terror underneath the formal language.

Unmeasurable.

Highly suspected to have broken through human limits.

God-rank.

A person like that obeyed rules because she chose to.

No one sane treated that choice carelessly.

When Nana returned to her office, Dong Qianqiu was waiting inside.

The girl's expression said she was not angry.

Her eyes said she absolutely was.

Nana smiled. "They have gone. Are you still mad?"

"Who is mad at him?" Dong Qianqiu turned her face aside. "He isn't a good person. I only don't understand why Teacher likes him so much."

"And yet you waited here to say that."

"Teacher!"

Nana laughed softly, then let the teasing fade. "You should prepare for Shrek. It will not be easy."

Dong Qianqiu nodded.

After a pause, she asked, "Teacher Nana, if I really get into Shrek, will you follow me? Will you become a teacher there?"

"No," Nana said. "I am used to life here. If you have cultivation questions, you can contact me. Communication is convenient now."

Dong Qianqiu bit her lip.

"Then if you are not by my side... will they still be able to summon me?"

Nana's eyes brightened with laughter. "Qianqiu, have you fallen for Xuanyu?"

"No!" Dong Qianqiu nearly jumped. "Who would like him? I hate him. I will never like him. Teacher, don't say nonsense. I just... I just mean the summoning is useful for training."

"Of course."

Nana lifted her hand.

Silver light gathered between her fingers and condensed into an oval scale-like piece, fine silver patterns glowing across its surface. In the gentle halo, faint colours shifted like hidden elements beneath ice.

It flew to Dong Qianqiu.

She caught it carefully. "What is this?"

"A piece of scale," Nana said. "It carries my aura and energy fluctuation. It can act as a spatial positioning point. If Xuanyu's team summons you again, the positioning should allow them to find you even if I am not beside you. I have solidified elemental power on it as well. Wear it like a necklace and keep it with you. Treat it as my gift."

Dong Qianqiu did not fully understand how impossible the words solidified elemental power should have sounded.

She only understood that Nana was giving her something precious.

Her anger at Lan did not stop her smile.

"Thank you, Teacher. I will prepare well."

After Dong Qianqiu left, Nana touched her own chest.

A delicate golden chain appeared between her fingers. At the end hung a diamond-shaped golden plate marked by an indistinct arrow, faint golden light breathing across it.

It had come to her after she woke from ancient ice.

Not from a drawer.

Not from a person.

From a space only she seemed able to sense.

Every time she touched it, she knew it was important.

Every time she tried to remember why, pain split through her head and something deep in her resisted.

The silver scale she had given Qianqiu belonged to her.

Then whose golden scale was this?

Far away from Heaven Dou Academy, anger shook the City of Sin.

"Failed? How could they fail?"

The voice in the dark chamber was hoarse with fury. A meteor-class attack ship and five surveillance ships should not vanish against a civilian spacecraft. Not for the price they had been paid. Not with the target already identified.

The report offered little comfort.

The final message had been one word.

Dragon.

Then nothing.

Every pirate ship had been lost. No usable data returned. No body, no trophy, no confirmed explanation.

"A fleet must have been protecting the civilian craft," someone said uncertainly.

"Nonsense!" the furious voice roared. "Why would a civilian craft have a fleet? Investigate. Find out what happened. We accepted money to kill Young Noble Le, and now we have nothing but losses. If you cannot bring back an answer, do not come back at all."

The word dragon remained in the report like a claw mark.

Lan Xuanyu knew none of this.

His family's return flight was peaceful.

No pirates.

No alarms.

No golden dragon outside the window.

Peace made Nan Cheng exhale properly for the first time since the original attack. Lan Xiao watched his son during the flight, noting the way Lan sometimes looked toward the stars with too much excitement and too little fear.

That needed correcting.

When they reached home on Heaven Luo Planet, Lan felt both weary and triumphant.

He was Rank20.

A Soul Grandmaster.

The gold and silver Blue Silver Grass had both evolved. His soul power growth remained slow, yes, and the repeated fusion rule meant future cultivation would still be harder than anyone else's. But his body, meridians, and martial souls had gained something ordinary Rank20 students would not have.

That did not stop Nan Cheng from complaining as she unpacked.

"You rest one day and then return to school. The Elite Junior Class works children like machines. There should be more time to rest."

Lan Xiao put an arm around her shoulders. "Our son is about to spread his wings. The curriculum is harsh because the road is harsh. We should support him."

Then he looked at Lan Xuanyu.

"But there is something Daddy must say."

Lan straightened.

Lan Xiao's expression was serious. "You cannot be reckless again. You piloted a real space warship without proper battlefield experience. I know you wanted to protect us. I am proud of your courage. But courage without enough thought can kill you and everyone waiting for you. What would your mother and I do if something happened? Promise me that next time, before acting, you will think of your own safety and of the people waiting for you to come home. And no lying when things are serious."

Lan lowered his head.

He had lied to reach the warship.

He had not thought far enough ahead.

He still did not regret trying to protect them.

Those truths sat together uneasily.

"I understand, Daddy," he said. "Next time, I will think more carefully. I will become strong enough to protect you properly."

Lan Xiao ruffled his hair.

The next day, after rest and meditation steadied his breakthrough, Lan returned to the academy.

He had thought home rest would feel wonderful after travel, breakthrough, and Nana's training. Instead, his body became restless by afternoon. The Elite Junior Class had made suffering into habit. A whole quiet day felt suspicious.

The campus noticed his return in fragments.

Qian Lei nearly tackled him in the dormitory corridor, demanding to know whether Teacher Nana had turned him into a monster.

Liu Feng looked at him once and said, "You broke through."

"En," Lan said. "Rank20."

Qian Lei's eyes shone and then tightened. "Good. Very good. Also terrible. Now Teacher Ji will torture us more because you made progress."

"You say that as if Teacher Ji needs a reason."

Liu Feng gave the smallest possible smile.

Lan wanted to say more.

About fire.

About yellow rings.

About a vortex almost tearing him open.

He did not.

Nana's warning held his tongue.

At the turn near the profession wing, he saw Yan Shuo.

Publicly, everyone still called him Yan Shuo and used he, because the academy records, uniform, posture, and years of habit said so. But after the full Fire Phoenix second awakening, the disguise had to work harder. The copper-red hair was clipped and muted, yet when soul power stirred beneath restraint, red-gold warmth caught along the strands. His features were too vivid to be ordinary, too refined for careless words. To classmates who did not know the truth, he looked like an unusually beautiful, handsome boy with a Fire Phoenix's noble edge. To anyone who understood why a family might hide a daughter behind a son's paperwork, the danger of attention would have been obvious.

Lan did not understand that truth.

He only knew that Yan stood near Song Yichen and Luo Haoran, a stack of sensor notes in one hand, and that seeing him made the corridor feel less like returning to school and more like returning to an unfinished sentence.

Yan looked up.

For one breath, worry crossed his face before discipline smoothed it.

"You're back."

"I'm back," Lan said.

"Rank20?"

Lan blinked. "How did you know?"

"You stand differently. And you look like Qian Lei when he wants praise but is trying to pretend he doesn't."

Qian Lei, behind Lan, objected loudly.

Lan laughed despite himself. "Rank20. Finally."

Yan's shoulders eased by a fraction. "Good. The preliminary test will not wait for anyone."

There were questions in his eyes.

There were answers Lan could not give.

Nana's bloodline explanation belonged behind Nana's door. Tang Le's secret belonged outside a warship window. Dong Qianqiu's anger belonged to a plaza on another planet and a girl whose reputation he had harmed. None of it belonged in this corridor.

So Lan only said, "I'll tell what I can after Teacher Ji sees me."

Yan accepted that.

Not happily.

Correctly.

Song lifted his shield-file tablet in greeting. "Dorm336 will be in the west practice wing later. Return-point drills. If Teacher Ji leaves you alive."

Luo added, "If he does not, we will mark the route and retrieve the remains."

"Your comfort is strange," Lan said.

"Strange comfort is still comfort."

Yan's mouth curved, small and bright enough that Lan looked away first.

He went to find Ji Hongbin.

The office smelled of paper, tea, and training plans that wished students no mercy.

"Teacher Ji," Lan said after bowing, "I have some doubts. Can I talk to you and Master Yin together?"

Ji Hongbin looked up. "Oh? What doubts? Also, has your Soul Power shown signs of breakthrough?"

Lan smiled. "Teacher, I was about to report that. I broke through to the second ring. Also... my soul ring seems to have changed into a hundred-year ring."

Ji Hongbin's eyes sharpened.

"Hundred-year? I remember you said your father found you a plant-type Spirit Soul, but the details were unclear."

That had been the safe story for years. Lan kept it.

"I am not sure either. After the breakthrough, it changed."

He raised his left hand.

Silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass emerged.

Two yellow rings rose.

Ji Hongbin stood.

For several breaths, he said nothing.

The rings mattered.

The grass mattered more.

The weak Blue Silver Grass Ji remembered had transformed. The silver patterns were structured, the strands tougher, the overall aura born-again in a way only beneficial mutation could explain without reaching for more dangerous answers.

Disappointment Ji Hongbin had buried under professional patience burned away.

"Good," he whispered.

Then louder, "Good. Release your soul skill. The new one."

Lan activated the second ring.

A small flame gathered over the silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass.

Ji Hongbin's expression went blank.

Then it changed so quickly that Lan almost stepped back.

Excitement.

Pure, almost youthful excitement.

"Fire. Your silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass controls water and now fire. Water and fire are homologous through opposition. This mutation is extraordinary."

Lan hesitated. "Teacher Nana said they could help each other if used correctly. But aren't they incompatible? Is that not a problem?"

"Silly child," Ji Hongbin said, too excited to be stern properly. "Who told you incompatibility is always bad? Two compatible powers may add together. Two opposing powers, treated correctly, can react and multiply. Collision, compression, transformation — if you control it well, this can become the basis for a self martial soul fusion technique you can use repeatedly. Your Spiritual Power is high. This path suits you."

He circled Lan once, then twice, eyes bright.

"Very good. I was not wrong about you. You finally surprised me. During this period, do not go to Yin Tianfan first. You will focus on controlling fire and then on using water and fire together."

"Old Ji, have you no shame?"

The voice arrived before the body.

Yin Tianfan strode in, belly first and anger second.

Lan bowed quickly. "Master Yin."

Yin Tianfan did not spare him more than a glance before confronting Ji Hongbin. "He just returned and you are already stealing him? What was our agreement?"

Ji Hongbin snapped back, "Can you not see what happened? His second soul skill is fire-element control. Water and fire in one martial soul! This is a rare opportunity."

"Rare opportunity?" Yin Tianfan sneered. "The preliminary test is in days. The qualifier follows. He is only Rank20. He will face children of Rank30 and above from the whole planet. What are you going to fix with a few days of hurried polishing?"

"Even if polishing before battle cannot sharpen the blade much, it can make it shine," Ji said. "More importantly, he needs direction. Actual combat will force growth only if the student knows what to look for."

The two glared at each other.

Lan stood obediently to the side.

He had learned that interrupting too early only redirected both storms at his head.

Yin Tianfan lifted his communication device. "Since you want direction, let me show you direction."

A holographic image projected into the air.

The darkness of space opened above Ji Hongbin's desk.

A small warship rushed through mecha fire, changing angle under pressure, retreating and advancing with a precision that made the teacher's eyes narrow. It evaded, counterattacked, and destroyed two pirate mechas. The recording stopped before the space hunting net appeared.

Ji Hongbin turned slowly toward Lan.

"That was you?"

Lan nodded.

Yin Tianfan's chest lifted with satisfaction. "The spacecraft he rode was attacked by pirates on the way to Heaven Dou. This brat entered the reserve warship, launched, destroyed two mechas, and returned alive. I received the commendation report. With this performance and his control standard, he can enter the Space Commander System. That road is practically secure."

Ji Hongbin did not like Yin Tianfan's smugness.

He disliked more that the evidence was real.

Lan's movements in the recording showed judgment, courage, and training. High-speed directional shifts, correct attack timing, control under battlefield pressure — those were not accidents. Yin Tianfan's teaching had borne fruit.

But Ji Hongbin looked at the fire still flickering above Lan's palm and refused to retreat.

Before they could begin another round, Lan spoke.

"Teacher Ji, Master Yin, may I ask a question?"

Both adults paused.

Yin Tianfan was in a good mood after displaying the recording. "Ask."

Lan thought of the golden dragon again.

He did not mention it.

"In the current Federation, can a person's own strength compete with a spaceship? If an elite Duo Mecha Master faced a Federation spaceship, which is stronger?"

Yin Tianfan answered immediately. "The spaceship. You have studied data analysis. Do you not understand the attack power of a Mothership? It can destroy a planet. A person has limits."

Lan looked at Ji Hongbin.

Ji smiled coldly. "An ignorant person is fearless. Values are not everything. Pure total energy? Of course an individual cannot compare to a Mothership's full power. But a Mothership is huge. Does it have no openings? Can its defenses stay at maximum every moment? Can it prevent an opponent it cannot lock from entering?"

Yin Tianfan snorted. "You want to say a Battle Armor Master can destroy a Mothership? Ridiculous."

"A god-rank elite or six-word Battle Armor Master may accelerate faster in an instant than a Mothership can before full drive," Ji Hongbin said. "If such a person infiltrates the interior, only someone of the same level can stop them. Why does every Mothership have a god-rank elite stationed inside? Not to decorate the bridge. To prevent intrusion, to guard the ship, and in some sense to supervise power that large."

Yin Tianfan fell silent for a moment.

Ji pressed on. "After the Mother Planet evolved, humanity's upper limit changed. Rank99 was not the end anymore. Rank120 became possible. Battle armor did not stop at four-word either; six-word exists at the peak. You measure only cannons and shields. Human potential cannot be reduced to cannon output. A fleet can destroy homes, planets, cities. But a group of true god-rank Battle Armor Masters may destroy the fleet from within. Both roads matter. Do not pretend only metal is power."

"And do not pretend children can become six-word Battle Armor Masters because you became excited over one flame," Yin shot back.

"I am saying the path exists."

"I am saying the path will eat him before Shrek if you steal all his time."

Lan listened, heart beating faster.

Yin Tianfan's road had carried him through real space battle.

Ji Hongbin's road might one day explain how a person could stand against a ship.

Teacher Nana's seven elements floated in his memory.

Young Noble Le's golden dragon cut across it.

He made his choice before fear could make it smaller.

"Master Yin," Lan said, "if you think I can already qualify for the Space Commander System, then I want to spend more time learning elemental control from Teacher Ji. I still want space command. I won't abandon it. But I also want to try the Battle Armor path."

Yin Tianfan stared at him. "Have you taken the wrong medicine? Do you know what sort of test you face? The Space Commander System can save you ten years of struggle. Your Soul Power is only Rank20. Do not think Heaven Luo Academy is the whole Federation."

"I know," Lan said. "That is why I want both."

Ji Hongbin laughed loudly and pulled Lan toward his side as if claiming territory. "Good. A child with long sight. Fatty, what else can you say?"

"Plenty," Yin Tianfan said darkly.

The argument lasted another quarter hour.

In the end, compromise won because neither teacher could deny the other road anymore.

Ji Hongbin gained the larger share of Lan's time for elemental control, body use, and Battle Armor direction.

Yin Tianfan kept two hours every day for real warship piloting and operational integration.

Lan was not sure whether this was victory.

It sounded more like sentencing.

Ji Hongbin clapped a hand on his shoulder after Yin left. "Tell me honestly. Why did your interest in Battle Armor Masters suddenly rise?"

Lan looked down at his hands.

Gold and silver had nearly torn him apart.

Fire and water contradicted each other but might create something greater.

A person had destroyed a spaceship outside his cockpit window.

He could not reveal the last reason.

So he gave the part that was true enough.

"Teacher Ji, I want to become an elite who can destroy a spaceship by myself."

Ji Hongbin burst out laughing.

He thought the child did not understand how strong a spaceship's protective shield was.

He did not pour cold water on him.

"Then you will have to work much harder. Come. We begin now."

Lan soon learned that Ji Hongbin's much harder and Yin Tianfan's not abandoning space command could coexist inside one body only because rare ingredients, meditation, and child stubbornness were all very unreasonable things.

The next several days blurred.

Ji Hongbin fed him knowledge and training until his muscles shook: fire gathering, water control, switching without delay, not letting one soul ring's rhythm crush the other, using Spiritual Power to sense the smallest change in elemental density. Sometimes Lan produced steam when he wanted mist. Sometimes he extinguished his own fire. Sometimes he made a tiny burst correctly and Ji Hongbin's eyes shone as if the academy had gained a treasure.

Then Yin Tianfan threw him into warship training.

Not simulator only.

Real craft.

Real space.

Calculations, thrust, angle, acceleration, retreat line, false opening, weapon lock, mental stress. Yin cared most that Lan had not frozen in real battle. Courage mattered. So did wisdom. Courage without calculation was suicide with decorations; calculation without courage became useless once alarms screamed.

Lan was required to have both.

When he staggered back, Ji Hongbin made him eat.

When he finished eating, he meditated.

When he recovered, the cycle resumed.

He was not the only one suffering.

The west practice wing remained lit most nights.

Yan Shuo, Song Yichen, and Luo Haoran continued the unglamorous work that made Dorm336 a team rather than a Fire Phoenix with two shadows.

Yan's wings could already carry him through training courses, but the Battle-Armor Interface Foundation demanded more than flight. Wing-root clearance. Heat-channel discipline. How to let future armor exist without blocking living feathers. How to lower Ultimate Fire output before it warped a test bracket. How to land in a formation without forcing Song to choose between blocking an enemy and catching his own teammate.

Song mapped shield-load and return points until his wrists ached.

Luo turned sensor lines into spoken timing: high lane, false right, cut down, hold, return, now.

Yan's beauty drew looks even in exhaustion. Sweat darkened training collars; soul power sometimes lit copper-red hair into phoenix red-gold before restraint settled again. The public boy cover did not shatter, but it strained in small, constant ways: a pause in a younger student's speech, a second glance from a teacher, an old file clerk frowning as if paperwork and eyes disagreed. Yan carried all of it with straight posture and careful voice.

No reveal.

No explanation.

Only more control.

During one late practice changeover, Lan passed the west corridor on the way back from warship drills. His steps were unsteady enough that Luo immediately said, "Corpse retrieval may be needed sooner than expected."

"Not yet," Lan muttered.

Qian Lei, equally exhausted from his own summoning and Spiritual Power assignments, leaned out of the Dorm333 room behind him. "If he dies, can I inherit his rare ingredients?"

"No," Liu Feng said.

"You answered too quickly."

Yan looked from Lan's pale face to the burn mark on his sleeve and then to the faint dampness still steaming from his hair.

"Fire and water?" Yan asked.

Lan blinked.

"How did you know?"

"You smell like a kettle that lost an argument."

Song choked.

Even Liu Feng's mouth twitched.

Lan wanted to defend himself and lacked the strength.

"Teacher Ji says losing arguments can be educational."

Yan's expression softened. "Then survive the education. We still need to pass the preliminary test."

"I thought you were supposed to say something encouraging."

"That was encouraging."

Lan laughed quietly and continued toward his room.

He still had not told Yan details.

Yan still had not asked for what Lan could not give.

That, too, was a kind of trust.

At last, on the night before the preliminary test, Ji Hongbin looked at Lan Xuanyu and decided even ambition required a living student.

Lan's gaze had gone slightly empty.

He stood because discipline told him to stand. His mind had clearly wandered somewhere between a fireball, a warship vector, and bed.

"Enough," Ji Hongbin said. "You may rest for eight hours. Do not meditate tonight. Sleep. Recover your energy and relax."

Lan blinked slowly. "Teacher Ji... when does the trial begin? Where is it?"

"Nine hours from now. The preliminary test is at our school. Only the Elite Junior Class participates. It is mainly qualification verification. After that comes the qualifier in three days. The preliminary should be simple, so do not worry."

"Oh."

The word contained no understanding.

Only obedience.

Lan somehow returned to the dormitory. Qian Lei asked him something about tomorrow. Liu Feng told Qian Lei to let him sleep. Lan remembered his head touching the pillow.

Then the world disappeared.

Outside the dormitory, Heaven Luo Academy quieted.

Teachers finalized records.

Students pretended not to be nervous.

Yan Shuo checked Dorm336's formation notes one last time before closing the folder. Song's shield-load marks were clean. Luo's sensor timings were legible enough to count as a miracle. Yan's own page contained no new ring, no forbidden advance, no sudden answer to the identity problem. Only Rank39, SP962, three purple rings under protected record, and a Fire Phoenix that still had to fold its wings carefully inside public rules.

In another dormitory, Lan Xuanyu slept too deeply to dream.

Rank20 at last.

Two yellow rings.

Water and fire.

Warship and Battle Armor.

Secrets on three planets pulling quietly toward one schoolyard test.

Morning would begin with a scan.

No scan had ever cared how tired a child was.
