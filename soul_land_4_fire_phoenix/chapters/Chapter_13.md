# Chapter 13: The Seat That Waited

The first lesson Yan Shuo learned from the Elite Junior Class was that an empty seat could be heavier than a thousand-year beast.

The class did not wait to begin.

Heaven Luo Academy did not pause its schedule because one child lay in a hospital room, because a simulator accident had become a line in the emergency reports, because parents had cried outside a closed door until their voices became hoarse. The academy moved the selected students to Heaven Luo City, issued uniforms, assigned dormitories, opened training rooms, prepared food, locked schedules into wrist devices, and taught the children that entering the Elite Junior Class was not a prize.

It was a contract.

Yan Shuo understood contracts. His father loved them almost as much as he loved caution.

A contract meant the adults gave resources.

A contract meant the child gave results.

A contract meant if the child failed, the door would open behind him and someone else would be invited to walk in.

The classroom had thirty places on the register.

For the first two months, only twenty-nine chairs were used.

The empty chair was not always physically empty. Sometimes a bag rested there. Sometimes no one sat near it because the teachers disliked untidiness. Sometimes the arrangement changed for combat theory or team grouping, and the empty place vanished into a digital screen instead of wood and metal.

But the name stayed.

Lan Xuanyu.

Reserved.

Yan Shuo saw it every time the attendance board flickered on.

Ye Lingtong saw it too.

She pretended not to.

That was the second lesson of the Elite Junior Class: children could pretend very seriously when adults gave them enough pressure.

The first week after the simulation was not glorious.

Yan Shuo had entered the new academy with bruises hidden under his uniform and a stiffness in his shoulders that made every lift of the arm feel like someone had tied hot threads through his back. The high-output wing-frame burst that had turned the Stonehide Boar's charge aside still lived in his tendons as a warning. Dawnflame Kite remained warm in his soul, but if he tried to pull too much fire through the mantle, the bird's response became sharp and annoyed.

Not danger.

Idiot.

It did not speak. It never spoke.

Yan Shuo still understood the meaning well enough.

During the first morning drill, he lowered himself into Dawnflame Form on instinct before Gong Yinghao's replacement instructor finished explaining the exercise. Copper-red warmth began to gather along his shoulders.

A whistle cut the air.

"Yan Shuo," the instructor said, "cancel it."

Yan Shuo blinked. "Teacher?"

"I said cancel it."

The feather-light edge sank back under his skin. His shoulders remained warm, but the visible mantle vanished.

The instructor pointed at the painted line beneath their feet. "Elite Junior Class rule. Martial souls and soul skills are not casual decorations. Unless an exercise requires activation, unless a teacher permits it, or unless there is danger, you do not light yourself up in the hallway, classroom, dormitory, cafeteria, or waiting line. Understood?"

Several students laughed softly.

Yan Shuo's ears heated.

"Understood."

"Again."

"Understood, Teacher."

The instructor nodded. "Good. Your form-entry record is excellent. Your restraint record begins today."

That sentence was worse than punishment because it sounded like a file.

Yan Shuo spent the next week learning that the academy had files for everything.

Morning physical conditioning. Martial soul theory. Basic mecha recognition. Spiritual Power steadiness. Team drills. Cafeteria nutrition. Sleep record. Injury report. Dormitory cleanliness. Weekend permission. Psychological adjustment.

Heaven Luo Academy was nothing like the Zi Luo Branch building that had fit into familiar streets and familiar smells. This place was a city inside a city. White walls, metal doors, vast training grounds, teaching towers, separate academy districts, and a cafeteria that could feed children until their bodies remembered they were supposed to grow into weapons and pilots and commanders.

The Elite Junior Class floor was quieter than ordinary dormitories.

That did not mean peaceful.

Every quiet moment had teeth.

Because the academy file said Yan Shuo was a boy, Yan Shuo was placed in a boys' dormitory that was not Dorm 333. The cover held. That was good, because by the second week everyone knew Dorm 333 as the unlucky room with only two boys and no third teammate.

Qian Lei smiled at people too easily and lost arguments too quickly.

Liu Feng did not smile unless he was imagining cutting something.

Together they occupied the bottom of the temporary team rankings before the thirtieth student arrived.

Yan Shuo watched them once during partner rotation. Qian Lei's summoning gate failed to produce anything useful three times out of five. Liu Feng's speed was sharp enough to make the air feel scratched, but his body was too thin to hold a direct exchange. They were not weak in the way ordinary children were weak. They were uneven.

The Elite Junior Class punished unevenness.

It also punished pride, hesitation, laziness, waste, sleeping late, eating too slowly, eating too much of the wrong thing, sloppy uniform cuffs, and looking at an instructor as if a question were disrespectful instead of necessary.

Yan Shuo's body liked the food.

His father's budget liked it more.

The academy did not give the Yan family the same compensation arrangement Lan Xuanyu would later receive. Yan Shuo had not been the one left in a coma. But Elite Junior Class meals, basic training materials, dormitory housing, and several facility uses were covered for all selected students. Yan Ke read the agreement three times before signing.

"It is generous," Yan Ke said.

Lin Mei's mouth tightened. "It is an investment."

"Generosity is usually an investment with better clothing."

Yan Shuo looked from one parent to the other. "So I eat all the rare food I'm allowed to eat?"

"You eat what your training plan says," Lin Mei said.

"That is less heroic."

"Heroes who ignore meal plans vomit on the running track."

Yan Ke nodded gravely. "I have seen the paperwork."

So Yan Shuo ate what the plan allowed, slept when the dorm lights went down, and wrote to his parents every night because Lin Mei said discipline without reporting became imagination.

He did not write everything.

He did not write that the empty seat made his chest feel cold.

He did not write that Ye Lingtong trained until her hands trembled, then looked angry if anyone noticed.

He did not write that some students whispered about Lan Xuanyu as if a child in a hospital bed were already a legend, a burden, and a useful rumor all at once.

He did not write that when instructors mentioned simulation safety revisions, his Dawnflame Kite tucked itself tight against his soul and refused to flare.

He wrote his numbers because numbers were safer.

Rank 16.

Spiritual Power 61.

No new test.

Dawnflame Form: stable at low output.

High-output burst: restricted until shoulder pain clears.

Dawnfeather Mantle: cleaner layering under teacher supervision.

Dawnflame Kite: responsive, annoyed by overuse, steady during sleep.

Then, below the records, he wrote the line he did not like but could not remove.

Reserved seat still empty.

On the twentieth night after the accident, Yan Shuo woke before the dorm alarm.

The room was dark except for the thin safety strip near the floor. One roommate breathed loudly behind his privacy curtain. The other muttered equations in his sleep, because the Elite Junior Class had made even dreams studious and unpleasant.

Yan Shuo sat up.

His shoulders were warm.

Not the ordinary warmth of his martial soul. Not the clean shape of Dawnflame Form. Something in the Dawnflame Kite's little core had lifted its head, listening toward a place Yan Shuo could not see.

Danger? Yan Shuo asked without words.

The answer came slowly.

Not ours.

Pain.

Far.

Then the feeling was gone.

Yan Shuo sat in the dark for a long time, hand over his chest, listening to the two other boys breathe.

In Heaven Luo City, a concert had stopped mid-song.

In Zi Luo City's Huixin Hospital, Nan Cheng had finally fallen asleep outside the ICU because fear could hold a person upright for only so long before the body betrayed the heart.

No camera recorded the silver-clad man who appeared beside the bed.

Tang Le looked at the child lying among the medical instruments and did not understand why his own chest hurt.

The boy was pale, quiet, and much smaller than the weight of the feeling that had dragged Tang Le across the distance. He was not supposed to know this child. He did not remember knowing him. Memory, for Tang Le, was a shore hidden behind fog. Songs reached it sometimes. Faces did not.

But when his hand touched Lan Xuanyu's forehead, something in him answered.

Golden light entered quietly.

The violent gold and silver inside Lan Xuanyu retreated from that pressure as if recognizing a law older than the hospital, older than the academy, older than the name Master Le. The child's breathing steadied. The conflict inside him did not vanish, but it stopped tearing at him with the same wild edge.

Tang Le felt the vortex.

He felt danger.

He felt familiarity so deep that not remembering it felt like betrayal.

Two strands of Blue Silver Grass appeared from Lan Xuanyu's palms, one carrying golden patterns and one carrying silver.

Tang Le's own hands lifted before thought.

Blue-gold vines answered from him.

For one silent breath, the hospital room held a truth no doctor, parent, teacher, or child outside the room could name.

Then his communicator screamed.

Le Qingling's voice nearly broke through the device by force.

Tang Le looked once more at Lan Xuanyu. He touched the child's cheek with a gentleness that did not belong to a superstar leaving a concert in chaos.

"Wait for me," he whispered, though he did not know why.

Gold light vanished from the room.

Nan Cheng slept on.

Lan Xuanyu's fingers moved once beneath the blanket.

The next morning, Heaven Luo Academy's Elite Junior Class had no official lesson about Master Le.

That did not stop anyone from talking.

"He disappeared!"

"My aunt was there. She said people almost rioted."

"He came back with a new song."

"No, he said it was a surprise."

"If a teacher disappears for an hour, is it also a surprise?"

"Only if the homework disappears too."

Yan Shuo listened with half an ear while tightening his wrist guards.

A girl two rows over clasped both hands dramatically. "My cousin said his new song was about protecting time and protecting someone. It made everyone cry."

"Everything makes your cousin cry," another student said.

"Your face would make anyone cry."

The argument became loud enough that the instructor made both of them run five laps.

Yan Shuo ran too, because it was a conditioning period and life was unjust without needing help.

Ye Lingtong ran on the outside lane.

Her face had sharpened over the two months. Not thinner exactly. Harder. The old pride was still there, but guilt had wrapped around it like wire. She did not speak during drills unless a teacher asked. She did not boast about her rank. She did not mock Qian Lei's failed summons. She did not look at the empty seat unless she thought no one was watching.

Yan Shuo watched because he knew what not looking looked like.

At the end of the run, she slowed beside him.

"Did you hear?" she asked.

"About Master Le disappearing?"

"About Xuanyu."

Yan Shuo stopped wiping sweat from his face.

Ye Lingtong's throat moved once. "My father heard from Teacher Mu. The hospital said his signs are better. He moved. Maybe he will wake soon."

The track seemed too bright.

"Soon is not awake," Yan Shuo said.

Ye Lingtong flinched.

He regretted it immediately.

"I mean..." He gripped the towel. "I mean I want soon to hurry up."

Ye Lingtong stared at the ground.

"Me too."

Neither of them said anything else.

The academy whistle blew, and the Elite Junior Class ran again.

In Zi Luo City, morning entered the hospital as a thin gold line across white walls.

Nan Cheng woke with a start and hated herself for sleeping.

Then Lan Xuanyu's hand moved.

The first sound she made was not a word.

Doctors came running. Instruments flashed. Questions flew. Lan Xuanyu's hands shifted to his stomach as if his body had remembered its oldest complaint before anything else.

When his eyes finally opened, Nan Cheng cried his name so fiercely that every nurse in the room understood what kind of miracle had happened.

Lan Xuanyu looked at his mother through the fog of waking.

"Mommy," he said weakly, "I'm hungry."

Nan Cheng laughed and cried harder.

The message reached Heaven Luo Academy late that day.

Yan Shuo was in martial soul control practice, holding Dawnfeather Mantle at low output while an instructor threw padded rods at his shoulders, ribs, and knees. The task was to catch force with the feather layer and pass it through his feet without flaring high-output fire.

The sixth rod struck his left shoulder.

The mantle folded, caught, bent, and returned the pressure into the ground.

Good.

The seventh rod came faster.

His wrist device vibrated.

Yan Shuo lost half a beat.

The rod hit his ribs and sent him sideways.

"Focus," the instructor barked.

Yan Shuo clutched his side, breath broken.

"Teacher," he said, "my father doesn't message during drills unless something happened."

The instructor frowned, then glanced at the wrist device. "Read it. Ten breaths."

Yan Shuo opened the message.

Xuanyu woke. Stable. Still recovering. Do not run out of class. Your mother says this means you.

For a moment, Yan Shuo forgot how lungs worked.

Dawnflame Kite pulsed so hard the mantle nearly appeared on its own.

Not danger.

Alive.

Yan Shuo bowed his head.

The instructor's voice softened by one degree, which in the Elite Junior Class counted as tenderness. "Good news?"

"He woke up."

Several students nearby turned.

Ye Lingtong, waiting for her turn on the next mat, went completely still.

Yan Shuo looked at her.

She looked away first.

The instructor tapped the rod against his palm. "Then honor the news by finishing the drill cleanly."

Yan Shuo swallowed. "Yes, Teacher."

The next rod came.

Dawnfeather Mantle caught it without flaring.

That night, Yan Shuo sent Lan Xuanyu a message through their family contacts because the doctors and parents were still controlling communication.

Wake first. Eat second. We can argue about who was more heroic later.

The reply did not come until two days later.

It contained only five words.

I am eating first.

Yan Shuo stared at the message, then laughed until one of his roommates threw a pillow at his curtain.

Lan Xuanyu stayed in Zi Luo City long enough for adults to worry properly.

He ate until Nan Cheng stopped looking frightened by his appetite and started looking offended on behalf of the kitchen. Doctors checked him. Teachers visited him. The academy spoke gently and formally and with the anxious generosity of an institution that knew it had nearly lost a child inside its own test.

Then three tickets arrived with no sender name.

Yan Shuo learned about them only after the fact.

By then, half the class had already seen the concert clips.

"Your friend was carried by Master Le," Qian Lei announced at breakfast, as if he had personally solved a mystery.

Yan Shuo paused with a spoon halfway to his mouth. "What?"

"Lan Xuanyu. The late student. The coma one. Pretty boy. Your friend. Master Le picked him from the audience and carried him onstage."

"That sentence became worse each time you added words."

Qian Lei beamed. "But accurate."

Across the table, Liu Feng looked up from his meal. "You know Lan Xuanyu?"

"We grew up in the same institute housing area," Yan Shuo said. "Different classes later."

Qian Lei slapped the table. "Good! Then when he comes, you tell him Dorm 333 needs strength."

"I am not in Dorm 333."

"Exactly why your advice is valuable. It comes from outside tragedy."

Liu Feng said, "Eat."

Qian Lei picked up his chopsticks. "Frenzie, if you interrupt my strategy again, I will summon your patience away."

"You cannot summon reliably."

"Cruel truth is still cruelty."

Yan Shuo took another bite and looked toward the cafeteria screen. It showed silent news highlights from entertainment channels: Master Le under pale light, a beautiful black-haired child in his arms, the audience clapping instead of screaming. The clip did not show why the tickets had arrived. It did not show the hospital room. It did not show golden light quieting a storm inside Lan Xuanyu's body.

To the public, it was a sweet moment between a star and a lucky child.

To Yan Shuo, it was proof that Lan Xuanyu could stand under stage lights after lying silent for weeks.

That was enough.

He sent another message.

You are famous now. Try not to let it make you slower.

Lan Xuanyu replied that evening.

Does famous get extra food?

Yan Shuo considered this carefully.

Ask your mother. Then run.

He did not ask how the tickets arrived.

He did not ask why Master Le had chosen him.

He did not ask why the singer's hand on Lan Xuanyu's head made the news clip feel warmer than ordinary light.

Some questions belonged behind doors Yan Shuo had no right to open.

The Elite Junior Class continued.

It continued through the kind of time adults called recovery and children called waiting. Morning conditioning, martial soul control, rare-food meal plans, Spirit Connection steadiness lessons, pressure rooms, team drills, supervised low-output Dawnflame Form, sleep logs, and recovery checks stacked day after day while Lan Xuanyu's seat stayed reserved.

The mistake would have been treating those two months as empty.

They were not empty. Heaven Luo Academy's environment pressed on Yan Shuo every hour. The food was better matched than anything his family could afford every day. The training rooms measured heat leakage, shoulder strain, breath rhythm, soul-power circulation, and mantle stability. His hidden foundation did not sleep through any of it. It chewed through soreness, hunger, loneliness, guilt, Jin Xiang's fire-bird pressure, Qian Lei's failed gates, Liu Feng's cutting speed, teachers' corrections, and the cold weight of the empty seat.

By the end of the second month, the routine class recheck no longer matched the old entrance record.

The soul-power device confirmed **Rank 18 / Level 18**.

The Spiritual Power cap rose to **72 points**.

Still Spirit Connection Realm. Still one ring. Still no second soul skill. But the old Rank 16 file had become a last entrance record, not the truth of his current body.

The instructors wrote the result without applause.

Yan Shuo liked that. Applause made growth feel finished.

One instructor added a separate internal note after watching Dawnfeather Mantle fold, catch, and return three rapid impacts without fraying: low-output Mantle and Dawnflame Form had reached reliable school-use proficiency, while high-output wing-frame and True combat Dawnflame Fusion remained supervised trump-card layers.

Yan Ke's private support record for Dawnflame Kite and the first ring moved again: **actual living age of 233 years, yellow tier**, with practical output higher than a normal same-age yellow-tier ring. The official paperwork still recorded the original one-hundred-year acquisition.

The original/legal acquisition paperwork stayed exactly one hundred years.

The living response no longer belonged to the same child who had entered the assessment two months earlier.

Yan Shuo learned Heaven Luo City's shape through weekend trips and school maps. White district for residences. Yellow for manufacturing. Purple for entertainment. Black for military control. Red for government. Orange for schools. The city spread larger than Zi Luo City in every direction, as if someone had decided a planet's ambition should be visible from the sky.

Lan Xuanyu's family moved into the red district after deciding he would attend.

Yan Shuo's family did not move so smoothly.

Yan Ke's work could not be folded into a new city with one signature. Lin Mei's teaching arrangements had their own knots. For now, Yan Shuo boarded full-time and returned when permitted, while his parents traveled when schedules allowed.

At first, he tried not to mind.

Then he minded and tried not to show it.

Lin Mei noticed anyway.

On the first weekend she visited Heaven Luo City, she inspected his shoulders, his food plan, his sleep record, and the small scorch mark he had hidden inside his spare training shirt.

"Careless," she said.

"It was inside the shirt. No one saw."

"I saw."

"You are not people. You are Mother."

"Correct. That is worse."

Yan Ke stood behind her with a packet of approved snacks and the expression of a man unwilling to enter a battlefield without orders.

Lin Mei pressed two fingers between Yan Shuo's shoulder blades. He hissed.

"Still sore?"

"Only when interrogated."

"Answer."

"A little."

"Then no high-output burst practice outside supervised class. Low-output form only. Let the mantle heal into the body instead of tearing the body to prove the mantle exists."

Yan Shuo sighed. "Teachers already said that."

"Good. Then you will hear it twice and disobey half as much."

Yan Ke held out the snack packet. "Your mother approved this after reading the ingredients. It contains enough protein to make the price offensive."

Yan Shuo accepted it with both hands. "I will honor its sacrifice."

Lin Mei's mouth twitched despite herself.

Later, when Yan Ke went to speak with a dorm administrator about weekend transport, Lin Mei stood beside Yan Shuo under a tree at the edge of the orange district.

"You are lonely," she said.

Yan Shuo stared at the training field beyond the fence. "I am busy."

"Busy is not an answer."

"It is an answer adults like."

"Not from you."

The leaves overhead trembled in the afternoon wind. Dawnflame Kite stirred softly, not flaring, only listening.

Yan Shuo scuffed one shoe against the path. "The class started because we were selected. Xuanyu was not there. Then he woke. Then he sang with a celebrity. Now he is coming. Everyone keeps making him a story before he arrives."

"And what is he to you?"

"My friend."

"Then remember that before you remember the story."

Yan Shuo lowered his head.

"Also," Lin Mei added, "if you try to solve his class problems for him before he asks, I will know."

He looked up in alarm. "How?"

"Mothers have better intelligence networks than schools."

"That is terrifying."

"Good."

So when Lan Xuanyu finally arrived at Heaven Luo Academy, Yan Shuo did not run across the campus and drag him away from Mu Zhongtian.

He saw them from the second-floor corridor: Mu Zhongtian walking carefully beside Lan Xuanyu, explaining the academy buildings with the patience of guilt. Lan Xuanyu wore the new purple-and-white uniform as if it still belonged to someone else. He looked healthy. A little thinner in the wrong places perhaps, a little too bright-eyed from curiosity and recovery, but alive.

Alive mattered more than uniform fit.

Yan Shuo gripped the corridor railing until his fingers hurt.

Dawnflame Kite pressed warm against his ribs.

Go?

Not yet.

Mu Zhongtian had brought Lan Xuanyu here. Dorm 333 was waiting. Qian Lei was probably already preparing an enthusiastic attack disguised as friendship. Liu Feng was probably preparing to judge the new arrival by rank, posture, and whether he got in the way.

Lan Xuanyu needed to enter his own door.

Yan Shuo turned away before Lan Xuanyu looked up.

He waited until lunch.

Waiting was terrible.

It was also, apparently, a skill.

The top-floor Elite Junior Class cafeteria smelled of meat broth, steamed grain, roasted vegetables, and the faint medicinal sweetness of rare ingredients. The first time Yan Shuo had entered, he had nearly forgiven the academy for every running drill. Two months later, the smell still made Dawnflame Kite perk up like a bird pretending not to beg.

By then, the class already knew the monitor's name.

Yan Shuo had not received the duty because he spoke prettily, because he was older, or because the teachers wanted a harmless symbol. The opening measurements, first combat drills, Fire Phoenix route, Dawnflame Form control, Rank 18 file, and Spirit Connection response had placed him at the front of the public comprehensive list. In an Elite Junior Class where strength decided too much, that was enough to put responsibility on his shoulders whether he wanted the attention or not.

Lu Qianxun's group still behaved like the hardest team block in the room. Golden Lion King, Celestial Dragon, and Chang Jianyi's judgment made them dangerous in a way no single badge erased.

Yan Shuo sat with his assigned team near the middle rows. Ye Lingtong sat farther ahead with Lu Qianxun's group. Jin Xiang lounged near them, golden hair catching the light whenever he moved his head. Among all the students in class, Jin Xiang was the one Yan Shuo had been measured against most often by gossip.

Both were bird-type.

Both carried fire.

Jin Xiang's Flaming Golden Bird had metal heat and sharp wing-strikes, with old Golden Crow ancestry in its story.

Yan Shuo's Fire Phoenix and Dawnflame Kite were a different route entirely.

The class enjoyed comparisons the way hungry dogs enjoyed bones.

Yan Shuo did not.

Comparisons made people careless.

They also made Jin Xiang smirk.

The cafeteria door opened.

Qian Lei entered first, talking before his feet fully crossed the threshold.

Beside him walked Lan Xuanyu.

For a heartbeat, the cafeteria noise became far away.

Yan Shuo's body rose halfway from the bench before he stopped it.

Not too much, he told himself.

Do not make him the coma story. Do not make him the concert story. Do not make him the empty seat.

Friend first.

Ye Lingtong moved before anyone else.

She walked over with a brightness in her eyes that looked almost painful. "Lan Xuanyu. You're better now?"

Lan Xuanyu nodded. "Much better."

"That's great." Her voice softened in a way the old Class A Ye Lingtong would have hated. "I really have to thank you for that day."

Lan Xuanyu shook his head. "It had nothing to do with you. The spirit beast was unique, and I'm fine." His eyes had already drifted toward the food counters. "I'm hungry. I'm going to eat first."

That was so exactly Lan Xuanyu that Yan Shuo had to bite the inside of his cheek.

Ye Lingtong froze for half a second, then let him pass.

Qian Lei whispered loudly, "You know Ye Lingtong?"

"We used to attend the same school," Lan Xuanyu said.

"You seem to know many dangerous people."

"She was from Class A."

"That explains everything."

Yan Shuo finally stood.

Lan Xuanyu turned at once.

For a moment neither of them spoke.

Then Yan Shuo said, "You are late."

Lan Xuanyu blinked.

Qian Lei's eyes widened, delighted by potential drama.

Lan Xuanyu considered the accusation seriously. "I was unconscious."

"Bad scheduling."

"I woke up."

"Acceptable correction."

Lan Xuanyu smiled.

It was small and tired and real.

Yan Shuo's chest stopped hurting in a place he had not known was still clenched.

"You look taller," Lan Xuanyu said.

"You look famous."

"Does that help in ranking?"

"Only if Master Le fights for you."

Lan Xuanyu tilted his head. "He sings."

"Then only if the opponent has ears."

Qian Lei stared between them. "You two always talk like this?"

"No," Lan Xuanyu said.

"Yes," Yan Shuo said at the same time.

Liu Feng, who had appeared behind Qian Lei without wasting a word, looked Yan Shuo up and down. "You are Yan Shuo. Rank 18. Fire Phoenix."

"That is my file's least embarrassing summary."

"He is not in our team," Qian Lei complained to Lan Xuanyu. "Do not become attached. Dorm 333 is tragic enough."

Lan Xuanyu looked confused.

Yan Shuo pointed toward the food. "Eat first. Qian Lei explains tragedy better with a full mouth and less accuracy."

"Hey!"

Lan Xuanyu went to the counters.

The cafeteria noticed him properly then.

Some students stared because he had arrived late. Some stared because he was the coma accident child. Some stared because of the Master Le clip. Some stared because even without understanding anything hidden, they could tell Lan Xuanyu did not feel like an ordinary Rank 14.

Yan Shuo sat back down.

His Dawnflame Kite pulsed once.

Not danger.

Change.

Lan Xuanyu returned with a tray piled high enough to make Qian Lei look reverent.

Then he went back for another.

And another.

By the fourth trip, Jin Xiang pushed away from his table.

Golden hair, sharp smile, irritated pride.

Yan Shuo's fingers tightened around his chopsticks.

Lin Mei's voice rose in memory.

If you try to solve his class problems for him before he asks, I will know.

Ye Lingtong stood again. "Jin Xiang, stop bullying him. Xuanyu was my schoolmate."

Lu Qianxun, tall and calm beside her, only said, "I don't know him."

Jin Xiang laughed and stepped into Lan Xuanyu's path.

"Hey," he said, blocking the tray. "The budget from the school is for everyone. You're eating the share of others by yourself."

Lan Xuanyu looked at him as if the sentence had been built from the wrong end.

"What is wrong with eating?" he asked.

Yan Shuo lowered his chopsticks.

He did not flare.

He did not stand.

Not yet.

The empty seat had waited two months.

Lan Xuanyu had finally arrived.

Now the class would learn what had been reserved.
