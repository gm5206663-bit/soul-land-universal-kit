# Chapter 12: The Forest That Answered

On Heaven Dou Planet, Nana stood before a class of children who did not know what she was.

That made teaching easier.

If adults watched her, their eyes always carried weight. Curiosity. Fear. Greed. Restraint. Questions hidden under polite faces. The children only saw a beautiful silver-haired teacher whose voice was soft and whose movements were impossible to copy even when she slowed them down.

"Ancient martial arts begins before force," Nana said.

She lifted one hand.

No soul power shone. No martial soul appeared. Her sleeve drifted, her wrist turned, and the air in front of her seemed to lose the right to resist.

The observing teachers at the side of the training hall went silent.

The children tried to imitate her.

Most of them made windmill arms.

One boy slapped his own ear and looked betrayed by his body.

Nana blinked once, then corrected his elbow.

"Not there. Here."

Her fingers were gentle.

The boy's arm moved again, this time without attacking himself.

Yun Yan stood at the back of the hall with two official observers. She had worried that Nana would refuse to cooperate. She had worried that Nana would break the observers. She had worried that the children would annoy her.

Instead, Nana taught.

Not warmly, exactly. Her warmth was distant, as if some part of her had remained on Heaven Luo Planet in a small apartment where a boy had cried for his teacher. But she was patient. She corrected stances. She stopped one child from falling before gravity understood its own plan. She answered questions simply when she could and ignored the ones that came from adults pretending to be students.

Then a little girl entered late.

She was nearly seven, with a cold, clean aura that did not belong to ordinary childhood. Her eyes were bright and calm. When she bowed, the movement carried a softness that made the air around her feel like winter water.

"Dong Qianqiu," the assistant teacher said quietly. "She transferred into this lesson group today."

Nana turned her head.

The girl looked at her.

For a breath, the training hall blurred at the edges.

Nana did not know why the child's aura felt familiar. Her memory remained a locked room with no door. But familiarity rose anyway, not as thought, but as an ache beneath the ribs.

She walked to Dong Qianqiu and crouched.

The girl did not step back.

Nana placed her hand lightly on the top of Dong Qianqiu's head.

Cold and depth answered her palm.

Yun Yan's heart climbed into her throat.

Nana only closed her eyes for a moment.

"I understand," she said softly.

Dong Qianqiu tilted her head. "Teacher?"

Nana opened her eyes.

"Your stance begins from the spine, not the feet. Watch."

The lesson continued.

Far away, on another route through the stars, Le Qingling checked a concert schedule for the fifth time and glared at the man beside her.

"Heaven Luo Planet," she said. "Three concerts. Interviews. No disappearing. No walking off because you hear a song no one else hears. No staring at clouds until the media thinks it is a spiritual crisis."

Tang Le wore a cap low over his blue hair. Even so, two flight attendants had already recognized him, one passenger had nearly fainted, and a little girl had asked whether Noble Le's eyelashes were insured.

"Do eyelashes need insurance?" Tang Le asked.

Le Qingling pressed two fingers to her brow. "Not the point."

He smiled faintly and looked through the spacecraft window.

Heaven Luo Planet hung ahead, blue-white and peaceful from orbit.

For no reason he understood, his chest tightened.

A melody slept somewhere inside him.

It turned once in its sleep.

In the simulation pod beneath Heaven Luo Academy's Zi Luo Branch gymnasium, Yan Shuo fell into a forest.

He did not drop from the sky. The world simply changed its mind.

One moment there was gel around his arms and a metal mask over his face. The next, damp earth pressed under his shoes, a root twisted near his left foot, and green light broke through leaves overhead in thin, watchful lines.

His body was already warm.

Dawnflame Form rested over him at its quietest level, so close to the skin that it did not look like armor unless the light caught the feathered edges. Copper-red warmth lay along his shoulders and forearms. Pale gold threads gathered at his wrists. Behind his back, the narrow wing-frame slept like a drawing waiting for breath.

The Phoenix seed from his first absorption had not gone anywhere. It sat in the Dawnflame Kite's soul-core, in the yellow ring that answered from his body, and in the feathered edge of Dawnfeather Mantle. The original acquisition paperwork still said one hundred years, but Yan Ke's private notes now recorded the bird and ring's actual living age as two hundred and three years, still yellow-tier. With perfect absorption, perfect compatibility, Fire Phoenix pressure, and the unnamed adaptation of his body, the result was stronger than a plain one-hundred-year ring should have been.

The Dawnflame Kite pulsed once.

Not danger.

Question.

Yan Shuo crouched behind a mossy stone and touched the soil with two fingers.

It felt real enough to make lying to himself useless.

"If fake mud ruins real shoes, I will complain," he whispered.

The forest did not answer.

That was suspicious. Forests were never truly silent. Even city park trees had insects, small birds, leaf ticks, and the rude confidence of things that did not pay rent. Here, the silence had layers. Insects existed, but they kept low. Birds existed, but not near him. Somewhere to the east, water ran. Somewhere closer, a branch had been broken recently and not by wind.

Yan Shuo breathed in.

Low-output form sharpened the world without making it brighter. Heat clung to paw marks in the soil. A scrape on bark held the memory of claws. The air had the sour-metal smell of predators and the green bitterness of crushed leaves.

His first thought was to climb.

His second thought was that every exam teacher in history probably put things in trees to punish children who thought climbing solved life.

The hidden shape inside him made no words. It never did. But his ankle shifted before he chose a direction, placing weight on a root that would not crack. His shoulder lowered out of the sightline between two bushes. Dawnflame Kite's pulse tucked closer to his spine.

Not east.

Not downwind.

Up and left.

Yan Shuo moved.

In the monitoring room, ten screens unfolded in a curve before Mu Zhongtian and the examiners.

The virtual forest was not a game built for entertainment. It was an assessment environment with pain feedback, fear pressure, spirit beast behavior models, and emergency extraction protocols. For children under ten, the parameters had been softened.

Softened did not mean safe.

Mu Zhongtian watched the candidates appear.

Two older students panicked immediately and released martial souls with too much noise. One chose a tree and climbed exactly the wrong one. Ye Lingtong landed in a patch of low shrubs, recovered quickly, and moved with the confidence of a child whose soul power had always answered loudly. Lan Xuanyu appeared by a stream and did not move at once.

Mu Zhongtian leaned forward.

"Good," he murmured.

Another examiner pointed to a different screen. "Yan Shuo has entered form already."

On the display, the Fire Phoenix boy moved through the dry ridge with a feathered glow held so tightly it barely disturbed the leaves.

"Low output," Mu Zhongtian said. "Not flaring. Smart. Fire-type children often forget they are in a forest."

"His old acquisition file says the first soul spirit began as a legal hundred-year Dawnflame Kite," the examiner said, reading. "This basic assessment did not include a soul-spirit or soul-ring age retest."

"Then do not treat paperwork as the ceiling," Mu Zhongtian said. "Watch the way the soul spirit and ring answer him."

He glanced at the screen again.

A child his age with Rank 16, Spiritual Power 61, and a first soul spirit that moved with him far beyond an ordinary new yellow ring.

That was not ordinary.

But another screen held Lan Xuanyu standing calm beside water after producing Spiritual Power 99.

The examiner smiled despite himself. "This Zi Luo Branch sent us trouble."

"Good trouble," Mu Zhongtian said.

By the stream, Lan Xuanyu listened to water.

He did not know where the others had been placed. He did not know whether Yan Shuo was nearby, whether Ye Lingtong had already started a fight, or whether the older students were doing something clever. Teacher Nana was not here. Daddy was not here to explain every beast footprint and plant smell.

But Daddy had taught him many things.

Water was useful.

Plants were useful.

Panic was not.

Lan Xuanyu crouched near the stream and let his silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass stay quiet beneath his sleeve. He touched the water lightly. Coolness answered him. The plants along the bank were small, but the world around him felt friendlier with water nearby.

A heavy rustle came from the bushes.

Lan Xuanyu stilled.

A massive head pushed through leaves.

The creature was huge, round-bodied, thick-skinned, with a mouth that looked large enough to swallow a school desk. Its small eyes blinked slowly. Its ears twitched. Water dripped from its chin as it huffed.

A Hippo-type spirit beast.

Lan Xuanyu searched his memory.

Daddy had said many large herbivore-type beasts looked frightening but were not aggressive unless provoked. But if angered, their bite and weight could crush predators. Their temper was like deep water: quiet until something foolish fell in.

He did not attack.

He did not run.

He lowered his presence and let the water move around his fingers.

The Hippo snorted.

Lan Xuanyu held his breath.

In another part of the forest, Yan Shuo stopped because the bushes ahead blinked.

Leaves should not blink.

His left shoulder warmed before the first Ironclaw Lynx leapt.

It came from a low branch, grey-black body stretched flat, front claws shining dull iron. The strike aimed for his throat with the confidence of a beast that had already decided he was small.

Yan Shuo stepped in instead of back.

Dawnfeather Mantle thickened over his forearm. Not a flat shield. Feathers layered, angled, and caught the claws along their sides. The impact ran from wrist to shoulder, down spine, into the foot he had placed on the root a breath earlier.

His body accepted the force.

The root cracked.

Yan Shuo turned with the crack.

The lynx flew past him instead of through him, slammed shoulder-first into the mossy stone, and hissed.

Two more came from the brush.

"Three," Yan Shuo counted. "Bad manners."

A fourth pair of eyes opened behind a fern.

"Four. Worse manners."

Dawnflame Kite pulsed, sharper.

Left low.

Yan Shuo jumped before the fourth lynx lunged at his ankle. The narrow wing-frame brightened, not enough to fly, enough to turn a clumsy jump into a pounce. He twisted over the beast's back, landed on a slanted trunk, and pushed off again as claws ripped bark where his leg had been.

Heat gathered at his wrists.

Do not burn the forest.

He shaped the heat thin.

Three pale-gold feather-lines snapped into the leaf litter in front of the lynxes. They did not explode. They hissed, flashed, and left a waist-high shimmer of hot air and sharp light. The lead lynx recoiled as its whiskers curled.

The pack spread out.

Predators did not like prey that changed shape.

Yan Shuo grinned.

"Good. Now we negotiate. You leave. I keep my face. Everyone wins."

The lead lynx did not appreciate diplomacy.

It lunged again.

Yan Shuo met it with a shoulder turn. Dawnfeather Mantle folded, caught, and released. Copper-red feathers brushed the lynx's side, not deep enough to cripple, hot enough to teach. The beast yelped and hit the ground rolling.

Another attacked from behind.

The hidden shape inside Yan Shuo moved his back before fear reached his head. Wing-root muscles pulled. The narrow frame snapped bright for half a breath. He dropped flat, felt claws skim heat instead of flesh, then kicked backward into the lynx's chest.

It tumbled into the feather-line barrier.

Two ran.

The burned-whisker leader hesitated.

Yan Shuo let the mantle rise along his shoulders. Fire Phoenix pressure, immature but real, opened like a hot eye behind him.

The lynx fled.

Yan Shuo exhaled.

His form settled back to quiet.

No wildfire.

No injury.

One scraped elbow because bark had opinions.

He looked at the broken root beneath his shoe and frowned. "Thank you for your service."

In the monitoring room, an examiner laughed once before remembering to be dignified.

Mu Zhongtian did not laugh. He watched the replay of the first block.

"That mantle transferred force through the body," he said. "Not pure defense. Movement correction too."

"His first ring is listed as one hundred years."

"The legal age is one hundred," Mu Zhongtian replied. "The response is not. Keep recording."

On Lan Xuanyu's screen, the Hippo had begun to wander toward the water.

Three shadows approached behind him.

The Nether Wolves came low and silent.

Lan Xuanyu did not see them first. He heard the difference in grass. A normal step bent grass down. The wolves' paws brushed sideways, soft and practiced.

Three hundred-year Nether Wolves.

Fast. Cunning. Good at teamwork. Not the strongest hundred-year beasts, but dangerous when they circled prey.

Lan Xuanyu moved before the first pounce.

Ice formed in his palm and shot toward the wolf's nose. The beast twisted away, but the splash of cold struck its eye. Lan Xuanyu rolled toward the stream. Water rose in a thin arc behind him and froze into beads that scattered like stones.

The wolves split.

He could not overpower them.

He did not try.

Footwork carried him along the bank. He used small ice balls, water splashes, low shrubs, and the slope of mud to make the wolves adjust again and again. Each time they nearly caught him, he changed the angle by half a step.

The Hippo snorted, annoyed by noise near its water.

Lan Xuanyu's eyes brightened.

He did not attack the Hippo.

He let the wolves do the offending.

One Nether Wolf lunged. Lan Xuanyu slid aside. The wolf's claws scraped mud and splashed water across the Hippo's face.

The massive beast opened its mouth.

The monitoring room went very quiet.

The Hippo's roar shook leaves from branches.

The Nether Wolves froze.

Lan Xuanyu froze too, but only for a breath. Then he sent another ice ball behind the wolves, not at the Hippo, not enough to hurt, only enough to make the pack think danger had surrounded them.

The Hippo charged two steps.

Three Nether Wolves decided survival was wiser than lunch.

They fled.

Lan Xuanyu stood by the stream, wet, muddy, breathing hard, and still alive.

Mu Zhongtian slapped the arm of his chair.

"Outstanding."

He did not mean soul power. He did not mean brute strength. He meant judgment.

"This child," he said, eyes fixed on Lan Xuanyu's screen, "I want him."

An examiner nodded, then glanced sideways. "Yan Shuo's screen has a new contact."

Yan Shuo heard the human shout before the beast.

That was usually a bad order.

He moved toward the sound, low-output Dawnflame Form hugging his body. The forest dipped into a ravine where roots held the soil like old fingers. A third-grade candidate clung to one of those roots, face pale, while something enormous slammed its head into the slope beneath him.

Stone plates covered the beast's shoulders and back. Its tusks curved upward. Mud and broken leaves streaked its hide. Every time it charged, the ravine wall trembled.

Thousand-year Stonehide Boar.

Yan Shuo's mouth went dry.

"That is not a pig," he whispered. "That is a moving wall with anger."

The Dawnflame Kite pulsed hard.

Too heavy.

Yes, thank you.

The older candidate saw him. "Help!"

The boar saw him too.

Its small eyes shifted.

Yan Shuo immediately regretted being visible.

The Stonehide Boar turned and charged.

Direct collision was stupidity wearing heroic clothes. Yan Shuo's body knew that before pride tried to argue. He stepped backward, then sideways, then up the root web. The boar hit the slope below him and shattered the place where he had been.

Stone fragments flew.

Dawnfeather Mantle layered across his arms. Pebbles struck, hissed, and fell away.

The older candidate screamed as his root cracked.

Yan Shuo had two choices.

Run and score survival.

Stay and become educational paste.

Chapter 10's falling panel flashed through him. Yan Ke's hand on his shoulder. Civilians behind him. A friend elsewhere he could not reach.

Protect what is beside you first.

Yan Shuo inhaled.

Dawnflame Form rose from quiet low-output rhythm into **True combat Dawnflame Fusion**.

This was not only a better stance and not merely a louder version of the casual form. For the first breath of true fusion, Yan's Fire Phoenix, the Dawnflame Kite's fire-and-light instinct, the grown yellow ring, Dawnfeather Mantle, soul power, Spiritual Power, lungs, spine, and wing-root muscles all pulled into one current. Yan Shuo did not become a two-ring Soul Master. He did not gain a second skill. But in a short burst, the fused whole could touch the combat line of an ordinary two-ring Soul Master in speed, pressure, defense, and pounce force.

Only touch it.

The price arrived before the power finished forming.

The narrow wing-frame brightened. Copper-red fire ran along feather edges, pale gold light threading between them. The world sharpened into angles: boar charge line, cracked root, older candidate's grip, loose stone overhang, wind from the ravine mouth.

"When I say jump," Yan Shuo shouted, "jump left!"

"Left where?"

"The left that is not tusks!"

The boar charged again.

Yan Shuo dropped directly into its path.

For one heartbeat, every observer in the monitoring room leaned forward.

Then Yan Shuo burst upward.

Not flight.

A high-output wing-frame burst inside True combat Dawnflame Fusion.

His body shot above the tusks by a child's height. Dawnfeather Mantle wrapped his legs and shoulder as he kicked off the boar's stone-plated forehead. The force nearly tore his balance apart. Heat screamed along his spine. Dawnflame Kite clamped into rhythm with him, fierce and bright.

The kick did not hurt the boar.

It changed the angle.

The Stonehide Boar smashed into the ravine wall just to the right of the older candidate.

"Jump!"

The third-grader jumped left.

Yan Shuo caught his sleeve with both hands, mantle feathers gripping cloth without burning it. The added weight dragged him down. His heels struck a slanted root. Pain shot through both legs.

The hidden foundation caught the pain, broke it into placement, and moved his knee before it buckled.

They landed in a roll behind a fallen trunk.

The boar shook its head.

The ravine wall groaned.

Loose stones above it shifted.

Yan Shuo looked up.

A bad idea appeared.

He liked it immediately, which meant it needed supervision.

No supervision existed.

"Can you run?" he asked.

The older candidate nodded too quickly.

"Then run when I count. One."

"Only one?"

"Two is for people with plans. Go!"

The older candidate ran.

The Stonehide Boar charged after the moving target.

Yan Shuo flung three feather-lines at the slope above the beast. Not fireballs. Thin, cutting, heated arcs of pale-gold light wrapped in copper heat. They struck cracked stone and root knots already loosened by the boar's impact.

The slope collapsed.

Not enough to bury a thousand-year beast.

Enough to blind, slow, and insult it.

Dust and stones poured across the boar's head. It roared and skidded. Yan Shuo's wing-frame flashed once more, shorter this time, dragging his body sideways as a tusk clipped the air where his stomach had been.

His soul power dipped sharply.

Low-output form could last.

High-output bursts were thieves.

He landed badly, rolled, and came up with one hand against a tree. The bark smoked beneath his palm.

Control.

Not wildfire.

The Dawnflame Kite pressed hot urgency into his chest.

Leave.

Yan Shuo agreed with the bird. Agreeing with birds was new personal growth.

He drew the boar's attention with one bright flare, then vanished behind a curtain of heat-haze feathers and dust. The beast charged through the shimmer and hit empty forest.

By the time it recovered, Yan Shuo and the older candidate were already crawling through a root tunnel toward higher ground.

The third-grader panted, "You attacked a thousand-year beast."

"I annoyed a thousand-year beast," Yan Shuo corrected. "Important difference. Attacking suggests confidence. I had geometry."

"Geometry?"

"Angles are cheaper than funerals."

In the monitoring room, one examiner whispered, "He did not defeat it."

Mu Zhongtian's mouth curved. "He was not supposed to. He knew that."

On another screen, Ye Lingtong was running.

Her pride had survived the first part of the simulation. Her temper had not.

The small white monkey had appeared too close, too quick, too mocking. It had perched on a branch and chattered at her after she missed it once. Then it had snatched a leaf from her shoulder like a prize.

Ye Lingtong grabbed it.

For half a breath, she felt victory.

Then the forest behind her stood up.

The Three-Eyed Witch Ape crashed through trees with both arms swinging. Its third eye burned on its forehead. Every step carried thousand-year pressure, and every roar scraped at the inside of Ye Lingtong's skull.

She released the monkey at once.

The ape did not accept apologies.

Ye Lingtong ran.

Branches tore at her sleeves. Her Celestial Body martial soul flared and strengthened her legs, but fear made her breathing ragged. She was Rank 16. She was not weak. She was Ye Feng's daughter. She was supposed to be better than this.

The ape's shadow swallowed the ground behind her.

A figure appeared ahead near the stream.

Lan Xuanyu.

"Run!" Ye Lingtong shouted.

He turned.

The ape came through the trees like a collapsing wall.

Lan Xuanyu moved before thought finished.

Blue Silver Grass flickered. Ice formed. Water answered. He pulled Ye Lingtong aside as the ape's arm smashed into the earth where she had been. Mud exploded. A broken branch struck his shoulder hard enough to numb his arm.

Ye Lingtong stumbled.

"Why did you come this way?" Lan Xuanyu asked, breath quick.

"I didn't choose the ape!"

"Then why is it following you?"

"It misunderstood something!"

The little white monkey shrieked from a distant branch as if disagreeing with her legal defense.

They ran together.

Lan Xuanyu was slower in straight speed, but calmer. He used water to slick roots behind them, ice to force the ape to step wider, vines and shrubs to hide turns. Ye Lingtong's stronger body kept them ahead when the path opened. For a short time, they survived because neither method was enough alone.

Then the ape's third eye opened fully.

A pressure swept forward.

Ye Lingtong cried out and stumbled.

Lan Xuanyu caught her, shoved her behind a tree, and saw the ape raise one long arm.

Teacher Nana's voice sounded in memory.

A man should protect the lady.

He did not have time to decide whether the sentence was fair, old-fashioned, or troublesome.

He only had time to act.

Lan Xuanyu turned and threw an ice spike at the ape's third eye.

The spike shattered before it struck.

The ape's attention locked onto him.

Good.

Bad.

Both.

"Go!" Lan Xuanyu shouted.

Ye Lingtong froze.

"Go!"

The Three-Eyed Witch Ape screamed.

Mental force struck Lan Xuanyu like a hammer made of darkness.

In the distance, crawling out of the root tunnel, Yan Shuo staggered.

He was nowhere near the stream. He saw no ape. He saw no Lan Xuanyu. He saw only the forest light bend strangely for a breath and felt his Fire Phoenix center flare as if something cold and sharp had touched the edge of his mind.

Dawnflame Kite slammed hot wings against his ribs.

Danger.

Not yours.

Friend.

Yan Shuo's head snapped toward the far roar.

Lan Xuanyu?

His body tried to move.

His knees nearly folded. Two high-output bursts had taken more than pride wanted to admit. The older candidate he had pulled from the ravine clutched a bleeding arm beside him, eyes wide.

The forest gave Yan Shuo the same cruel lesson twice.

Something terrible might be happening elsewhere.

Someone helpless was beside him.

He hated the answer before choosing it.

"Stay behind me," he told the older candidate.

Dawnflame Form settled around him, low and steady, not bright enough to run toward the roar, strong enough to keep the wounded boy from being easy prey.

A silver-white tremor flashed far away through the trees.

Yan Shuo saw it only as a blur, a feeling, a piece of sky through leaves that made his mantle bristle.

Then the whole simulation shuddered.

In the monitoring room, alarms erupted.

Lan Xuanyu's screen went white at the edges. His small body collapsed, blood running from eyes, nose, ears, and mouth. The Three-Eyed Witch Ape recoiled from him, not from the ice spike, not from any visible attack the examiners could explain, but from something that flickered silver and ancient around his left side for less than a blink.

"Disconnect him!" Mu Zhongtian shouted.

Technicians moved.

Emergency extraction protocols lit red.

Ye Lingtong's screen showed her screaming his name.

Yan Shuo's screen showed him turning toward a distant tremor with fire rising around his shoulders.

"Stop the assessment," Mu Zhongtian ordered. "All pods, immediate extraction! Medical team now!"

The forest vanished.

Yan Shuo came out fighting the lid.

The transparent cover lifted, but not fast enough for his body. Dawnflame Form flared along his shoulders before he remembered he was in the gymnasium and not under trees. Heat splashed against the inside of the pod and died because the safety array suppressed it.

"Student Yan Shuo, calm down!"

"Where is Lan Xuanyu?"

The staff member looked past him.

That was answer enough to make fear climb his throat.

Yan Shuo tore the breathing mask away and sat up. His legs shook. His soul power felt chewed thin at the edges, but he could stand. Across the room, Ye Lingtong was being lifted from her pod. Her face was white. She kept saying Lan Xuanyu's name as if saying it could pull him back.

Lan Xuanyu's pod was surrounded by adults.

Too many adults.

Nan Cheng and Lan Xiao were not there yet, but teachers were already running, shouting for the emergency medical route. Mu Zhongtian carried Lan Xuanyu himself, face grim in a way that made every child in the room understand that exam rules had ended.

Yan Shuo slid from his pod and nearly fell.

Gong Yinghao caught him by the shoulder.

The teacher had rushed back in at the alarm. His grip was too tight, but Yan Shuo did not complain.

"Teacher Gong," Yan Shuo said, voice small despite the fire still trembling over his back, "what happened?"

Gong Yinghao did not lie quickly enough.

"Lan Xuanyu was injured. The medical team is taking him."

"Injured how?"

"We don't know yet."

Ye Lingtong heard that and made a sound like something breaking without pieces.

Yan Shuo looked at her.

The forest had ended.

The fear had not.

At the hospital, adults took over the world.

Children sat in chairs too large for them. Parents arrived with hurried steps. Teachers spoke in low voices. Doctors crossed corridors with expressions trained not to reveal what they knew until they knew enough.

Lan Xiao and Nan Cheng reached the emergency floor together.

Nan Cheng's face had no color. Lan Xiao held her hand so tightly that both their fingers had gone white.

"Where is my son?" Nan Cheng asked.

No one answered fast enough.

Mu Zhongtian stepped forward, then bowed.

"I'm sorry. The injury happened during the simulation assessment. He suffered a severe mental attack. The doctors are treating him."

Nan Cheng swayed.

Lan Xiao caught her.

"A simulation assessment?" Lan Xiao's voice was too calm. "How can a simulation assessment give a child severe mental trauma?"

Mu Zhongtian had no defense that mattered.

Inside the treatment room, Lan Xuanyu lay silent.

The doctor later explained what could be explained. Brain trauma was severe. The mental shock should have produced a predictable collapse, yet his physical condition and brain waves were strange, active in ways that did not match ordinary injury. He might wake. He might not. The word vegetative entered the corridor like winter.

Nan Cheng covered her mouth.

Lan Xiao closed his eyes for one breath.

When he opened them, he looked older.

Yan Shuo sat two corridors away with Yan Ke and Lin Mei.

The academy had not wanted the other children near the treatment room. Parents had agreed because adults agreed to many things when fear needed chairs and doors. But the hospital was not large enough to keep grief from traveling.

Yan Shuo heard Nan Cheng cry once.

He bent forward until his forehead nearly touched his knees.

Dawnflame Kite pulsed softly.

Not danger.

Pain.

Yan Shuo pressed a fist over his chest.

"I was in the forest too," he whispered.

Yan Ke sat beside him. "You were in a different place."

"I heard something. I felt something. I couldn't go."

Lin Mei's hand settled on his back, exactly where the wing-frame had burned brightest in the simulation.

"You had someone beside you."

"I know."

The words tasted like ash.

He knew. That was the problem. Knowing did not make it hurt less.

Chapter 10 had taught him to protect what stood beside him first.

Chapter 12 taught him that the lesson did not become easier after passing once.

Down the corridor, Ye Lingtong stood with her father.

Ye Feng had arrived in civilian clothes, but no cloth could hide the soldier in his posture. His hand rested on his daughter's shoulder. Ye Lingtong stared at the floor.

"It was my fault," she said.

Ye Feng's jaw tightened. "Tell me what happened."

"I grabbed a monkey. It brought the ape. Lan Xuanyu saved me. Then he made it chase him instead. Then..."

She stopped.

Ye Feng did not comfort her immediately. Perhaps he did not know how. Perhaps he knew too well that some guilt could not be pushed aside by a father's sentence.

Yan Shuo saw them from his chair.

Ye Lingtong looked smaller than she ever had in class.

He wanted to say something. A joke, maybe. A bad one. Something about monkeys being illegal exam assistants.

Nothing came.

The academy's decision arrived before Lan Xuanyu woke.

Mu Zhongtian accepted punishment. The accident had happened under his assessment. A thousand-year mental-type beast model had overwhelmed a child. No praise, no intention, no previous score erased that responsibility.

The Elite Junior Class would still open.

Ye Lingtong was selected.

Yan Shuo was selected.

Lan Xuanyu's place would be held permanently. If he woke, he would enter.

When Gong Yinghao told the Yan family, Yan Shuo did not feel victorious.

"Selected," he repeated.

"Yes," Gong Yinghao said. "Your simulation score was excellent. You controlled fire in a dry zone, survived hundred-year predators, protected another candidate, handled a thousand-year threat without prideful direct combat, and maintained Dawnflame Form cleanly. The academy wants you."

Yan Shuo looked toward the corridor where Lan Xuanyu's parents had disappeared.

"And Xuanyu?"

"His place is reserved."

"Reserved is not awake."

Gong Yinghao's expression softened.

"No," he said. "It is not."

Yan Shuo's hands tightened on his knees.

The Dawnflame Kite did not flare. It tucked close instead, a small hot weight against a larger cold one.

"Then I will enter," Yan Shuo said finally, "but I am not counting it as winning."

Yan Ke's eyes reddened.

Lin Mei squeezed his shoulder once.

"Good," she said. "Some things should not be counted that way."

Night came over Zi Luo City.

Hospital lights burned white against the windows. News screens elsewhere spoke of concerts, academy openings, military investigations, and traffic notices. Life continued with the terrible confidence of things that did not know a child lay silent in a room upstairs.

At the spaceport, Tang Le stepped onto Heaven Luo Planet.

Fans waited behind barriers. Cameras flashed. Le Qingling moved like a general entering battle, issuing reminders to security, assistants, and one famous singer who had already stopped walking.

"Tang Le," she hissed. "No disappearing."

He looked toward the city.

He could not see the hospital from there.

He did not know Lan Xuanyu's name.

He did not know why a faint pain had woken under his song.

But the melody inside him turned again, clearer this time, like someone calling through water.

Tang Le placed one hand over his chest.

"I won't disappear," he said.

Le Qingling relaxed for half a breath.

Then he added, "Not yet."

She nearly screamed.

Far away, in a hospital room no singer had entered, Lan Xuanyu slept.

Outside that room, Yan Shuo sat between his parents with Dawnflame Form gone from sight and Dawnflame Kite warm against his soul.

The forest had answered all of them.

None of the answers felt finished.
