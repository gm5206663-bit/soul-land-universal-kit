## Part 1: The Long Afternoon

The same doubt had been sitting in all of them. Points for surviving — but two hours without a peep. You could *forage* in a forest. How was this a trial?

"Should we go exploring?" Xie Xie asked.

Wulin read the sky before answering. "Not yet. It's early afternoon. We'll wait for nightfall — it'll be safer if Xiaoyan can use her Starwheel Ice Staff."

Impatience was enemy number one, and the captain did not feed enemies. Safe here meant whole later: everyone at their best condition before the forest was spent. So they waited out the sun, and the sun took its time.

The fifth spent the afternoon the way he spent every waiting period — on maintenance. Frost Abyss lay across his knees for an hour, the cloth off, the steel getting its oil; and the steel, under his hands, was *strange* today. Restless. The sword had been poured in a forge that mattered, and it had opinions about pressure the way bells have opinions about thunder — a fine continuous trembling in the metal that was not his hands. He sheathed it when the light went amber, and the trembling did not entirely stop.

*You feel it too,* he thought. *Noted.*

His brother watched him do all of this from the other side of the camp, the way Wulin always watched him do everything — completely, and without questions — and at some point had simply moved his rest-place six meters closer, so that when the forest finally spoke, the first hand in reach would be where it always was.

## Part 2: The Howl

The sun went down beyond the horizon, and in the twilight Wulin climbed a tree to look. The world ran green and endless to every edge — trees as far as the eye could see, the way the Great Star Dou Forest must have looked before maps.

The howl arrived without escort.

It did not echo so much as *flip the forest upside down* — every leaf between here and the horizon shivering once, in order, like a page being turned. Wulin's body broke out in goosebumps and his mind went white at the edges, and it was only his blood essence flaring up in his defense that dispersed the terror at all.

He climbed down madly, one question hammering: *if a single howl can do that, what is the thing that made it?* The strongest soul beast he had ever encountered was the Three-eyed Demon Ape whose spirit energy he carried — and he would never forget his teacher fighting the ten-thousand-year one. That had been the ceiling of his nightmares for two years.

This howl had walked through that ceiling without slowing down.

At the bottom, his companions wore matching horror. "W-what was that sound?" Xie Xie was pale, aghast, whispering. "That scared me to death." Even Gu Yue was restrained in the specific way that meant she was doing it on purpose.

"I don't know either," Wulin said, grim. "But it doesn't seem too far from here."

A second roar thundered before the sentence finished — sharper, a blade of a sound, and it *penetrated* their bodies and, impossibly, **dispelled the fear**, scouring it out like frost under hot water. The class looked at each other with clean, cold nerves and no idea whatsoever what to do with them.

*Two voices,* the fifth thought, from the seam. *One installs the fear. One removes it.* Only two things at the very top of a forest argue like that.

"Let's go take a look," Gu Yue said.

Wulin nodded — the logic was already load-bearing in him: a trial would not be as simple as it looked; and if they didn't look at *it*, it might come looking at *them*. Initiative was the only discount available. "Let's go."

## Part 3: The Crawling

Wind birds went ahead; Wulin led, opening the path, dragging his blood essence aura over their scent like a blanket — since they were not strong enough to be invisible, they would at least be confusing. Twenty minutes in, Gu Yue's face twitched.

"There's a valley up ahead on the left. It's over there." A beat. "My wind birds were dispersed by a gust of wind."

Nobody said what that meant about the gust. The silence said it for them.

"Everyone, be careful." Wulin's voice had gone quiet the way it did. "Follow closely behind me."

Golden scales rippled from his arm to his shoulder; the golden ring lit beneath him; the blood essence banked their nerves like a hearth. The closer they came to the valley, the more the air pressed — a weight with direction, waves of power rolling out of the valley's throat, and the five of them circulating soul power just to keep their spines straight under it.

"We're crawling from here onward." Wulin dropped to his belly, and the pressure instantly lessened — a fact they filed without discussion.

"Crawling," Xie Xie whispered, flat as a lizard. "At Shrek. On our bellies."

"It counts as a trial," Xiaoyan breathed. "It's in the brochure."

Five examinees inched to the rim of the valley and looked over it, and five breaths went in and did not soon come out. Five examinees inched to the rim of the valley and looked over it, and five breaths went in and did not soon come out.

Below, booms rolled like weather. **Two lights, each more than thirty meters across, struck each other countless times** — the earth jumping, the air whipped to froth, boulders the size of bedrooms riding the shockwaves into the walls.

Purple light rose in Wulin's eyes, and the lights resolved into shapes.

The first was dark-gold, immense, and *intimately* familiar — the ruler of the forest, the **Duskgold Dreadclaw Bear** — except the ones they had met before would have come up to this one's *ankle*. This was a colossus over ten meters tall, dark-gold light pouring down arms like bridge pylons, each slash tearing gouges into the valley walls with the casualness of a man parting curtains. The taxonomy climbed the spine uninvited: a hundred-year one had made a pride of Blazing Demon Lions beg; a thousand-year one was the absolute tyrant of any forest; a ten-thousand-year one could tear apart *ordinary hundred-thousand-year* soul beasts.

This one was beyond the taxonomy. And it was the *defender* look of the two — the cornered, furious party — because the thing it was fighting was not losing.

The second beast stood inside a ball of blinding gold: a body translucent as crystal, a lion's general plan with **flaming draconic claws** and a longer snout, fine golden scales showing through the fur like mail under a cape. And on its forehead, a vertical slit, and in the slit — **a third eye**, glaring out a bewitching red glow over the whole valley.

Wulin described it in a whisper, because knowledge is the class's currency. All eyes went to Xie Xie, who knew the most.

"From what you've said—" His brows knotted; his mouth went dry. "I think I might know. Give me a moment to think."

"Take your time," the fifth murmured, which for once was not irony.

## Part 4: The Read at the Rim

While Xie Xie thought, the fifth lay flat at the rim and let the oldest thing he owned do its work.

The valley's pressure had a grain like everything else, and the grain said *boundary*. Two apex territories had collided here — that was the evacuated forest's answer, delivered in full: not one owner but two, and everything with legs had left days ago. His body was reading the two roars even now, cataloguing them the way it catalogued forge-heat — *that one shakes the animal; that one unmakes the shaking* — and pricing, quietly, the difference between surviving a forest and surviving *between* these two.

Against his spine, Frost Abyss had gone still in the way that steel goes still — the total attention of a drawn thing not yet drawn. Whatever was in that valley, the sword had finished its arithmetic about it before he had, and its answer was a silence he chose to respect. The Intent stayed sheathed. Some pressures you did not answer with edges; you answered by being exactly where you were, flat, patient, uninteresting.

His brother's shoulder was touching his. That was all — no one watching would have called it anything — but it was the same grammar as the hand that had pulled him out of the wall at Eastsea, first in, because it always was, and the fifth let it carry its share of the weight.

Below them, the bear roared, and the crystal lion's third eye burned redder, and two kings threw themselves together again with a sound like the closing of a very large book.

"Some trial," Xie Xie breathed, barely audible, still thinking. "The highest level. Even adults find *this* hard."

"We're not fighting them," Wulin said quietly — captain's voice, arithmetic under it. "We're surviving near them. There's a difference, and our points are in the difference."

"And the exam has a way through," Gu Yue said. "It always has one."

At the rim, flat as five commas in the valley's great sentence, class zero held its ground in six-centimeter increments and waited for Xie Xie's memory to finish loading.
