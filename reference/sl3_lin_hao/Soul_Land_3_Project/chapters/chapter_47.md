## Part 1: What Wu Zhangkong Explained

He explained it in the room above the forge, with the window open, and he explained it slowly, which was how Lin Hao knew it mattered.

"There are **unnatural existences,**" Wu Zhangkong said.

Xie Xie said, "That's the most frightening sentence you have ever opened with, and you have opened with some."

"Be quiet. Some large clans and sects use **heavenly treasures or unique methods to stimulate their disciples' bodies.** With a stronger body, they can contain more powerful soul rings and spirit souls." He said it without approval, which was not the same as disapproval, and Lin Hao noticed the difference. "This isn't done normally. Only in rare cases, where a disciple far excels in one aspect, is such a method even considered."

He paused.

"**This is the key point that allows a person to possess a pair of thousand-year soul rings and a thousand-year spirit soul.**"

Lin Hao stopped breathing.

Not dramatically. He simply stopped, for about two seconds, because he had three rings and two of them were purple and he had been carrying the fact for six days without knowing what it *was*, and a man in white had just said the words that described it exactly.

*A pair of thousand-year soul rings and a thousand-year spirit soul.*

That was him. That was precisely, exactly, numerically him.

And then the second thing arrived, about four seconds later, the way the second thing always did.

**A pair.** He had **three.**

He had been doing the arithmetic wrong for six days, and he understood now that he had been doing it wrong because there was no version of the sum in which he came out sensible. So he did it the only other way there was, which was against people, and he had been in this city long enough to have the people.

Mu Xi. Three rings, **yellow, yellow, purple** — and Mu Xi was fourteen, and Mu Xi was a fourth-rank Grandmaster, and Mu Xi was the person the Association held up to its apprentices as what a serious child looked like. The captain of Skysea Academy's secondary team: **three yellow rings**, and grown. The youth at the platform gate, seventeen or eighteen, **two yellow and one purple**, and the plate had called him a Soul Elder like it was an achievement. The Shrek teacher who had come to Eastsea to recruit: **twenty-three years old and a four-ring Soul Ancestor**, and Ye Yingluo had said her name in the voice you use for weather.

Every single one of them was older than him. Every single one of them had worse rings than him. And the rule Wu Zhangkong had just read out — the rule about clans spending fortunes on heavenly treasures to force a body to hold a *pair* — described a thing that Lin Hao had done **three times, at ten, for free, because his mother was a seamstress and his father made horseshoes.**

"That isn't right," Lin Hao said.

"What isn't."

"What you just said. It's right and it isn't right." He put his hand flat on the table, because he had noticed that he wanted to gesture and that gesturing was what Xie Xie did. "You said a pair. You said the key point allows a person to possess a **pair.**"

Wu Zhangkong looked at him.

"I have three."

And Wu Zhangkong — who had said *unnatural existences* without flinching, who had described clans doing things to children that Lin Hao had not fully understood and had not enjoyed not fully understanding — did the thing that Lin Hao would remember for longer than any of it, which was that he closed his eyes for about a second and a half, and when he opened them he did not look annoyed.

He looked like a man who has been handed a problem that is not his problem and has decided, in the space of a second and a half, to make it his.

"Yes," Wu Zhangkong said. "I know. That is why I said *pair* rather than looking at you while I said it."

## Part 2: What Gu Yue Cost

"Take Gu Yue," Wu Zhangkong said.

Gu Yue did not look up.

"With her spiritual power, all she needs is to strengthen her body, and then she could upgrade her spirit soul the way Wulin did." He said it flatly. "Unfortunately, with two soul rings, it would now be too difficult for her to strengthen her body to the level required."

Nobody said anything.

"**It is a pity,**" Wu Zhangkong said. "If her situation were better, I would help her regardless of whether she joined the Tang Sect."

Lin Hao looked at Gu Yue.

She was looking at her hands. Her face was her face, which meant it was not telling him anything, and he had known her long enough now to understand that her face not telling him anything was itself information — because Gu Yue's face told him things constantly, in the small way that a person who does not emote tells you things.

She had known.

She had known for some time, and she had not said, and she was not going to say, and the reason she had not said was sitting in the room with them and had just been described by a man who did not know he was describing her grief.

Lin Hao looked back at the window.

He did not say anything clever. That was twice in two days now, and he was beginning to suspect that not saying anything clever was itself a skill, and that he was learning it late.

## Part 3: The Category

"Wulin's advantage is different," Wu Zhangkong said. "His soul power has grown **inversely proportional** to his physique and his spiritual power. That lets him evolve his spirit soul further than his peers **without unnatural methods** — free of side effects, and on a firmer foundation. That is why I am going to take him into the **intermediate platform.**"

"Intermediate," Xie Xie said.

"Only those with **seven rings and under** may enter. There is **no minimum rank.** With an entry card you can walk into an advanced platform at rank one. The beasts are more vicious and their year levels are higher — about the same as the **Great Star Dou Forest.**"

Lin Hao's head came up.

He had been waiting for that all week. He had not known he was waiting for it.

"The forest," he said.

Wu Zhangkong looked at him.

"The intermediate platform's beasts are the same year levels as the Great Star Dou Forest."

"Yes."

"And the hawk was still for four hours at the Great Star Dou Forest."

There was a silence in the room above the forge with the window open, and Lin Hao understood that he had said the thing out loud, and that he had not decided to.

Wu Zhangkong did not move.

"Explain," he said.

"I can't."

"Then describe."

"It went still." Lin Hao said it carefully, because he had tried to write it and it had only been six words. "Not the way it goes still at something it's deciding about. I've felt that twice. This was different. This was—" he stopped, and chose the plainer word, because that was the only kind that had worked all week. "**Recognition.**"

Wu Zhangkong looked at him for a long time.

---

## Part 4: The Sentence He Did Not Finish

"A pair of thousand-year rings and a thousand-year spirit soul," Wu Zhangkong said at last, "requires one of two things."

He held up a finger.

"**An unnatural body.** Stimulated by treasure or method, by people with the resources to do it and a reason to spend them." He held up a second finger. "**Or an exceptional soul power** that has grown out of proportion to everything else, the way Wulin's has, which lets the spirit soul evolve on its own without forcing."

He put his hand down.

"You have three rings. Two are full. Your soul power is not exceptional — it is *good*, and good is not what I am describing, and I have measured you often enough to know the difference." He said it without cruelty, which was the only way he said anything. "Your spiritual power is two hundred and forty-six, which is respectable and is not Gu Yue's. Your physique is that of a blacksmith, which is better than most and is not what I am describing."

Lin Hao sat very still.

"So I'm not in either category."

"You are not in either category."

There was a silence.

"Then what am I in."

Wu Zhangkong opened his mouth.

And then he closed it.

Lin Hao watched him do it. He watched a man who had explained a bear, and a legend, and a grave, and the arithmetic of eight thousand years of an organisation, open his mouth to answer a question and decide — in the space of about one second — that the answer was not a thing he was going to say.

"I don't know," Wu Zhangkong said.

It was the first time in four months.

---

## Part 5: What He Offered Instead

"I am going to take Wulin into the intermediate platform," Wu Zhangkong said. "And I am going to take you, because you have three rings and I would like to see what happens in there with three rings, and because—" he paused, and Lin Hao understood that he was choosing, and that the choosing cost him something, "**because I do not know what you are, and I would rather find out where I can see it.**"

"The Spirit Pagoda wants weak entrants," Lin Hao said.

"Yes. Quicker deaths, fewer resources."

"So a boy with three rings who isn't in either category is the opposite of what they want."

"He is." Wu Zhangkong folded his arms. "Which is a reason to be careful, and not a reason to stay home."

Xie Xie, from the corner, said, "Can I come."

"No."

"You're taking *him.*"

"I'm taking the two of them because one of them has an advantage I understand and the other has one I don't." Wu Zhangkong looked at Xie Xie. "You have twin daggers and you go left. That is a thing I understand, and it will get you killed in the intermediate platform in about four minutes, and I would have to carry you out, and I would rather not."

Xie Xie opened his mouth, and closed it, and then said, "That's the longest you've ever spoken to me."

"It will not happen again."

---

## Part 6: What Lin Hao Wrote

He wrote it that night with the window open onto a street with no tall buildings in it.

*There are two ways to have what I have. An unnatural body, or an exceptional soul power. I have neither. Wu Zhangkong measured me and said so, and then I asked him what I am in and he opened his mouth and closed it and said he didn't know.*

*He has never not known.*

*He explained a bear. He explained a dead man's record. He explained a stone. He explained eight thousand years. And then I asked him one question and he said I don't know, and I believe him, and that is the most frightening thing that has happened to me, because a man who doesn't know is not a man who is hiding it. He is a man who has run out of categories.*

Then he looked at the page, and he added the line at the bottom, and this time he did not leave it unfinished, because he had decided something.

*The hawk recognised the forest. The intermediate platform's beasts are the same year levels as the forest. Wu Zhangkong is taking me in there because he would rather find out where he can see it.*

*So I am going to find out too.*

*And I am going to find out before he does, because he has been wrong once now, and I have never seen him wrong before, and I do not think either of us knows what that means yet.*

He closed the book.

The *hawk-gold lock* at his temple lay across his cheekbone, and the *marks on his forearms* were faint under his sleeves, and the hawk turned over in him — *one thousand one hundred and ninety-three* — and did not lean toward anything, because there was nothing in a room that small.

But it had recognised a forest.

And it was going back.
