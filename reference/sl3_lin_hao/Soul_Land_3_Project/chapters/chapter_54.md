## Part 1: What The Starwheel Did

Xu Xiaoyan used it on Xie Xie, because Xie Xie had asked, and because Xie Xie had asked in the particular way that meant he had already decided and was informing them.

The starwheel came up over her head.

It was not large. It was a ring of light with marks in it that Lin Hao's eyes followed and could not read, and it turned, and the turning had a *sound* that was not a sound, and Xie Xie's soul power went out.

Not reduced. **Out.**

Lin Hao counted, because that was what his head did.

**One. Two. Three. Four. Five.**

And then it came back, and Xie Xie was standing in the training field with his mouth open, and the Twin Dragon Storm — the thing he had built in three weeks and thrown up twice for — had not been available to him for five seconds, and in five seconds a great many things could be done to a person who could not use their soul.

"How long," Xu Xiaoyan said. She was sitting down. She had not been sitting down before.

"Five seconds."

"That's the most I've ever got." She smiled, bitterly, the way people smile when they have been given something that costs. "And I can't move while it's up. If I move, the seal unravels."

## Part 2: What Lin Hao Worked Out

He worked it out that evening, in the room, with the six of them around a table that was too small, and he worked it out aloud, because he had learned that the saying was the same act as the thinking.

"It's not a weapon," he said.

Xu Xiaoyan looked at him.

"Explain."

"You said it's best used in coordination with a team," Lin Hao said. "And you said you can't move while it's up, and that if you move it unravels. And I've been thinking about that, because I've been thinking about it for three hours, and the thing I keep coming back to is that you've described something that is **entirely useless on its own.**"

"That's what I said."

"You said it's a restriction." Lin Hao said it carefully, because he had learned that the flat version got further. "I don't think it's a restriction. I think it's a **shape.** You can't move because you're not supposed to be the one doing anything. You're the one who makes five seconds happen, and somebody else has to be there to use them."

There was a silence at the table.

"That's the smartest thing anyone has said about me," Xu Xiaoyan said, "and my own clan's records didn't say it."

"Your clan's records say it'll gain more abilities as your cultivation rises."

"They say it's almost like a second set of soul rings. As if I had twin martial souls." Xu Xiaoyan looked at her hands. "And that during the day I'm weak, and can only use the ice staff."

"So the tournament schedule matters."

"It's at night," Xu Xiaoyan said. "The Skysea Alliance runs the finals at night. I didn't know why until about four seconds ago."

## Part 3: What Wu Zhangkong Said

He had been standing in the doorway.

Lin Hao had not known. That was worth noticing, because Lin Hao noticed, and because in four months Wu Zhangkong had never once arrived without Lin Hao knowing, and the not-knowing was its own small fact.

"Continue," Wu Zhangkong said.

So Lin Hao continued.

"Six of us," he said. "Xu Xiaoyan makes five seconds. Xie Xie has the Twin Dragon Storm, which costs him everything and takes three weeks to build and two vomits. Wulin has a thousand-year Bind that does not stop. Gu Yue has six elements and cannot be paired with anybody fairly. I have three rings and a Track I have learned badly and a sword that has an opinion." He stopped. "And the shape is that Xu Xiaoyan opens five seconds and the rest of us have to be somewhere useful when they happen."

Wu Zhangkong was quiet for a moment.

"That is the first time," he said, "any of you has described this team as a shape rather than a list."

"Is it right."

"It's the first version that could be wrong in an interesting way." Wu Zhangkong unfolded his arms. "Which is the only kind of wrong I have any use for."

## Part 4: What The Track Cost

He had been learning the Ghost Shadow Perplexing Track for nine days.

The hawk gave him nothing, which was what it did, and he had stopped asking it for things somewhere around the fourth month, and he would like to notice that **the question is still open** and that he is the one who stopped rather than the one who was answered.

He had learned it **badly** for six of them, and he wants that noted, because he had written *learned badly* in the ledger as though it were a stage, and it had not been a stage, it had been failing. The Track was not a movement. It was a decision about where you were going to be, made before you moved, and Lin Hao had spent four years being good at deciding where a blade was going to go and had assumed the skill transferred.

It did not transfer.

A blade is a thing you hold. The Track is a thing you do with the part of you that is not holding anything.

On the seventh day he stopped trying to move and started trying to **decide**, and the Track happened, and it was not impressive, and it put him four metres to the left of where he had been, and he vomited, which he had not expected, and which Xie Xie found extremely funny from the floor where he had also been.

"How long," Xie Xie said.

"Nine days."

"I took three weeks on the Storm."

"Yes."

"We're a very slow team."

"We're a team that builds things," Lin Hao said. "That's different from being slow, and I've decided I'd rather be the second one."

What he did not say, that night, in the ledger, was the part that had actually cost him.

**The thing that made the Track take nine days was the thing that made everything else take four hours.**

He wrote it three times before he wrote it correctly, because the first two versions were complaints and he had learned to recognise the shape of a complaint in his own handwriting. The correct version was this: for four years every problem he had ever been handed had been solved by reading it, and reading is a thing you do *to* an opponent, and the Track has no opponent in it. The Track is a decision you make about a piece of ground nobody is standing on. There is nothing to read. And so the single most useful thing about him — the thing Mu Chen had gone quiet about, the thing Wu Zhangkong had stopped holding back for, the thing that had let him answer six men in a row without Wind-Step or Gale Talon — had spent nine days getting in his own way.

He had been trying to read the floor.

*This is the first time a gift has cost me anything, and that I have been carrying a great many gifts for a great many months, and that I had begun to assume they only ever subtracted from the other person. That was not a belief I knew I had. I found it the way you find a stair in the dark, with the outside of the leg.*

Wulin had watched the whole nine days, which was the part Lin Hao had not written down yet.

He had not said anything useful. He had not been asked to. He had sat on the same low wall every evening with a piece of dried fish and had watched a person he has known since he was six fail at something for nine consecutive days, and on the fourth day he had said *"do you want me to go,"* and Lin Hao had said no, and he had stayed; and on the seventh day, when the Track happened for the first time and Lin Hao came off it on his hands and knees, Wulin had been the one who was already standing up, and he had not come over, because he had worked out before Lin Hao did that the thing Lin Hao needed was not a hand.

**That is the oldest thing I know about him and I had stopped noticing it. He knows what I need before I do and he does not do it unless I ask, and he has been doing that for four years, and it took me failing nine days in a row to see it again.**

And then the second thing, which was smaller and worse.

On the ninth day, when the Track finally happened and he stood four metres to the left of where he had been with his hands on his knees, Xu Xiaoyan had looked at him for a long moment and said *"oh,"* in the voice of a person revising something, and had then gone and sat down with her staff across her knees and had not said anything else for an hour.

He knew what she had been doing. He had watched her do the arithmetic on the dormitory floor the morning she arrived, and he had watched it come out wrong.

**She came here at rank seventeen with two yellow rings and a month to prove herself. And there is a girl in her class with three purple rings who has been a Soul Elder since before she arrived, who took nine days to learn a movement she learned in four, who is going to be at the tournament, and who is not her fault.** I have been the person in the room that other people have to be measured against for most of this year, and that I have never once thought about what that is like from where she is sitting, and that the reason I have not thought about it is not that I am cruel. It is that nobody told me I was the weather.

He wrote that down and then he wrote one more line under it, and the line was:

*Wu Zhangkong knew. That is what "there is no one in this room who can be paired with Lin Hao and have it be fair" MEANT, and I took it as a compliment for eleven days, and it was a warning about what I do to a room.*

## Part 5: What He Wrote

He wrote it that night, above the forge, and he wrote the number first.

*two hundred and thirty-three.* It had been two hundred and seventy. Seven points, and the Track had cost him nine days and one vomit, and I cannot tell you which of those did it.

*Thirty-four.*

That arrived on the ninth day, in the training field, four metres to the left of where he had been, with his stomach emptying and the Track finally doing the thing it was supposed to do. It arrived the way the last one had, which was to say without asking, and he had stopped being surprised by that, and the not-being-surprised was its own small fact.

*One thousand three hundred and fifty-one.*

Then the rest.

*The Track is not a movement. It is a decision about where you are going to be, made before you move. I have spent four years deciding where a blade is going to go and assumed it was the same skill, and it is not, because a blade is a thing you hold and the Track is a thing you do with the part of you that is not holding anything.*

*Xu Xiaoyan is not the weakest of us. She is the one the whole thing depends on, and her own clan's records did not say that, and I said it in four hours, and I said it out loud rather than keeping it, because I have decided that the saying is the same act as the thinking.*

Then the line at the bottom.

*Eleven days to the tournament. Six of us. A shape rather than a list.*

*Long Hengxu has bet his standing and will not say it. Wu Zhangkong has bet something and has said it twice in the form of the words I don't know. Mu Chen has not been upset for eleven years and put me up for a rank I was not ready for.*

*I am a fourth-rank Grandmaster with three purple rings and a Track I learned in nine days and a sword that has an opinion, and there are five seconds in this team that belong to the girl who lasted eleven seconds against me, and I would like to be somewhere useful when they happen.*

He closed the book.

The *hawk-gold lock* at his temple lay across his cheekbone, and the *marks on his forearms* were raised and had been raised for nineteen days and had become a thing nobody mentioned, and the hawk turned over in him — *one thousand three hundred and fifty-one* — and did not pull toward anything.

It was waiting.

And so was he, and the waiting had started to feel like the same waiting, and he had decided that this was worth noticing too, and that noticing it was not the same as understanding it, and that he had stopped needing those to be the same thing.
