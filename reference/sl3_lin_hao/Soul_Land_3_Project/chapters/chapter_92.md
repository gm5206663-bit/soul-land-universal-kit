## Part 1: The Read at the Rim

"Either of them could erase us with one swipe," Gu Yue said. "So why does the exam *want* us watching them? Being here has to be worth points somehow."

"Then we watch," Wulin said. "They're spending each other. If they both end up one breath from finished, that gap is ours to walk into — maybe." He knew exactly what his team was and wasn't. Charging either of those two was a way to stop existing. And this place had removed the one comfort the ascension platform always offered: there was no leaving early here except by dying.

Below them the two lights dimmed, and for the first time the shapes stood clear: the bear's dark-gold shell cracked and guttering, its huge head low; the crystal lion hardly marked, bright as a struck bell, the third eye burning a clean red.

"It's afraid," Xiaoyan whispered. "The *bear's* afraid."

"It's not watching the bear," Lin Hao said.

"It's staring at the bear," Xie Xie hissed. "Obviously—"

"The bear. The valley. The rim." Lin Hao hadn't moved; his voice stayed down where the wind wouldn't find it. "All at once. That eye took our measure before Gu Yue's birds ever left her hands. We've been seen." A beat. "And passed over."

The class went quiet in a new way. Being hidden and being *spared* were different weights, and everyone felt the difference arrive at once.

"So it *chose* not to," Xiaoyan breathed. "We're alive on purpose."

"For now," said Gu Yue, and sent new birds north.

## Part 2: The Legend

"I've got it!" Xie Xie's whisper cracked with the effort of staying a whisper. "The golden one — it's the auspicious beast! From the legends!"

Three faces turned. Lin Hao's stayed on the valley, but tipped, listening.

"You know the Spirit Ice Douluo's story. Once, the saying goes, the Great Star Dou Forest kept an emperor of luck — while it lived, every beast under the canopy cultivated faster, like the whole forest was fed. When it died, the luck inverted. Misfortune, decline — and in the end, the tide that broke against Shrek City." Xie Xie's finger stabbed toward the gold. "That's no lion, that's the *Auspicious Emperor*. A Three-eyed Golden Lion. The Spirit Ice Douluo himself took it — its ring, its bone. The skull, Wulin. The number-one spiritual bone ever ranked."

"How smart is it," Lin Hao asked, "for 'smarter than humans'?"

"Talks-in-legends smart. Schemes smart."

"And nobody wrote down that it *talks*?"

"My family collects biographies," Xie Xie said, with the smugness of a boy standing in front of a library nobody else could visit. "There are versions normal people never touch. In one of them—" he dropped his voice further, delighted, "—the lion took a human shape. Fell in love with him. The Spirit Ice Douluo. And when death came for the man, the lion stepped in front of it — became his ring to save him. Which is why the forest after him went hungry and angry and marched on Shrek."

Xiaoyan's eyes had gone star-shaped. "It's *cool*. I want it. Can I want it? As my spirit soul—"

"Keep dreaming," Xie Xie said.

"Get in line anyway," Lin Hao told her. "It saw you first."

But Gu Yue wasn't laughing. "It traded every beast's future for one person," she said slowly. "Why? It knew what the forest was without it. It did the arithmetic and *still* chose him."

"Love doesn't do arithmetic," Xie Xie said, with the authority of a boy who had read about it.

Gu Yue's eyes did something involuntary then — a flick to Wulin, gone as fast as it came — and Lin Hao, who caught it because he caught everything at that angle, looked back at the valley and said, mostly to himself:

"Most expensive gift I've ever heard of."

Gu Yue glanced at him this time — a full second of it, filing the answer and its author somewhere — and said nothing.

## Part 3: The Correction

The earth answered for them. A rumble rolled up through the stone under their ribs, the wind stood up, and the two lights collided one last furious time and then *moved* — grinding away down the valley, north, the sound of them shrinking like a storm changing counties.

"Gone?" Xie Xie asked, tiny.

"Gone," Gu Yue confirmed, birds aloft. "Two lights. Moving fast. Arguing the whole way."

The class let a population's worth of air out through its nose.

"That's why the forest was empty," Xiaoyan said. "Them."

"Mm. This valley is somebody's ground," Wulin said. "The other came to take it."

"And if it's the *lion's* ground—" Xie Xie was already vibrating, "—then luck lives here. Treasures, Captain. They said we can carry out whatever we find."

"They also said this is an illusory world," Xie Xie barreled on. "Worst case, we die here and just… wake up. No real consequences. If we *don't* look, we'll regret it the rest of our lives."

"The rings are real," Lin Hao said.

Everyone looked at him.

"The rules said it — the beasts here are real, in a sense; kill one at a bottleneck and the ring comes home with you. Anything real enough to hand you a ring," he said, "is real enough to collect one. There's no version of this with no consequences. There's just the ones we haven't met."

"He's fun at festivals," Xie Xie told the group.

"He's right, though," Wulin said, and the captain arrived at the decision in the same breath, fire lighting behind his eyes. "We're going. But we go *together*, quiet, and nobody touches anything shiny until we're standing on real grass again. That's the price of the trip."

Groans, from exactly one person.

"It's up to you, Captain," Xiaoyan said, already smiling.

Gu Yue nodded once.

## Part 4: Down

The valley wall was steep and stupid with loose stone. Wulin's Bluesilver Grass made ropes of itself, and the plan came out of the captain already cut to the shape of five people, not four.

"Lin Hao anchors at the lip — heaviest grip, and the wall's his to watch. Xiaoyan, his line. Gu Yue with Xie Xie on mine. Nobody free-climbs past anybody."

Lin Hao went over first, found his ledge, and then did the thing none of them had thought to do and all of them now watched him do: he took each grass-line in turn where it met the stone, read the knot, and re-tied two of them.

"Smith's knots," he said, when Wulin's eyebrow went up. "If one loosens, it's my name on it."

They came down slow and steady through the battle's leavings, and the leavings were the lesson. The valley floor lay stripped bare, and its walls carried wounds in rows — gouges deep as a man, runs of stone sheared off like rind. Lin Hao put two fingers into one as they passed and stopped.

"The lip's glassy," he said. "It melted, a little, then froze. That wasn't cutting. That's weather with an edge." He wiped his fingers on his trouser leg, quietly pleased in the professional way, and added the whole experience to whatever ledger he kept about the difference between ten-thousand-year power and everything else.

The mist stood thick at the far end. And out of it, when they drew level — a mouth of dark in the rock, five meters of height swallowing itself in shadow.

Xie Xie ran the last stretch before anyone could rule on it, sketched a bow at the entrance, and then his face changed. He inhaled at the threshold twice, like a sommelier with a death wish.

"Captain. We fought a Dreadclaw — you all remember the *smell*." He pointed into the dark. "That's not it. This is clean. Faintly animal. The biographies describe it." His gulp was audible. "It's the *lion's*."

"Then we don't stand in its doorway deciding," Wulin said. "In. Fast and quiet — they could come back."

## Part 5: The Floor

The dark inside was total, a different country; then the ceiling climbed away and the space widened, and around a bend the light found them first.

Gems. The floor was *littered* with them — loose stones catching somebody's light and returning it in pieces, red and gold and green washing over the walls with every step, so that the five of them walked into a slow kaleidoscope.

"Oh," Xiaoyan said, the politically safest available word.

"Treasures," Xie Xie whispered, hands physically clasping each other behind his back in tribute to the captain's law.

Gu Yue crouched, lifted one, let it roll in her palm — careful, pricing nothing aloud.

Wulin turned a slow circle, grass already spread across the floor behind them like a tripwire crop. "Eyes working. Hands empty. That's the rule until grass."

Lin Hao knelt last, picked nothing up, and read the floor the way he read everything — the spread of it, the sizes, the way the pieces had settled all one way, like something had been bedded here and had risen.

"Eyes up," he said, rising out of the crouch. "A floor this generous has an owner."

Somewhere north, muffled by a mountain's worth of stone, the two lights were still arguing.
