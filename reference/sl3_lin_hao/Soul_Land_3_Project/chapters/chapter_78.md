## Part 1: The Nape

If Xu Xiaoyan's shackles could restrain an expert of Wu Zhangkong's level — and they could — then the Earth Demon Rhinoceros had never had a chance of arguing with them.

Gu Yue, counter ready, did not launch it. She recalled her soul power and her rings, and stood down: the opening was better spent.

And the opening was spent. Wulin came down on the fallen beast's head, and the resplendent golden dragon claw descended on the rhinoceros's nape — the neck, weaker than the skull he wasn't sure he could pierce — and the moment the claw touched hide, every drop of the golden light clinging to his body converged into it, bathing it in one magnificent glow. The crushing effect activated on contact. The outer armor of the skin shattered like slag off a pour, the claw went into the breach, and a moment later the Earth Demon Rhinoceros collapsed with a rumble that ran through the whole arena floor — spine severed, and its control over its body, and its life.

It's really strong. He stared at his sparkling claw, which lacked even the tiniest bloodstain, and felt a flurry of emotions his vocabulary didn't cover.

He had reaped the second seal's benefits in full. The mysterious golden ring appeared when the claw appeared — and where Bluesilver Grass drank soul power, this ring drank **physical strength and blood essence**. The claw no longer cost him soul power at all; he could feel the blood going to it, and if he ever spent all his blood essence, the bill would arrive as — hunger. Just hunger, enormous and immediate. He could now hold the claw far longer, and it was stronger, and the scales amplified everything further and now covered the ENTIRE right side of his body, strengthening every part they lay on. He could not have slaughtered this rhinoceros so easily without any of it.

And in the back of his mind, where knowledge he had never been taught lived, the name of it surfaced the way a temper color surfaces in steel.

*Golden Dragon Body.*

An all-around amplifier — strength, speed, attack, defense — with strength amplified most of all. Today had been the demonstration: a thirteen-year-old, toe to toe with a top-three beast of its level, not backing down.

None of which, he knew, looking at the small fierce girl with the staff, happened without Starlight Shackles buying him the instant. One attack, one kill, because for one split second three thousand years of rhinoceros could not move.

## Part 2: The Score

The arena faded mid-thought, and they were standing in the dark hall, hands joined in a circle, exactly as before — as if the whole fight had been an illusion they had held hands inside. It had not been the spirit ascension platform; that was technology, and this was something else, some craft they couldn't fathom, and they were awestruck in the residue of it, five pairs of hands slowly letting go.

Shen Yi reappeared, and her expression was stranger than ever — and it was carrying two strangenesses, though only the class knew the second one. She had watched a golden soul ring replace a boy's purples. And the proctors' record showed one other thing her file had no shelf for: a single sword-line, one-handed, laid across the girls' line at the stomp — defensive, precise, taking the edge off a three-thousand-year area attack like a man trimming flashing off a casting. She had read the letters for three winters. The letters had never mentioned that the boy could do THAT.

The beast arena was the second trial. Survive one minute to pass; harm the beasts for bonus; the strength and number of beasts scaled to the number of examinees. She had deliberately not told them the minimum — and she had never imagined this. Even for HER, a three-thousand-year rhinoceros would have been annoying to kill without her battle armor. These children had slaughtered it. And the golden ring — no matter how she looked at it, the boy had to have twin martial souls. But surely Wu Zhangkong would have mentioned such a thing to her.

"The results for the second trial, the beast arena: Tang Wulin, ten points. Xie Xie, ten points. Gu Yue, eight points. Xu Xiaoyan, ten points. Lin Hao, ten points."

"Wow, how did we score so high?" Xie Xie asked, honestly surprised.

"You scored highly because you killed the Earth Demon Rhinoceros together," Shen Yi said. "The criteria for passing didn't involve killing it — so even Gu Yue, who didn't attack it, scored well. We shall now continue. Follow me."

And follow they did, while her heart welled with expectation behind her proctor's face. *These kids really match Shrek's motto. Little monsters.* High combat ability, good teamwork — and calm. Not one of the five had panicked. Gu Yue had been the calmest of all, and the rest had had excitement in their eyes — Xie Xie had named the beast out loud, so all of them had known exactly how formidable it was, and knowing it, none had cowered. You could see they had faced worse. *Senior brother, it seems you've made sure to give them a lot of combat experience!*

Wulin, meanwhile, was frowning over the ledger of costs. The scores were good; the tank was not. Soul power well spent across all five — Xiaoyan worst: ONE-THIRD of hers, gone, for a split second of absolute restraint. If he had been any slower finishing the beast, it would have cost her more. He checked Gu Yue with a look; she nodded, fine, which meant fine. And the gates of his own new strength had a tollbooth of blood essence with HUNGRY painted over it in letters he could already read. The class had learned at breakfast what three days of refining cost a captain. The bill got bigger.

After the last two trials, all of them understood what kind of exam this was. Many more unknown trials waited. This was just the beginning — and no rest came with the beginning.

## Part 3: The Gates

Shen Yi pulled a sliding door open and brought them out into the space between the stone buildings, and the third trial was already standing there like a sentence that had been waiting for them all day.

Gates, in rows, one after another — at least a hundred of them, the number in each row varying, a corridor of doorways with nothing doors in them. Each gate carried a guillotine blade: two meters wide, a meter tall, half a meter THICK — a slab of falling metal against which a full-grown cow would not be a delay.

"The third trial tests your ability to adapt." Shen Yi's voice was the same one that gave them scores; the words were the same ones that could take points back. "Once I tell you to get ready, you will have one minute to prepare. Once I shout for you to begin, you will have to pass through all of the gates. If you finish within thirty seconds, you get six points. For every three seconds you finish early, you get an additional point. For every three seconds you're late, you will lose one point. Two things you need to know. First — the closer you are to each gate, the faster its guillotine will fall. These are real guillotines. If one strikes you, you will die. Second — you can get a negative score for this trial. If you can't pass this trial, then forget about taking the later ones."

She finished speaking, gestured to the side, and the clank of metal rang across the yard as a hundred blades began to rise.

The front-most gate's guillotine fell — a booming CLANG, sparks erupting as it thudded into its recess, a sound built to put terror in the heart and doing its job on five hearts at once. Then the blade rose, and fell again. Rise. Fall. Rise. Fall. The yard filled with the smell of struck iron.

## Part 4: The Minute

"Get ready."

One minute. Five people spent it five ways.

Wulin rolled his right shoulder, feeling the scales' new whole-side country, and did his arithmetic out loud, quietly, to the class: "Thirty seconds is six points. It's a bonus trial. The rhino was the trial; this is the purse." Gold and hunger, both in his eyes.

Xie Xie was already up on the balls of his feet, grinning at the corridor of falling iron with an expression his mother would have called asking for it.

Gu Yue stood still, eyes half-closed, doing whatever she did that made stillness itself look armed.

Xiaoyan breathed the star-count breath, tired to a third of her tank, conserving with the discipline of a girl who knew exactly what one more skill would cost her — and knowing it, set it aside: the gates asked for feet, not stars.

And Lin Hao walked to the line and listened to the yard the way he had listened to shops his whole life.

Because here was the thing the trial-makers could not know about the sixth examinee, and Shen Yi's three winters of letters had never quite said: **a guillotine gate is a trip-hammer that ate a doorway.** He had grown up in a forge with a power hammer's voice through the wall — the stroke, the rise, the stroke — and a smith's child learns its cadence the way a sailor's child learns weather, in the body, before words. Rise-fall, rise-fall, and the rule of it was the shop's first rule, the one the guillotines had just confessed in their own falling: *the closer your hand, the faster the stroke.* The hammer knows your hand. Every apprentice learns where the line is by watching the man who never takes his hand back too late.

He counted blade-falls down the rows — each row's number different, each gate's timing its own, the whole yard a hundred hammers in an orchestra nobody was conducting — and found the seam in it inside forty seconds, the way the yard's rhythm opened and closed, and where a body could live between the strokes.

"I grew up next to one of these," he said, to his class, in the flat voice he gave numbers, and there was — visible, brief, real — a grin in it somewhere. "Ours was smaller. The rules are the same rules. Rows breathe in threes; the gaps run with the fall, not against it; and the blade only knows where your hand IS, so be somewhere it was. That's the whole grammar."

He gave them the grammar the way he had given them the stomp-read: free, flat, and spendable.

"Ten seconds," Shen Yi said.

The hawk shifted on his shoulder — winter-tipped, unbothered, a bird that had ridden a forge's rafters through its whole first life — and settled its grip like a passenger taking a familiar corner.

"Begin."
