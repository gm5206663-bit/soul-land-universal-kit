## Part 1: The Hand

Wulin put his hand out first.

That was the thing Lin Hao would remember later, and not the tournament — that Wulin put his hand out first, in Eastsea Square, in front of a crowd, without deciding, the way he did most of the things that mattered.

Gu Yue put her hand on top of his.

Then she reached sideways and took Xu Xiaoyan's hand and put it on hers, which was a thing nobody expected, and Xu Xiaoyan looked at her own hand as though somebody else had placed it there.

Xie Xie was last.

He glared at Gu Yue. "Just how much do you loathe me."

Gu Yue thought about it.

"About as much as one would loathe a housefly."

"*You—*"

Lin Hao put his hand on top, because he was the sixth, and because he had learned that the sixth hand is the one that decides whether it is a gesture or a shape.

"Six," he said. "That's a shape."

Long Hengxu, who had been standing behind them in the manner of a man who had bet his standing and was not going to say so, made a sound that was almost a laugh, and caught it, and did not make it.

## Part 2: What Skysea City Was

It was bigger than Heaven Dou, and it was not quieter.

Lin Hao had been to a city with no skyscrapers and understood something there, and he had assumed the understanding would transfer, and it did not, because Skysea City was a city that had decided to be large and had been large a long time and had no interest in being anything else.

The smithy was four blocks from the sea.

That was the first thing Lin Hao noticed, and it was the thing that mattered, because a smithy four blocks from the sea meant the metal came in by water, and the metal that comes in by water is the metal worth working, and he understood from the smell of the street that this was the best smithy he would stand in for a very long time.

He stood in front of it about eleven seconds.

"You're doing the face," Wulin said.

"I'm reading the building."

"You're doing the face."

---

## Part 3: The Boy Who Was Hungry

He was sitting on the seawall eating dried fish, and he was round, and he was younger than any of them, and he was wearing a uniform Lin Hao did not recognise.

That was the first thing.

The second thing was that the uniform was better.

Not richer. Lin Hao had seen rich uniforms and could read them the way he read a bar. This one was not rich. It was made well, and the making was the kind that costs more than the material, and Lin Hao stood on a seawall in Skysea City and understood in about four seconds that he was looking at something from a place that had been doing it longer than his city had existed.

"Hi," said the boy. He had dried fish in both hands. "You're from Eastsea?"

"Yes."

"Do you know what seafood is good here?" The boy said it with total sincerity. "It's my first time seeing the sea. I'm going swimming later."

"There's briny sea urchin," Wulin said, because Wulin could not help it. "Sea cucumber. Bluefin's good. The eels taste like honey. Goldhair crab beats king crab by a distance — the meat's more tender. If you want lobster, our eastsea lobster's the best, though it's pricey. Skysea lobster's firmer but—"

"You really know your food," the boy said, delighted.

"My name's Tang Wulin."

"Xu Lizhi." The boy offered a hand that was sticky. "Everyone calls me Little Glutton. You can too."

"That's the same as my friend," Wulin said, and pointed at Lin Hao, who had once eaten forty-one steamed buns in a dining hall and had never been allowed to forget it.

"That's a good nickname," Xu Lizhi said. "What school are you from?"

"Eastsea Academy."

"Oh." Xu Lizhi said it the way you say a thing you have not decided about. "I'm from Shrek."

## Part 4: What Lin Hao Noticed

He noticed three things, and he wrote all three down that night, and the writing was the thinking.

The first was that Xu Lizhi was not impressed.

Not rude. Not dismissive. Xu Lizhi was delighted about the sea and the food and the fact that Wulin knew about eels, and he had looked at six children from Eastsea Academy with three purple rings among them and a fourth-rank Grandmaster and a Twin Dragon Storm, and he had been pleasant, in the specific way a person is pleasant when they have no reason to be anything else.

That was the first thing. Being the largest thing in a room had been a local condition.

The second was the uniform.

The third was that Xu Lizhi had a storage ring, and that he had taken dried fish out of it twice, and that a storage ring was a thing Lin Hao had read about and never seen, and that the boy used it the way other people used a pocket.

"Where did you get the ring," Lin Hao said.

Xu Lizhi looked at his hand.

"Shrek gave it to me."

"They just give them out."

"I don't think they just give them out." Xu Lizhi considered the dried fish. "I think they give them to people who are going to need one. I didn't know I was going to need one. But I've needed it, twice, for fish, so maybe they know something."

## Part 5: What The Track Did

The tournament's first round was that evening, and it was not a difficult round. Lin Hao had been building toward it eleven days, and the round was not difficult, and the not-difficult was its own small fact.

Two opponents. Both one ring. Both from a city that was not Skysea.

Xu Xiaoyan's starwheel went up at the start, because it was night, and Xie Xie's soul power went out for five seconds, and the five seconds happened, and Lin Hao used the Track.

He had learned the Track in nine days, six of them failing, and had used it on a training field perhaps forty times, and this was the first time he had used it in front of anybody who was not in his class, and what he discovered was that the Track did not care.

The Track was a decision about where he was going to be, made before he moved, and the decision did not become harder because people were watching.

He was four metres left of where he had been, and the first opponent was on the ground, and the second opponent was looking at the place Lin Hao had been with the expression of a person who had been told a thing and had not believed it.

It took nineteen seconds.

Gu Yue put her hand on his arm afterwards, which she had never done.

"That's new," she said.

"It's nine days old."

"It's the first time I've seen it."

"Yes."

"I've now seen you do four things I can't do," Gu Yue said, "and I've beaten you five times, and I haven't worked out how both of those are true."

"I've been working on that too."

"Have you worked it out."

"No."

"Good," Gu Yue said. "Neither have I."

---

## Part 6: What Lin Hao Wrote

He wrote it that night, in a room in Skysea City that smelled of the sea, and he wrote the number first out of habit, and there was no number, and he wrote a dash, and the dash did not bother him, and the not-bothering had become a habit too, and that was worth writing down.

*Three things about the boy on the seawall.*

*One: he wasn't impressed. Not rude, not dismissive — pleasant, the way a person is pleasant when they've no reason to be anything else. I've been the largest thing in a room four months and I've discovered that was a local condition. I'm not upset about it, and the reason I'm not is that I've seen what standing in front of costs, and a boy who's pleasant about a storage ring has never had to stand in front of anything.*

*Two: the uniform. It isn't rich. It's made well, and the making costs more than the material, and I read that in four seconds, and I'm a fourth-rank Grandmaster, and I read a uniform the way I read a bar, and it's the same skill, and I've never once thought about what it'd be like to make something that good.*

*Three: the ring. Shrek gave it to him because they thought he'd need one. He didn't know he'd need one. He's needed it twice, for fish.*

And then the line at the bottom, which he wrote last.

*They give things to people who are going to need them.*

*Wu Zhangkong gave me the Track before I could use it. Mu Chen put me up for a rank I wasn't ready for. Both gave me a thing before I could hold it, and I said at the time that this was what it sounds like when adults stop protecting you, and I was wrong, and I'm correcting it in the book.*

*That isn't what it sounds like when adults stop protecting you. That's what it sounds like when somebody can see what you're going to need.*

*Zhang Yangzi asked me in the corridor whether the Track was a Shrek thing and I said I didn't know and he said everybody says that. I almost answered the second half of it to Wang Jinxi, who isn't here any more and hasn't been since he packed, and I still turn my head toward the place he used to stand, and three weeks hasn't fixed it, and this is the thing I chose not to say.*

*Wei Xiaofeng isn't in class zero and hasn't spoken to any of us since the tournament, and I haven't thought about him in twenty-six days.*

*I'm going to find out who forged the blade. And I've started to suspect the reason I'm the one who can read it is that somebody gave me something before I could hold it, and that this is the same thing, and that I'd like to know who's been doing it.*

He closed the book.

The lock at his temple lay gold across his cheekbone, and the marks on his forearms were raised and had been raised for twenty-six days and had become a thing nobody in this city had mentioned, and the hawk turned over in him — *one thousand three hundred and fifty-one* — and did not pull toward anything.

But the sea was four blocks away, and the smithy was four blocks away, and Lin Hao lay on his back in a city that smelled of salt and hot metal, and thought about a boy who was pleasant about a storage ring, and about the fact that he had been the largest thing in a room for four months, and about the fact that he was not upset.

He slept.
