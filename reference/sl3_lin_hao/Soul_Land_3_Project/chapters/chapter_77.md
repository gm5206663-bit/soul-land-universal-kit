## Part 1: The Shape, Tested

The rhinoceros put its head down, and the shape got its first test in the only school that would have known the difference.

Before the charge, three seconds of stations: Wulin front; Xie Xie flanking wide; the girls backline in the star-field's silver; Lin Hao at the right edge, where the ground told things. He had read the beast the way he read metal, and the read took one breath, and he spent it out loud, flat, the way numbers go out.

"The horn is a feint. It's a strong-beast feint, but the foot is the weapon — everything it has lands through the ground. The stomp is the fight. Xiaoyan, the ground is yours; it always was tonight. Xie Xie, the head stings best. Captain—" the ghost of a smile, one beat, gone— "hit it somewhere that offends it."

Then Tang Wulin shot forward like a rocket, Bluesilver Impaling Array bursting out of him into the charge's path, and the Earth Demon Rhinoceros ran straight into the array, its huge body barely restrained for a moment — and in that moment Wulin closed and, drawing all his strength into his fist, punched it right in the face.

The rhino lowered its horns to answer the fist.

Bang.

It was like being hit by a train. Tang Wulin flew back like a cannonball, his whole arm numb — and the rhinoceros recoiled. Both of them, off their marks. Nobody was hurt on either side, and by the arithmetic of soul beasts that was a triumph: in brute strength this thing ranked in the top three of its entire level; the Dreadclaw Bear they had never met ranked merely in the top ten. It was feared for exactly this. And a thirteen-year-old had just made it take a step back with his fist.

Wulin was proud of it in the air, even flying. He flung a strand of Bluesilver Grass as he went and lassoed the beast's neck, and the trial-space glittered around them all like the beast arenas of old — courage and riches, great rewards, a place built to seduce.

The rhino's defense was better than the bear's; its attack, worse. The problem was never surviving it. The problem was getting through.

## Part 2: The Edge Taken Off

Wulin pulled on the grass and went flying back into the fight like a released spring. Xie Xie took his moment at the flank — second ring lit, **Light Dragon Storm** — agility's explosive edge aimed with three years of instinct at the head, the softest thing on the mountain. It could not breach the hide. It stung. That was the whole assignment, and he graded himself complete.

The rhinoceros grunted, raised its right foot, and stomped.

**War Stomp.** Yellow light detonated outward from the descending foot in a ring, and the ring did not care who it caught — paralysis lived inside it, twenty-five meters of it, Wulin already clear in mid-flight and hauling Xie Xie up by their shared strand so the numbness only caught him half —

— and the ring ran to the end of itself twenty meters short of the girls.

Canon's own margin, and here is what the margin was made of: the sword.

Lin Hao had been moving before the foot came down — the read had said *the foot*, the read is why the girls stood where they stood — and as the yellow wall rolled out across the star-field floor he drew the Frost Abyss one-handed and laid the Still Water line flat in its path, edge on, and CUT the top off the wave. Not the wave. Never the whole thing — the trial was the team's and the danger was the team's to answer, and a boy who deleted his class's examinations would be doing nobody any favors. He took the edge off it. The paralysis ring crossed the girls' line with its teeth pulled, a shove of warm wind where a seizure should have been, and Gu Yue's hands were already moving through the counter and Xiaoyan's staff was already rising, unafraid, because their floor was theirs.

One stroke. In and gone. Xie Xie, ringing with numbness in Wulin's grip overhead, got one look at it and understood exactly what he had seen, and would describe it later as *unfair, in the best way.*

## Part 3: The Gold

Wulin landed on the rhinoceros's head.

Golden scales ripped across his skin and his arm became the supreme golden dragon claw, and earth's stalwart defense — the whole problem of the beast — was about to stop being one, because if the claw got through the hide, the crushing effect did the rest. A matter of time.

Then the unexpected thing happened, and the night changed color.

The instant the claw finished forming, his body blazed with heat — fire searing up the blood — and his two purple soul rings vanished.

In their place: **a golden soul ring.**

Xu Xiaoyan and Gu Yue went dumbstruck, skills forgotten. Xie Xie gaped mid-air. The golden ring shone resplendent and showered him in splendid gold, and the scales and the claw stopped being dull — brighter than diamond, a treasure-smith's dream of finish — and blood essence gathered around him in a visible aura while his body swelled with power that was not soul power at all.

At the right edge of the shape, a smith watched the gold come up in his captain like a pour completing, and knew the temper on sight. *Same gold as the water.* Three days of mist over a bathtub, and now the same temperature standing up inside a boy — the break hadn't added something; it had *finished* something. He filed it where he filed metal.

The rhino threw its head and hurled Wulin off. Too busy being born new to attack, he got the scaled arm up in front of the horn — and this time the arm did not go numb, did not weaken, did not care.

"Be careful!" Gu Yue — yellow light surging at him, earth spikes riding it. He braced on landing, and the spikes struck, and the golden light merged with his skin, and he was thrown into the air entirely unharmed, groaning only at the tumble's dead weight.

*That golden light strengthened my body?*

*Is this the surprise Old Tang spoke of?*

No time. He spun mid-air, caught the lasso-strand wrapped around the beast's head, and dove down like a hawk — and his reinforcements arrived at last. Xie Xie, still airborne, split — three light clones, and behind them three shadows hidden from sight: **Twin Dragon Clones**, both third skills at once, six of them, every angle. Two went straight for the eyes, because skin is negotiable and eyes are not, and the rhinoceros shut its thick eyelids like portcullises and took the barrage as six ringing headaches.

And then the ground it trusted betrayed it, because the ground had been hers all along.

**Starlight Shackles** burst up beneath the beast and bound it — an instant only, chains of starlight, but an instant was the whole currency of this class — and the rhinoceros's center of gravity went with it, and three thousand years of savanna lordship tipped, and toppled.

Stars littered the illusion sky. The Starwheel Ice Staff accepted the night as fact — that was the secret of it, the reason the hall had gone dark at noon — and the absolute control those false stars bought her turned a binding into a fall.

The captain came down like a hawk on a falling mountain, gold burning.

## Part 4: Win

At the door of the trial hall, unnoticed by design, a man in white robes had been standing since the stomp.

Wu Zhangkong did not enter. A teacher does not touch an examination; he had drilled that into four generations and he was not going to make an exception for the one exam his own arrogance had nearly cost them. Shen Yi stood at his shoulder, and had said, quietly, the four words she had brought him from the corridor: *your student made terms.*

He had watched the punch. He had watched the stroke that took the teeth off the War Stomp — one draw, one line, holstered before the yellow light finished dying — and his face had done nothing at all, which on him was applause. He had watched the gold come up out of his captain's body and replace two purple rings with a color he had never seen on a soul master in his life, and only then, at the door, did he look down at the boy stationed at the right edge, and the look lasted four seconds, and the boy looked back and did not flinch, because he had done the arithmetic before he spoke in that corridor.

The exchange happened between the shackles and the fall, low, between the only two people in Shrek City who talked this way.

"You wagered."

"Yes, sir."

"Terms."

"Any student of the outer court, of the hall's choosing, against me. If I lose, the terms stand and we thank you for the papers. If I win, the lateness dies, the penalties die, the working mark dies. All five pass every trial first, or it's void."

"You wagered my apology."

"No, sir." Flat. Carried level. "You paid it. I wasn't spending it twice."

Four more seconds of the look. Somewhere in them, something that was not disapproval finished becoming something else — the thing his class would have called, if they had dared, the file coming shut.

"Win," Wu Zhangkong said.

One word. It was not permission; it was not a blessing. It was a standard, set where the boy could reach it and nothing below, and it came with exactly one addition, delivered in the voice of a man assigning a smith his quench:

"The five of you pass everything. That was already the assignment. The duel is what happens if you fail."

Then the rhinoceros finished falling, and the door's two watchers were moving — Shen Yi forward to proctor, the white robe stepping back out of the light — and the golden ring burned down out of the false stars like a struck match, and the shape that had held at midnight in a laundry yard held now in the oldest school on the continent, with three seconds left on its first test.
