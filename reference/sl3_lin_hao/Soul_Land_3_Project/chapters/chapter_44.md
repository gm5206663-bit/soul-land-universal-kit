## Part 1: The Vest

The test was in a room with no windows and a floor that gave slightly underfoot, and Wu Zhangkong put the five of them in it one at a time and did not explain anything.

Lin Hao went fourth.

A girl was standing in the middle of it.

She was about his age, and she had a cauldron, and she had **one soul ring**, and the first thing Lin Hao noticed — because the first thing he noticed was always the wrong thing, and he had learned to check it — was that she was standing very still in a way that was not nervous.

The second thing he noticed was the **vest**.

He was wearing it. Wu Zhangkong had given it to all five of them that morning without comment, a thin grey thing under the uniform that weighed almost nothing and felt like nothing, and Lin Hao had put it on because he had been told to and had not thought about it again.

**Thousand Refined Cloud Titanium.**

He thought about it now.

---

## Part 2: Four Seconds

She did not throw the cauldron. That was the thing the other four got wrong.

She *moved* it, and the room went into it — the floor, the air, the light, the nine metres of distance between them, all of it folded into a shape that Lin Hao's hawk-read gave him a full second of warning about.

And a full second was four times what he needed.

He did not dodge the shape. **He went to the place the shape had to be built from** — the girl's left hand, the half-turn of her waist, the breath she had taken a moment before she committed, because a cauldron that size does not move on a decision, it moves on a *decision made early* — and the Stormbringer was already there when the decision arrived.

Four seconds.

The cauldron came down on nothing. The girl was on the floor with her hair across her face and her hand open, and the room gave her back to herself slowly, the way a room does, and Lin Hao stood over her with his sword still out and understood two things at once.

The first was that the Thousand Refined Cloud Titanium vest he was wearing had not been used.

The second was that the room was not finished.

The floor gave again. The light thickened.

**The Hall was giving him the next one.**

---

## Part 2-B: What The Hall Did Next

It gave him a boy with a cauldron and **two soul rings.**

He knew the shape of it before it finished forming, because it was the same shape wearing more of itself, and because he had spent four years learning that a thing which works does not change its mind about working. He took the boy apart in about eleven seconds and did not enjoy it, because the boy had been very good, and because the boy had been **dead for several hundred years** and was doing his best anyway.

Then the floor gave a third time.

And nothing came.

Lin Hao stood in the middle of an empty room with his sword out and waited, and waited, and after a while he understood that the room was not hesitating. **The room was looking for a record and there was not one.**

They let him out.

Wu Zhangkong was standing on the other side of the door with his arms folded, and he had plainly been standing there for some time, and the first thing he said was not a comment on the fight.

"How many," he said.

"Three. The third one didn't come."

"I know it didn't come." Wu Zhangkong did not unfold his arms. "**There is no record of the Spirit Ice Douluo at three rings at your age. There is no record of anybody at three rings at your age.** The Hall keeps what legends *were*, boy, and it has been keeping them for a very long time, and it has nothing on file for what you are."

## Part 3: What Came Out

Wulin came out **green and purple.**

That was the phrase he used on himself, in the corridor, looking at his own arm with the detached interest of a boy who had been beaten up often enough to have standards. *"Green and purple."*

Xie Xie's appearance was worse. He had passed **one trial**, and he said the number the way you say a score in a game you had not known you were playing.

"I passed one," he said. "This test is too hard."

"I cleared three," Lin Hao said.

Xie Xie looked at him for a long moment.

"That's not— that's not how it works. It gives you *one* and then it throws you out. It threw me out. It threw **Wulin** out and he's got a vest made of a metal I can't pronounce."

"It gave me three," Lin Hao said. "And then it ran out."

"Ran out."

"It went looking for the next one and there wasn't one."

Xie Xie sat down on the floor of the corridor, which was not something he did, and said, "I would like everybody to notice that I passed one trial today and that I am not going to be able to think about anything else for a week."

Gu Yue came out last, and she came out with her eyes **blank**, and Wu Zhangkong looked at her and was, remarkably, not surprised.

"Gu Yue," he said. "Your opponent was the Spirit Pagoda's founder. The **Spirit Ice Douluo, Huo Yuhao** — back when he only had two soul rings."

There was a silence in the corridor that had a great deal of weight in it.

"The girl with the cauldron," Wulin said.

"Was him. At that age." Wu Zhangkong folded his arms. "So none of you should be discouraged. You were beaten by legends. That is what the **Hall of Heroes** is — it keeps them, and it lets you fight them, and if you do well in it and keep rising, you may even get the chance to fight the original **Shrek Seven Monsters** one day."

⚠️ *The Seven are named as a legend and a future possibility. No place is named. The lock holds.*

And then Wu Zhangkong looked at Lin Hao, and what he said next he said to Lin Hao alone, and he said it in the voice he used for things he had not decided how to feel about.

"Except you. You were not beaten by a legend, and I want to be careful about what I say next, because I have been wrong about you twice and I do not enjoy it." He unfolded his arms at last. "The Hall of Heroes has been measuring children against the greatest souls who ever lived for longer than this city has had walls. **It has never had to stop.** Every legend in it was a genius. Genius is common, boy — I have taught it, I have been it, it is a thing a body can be born with." A pause. "**What it has never had on file is a monster.** And it does not have you on file either, which is why it ran out."

---

## Part 4: Why Wulin Said Yes

Wulin did not think about it.

That was the part Lin Hao would remember — not the words, but the *speed*. Wulin had been asked a question and had answered it before the question had finished, which was not a thing Wulin did, because Wulin worked everything out and then said the worked-out thing.

"**I want to join.**"

Wu Zhangkong looked at him.

"You don't need to decide so quickly," he said. "Go home and ask your family first."

"I know what it is," Wulin said. He was not shouting. He was doing the thing he did when something had been true for a long time and had finally been said out loud in front of him. "The founder of the Tang Sect had **Bluesilver Grass.** The same martial soul as me. Everybody in Glorybound City knew the story — I knew it before I knew what a martial soul was. He had the trash one and he—" Wulin stopped, and started again, plainer, because that was the only way he could do it. "He had the trash one. And there's an organisation that remembers him for it."

Nobody said anything.

Xie Xie said, after a moment, "I want to join too."

"You just failed a trial."

"I'll pass the next one." Xie Xie shrugged, which cost him something, because his shoulder was the colour of a plum. "I don't want to miss out on this. I'll convince my family."

---

## Part 5: What Lin Hao Asked

He was the last one left in the corridor, and Wu Zhangkong had started to leave, and stopped, because Lin Hao had said his name.

"Teacher."

"Mn."

"The Hall of Heroes." Lin Hao said it carefully, because he had been working the question out since the room with no windows, and he had arrived at a part of it he did not like. "It keeps them. It lets you fight them."

"Yes."

"How?"

Wu Zhangkong looked at him for a moment.

"That's not a question a ten-year-old usually asks."

"It's the only question I have." Lin Hao said it without any heat, because he had learned that the flat version got further. "A dead man can't fight me. So it isn't him. It's something that remembers what he did, well enough to do it at me in a room with no windows, at the age he was, with the ring he had. That's not a person. That's a **record.**" He paused. "And if it's a record, then it's a record of exactly what he did and nothing else — and I beat records by not doing what the other person expects, and a record can't be unexpected by."

Wu Zhangkong was quiet for a while.

"You're right," he said.

"Then what's it for."

"To find out what you are when you cannot be clever." Wu Zhangkong said it in the flat voice, which was the only voice he had for the things that mattered. "You took four seconds off a boy with one ring and eleven off the Spirit Ice Douluo himself, and you will notice that you learned **nothing** from either of them, which is the opposite of what the other four got. Everybody else in that room was taught something by being hurt. That is not the same thing, and one of them is worth more."

Lin Hao stood in a corridor with three purple rings going slowly dim around his boots, and the **hawk-gold lock** at his temple, and the **marks on his forearms** faint under his sleeves, and he understood that he had been given the exact opposite of the answer he wanted and that it was the right one.

"I want to join," he said.

"Go home and ask your family."

"My father will say yes because he thinks I'm good at metal, and he'll be wrong, and it won't matter." Lin Hao said it without any particular feeling, because it was simply the arithmetic of his family. "So. I'm joining."

---

## Part 6: What Gu Yue Did Not Say

She was still standing at the end of the corridor.

She had not said whether she was joining, and Wu Zhangkong had not asked her, and Lin Hao understood — in the slow way he understood her, which was always too late to use — that she was the only one of the six who had been asked the question as a **formality.**

"Gu Yue," he said.

She looked at him.

"He told you who your opponent was."

"Yes."

"And you've been standing there for six minutes."

"Yes."

Lin Hao waited, because he had learned that with her the waiting was the whole of it.

"Everyone else got a legend," Gu Yue said at last. She said it very flatly, which with her meant it was the part that mattered. "**I got the founder of the Spirit Pagoda.**" She turned her head a few degrees. "Do you understand what that means?"

"That you're good."

"That they wanted to see what I'd do against **him.**" Her face did not change. "Not against a strong opponent. Against *him.* That is not a test of strength, Lin Hao. That is somebody asking a question about me."

There was a silence.

"And I don't know what I answered."

---

He wrote it in the ledger that night.

**Nine seconds. I spent them working out how to win. Wu Zhangkong says that is the wrong thing to spend them on. I don't know if he's right, but I know he isn't saying it to be unkind, which is the closest thing to kindness he has.**

**Gu Yue was not tested. She was asked about.**

Then he looked at the page, and he added the line he had been avoiding since the corridor.

*I want to join because I want to fight the record of a man who was very good, and I want to find out whether I am good or whether I am only difficult to predict.*

*I have never not been difficult to predict. So I have never known which one I am.*

He closed the book.

The hawk turned over in him — **one thousand two hundred and five** — and did not lean toward anything, because there was nothing in a room that small to lean toward.

Lin Hao lay on his back and thought about a girl with a cauldron who had been the founder of the Spirit Pagoda at the age of ten, and about the fact that he had taken her apart in four seconds, and about the fact that **the four seconds had taught him nothing at all** — and that this was the first time in his life that had happened to him, and that he did not know what to do with it.

*Right,* he thought.

And then Wulin came up the stairs with two bowls, because Wulin had decided some time in the last hour that Lin Hao was going to be awake and was going to be awake alone, and because he had never once in his life announced that he was doing a thing like that.

"Xie Xie says you cleared three and the Hall ran out."

"Nine seconds."

"Against Huo Yuhao." Wulin put a bowl down and sat on the floor, which was where he sat when he intended to stay. "He's saying it to everybody. Two second-years and a man who sells noodles."

"I know."

"He's not saying it like it's a joke." Wulin looked into his own bowl for a moment. "That's the part I noticed. Xie Xie says everything like it's a joke, and he's saying this one like it's a **number.** And I think the reason is that he has been the fastest person in our grade for three years and has just found out that the thing he is fastest at is not the thing that matters, and instead of being bad about it he has decided to be *loud* about you, which is the most Xie Xie thing I have ever seen a person do."

Lin Hao did not answer, because there was nothing to say that would not have made it smaller.

"And Gu Yue," Wulin said, "has been standing at the end of a corridor for eleven minutes, and Xie Xie has gone and stood at the other end of it, and he has not spoken to her, and he says she has not moved, and he says he is going to keep standing there until she does." He ate. "So. That's the evening. I thought you ought to have it, since you were up here."

**I have spent four years measuring what people can do and have never once measured what they do when nobody is measuring them, and that this is the second time today the ledger has been the wrong book.**

And went to sleep, and did not dream.
