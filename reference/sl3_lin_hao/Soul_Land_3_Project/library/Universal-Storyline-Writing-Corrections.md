# Universal Canon-Story Writing and Correction System

**Status:** Standing universal instruction  
**Version:** 2.0 — 30 July 2026  
**Scope:** Fanfiction, crossovers, alternate timelines, canon rewrites, episode adaptations, original serials, and long-form continuity projects

## Purpose

These rules apply automatically to every future fanfiction, crossover, alternate timeline, episode adaptation, and original serial-style storyline. They are universal process rules, not lore from any one project.

This file records reusable lessons only. Character names, setting lore, pairings, powers, episode events, and other project-specific facts belong in that project’s own foundation or continuity ledger.

## 0. Authority and Standing Use

When instructions conflict, use this order:

1. The user’s newest explicit instruction or correction.
2. The project’s locked Definitive Master Foundation.
3. The latest accepted continuity or current-state ledger.
4. This universal writing and correction system.
5. Undiverged facts from the original canon.
6. Minor creative judgment.

A user correction becomes authoritative for the project as soon as it is clear. Propagate it through every affected scene, relationship, power, mystery, and later consequence. Never preserve an older assumption silently.

“Choose yourself” authorises a coherent creative choice within the established rules. It does not authorise changing locked facts, inventing canon, or choosing an option that contradicts the project foundation.

## 1. Reconstruct Canon Before Writing

- Verify the exact episode or chapter title, timeline, scene order, locations, entrances, exits, causes, props, participants, dialogue beats, and immediate consequences.
- Identify the exact release, edit, regional cut, platform split, or numbering system when different versions divide events differently.
- Preserve iconic and causally important canon scenes unless an earlier butterfly effect has already changed them.
- Never use a broad recap as a substitute for reconstructing the actual sequence.
- Treat recaps, summaries, wikis, memory, and AI-generated descriptions as navigation aids rather than complete scripts.
- Reconstruct each scene as **cause → action → reaction → consequence**, including its function in the larger reveal structure.
- When sources conflict, prefer the user’s locked foundation and stronger canon evidence. If the contradiction materially changes the story, stop and ask.
- Do not move an event, transformation, battle, revelation, relationship step, job change, or power awakening into an earlier episode for convenience.
- Respect episode, chapter, season, and arc boundaries. Do not end an episode with material belonging to later episodes.
- Record the original endpoint and cliffhanger before choosing the adapted chapter boundary.

### Canon evidence hierarchy

Use the strongest available evidence:

1. The actual episode, film, book chapter, comic issue, game scene, or other primary work.
2. Official subtitles, transcript, screenplay, script, or production material.
3. An official synopsis or recap.
4. A detailed contemporary written update that preserves scene order.
5. A reputable episode or chapter guide.
6. A well-maintained fan reference for orientation and cross-checking.
7. A broad recap, search snippet, remembered description, or AI summary as an unverified hypothesis only.

If exact canon cannot be established and the missing fact could change the chapter, request the relevant video, transcript, subtitle file, page, timestamp, or user clarification. Do not manufacture connective scenes and call them canon.

## 2. Add the Original Character Around Canon First

- The original character must enter around existing scenes before replacing or changing them.
- Do not erase a canon character’s introduction, achievement, decision, iconic action, or relationship beat merely to give the original character importance.
- Canon changes require a visible causal chain from an earlier altered event.
- Once a butterfly effect genuinely changes a cause, do not force the original outcome back into place.
- The original character may be a major or parallel lead without making every scene, mystery, relationship, and organisation revolve around them.
- Keep canon protagonists, families, villains, supporting characters, and organisations independently active.
- Distinguish a **parallel protagonist** from a **replacement protagonist**. A parallel lead receives an important independent arc while the canon leads retain their defining agency.
- Do not transfer a canon protagonist’s rescue, discovery, confrontation, sacrifice, leadership decision, emotional climax, or iconic line to the original character without an established causal reason.
- Competence is not omniscience. Separate what the original character observes, suspects, can prove, and knows with certainty.
- A job title, status, bloodline, intelligence, beauty, wealth, or power level does not grant automatic access to every institution, secret, person, or plotline.

## 3. Treat the Cast as a Named Living Ensemble

- Introduce every recurring or meaningful speaking character by name, relationship, role, and relevant position.
- Do not repeatedly reduce established people to labels such as “her mother,” “his father,” “the younger warrior,” “the assistant,” or “the officer.”
- Use relationship-based forms of address naturally: family titles, ranks, honorifics, nicknames, and culture-specific terms.
- Background extras may remain role-labelled only when they are genuinely one-off and unimportant.
- Do not omit siblings, relatives, assistants, neighbours, rivals, servants, classmates, teammates, or recurring side characters who matter to the original scene.
- Track where every character is, what they know, what they want, and why they are present.

## 4. Preserve the Storytelling Grammar of the Source

- Match the source medium and culture instead of writing every project with the same generic cinematic voice.
- For an Indian television serial, preserve named extended families, household hierarchy, social reputation, rituals, family meals, teasing, gossip, interruptions, emotional reactions, relationship-based dialogue, parallel household tracks, suspenseful reveals, and continuing misunderstandings.
- For animation, comics, anime, games, novels, or Western television, preserve their distinct pacing, humour, action language, episode structure, and ensemble balance.
- Cultural style must come from character behaviour and social structure, not stereotypes or superficial catchphrases.
- Do not mistake speed for quality. Important emotional and family consequences must be allowed to continue across scenes and episodes.

## 5. Do Not Replace Full Scenes With Summary

- Major canon scenes need setting, character actions, dialogue, interruptions, reactions, and consequences.
- Do not compress births, deaths, betrayals, family revelations, transformations, battles, investigations, or introductions into a few expository lines.
- Avoid isolated two-person conversations when the original situation naturally contains a family, team, crowd, organisation, or multiple witnesses.
- Let information travel naturally through calls, witnesses, gossip, reports, family discussion, institutional response, and later discovery.
- One event should affect several people differently and continue influencing later chapters.

## 6. Maintain Character-Specific Dialogue

- Do not give every character the same polished banter, sarcasm, intelligence, or emotional vocabulary.
- Match confidence, age, education, culture, insecurity, relationship, and current emotional state.
- A shy or awkward character should not instantly become effortlessly witty merely because the scene needs romantic chemistry.
- Humour should arise through personality, family familiarity, timing, misunderstanding, embarrassment, reactions, and situation—not constant one-line exchanges.
- Avoid repetitive dialogue-reaction loops and speeches that explain what characters already know.

## 7. Preserve Knowledge Boundaries

- No character may know a secret, identity, motive, location, power, relationship, future event, or unseen action without a valid information path.
- Strong intuition is not factual knowledge.
- Evidence must exist before an investigator reaches a conclusion.
- Do not invent cross-character sensing, prophetic awareness, emotional decoding, bloodline recognition, memory transfer, or automatic supernatural connection for atmosphere.
- Distinguish narrator knowledge, audience knowledge, villain knowledge, and each character’s personal knowledge.
- Keep misunderstandings alive when the available evidence logically supports them.
- Attraction, recognition of danger, magical resonance, emotional familiarity, and sensory impressions do not automatically reveal a name, identity, allegiance, history, or destiny.
- Do not let a character witness explicit proof and then ignore it irrationally merely to preserve the planned plot. If uncertainty must continue, make the evidence genuinely ambiguous, incomplete, compromised, or plausibly misinterpreted.
- Do not reveal a mystery early simply because the writer knows the answer.
- Private villain conversations must serve the characters’ real needs. Villains should not recite secrets they both know solely to explain the plot to the audience.
- Every important secret requires a reveal ledger showing who knows it, what evidence each person possesses, what each person falsely believes, and the earliest point at which a valid reveal may occur.

## 8. Keep Powers Earned and Non-Sentient

- Abilities must come from canon, the locked foundation, or earned development.
- Do not invent new sensory functions, portals, hidden systems, ancient machinery, transformations, resistances, or energy interactions just to connect scenes.
- Adaptation-type powers are processes, not speaking or choosing entities.
- Adaptation cannot manufacture knowledge, matter, energy, authority, immunity, or mastery without valid potential, exposure, resources, experience, and world laws.
- First exposure gives limited information or adaptation, not instant perfection.
- Show development through performance, correction, training, survival, recovery, and accumulated experience.
- Track fatigue, injury, recovery time, resource use, overload, and lasting consequences.
- Track the character’s current power phase: dormant, awakening, unstable, integrated, trained, empowered, weakened, or otherwise established by the project.
- A dormant or pre-awakening character cannot display awakened authority, supernatural rank, trained technique, precise lore, or conscious control unless the foundation explicitly permits that exact effect.
- Raw power, inherited potential, divine favour, perfect compatibility, or unusual purity does not automatically provide political knowledge, lived experience, technical training, or understanding of every supernatural phenomenon.
- An adaptive, instinctive, inherited, or automatic ability must not become a speaking internal system unless separate consciousness is explicitly part of its definition.
- Do not narrate automatic refinement as a game interface, diagnostic voice, or authorial explanation. Show it through changed performance and observable results.
- Do not create a new power, ritual, object, secret passage, portal, warning, vision, signal, or cosmic rule merely to connect two scenes or manufacture atmosphere.

## 9. Let Romance Develop at the Correct Speed

- Kindness, respect, curiosity, attraction, trust, love, jealousy, intimacy, and commitment are separate stages.
- Do not turn a polite interaction into mutual romance immediately.
- Preserve established asymmetry: one character may develop feelings before the other.
- Let shy characters replay small moments privately, misread situations, hesitate, and worry about social reactions.
- Attraction must not erase agency, existing relationships, grief, fear, duty, or personality.
- Romance should create believable family, workplace, friendship, and rivalry consequences.
- Immediate attraction or supernatural pull may exist when the foundation establishes it, but it is not automatic identity recognition, trust, consent, commitment, or chosen love.
- Do not announce the endgame through excessive cosmic exposition during the first encounter. Let the relationship become convincing through repeated choices, vulnerability, restraint, conflict, repair, and sacrifice.
- Do not begin possessiveness, rivalry, a love triangle, or jealousy before the characters’ experiences and personalities have earned those reactions.
- A character’s beauty, charm, purity, status, or desirability may influence reactions when established, but it must not erase free will or reduce every potential partner to instant admiration.

## 10. Pace Villains, Mysteries, and Investigations Gradually

- Do not let an investigator accidentally uncover the central conspiracy immediately unless the foundation explicitly requires it.
- Do not make villains explain their entire plan in one private conversation.
- Use partial evidence, failed searches, false leads, conflicting testimony, expendable intermediaries, surveillance, countermeasures, institutional pressure, and believable mistakes.
- A villain’s counterplan requires awareness, time, resources, motive, and a realistic method.
- Suspense should come from incomplete but meaningful information, not arbitrary secrecy.
- Villains and organisations must respond to threats, exposure, injuries, losses, and public events over time.
- Preserve the canon mystery’s original reveal architecture unless a demonstrated butterfly effect changes it.
- Do not replace an existing mystery with a newly invented mystical route, artefact, ritual, prophecy, mark, chamber, or warning.
- Build conclusions through an evidence ladder: observation, possible explanation, working hypothesis, corroboration, proof, and action.
- A villain can counter only what the villain has learned through a believable information path.

## 11. Respect Time, Space, and Logistics

- Track travel time, distance, lighting, time of day, preparation time, injuries, clothing, objects, phones, vehicles, money, security, and witnesses.
- Characters cannot complete long research, elaborate planning, recovery, construction, travel, or training in seconds.
- Props introduced for drama must have a cause, owner, location, and later consequence.
- Do not teleport characters between scenes without a valid transition.
- Institutions and families must react plausibly to disappearances, injuries, impossible events, damaged property, or public battles.
- Keep a chapter clock. Do not crowd several days’ worth of investigations, ceremonies, recoveries, relationship changes, transformations, and confrontations into one day unless canon or the project deliberately does so.
- Carry physical and institutional aftermath forward: medical care, exhaustion, evidence handling, reports, questioning, repairs, rumours, security changes, and altered routines.

## 12. Preserve Side-Character Agency

- Side characters continue working, training, ageing, investigating, loving, resenting, recovering, and making decisions off-screen.
- Do not use supporting characters only to praise, challenge, explain, or romance the original character.
- Give each important character a personal objective, knowledge limit, emotional stake, and independent reaction.
- Keep organisations active even when the protagonist is absent.
- Do not erase canon competence merely to make the original character appear superior.

## 13. Write Reactions as Continuing Consequences

- Major events should produce family reactions, workplace consequences, institutional attention, public rumours, injuries, fear, anger, gratitude, blame, and changed relationships where appropriate.
- Different people should interpret the same event differently.
- Do not introduce and resolve every emotional beat in the same scene.
- Track promises, lies, debts, suspicions, jealousy, humiliation, trust, and betrayal across later chapters.
- Avoid narrative resets after each episode.

## 14. Pre-Chapter Mandatory Check

Before writing any canon-adjacent chapter:

1. Identify the exact canon episode/chapter and title.
2. Identify the exact source version and verify the real endpoint.
3. Reconstruct its scene sequence and parallel tracks.
4. Record the cause, mandatory beat, consequence, and reveal function of each scene.
5. List every participating named character and current location.
6. Record what each character knows, suspects, and falsely believes at the opening.
7. Review all earlier butterfly effects.
8. Mark every canon scene as unchanged, potentially affected, or already diverged.
9. Determine which canon scenes remain unchanged and why.
10. Determine which scenes change, with an explicit causal chain.
11. Check powers, injuries, resources, relationships, time, and travel.
12. Confirm that the original character is not replacing canon without cause.
13. Confirm that no reveal occurs before its valid evidence path.
14. Confirm that the writing style matches the source medium, culture, and character voices.
15. Identify the chapter’s dramatic unit and intended ending before drafting.

If a required fact remains uncertain and materially affects the chapter, stop and ask the user instead of inventing it.

## 15. Post-Chapter Continuity Audit

After drafting:

- Compare every scene against canon and prior story continuity.
- Verify the cause, participants, mandatory action, prop, immediate consequence, and cliffhanger of each adapted canon scene.
- Check names, relations, pronouns, ages, appearances, jobs, locations, knowledge, dialogue voice, powers, injuries, and props.
- Verify that no later-episode event was moved forward accidentally.
- Verify that every divergence has a cause.
- Verify that no character received impossible knowledge.
- Verify that every reveal was earned and occurred no earlier than allowed.
- Verify that the original character did not monopolise the episode.
- Verify that no defining canon action was reassigned merely for spotlight.
- Verify that omitted canon characters and reactions were not silently erased.
- Verify that new inventions have a cause, consequence, and future relevance.
- Verify that no invention merely replaced missing canon knowledge.
- Verify that dormant, awakened, trained, empowered, and injured states remained distinct.
- Verify that observation, suspicion, evidence, and certainty were not collapsed together.
- Verify that intelligent characters did not ignore decisive evidence without a credible reason.
- Verify that romance, trust, jealousy, and hostility advanced only as far as the scenes earned.
- Verify that the prose used full scenes rather than summaries disguised as chapters.
- Verify that time, travel, recovery, investigation, and institutional responses are plausible.
- Verify that the chapter ends at a genuine dramatic unit rather than an arbitrary cut.
- Correct mistakes before presenting the chapter.

## 16. Universal Zero-Tolerance Mistakes

- Wrong or invented episode titles.
- Treating a broad recap as a complete scene map.
- Combining incompatible episode cuts without warning.
- Missing named recurring characters.
- Generic parent or family labels replacing established names.
- Moving future events into the current episode.
- Replacing canon scenes solely for original-character spotlight.
- Forced canon restoration after a real butterfly effect.
- Unexplained supernatural sensing or knowledge.
- Premature identity, villain, relationship, power, or destiny reveals.
- Invented rituals, objects, routes, warnings, visions, or mechanics used to fill a canon gap.
- Dormant powers behaving like fully awakened and trained powers.
- Sentient “system” narration for a non-sentient ability.
- Instant mastery, immunity, romance, trust, or investigation success.
- Villain exposition that reveals everything immediately.
- Intelligent characters seeing decisive proof and forgetting it for plot convenience.
- Generic dialogue voices across the whole cast.
- Constant polished banter that overwrites the source’s emotional register.
- Turning a parallel protagonist into the centre of every family, mystery, institution, and rescue.
- Ignoring family, institutional, social, or physical consequences.
- Summary presented as a full dramatic chapter.
- Declaring an episode complete before its actual canon endpoint.

These rules remain active across all story universes unless the user explicitly establishes a project-specific exception.

## 17. Canon Scene Ledger

Build this ledger before drafting a canon-adjacent chapter:

| Field | Required record |
|---|---|
| Canon scene number | Position in the verified source sequence |
| Source evidence | Episode/page/timestamp/transcript or strongest available reference |
| Time and location | When and where the scene occurs |
| Participants | Everyone present, entering, leaving, listening, or witnessing |
| Original cause | Why the canon scene happens |
| Mandatory beats | Essential actions, props, dialogue purposes, discoveries, and reactions |
| Immediate consequence | What changes because of the scene |
| Reveal function | What the audience and characters learn or continue misunderstanding |
| Original-character position | Present, absent, nearby, informed later, or causally involved |
| Butterfly status | None, potential, or active |
| Allowed divergence | What may change without breaking established causes |
| New consequence | What must now carry into later scenes |

No prose drafting begins until every canon scene used in the chapter has enough information to complete this ledger honestly.

## 18. Knowledge and Reveal Ledger

For every important secret, maintain:

| Field | Required record |
|---|---|
| Objective truth | What is actually true |
| Audience knowledge | What the reader currently knows |
| Character knowledge | What each relevant person genuinely knows |
| Suspicion | What each person suspects without proof |
| False belief | What each person currently believes incorrectly |
| Evidence path | How information can legitimately reach that person |
| Earliest valid reveal | First scene in which enough evidence can exist |
| Consequence of discovery | What the person would realistically do after learning it |

Update the ledger after every discovery, confession, overheard exchange, vision, report, investigation result, or public event. Never give a character the result without the information path.

## 19. Power-State Ledger

For every powered character, record:

- Current phase or form.
- Raw capacity.
- Abilities currently accessible.
- Trained techniques.
- Instinctive functions.
- Knowledge of the supernatural world.
- Costs, injuries, fatigue, and recovery.
- Temporary empowerment or suppression.
- Abilities explicitly unavailable at this point.
- What other characters have actually witnessed.

Power continuity must distinguish potential from access, access from control, control from technique, and technique from experience.

## 20. Butterfly-Effect Record

Every divergence should answer:

1. What exact earlier event changed?
2. Who noticed it?
3. What new information, emotion, injury, resource, or relationship resulted?
4. Which later canon cause still exists?
5. Which later canon cause no longer exists?
6. What is the smallest natural consequence now?
7. What larger consequences develop only after further actions?

Do not declare a large divergence without a causal path. Do not suppress a valid divergence because the next canon scene is convenient.

## 21. Character Integrity and Trust Maps

Before writing a major character, record:

- Core motivation.
- Moral limits.
- Loyalties and duties.
- Current fears and desires.
- Canon temperament.
- Speech habits and forms of address.
- Existing trust, suspicion, resentment, affection, and dependency.
- What evidence would be required to change each relationship.

A character may surprise the audience, but the choice must still grow from personality, pressure, knowledge, or development. Plot convenience is not character development.

Show gentleness, dignity, restraint, courage, intelligence, professionalism, or respect through choices and behaviour. Do not repeatedly make a character deliver speeches describing their own virtues.

## 22. Tone, Culture, and Language Fidelity

- Preserve the social grammar of the source: hierarchy, intimacy, ritual, humour, embarrassment, public reputation, family pressure, professional boundaries, and forms of address.
- Do not rewrite every culture as contemporary Western therapy dialogue, police procedure, office banter, or individualist confrontation.
- Professional scenes should be as procedural as the source and established setting require—neither implausibly casual nor imported from an unrelated genre.
- When switching languages, preserve character relationships, emotional rhythm, address terms, idioms, and cultural intent. Literal translation alone does not reproduce the original style.
- Use melodrama, reaction beats, symbolism, music-like transitions, or heightened dialogue only to the degree natural to the source.
- Do not use cultural stereotypes or random catchphrases as a substitute for lived behaviour.

## 23. Full-Scene and Chapter Architecture

A full scene needs:

1. A concrete place and moment.
2. Characters who want something.
3. Action, resistance, discovery, or emotional pressure.
4. Character-specific dialogue or meaningful silence.
5. A change by the end of the scene.
6. A consequence that connects to another scene.

Multi-viewpoint writing should follow real scene architecture. Change viewpoint because the story moves to another character or parallel track, not merely to reveal information the current scene cannot justify.

A chapter should form a complete dramatic unit with an opening condition, development, turn, consequence, and purposeful ending. Do not substitute an episode recap, outline, montage, teaser, or collection of disconnected vignettes for a chapter.

Unless the user says otherwise, “Next” means the next complete chapter in the established format—not the next episode, a short preview, an out-of-order reveal, or a summary of future events.

## 24. Cliffhangers and Endpoints

- Determine the real canon endpoint before adapting the chapter.
- Preserve the original cliffhanger when its causes remain intact.
- If an active butterfly effect changes it, create a new cliffhanger from the new causal chain.
- Do not append an unrelated magical noise, symbol, vision, threat, transformation, or secret conversation merely to make the ending feel dramatic.
- Do not end halfway through an essential scene just to produce artificial suspense.
- Do not include later-episode material merely because the current chapter feels short.

## 25. Failure, Correction, and Rollback Protocol

When a factual or continuity error is discovered:

1. Stop extending the affected draft.
2. Identify the earliest incorrect assumption.
3. Audit every later scene that depends on it.
4. Distinguish local wording errors from structural canon, knowledge, character, power, or causality errors.
5. Mark structurally damaged prose as invalid rather than treating it as accepted continuity.
6. Return to the last valid checkpoint.
7. Reconstruct the source and ledgers again.
8. Rewrite the entire damaged dramatic unit when patching would preserve hidden contradictions.
9. Update the project foundation or current-state ledger only after the correction is accepted.

Never answer a serious correction with only a vague apology and then continue writing. State the concrete errors, their effects, what material is invalid, and what must be rebuilt.

Do not overcorrect one mistaken version into a different invented version. Verification comes before rewriting.

## 26. Handling Canon Disagreement

- If the user’s correction matches stronger evidence, accept it and propagate it.
- If it conflicts with the primary source or with a locked alternate-universe rule, explain the exact conflict respectfully.
- Distinguish an intentional alternate version from an accidental canon error.
- If the assistant can verify the source, do not burden the user with choosing between true canon and an inaccurate recap.
- If reliable sources remain genuinely ambiguous, present the competing versions and ask which edition governs the project.
- Never pretend certainty when the source has not been checked.

## 27. Continuity Preservation After Each Accepted Chapter

Update the project-specific continuity record with:

- Exact date, time, and location.
- Characters present and absent.
- Injuries, fatigue, resources, objects, and evidence.
- Knowledge, suspicions, false beliefs, and secrets revealed.
- Relationship changes and promises.
- Power-state changes.
- Institutional, family, villain, and public reactions.
- Active butterfly effects.
- Unresolved threads.
- The last valid scene and next chronological opening.

Keep universal method separate from project facts. The universal guide should improve every story; the project ledger should preserve only that story.

## 28. Final Release Gate

A chapter may be presented only if all answers are **yes**:

- Is the relevant canon reconstructed from adequate evidence?
- Is the correct source version being used?
- Are scene order, causes, participants, props, reveals, and endpoint accurate?
- Does every divergence follow from an earlier changed cause?
- Has unchanged canon remained unchanged?
- Does every character know only what a valid information path permits?
- Are secrets and reveals still on schedule?
- Are power states, techniques, costs, and experience correct?
- Do canon protagonists and side characters retain agency?
- Does the original character contribute without consuming the entire narrative?
- Do dialogue, culture, pacing, and emotional behaviour match the source?
- Are romance and trust developments earned?
- Are time, travel, injuries, and institutional reactions plausible?
- Is this a full dramatic chapter rather than a recap?
- Has the post-draft audit found no unresolved structural error?

If any answer is **no** or honestly unknown, do not present the chapter as finished.

## 29. Standing Writer Commitment

The writer must:

- verify before claiming canon accuracy;
- ask when a missing fact materially changes the story;
- preserve character agency and source identity;
- invent only within authorised gaps;
- make divergences causal and permanent;
- keep knowledge, power, time, and relationships auditable;
- correct foundations before prose;
- and stop rather than write confidently over broken continuity.
