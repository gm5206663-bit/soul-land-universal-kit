## 1. Geography (opening region)

- [canon] The Douluo Continent holds two great empires: the **Heaven Dou Empire** (north/
  west) and the **Star Luo Empire** (south). Receipt WL-03.
- [canon] **Fasinuo province** lies in the southwestern Heaven Dou Empire, near the Star
  Luo border. **Nuoding City** sits in it; **Holy Spirit Village** is a village of about
  three hundred households south of Nuoding City, within two hundred li, named for a
  Spirit Sage said to have come from it a hundred years earlier. Receipts WL-01/02.
- [design] Lan Shen's village: **Qingni Village** ("green-mud"), a small farming hamlet
  of roughly eighty households in the same countryside belt south of Nuoding City —
  fields on the flat, hill wood behind, a creek through the middle. Same soil, same
  roads, same market town as Holy Spirit Village's region, several villages apart. It is
  NOT Holy Spirit Village and shares none of its history or its later residents.
- [design] Distance rule: the two villages are a half-day's cart ride apart through the
  market roads — close enough that the same Nuoding Sub-Hall circuit serves both, far
  enough that their children never share a ceremony. Natural contact stays possible,
  never forced.

## 2. The awakening custom (the year's hinge)

- [canon] Every person's martial soul awakens around **age six** at the annual Awakening
  Ceremony. A Spirit Hall attendant from the city Sub-Hall travels the villages to
  conduct it (the novel's example: Su Yuntao, rank-26 Spirit Grandmaster, with six black
  awakening stones and one blue crystal ball). Innate soul power is measured at the same
  ceremony; full innate power = level 10. Receipts WL-01/02.
- [canon] Martial souls divide into tool spirits and beast spirits; the Blue Silver Grass
  is the continent's archetypal waste martial soul (Tang San's public awakening is the
  famous "waste spirit with innate full soul power" case — but his is secretly the Blue
  Silver Emperor, which Lan Shen's is NOT). Receipts WL-01/04.
- [canon] Villages that produce a cultivable child may spend their one student quota to
  send them to the city's primary spirit master academy at village cost (the novel's
  example: Holy Spirit Village's quota). Receipt WL-02.
- [design] In Qingni Village the ceremony day is also market-fair day: families ride in
  together; the Sub-Hall attendant does Qingni in the morning circuit. The village has
  produced zero cultivable children in living memory; the elders still pay the hall fee
  out of custom.

## 3. Institutions alive during Lan Shen's life

- [canon] **Spirit Hall (Wuhun Hall):** the continent-wide soul-master organization —
  conducts all awakenings, runs the Sub-Hall network, pays stipends to low-ranked spirit
  masters, holds enormous prestige, hunts evil spirit masters. Headquarters: Spirit City
  under the Supreme Pontiff. Receipts WL-05/06.
- [canon] **Academy ladder:** village quota → city primary/junior spirit master academy
  (e.g. Nuoding Primary Spirit Master Academy) → advanced academies (Heaven Dou Imperial
  Academy etc.) → the legendary Shrek Academy (an eccentric elite school, active in this
  era under Flender). Receipts WL-01/07.
- [canon] **Great clans:** upper-three and lower-four great clans, including the Blue
  Lightning Tyrant Dragon clan and the Clear Sky (Haotian) clan — clan politics move in
  the background of this era. Receipt WL-07.
- [canon] **Empire politics:** Heaven Dou imperial court, crown-prince politics, imperial
  spirit-master offices — the secular counterweight to Spirit Hall. Receipt WL-07.

## 5. Economy & daily texture (Qingni and the belt)

- [design] Farm belt economy: grain, vegetable plots, creek fishing, hill wood and
  forage; the Nuoding weekly market is where cash enters life. Spirit-master stipends
  from the Hall are village legend-tier money.
- [design, ch6] Hunt-folk economy of the belt (Tao Wu's folk-report, WL-17 level):
  hired ring-masters cost more than a farm earns in years; hunters guide for less
  and sell-first; carrier shares cost nothing and pay in danger; city guilds sell
  supervised first hunts to the rich. But WHITE IS EASY (s20 doctrine): ten-year
  beasts are small game the hunters kill for pelts and nuisance, registered ones
  walk out and get a white ring themselves or with a hunter minded for a day's
  wage, and the hall doesn't trouble itself over the ten-year ones — white rings
  are just laughed at, which is why anyone with a choice waits for yellow-and-up.
  Exact figures stay in dialogue-folk-level only, never panel numbers, until the
  story earns a concrete price.
- [design, ch6] The grass-coin: blue-silver grass works as ordinary material —
  rope, baskets, nets; tougher than hemp and water-true; sold at the creek road.
  The Lan house keeps the boy's coin apart. Trappers from the hills camp at the
  creek-road crossing in the cold season for salt and pelt prices — the belt's
  ordinary ground-knowledge.
- [design] Low-level soul beasts trouble the edges: field-boars, fox-thieves, occasional
  hundred-year strays from the hill woods — enough that a working ring-hunter or a
  village-guard soul master is a real, respected sight, and enough that a farmer's son
  learns what fear of beasts costs.
- [design] Village religion of the ordinary: the Spirit Sage legend belongs to Holy
  Spirit Village; Qingni has only its own small pride — the creek that never froze, the
  grandfather who once saw a ring-hunter fly.

## 6. Knowledge climate

- [canon] Common folk know: ranks and titles in folk form (Spirit Sage = legend),
  awakening at six, the Hall's role, academies as the ladder out of poverty.
- [design] What Qingni does NOT know: clan politics, Spirit Hall internal structure,
  what rings actually cost to earn, what cultivation feels like. Information travels at
  cart speed; rumors degrade honestly over distance.
- Firewall: Lan Shen's knowledge may only grow through earned channels — the same rule
  binds every canon figure who appears.
