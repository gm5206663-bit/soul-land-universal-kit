# 03 — STORY LAW

The expensive lesson. Every failure below was committed in a real serial, at length, and
each one was invisible from the inside until a reader said the word "nonsense."

---

## 1. THE CORE BELIEF

The job is not "write an exciting scene." The job is:

**Build a coherent living world → put characters in it → simulate what would really happen
→ convert that simulation into prose without breaking established truth.**

Priority order when these conflict:

```
Consistency  > convenience
Causality    > coincidence
Character agency > author control
Earned progression > arbitrary escalation
Consequences > spectacle
Truth        > memory
```

---

## 2. THE FIVE FAILURES

This is the whole file in miniature. Everything after is detail.

| Failure | Symptom | Cure |
|---|---|---|
| **No canon spine** | Nothing from the source world actually happens on the page | Name the canon beats you touch. Five minimum. |
| **Nothing contested** | No antagonist, no rival, no test. The protagonist's only verb is "keep." | Every chapter gets a threat and a face. |
| **No felt progression** | Ranks, rings and skills never move | Rank gates are structure. Use them. |
| **One register repeated** | Every chapter reads the same | Five registers, ≥3 per chapter. |
| **Apparatus outgrew the story** | Panels and ledgers longer than scenes | Slim panel. Bookkeeping lives in the kit. |

### Failure 1 — no canon spine

A serial ran fifteen chapters and 33,000 words without a single canon character or event
appearing. It was set in the Star Dou Forest, and it never once mentioned the thing the Star
Dou Forest exists for: **soul beasts die there so humans can take rings.**

The cure cost one sentence. *"Spirit Hall wants rings, and sends hunters into the forest to
take them."* Suddenly the protagonist — a soul beast living in that forest — had a spine, an
antagonist, and a payoff, and the ending recontextualised everything before it.

**Ask, before every chapter:** what does canon say happens in this place, at this time? If
the answer is "nothing," you have chosen the wrong place or the wrong time.

### Failure 2 — nothing contested

The same serial's protagonist had one verb across fifteen chapters: *keep*. Nothing opposed
him. Nothing tested him. Nothing he did could fail.

A story where the protagonist cannot fail is not a story, however lovely the sentences are.
Every chapter needs:

- a **threat** — a thing that can actually hurt someone, not "tension"
- a **face** — an opponent with a name, a motive, and lines of dialogue

The rebuild added nine named humans to a serial that had two. Every one of them was an
antagonist or a witness, and every one of them earned their space by wanting something.

### Failure 3 — no felt progression

Soul Land hands you a progression system that is *already dramatic*: you cannot pass a
multiple of ten without killing something and taking its ring. That is a built-in chapter
rhythm, a built-in moral problem, and a built-in reason to leave home.

A serial that never shows a rank move, a ring taken, or a skill earned has thrown away the
franchise's best gift. Show the number. Show the cost.

### Failure 4 — one register repeated

Fifteen chapters of interior meditation in the same voice. Technically fine prose,
unreadable in sequence.

See `07_PROSE_LAW.md` for the five registers and the ≥3 rule.

### Failure 5 — apparatus outgrew the story

Eleven-field status panels at the top of every chapter and a seven-item footer at the bottom
of each. The bookkeeping was longer than some scenes.

**Slim panel at the top. No footer.** Bookkeeping lives in the kit, where it belongs and
where a reader will never have to see it.

## 3. THE PER-CHAPTER CONTRACT

Every chapter must satisfy all seven. Check them **before** drafting, not after.

```
1. CANON BEAT   — what canon does this touch, and how does it stay true?
2. THREAT       — what can hurt someone here?
3. FACE         — who opposes, named, with dialogue?
4. MOVEMENT     — what changes: rank, ring, skill, bond, resource, knowledge?
5. BUTTERFLY    — what does the protagonist's existence change about canon?
6. REGISTERS    — ≥3 of the five (see 07_PROSE_LAW.md)
7. TURN         — what is true at the end that was not true at the start?
```

**A chapter that is only interior meditation fails and does not ship.** That is a hard rule,
not a guideline. It is the specific shape failure 4 takes.

---

## 4. CAUSALITY, NOT COINCIDENCE

Every important beat answers three questions:

> **Why does this happen? Why now? Why this way?**

If you cannot answer all three, the beat is not ready.

And every divergence propagates:

```
trigger → immediate effect → secondary effect → character reaction
        → institutional reaction → long-term consequence
```

A rule that is never broken is not a butterfly — it changes nothing downstream. If your
divergence changes nothing, do not claim it changed the story.

---

## 5. THE LIVING WORLD

While the protagonist does X, everyone else is doing something. The world functions when
the protagonist is absent.

Practical tests:

- **Pressure items age.** A debt unpaid in chapter 3 is worse in chapter 12.
- **Silence is a decision.** Someone not answering is a choice, not a gap.
- **An honest "not now" beats accidental forgetting.** If a thread is parked, park it
  deliberately and say so in the ledger.

---

## 6. STORY ECONOMY

Every element justifies the narrative space it consumes.

**No:** thin hinge chapters, copy-machine rooms (the same scene type repeated), kit-recap
presented as story, audits presented as story.

**Yes:** play full. Use the whole kit, the whole cast, the consequences. A named job
finishes in the turn it is given.

---

## 7. WHAT TO DO WHEN A READER SAYS "NONSENSE"

Do not re-word it. Find **what it skipped**.

The five failures above are the diagnostic. Walk them in order and be honest:

1. Is any canon actually happening?
2. Is anything contested?
3. Does anything progress?
4. Does every chapter sound the same?
5. Is the apparatus longer than the story?

In the case this kit was built from, all five were true simultaneously, and the correct
response was a full rebuild — not a revision. If more than two are true, you are not
editing. You are rebuilding. Say so plainly and do it, because polishing a foundation-less
serial wastes everyone's time and produces the same verdict twice.
