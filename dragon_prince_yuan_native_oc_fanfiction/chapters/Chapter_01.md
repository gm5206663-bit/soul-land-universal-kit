# Chapter 1: The Second Son in the Green Smoke

The inner palace of Great Zhou burned with green smoke.

It rose from blue sandalwood stones set inside bronze lamps, thin spirals twisting upward until they disappeared beneath carved beams and golden shadows. The fragrance was calm, expensive, and clean enough that ordinary people would breathe once and feel their thoughts settle.

Zhou Xu did not feel calm.

He stood beside the left screen with a small jade cup held between both hands. The wine inside was pale, watered until it carried more plum fragrance than heat. Master Qin had told him three times that children should not drink in a sickroom. Zhou Xu had answered, three times, that he was not drinking for courage.

He was drinking because his mouth tasted like iron whenever Yuan’er’s poison woke.

That answer had made Master Qin sigh and stop wasting breath.

On the bed, Zhou Yuan trembled.

Blood-red qi crawled beneath his pale skin like a living wound. It moved along his face, neck, and wrist in thin threads, then gathered toward his palm, where something dark and dragon-shaped squirmed under the flesh. Every time the thing stirred, a faint howl brushed the walls of the room.

Not a sound exactly.

A resentment.

Zhou Xu’s fingers tightened around the cup.

His eyes began to ache.

They had been strange since before he understood what strange meant. When he was very young, nurses said his eyes were too clear, as if washed by rain no dust could touch. When he learned to read, he saw ink strokes as more than ink: where the brush had hesitated, where the wrist had pressed, where intention had broken. When Master Qin sorted herbs, Zhou Xu could tell which root carried muddy bitterness before it was cut open. When palace guards trained, he saw tiny gaps where breath, foot, and blade failed to agree.

No one had given the eyes a proper name.

Zhou Xu privately called them useless whenever they showed him what he could not fix.

Today, they showed him Zhou Yuan’s pain too clearly.

The blood-red poison was not simply qi. It had teeth in its movement. It bit as it traveled. It hated the body it lived inside and needed that body alive so it could keep hating. The blue sandalwood smoke tried to soothe the air around it, but the poison chewed through calm like a worm through fruit.

Master Qin stood at Zhou Yuan’s bedside with a copper mirror held steady in both hands. Soft light poured from the mirror and covered Zhou Yuan’s body. Under that light, the blood-red threads slowly retreated, inch by inch, unwillingly, like snakes forced back into a hole.

A single incense stick of time passed.

No one in the room moved more than necessary.

Zhou Qing stood with his left hand behind his back. His right sleeve hung empty at his side. Even after so many years, that empty sleeve could still make a room colder than winter. Behind the king’s shoulders, faint power rose and sank, sometimes like fire seen through storm cloud, sometimes like muffled thunder pressed under stone.

Qin Yu sat close enough to reach the bed if Zhou Yuan cried out. Her palace robes were beautiful, but no beauty could hide the sickness in her face. Every time Zhou Yuan’s breath hitched, her hand pressed against her chest as if pain had crossed from son to mother through the air.

Zhou Xu looked away from her quickly.

His eyes showed too much there as well.

Qin Yu’s life warmth was not whole. It had hollows in it, places where something precious had been drawn out again and again. She was still gentle. Still beautiful. Still his mother in every way that mattered after the day she had taken an orphan into a palace that already contained too much grief.

But her light was thinner than it should have been.

Zhou Xu hated that his eyes knew it.

At last, the blood-red qi withdrew into Zhou Yuan’s palm.

The dragon-shaped lump beneath the skin settled.

Master Qin’s shoulders loosened. He lowered the copper mirror and bowed toward Zhou Qing and Qin Yu.

“Congratulations, your majesties. His Highness has passed this triennial ordeal. There should be no great problem for the next three years.”

Qin Yu’s fingers trembled against the bedcover.

Zhou Qing’s jaw tightened, but relief still crossed his face for one breath before worry buried it again.

“Master Qin,” he said, “Yuan’er is already thirteen. At this age, the eight meridian channels should be found, and cultivation should begin. This time…?”

Master Qin’s expression dimmed.

Zhou Xu did not need the answer. He had seen the old man search. He had watched light enter Zhou Yuan’s body and return with nothing but red resentment clinging to its edge.

Still, hearing it hurt differently.

“Your Majesty,” Master Qin said softly, “this old one was still unable to find His Highness’s eight meridian channels.”

The green smoke continued to rise.

For a moment, it seemed the whole palace breathed only because the lamps did.

Zhou Xu looked at Zhou Yuan’s face. His brother’s brows were no longer twisted so fiercely, but the weakness left behind was almost worse. Pain could make a person look alive because it forced the body to fight. After the pain, Zhou Yuan looked thirteen and younger than thirteen at the same time.

In this world, cultivation began in the body.

The eight great meridian channels were the first gates. Find them, open them, draw in Genesis energy, and a person stepped onto the path of a Genesis Master. Without them, talent, will, books, pride, and royal blood could all become things one carried while standing before a locked door.

Zhou Xu’s own body had never felt locked in that way.

That was another thing he did not say aloud.

Since childhood, his breathing had been clean. Food became warmth without leaving sludge behind. Bitter medicines separated themselves inside him into what could be used and what had to be expelled. When Master Qin guided him through basic body-sensing, Zhou Xu had felt fine threads of possibility long before he knew what to call them.

He was not the sacred dragon.

He had never mistaken himself for that.

But his body was clear.

Too clear, sometimes, when he stood beside Zhou Yuan and wished clarity could be shared like wine poured from one cup into another.

Qin Yu spoke, voice low and strained. “The poison erupts once every three years. Each outbreak is worse than the last. If Yuan’er still cannot find his meridians, what happens three years from now?”

Master Qin was silent for too long.

Zhou Xu’s jade cup gave a soft click as his thumb pressed against it.

Master Qin finally said, “After three years, external suppression will likely lose its effect. If matters remain unchanged, His Highness’s hopes are… bleak.”

Bleak.

Adults liked words that wore clean clothes over ugly bones.

Zhou Xu lifted the cup and drank the watered plum wine in one swallow. It was barely wine, really. More sweet than sharp. But it cut the iron taste from his mouth for half a breath.

A young voice came from the bed.

“Does that mean… I only have three years left?”

Qin Yu stiffened.

Zhou Qing turned sharply.

Master Qin froze.

Zhou Yuan’s eyes had opened. They were tired, dark, and too calm for someone who had just heard his death measured in years.

“Yuan’er…” Qin Yu whispered.

Zhou Xu stepped forward before he remembered he had not been asked to move.

Zhou Yuan’s gaze shifted to him for an instant. It did not ask why Zhou Xu was there. Of course Zhou Xu was there. He had been there three years ago too, though then he had been smaller and had cried behind the screen until Zhou Yuan woke and pretended not to notice.

This time neither of them cried.

That did not mean they were stronger.

It only meant the wound had grown up with them.

Zhou Yuan slowly raised his hand.

The blood-red lump in his palm wriggled faintly, like a tiny resentful dragon trapped beneath the skin.

“Father. Mother.” His voice was hoarse but steady. “I believe it is time to tell me exactly what is happening to me.”

Qin Yu looked as if the words had struck her.

Zhou Qing closed his eye for a breath. When he opened it again, something old and broken stood behind the king’s dignity.

“It is time,” he said.

He sat beside the bed.

Zhou Xu stopped near the foot of it. No one told him to leave. That, more than any permission spoken aloud, told him he was meant to hear this too.

Zhou Qing’s hand trembled when it touched Zhou Yuan’s hair.

“That is the Dragon’s Resentment Poison.”

“Dragon’s Resentment Poison?” Zhou Yuan frowned.

Zhou Xu’s eyes stung again. The name fit too well. Once named, the thing in Zhou Yuan’s palm seemed to become clearer: not only poison, not only qi, but resentment left with purpose.

Zhou Qing continued, “Yuan’er, you should know that you are the sacred dragon of our Zhou Clan.”

Zhou Yuan gave a bitter laugh. “A sacred dragon who cannot even find his eight meridians?”

The words were sharp because he turned them on himself.

Zhou Xu hated them more for that.

“Listen first,” Zhou Xu said quietly.

Zhou Yuan looked at him. For once, he did not argue.

Zhou Qing’s gaze lingered on Zhou Xu for a moment. In that look was a father’s gratitude and a king’s calculation, both tired. Then he began telling the story of Great Zhou.

Fifteen years ago, Great Zhou had not been the small country the world now saw. It had been powerful. Countries had paid homage. Its might had pressed across the continent. The Wu clan had been a loyal vassal, granted the hereditary title of King Wu for service, permitted privilege and authority enough that no one had thought their roots were growing around Great Zhou’s throat.

Then the Wu clan rebelled.

They had hidden strength for years. They had drawn conferred kings to their side. Great Zhou was defeated in less than a year and forced south, back to the ancestral land that was now all it could protect.

Zhou Xu had heard fragments of this before. Every adopted child in a royal house learned early which silences belonged to politics and which belonged to grief. But fragments were not the same as the whole blade.

Zhou Qing spoke the prophecy as if each word had rusted in his mouth.

“Python and sparrow swallow the dragon, and Great Wu will rise and flourish.”

The room seemed to darken around the green smoke.

Zhou Yuan repeated it under his breath. “Python and sparrow swallow the dragon…”

Zhou Xu’s eyes hurt so sharply that the edges of the lamps blurred.

For a heartbeat, he saw not the palace but a pattern: dragon qi inside a city, python and sparrow qi outside it, three newborn fates forced onto one altar by hands that had waited centuries. He did not see the future. He did not see the past as memory. He saw the shape of what Zhou Qing described because his eyes followed origin when origin was named.

And the shape was ugly.

“They were waiting for you to be born,” Zhou Qing said.

Zhou Yuan’s face lost more color.

Qin Yu covered her mouth, but her sob still escaped.

“When you were born,” Zhou Qing said, voice trembling, “a mystical sign appeared in the heavens. Dragon qi swirled around you, and a dragon roar shook the land. It was the sign of the sacred dragon. Your eight meridian channels naturally opened at birth. You would have skipped the Channel Opening stage and reached Qi Nourishing. That is the one-in-a-billion sacred dragon blessing.”

Zhou Yuan stared at him.

Zhou Xu had known Zhou Yuan all his life as a boy with frail wrists, sharp eyes, too many books, and a stubbornness that survived pain by becoming quiet. To imagine him born beneath dragon qi bright enough to shake the land did not feel impossible.

It felt stolen.

Zhou Qing’s voice fell. “But at the same moment, King Wu’s wife gave birth outside the city to a son and a daughter. Python qi coiled around the boy. Spirit sparrow qi gathered over the girl. They too carried destiny. King Wu had delayed their birth for three years, waiting for yours. Those born in the same year, month, and day can devour each other’s destiny. The Wu clan’s centuries of loyalty were all for that moment.”

“It was a scheme,” Zhou Yuan whispered.

“Yes.” Zhou Qing’s remaining hand clenched until the knuckles whitened. “King Wu entered the city and threatened us with the lives of Great Zhou’s people. He wanted your sacred dragon blessing taken before your mother and me.”

The king stopped.

Tears of blood slid from his eyes.

Zhou Xu had seen Zhou Qing angry. He had seen him commanding ministers, drilling guards, and standing before maps with one sleeve empty and one hand enough to make grown men lower their heads. He had never seen the king look so powerless.

Master Qin took up the words when Zhou Qing’s voice could not.

“His Majesty fought King Wu on Great Zhou Mountain to protect His Highness. He lost an arm. If King Wu had not feared that others might destroy the sacred dragon blessing before it was seized, His Majesty might have died there.”

Zhou Xu’s gaze dropped to the empty sleeve.

He had grown up with that absence. He had learned to read brush strokes from Zhou Qing’s left hand because there was no right. He had watched servants design clasps, sword-belts, and robes around what was missing. As a child, he had thought the sleeve was part of what made a king a king: a visible price paid for the country.

Now he understood it was also a price paid for the child on the bed.

Master Qin continued, “To seize the destiny smoothly, King Wu swore that Great Wu would not take even half a step into Great Zhou for a hundred years.”

“A hundred years,” Zhou Xu said softly. “After cutting the dragon out of a newborn child, he called that restraint.”

No one rebuked him.

Qin Yu suddenly bent forward and hugged Zhou Yuan, sobs breaking through all the dignity palace robes could provide.

“Yuan’er, my pitiful son. Mother has let you down.”

Zhou Yuan stiffened, then raised his hand slowly to touch her sleeve.

Zhou Xu’s Clear Origin Eyes burned.

He saw the hollows in Qin Yu’s life warmth flare as emotion shook her body. Too much grief moved through too weak a foundation. Before he could warn anyone, she coughed.

Blood spilled onto Zhou Yuan’s hair.

“Mother!” Zhou Yuan cried.

Zhou Xu was already moving. He caught the medicine cloth from the tray before the nearest maid could reach it and pressed it into Zhou Yuan’s hand, then stepped aside as Master Qin rushed forward. Gentle energy poured from Master Qin’s palm into Qin Yu’s body, calming the chaos in her qi and blood.

Zhou Yuan’s fingers shook around the cloth.

“Master Qin,” he said, voice tight, “what happened to Mother?”

Master Qin looked at Zhou Qing, then at Qin Yu, then finally at Zhou Yuan.

The old man’s face held the expression of someone who knew truth would hurt and had no medicine left to soften it.

“Your Highness, after your destiny was stolen, Her Majesty infused her own essence blood into your body. After that, she gave you blood every year so you could live until today. Each time, her lifespan was cut by three years. Over twelve years, thirty-six years have been taken. Her health has been greatly damaged. She has less than ten years left.”

Zhou Yuan went very still.

Sometimes rage arrived as shouting.

Sometimes it arrived as silence so complete that everyone heard it.

Zhou Xu looked at his brother’s face and saw the moment pain changed direction. Zhou Yuan had endured his own suffering with a scholar’s restraint and a prince’s pride. But Qin Yu’s blood on his hair did what Dragon’s Resentment Poison had never done.

It made hatred simple.

“What did you say?” Zhou Yuan whispered.

His eyes reddened.

The blood in his face surged. Killing intent, young and raw, rose from a body that could not yet cultivate properly and still made the lamps tremble in Zhou Xu’s vision.

“Wu clan,” Zhou Yuan said, each word shaking, “you dare harm my mother. You truly deserve to die.”

Zhou Xu did not speak over him.

That vow belonged first to Zhou Yuan.

But inside Zhou Xu’s chest, something answered.

His Adaptation Talent had always been quiet. It was not a voice and not a blessing written in gold. It worked like breath learning weather, like bone learning weight, like clear water refusing mud. When the palace air filled with Dragon’s Resentment Poison, grief, blood, and the old fate of Great Zhou, that quiet talent did not create a miracle.

It adapted.

It remembered the taste of resentment.

It learned the shape of stolen destiny without claiming it.

It taught Zhou Xu’s pure body not to flinch away from the pressure but to stand beside it.

Zhou Qing spoke again, voice hollow. “King Wu seized your destiny and granted it to his son and daughter. Since then, Great Wu has been protected by dragon and phoenix, allowing their empire to flourish. Everything Great Wu has today was built on what they stole from you.”

He looked at the blood-red lump in Zhou Yuan’s palm.

“When the sacred dragon blessing was forcibly taken, intense resentment was born. King Wu deliberately sealed that resentment inside you, forming the Dragon’s Resentment Poison. It devours your essence blood and strengthens itself. One day, when it matures, it will erupt and completely devour your life force.”

“And your sacred dragon root was damaged,” Master Qin said softly. “Your naturally open eight meridian channels disappeared. Until now, they have not shown themselves.”

The room fell quiet again.

Zhou Yuan looked at his palm.

Zhou Xu looked at Zhou Yuan.

The blood-red dragon under the skin twisted once, faintly, as if pleased to be remembered.

Zhou Xu set the empty wine cup down on the tray.

It made a small sound.

Everyone looked at him.

He bowed first to Zhou Qing and Qin Yu, then turned to Zhou Yuan. It was not the bow of a subject to a prince. It was the bow of a younger heart to a grief that had the right to stand first, even between brothers of the same age.

“I cannot cure it,” Zhou Xu said.

His words were plain because lies would have been insulting.

His eyes could see too many broken lines. His body could remain clean beside poison. His talent could learn pressure, impurity, pain, and hostile qi faster than it should. None of that made him Cang Yuan, none of that made him a saint, and none of that gave him the right to pretend Zhou Yuan’s wound was simple.

Zhou Yuan’s red eyes shifted toward him.

Zhou Xu continued, “I cannot give back what Great Wu stole. I cannot replace your dragon. I cannot return Mother’s blood.”

Qin Yu’s expression twisted.

Zhou Qing’s eye dimmed.

Zhou Xu raised his head.

“But I am your brother. If you have three years, then I have three years to stand beside you. If there is a way to find your meridians, I will help search. If there is a path through books, runes, medicine, ancestral ground, or blade, I will walk enough of it to know where it cuts. If I see poison, I will say poison. If I see a lie, I will say lie. If I see you trying to carry this alone, I will be annoying until you fail.”

For a moment, Zhou Yuan simply stared.

Then, despite the red in his eyes and blood in his hair, the corner of his mouth moved.

“You are already annoying.”

Zhou Xu nodded. “Then I am prepared.”

The smallest sound escaped Qin Yu.

It was not quite a laugh. It hurt too much to be one.

But it was not only a sob either.

Zhou Qing looked at the two boys: one born under the sacred dragon sign and left with poison, one found in war’s ashes and raised beneath a borrowed surname until the word borrowed became meaningless. Grief did not leave his face, but something steadier returned under it.

Great Zhou had lost too much.

It had not lost everything.

Zhou Yuan slowly closed his fingers around the blood-red mark in his palm.

“Father,” he said. “Mother. Master Qin. Zhou Xu.”

He lifted his head.

His body was frail. His meridians could not be found. His fate had been stolen before he knew what fate was. His mother’s lifespan had been spent to keep him alive, and his father’s arm had fallen on a mountain he could barely remember.

Yet his eyes no longer looked like someone waiting for the next three years to end.

“I will live,” Zhou Yuan said.

The words were not loud.

They did not need to be.

“I will find my meridians. I will remove the Dragon’s Resentment Poison. I will take back what was stolen.”

Zhou Xu felt his Clear Origin Eyes cool for the first time that night.

Not because the poison had weakened.

Because something in the room had aligned.

Outside the city, years ago, python and sparrow had cried beneath stolen light.

Inside the city, the sacred dragon had wailed and scattered into green smoke.

But green smoke did not always mean ending.

Sometimes smoke marked what still burned.

Zhou Xu reached for the empty jade cup, then thought better of it under Master Qin’s glare.

Fine.

There would be time for wine later.

Tonight, there was only the bed, the blood, the truth, and two sons of Great Zhou learning the shape of the same enemy from different wounds.

One carried the stolen dragon.

One carried eyes too clear to look away.

Neither would forget.
