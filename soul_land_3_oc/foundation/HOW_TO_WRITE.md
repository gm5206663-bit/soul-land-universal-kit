## 3 · R21 · THE ACTOR LAW (armed — the chapter's shape)

1. **The OC is the actor.** In every chapter he *wants* something, *tries*, meets *resistance*, and pays or wins. Scenes, not receipts.
2. **Event quota.** At least **two OC events that change a value** (goal · knowledge · relationship · resource · safety · standing · promise · skill · position · debt). At least one **costs something real** — money, trust, standing, a loss, a price paid.
3. **Canon complete, told tight.** Every canon beat keeps its scene, its outcome, its order — **unskipped** — but rendered at load-bearing length. No double-description; no narrating what the dialogue already did. Canon is **≤ half the chapter's words**; its full beat list stands in the coverage doc.
4. **Reaction.** At least one canon character behaves differently **because he is there**. Witnessing is not reacting; a nod is not reacting.
4b. **Contact.** The chapter must show the OC *with* canon's people **on the page** — not adjacent, not heard-of: a name from a canon mouth, a word to him, a hand, at least one per stretch. (Ch 5 v2 owed this to Na'er; paid three times across the years.)
4c. **No idle stretch.** No winter, arc, or block where the OC only cultivates, waits, or is summarized. Every stretch carries an act with a stake.
5. **Lines.** The six lines move — or the receipts say why one held, and the chapter still moves *standing* or *knowledge*.
6. **Failure exists.** Reads can be wrong; trades can be refused; promises can cost him. No plot armor; the failure state is written down before the success.
7. **Momentum.** No three chapters without a gain or a fired gun.
8. **Voice.** New chapters measure: **ALL avg ≤ 12 (aim 8–10), median ≤ 8, 0 over 60, dialogue-forward, fragments as structure** — the register object's own band.

## 4 · The engine (what happens before the prose)

1. **Locks.** Canon block fetched and read (R1). The OC's spine as one sentence, in the lock-4 form: *Su Yan wants ___, and ___ stands in the way, and it will cost ___.* If the sentence can't be filled, there is no chapter yet.
2. **The two-engine blueprint** (bible-side note, before prose): the canon beats marked public · private · parallel · butterfly-touchable; **the OC beats written as events** (want · obstacle · cost · outcome); the **collision seams** named (the town, the classroom, the market, the walk — ≤ canon's allowed touchpoints).
3. **Conflict type**, named and rotated (physical · social · economic · institutional · relational · investigative · moral). No adjacent repeats when another type is causally available; escalation on an axis that is not "bigger power."
4. **Three questions** per major event: why · why now · why this way.
5. **Branch check**: name the favourable / bad / catastrophic outcomes; pick by causality; write the failure state down **before** the success.
6. **After the draft, the five consequences** (bible-side): immediate · character · world · what's now impossible · what's now possible.

## 5 · What the drill adds (R21's tests, every chapter)

- **The actor test** — in his scenes, is he *doing*, or *watching / filing / noting*? (His verb test, run literally.)
- **The reaction test** — name the canon character whose behavior changed because he was there.
- **The event count** — ≥ 2 value-changes, ≥ 1 costing something real.
All three answers go into `audits/Chapter_0N_audit.md`, next to the deletion test.
