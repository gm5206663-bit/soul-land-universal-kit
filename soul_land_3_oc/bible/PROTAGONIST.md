## 2 · The boy and his people ([design], author-gated)

- **Name:** **Su Yan** — 苏 (revive; the family's word for what he did) + 晏 (a clearing sky). Chosen for the boy who came back from the illness; never explained in prose. (CJK 0 law: prose carries the pinyin only.)
- **Home:** **Glorybound City** — canon's own small town on the border between the ocean and the eastern coast (SL3 ch 1); the Su family lives above the physician's clinic. His academy: **Red Mountain Academy** (canon: elementary, white roofs and red walls, two thousand students, the circular Awakening Chamber with its seven floors). **G1 RESOLVED (author, 2026-10-02): same town as Tang Wulin — same streets, the same Awakening Day; the two roads run adjacent and separate.**
- **Father — Su Heng:** the city's physician; martial soul a **silver needle** (tool-type, support line), **four rings** (Soul Ancestor, rank 46) — **the first man of his trade in Glorybound; the Pagoda counter stands for him**. A quiet, methodical man. For six years he was the last line between his son and the dark — needles, decoctions, night watches — and the ledger of that war is the household's hidden wound.
- **Mother — Zhou Hui:** **two rings (Soul Grandmaster) — Golden Millet**; of the city's grain house (the Zhou family's warehouses fed the city for three generations) and its sharpest ledger before she was twenty. **Not an ordinary woman, and the lane knows it** — two rings, a ledger, and eyes like an abacus; she chose the counter over the climbing and never regretted it once. The household's spine and its arithmetic; the one who never once looked at her son like a bill. *(The full house web — grandparents, uncles, aunts, cousins, friends — lives in `bible/FAMILY_AND_HOUSE.md`, which wins over older notes; corrected 2026-10-03 on the author's word, MISTAKES H17.)*
- **Family standing:** "good" in the exact way canon means when it sends a family to the ceremony with hope: not grand, but solid — good name, enough money, a house with a stove room and a shelf of books, parents who show up. Six years of medicine bled the cushion; the recovery season emptied the last of it into hope. **After the reading, the world's pity becomes the family's new weather.** [design]

## 3 · The illness and the recovery ([design] on a canon base)

- **The illness ("the fading")** — **G2 RESOLVED (author, 2026-10-02): one problem wearing two faces.** His **soul-veins** never took root properly — the part of a person a soul grows in was built a size too small — and the **body** paid the bill: strength, warmth, weight, winters spent entirely indoors. Six years of medicine (his father's needle-soul, decoctions, night watches) could only **hold the line**. The soul itself was damaged by the same failure — which is why, when it finally woke, it came out **half-finished**. The mystery that stays open: *why the line held on its own in the last winter* (the recovery just before the awakening — the author's lock). Nobody in-world can explain it, and the boy was never told the question.
- **The recovery:** in the **last winter**, it stopped. He gained weight; by the first warm week he was running the lane past the grain warehouses; the physicians found nothing left to treat and no explanation to write. **The ceremony was weeks away.** The family's gratitude has no address, and that unanswered question (why then?) is the story's first buried seed.
- **The bill (the author's lock):** the illness is *why* — six years of the Adaptation Talent's entire pre-awakening budget went to **viability** (there was nothing left over for foundation, and the soul came out a half-finished thing); and the depleted childhood is *why the reading is low* (canon: soul-power development is affected by the body's condition and nourishment — AW/D-02). **The boy's low start is not bad luck; it is a receipt.** Nobody in-world knows this.

## 4 · What he is at the start (one honest paragraph)

Six years old, undersized for his age and embarrassed about it, with a reader's hands and his father's habit of asking why. He grew up inside two rooms and a sickroom window, so the world he knows best is the one he was told about — and he wants all of it, greedily, the way only a boy who almost didn't get it can. He is not timid and not tragic; he is **hungry**. His parents rebuilt a normal life around him with crooked care. He keeps a stick by his bed he uses as a sword. He does not know — no one knows — that he is carrying the second of two things: a soul the world laughs at, and the engine that spent six years saving him and has now, for the first time, nothing else to do.

## 4b · The second job, and the hands he brings to it (author directives, 2026-10-02)

- **He feeds the soul.** From the first night, Su Yan treats Luo Sanpao as a project: **good food, herbs, and everything he can get his hands on, as much as he can afford.** Canon already says this creature needs real nourishment (and canon's own feeders used humble white radishes); the author's directive is that this boy does it **seriously, from day one** — a lit stove, a full bowl, and a boy doing sums about what he can buy.
- **Where the skills come from.** **Cooking, from his mother** (Zhou Hui runs the grain house's books and the family's kitchen; she taught him knife-work, broth, and the arithmetic of a pantry). **Medicine and herbs, from his father** (Su Heng is the city's physician; the clinic's shelves were the boy's first library, and six years as a patient made him fluent in the language of doses and roots — he can tell a warming root from a cooling one, and he knows that medicine is prices and patience as much as knowledge).
- **What that money costs.** The household is **good**, not rich; the six sick years bled the cushion. Feeding a growing soul eats into what a physician's family can spare, and the ledger of that is part of the story's bookkeeping — every bowl, every root, every month.
- **Spiritual power (author directive).** Because of the past — six years of the hidden engine holding his body and soul-veins together while he read, watched, and learned patience — **his spiritual power is unusually good for his age.** Until the academy measures it, it shows as quality: long focus, quick study, a mind that stays level when other children panic. Never a readout, never named.
