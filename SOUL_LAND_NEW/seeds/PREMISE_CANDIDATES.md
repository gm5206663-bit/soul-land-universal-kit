# PREMISE CANDIDATES — pick a lane (or riff on one)
### New Soul Land story, new OC. Three different engines below — different martial-soul concepts,
### different emotional cores. None of them re-tread 顾渊 (illusion/eyes) or Lin Hao (multi-element sword).

---
## The one decision that shapes everything else first: CANON DISTANCE

- **(D1) Far from canon** — story set in a corner of the Douluo continent the canon never visits,
  same era, no canon cast on screen (or cameos only much later). Maximum freedom; we own the world
  model; canon spine is background law, not schedule.
- **(D2) Same era, parallel track** — concurrent with canon events (e.g., the SL1 generation's decade)
  but our OC walks a different road; canon events matter as distant weather until they intersect.
- **(D3) Later Douluo timeline** — a decade+ after a canon era, standing on its ashes/peace; our OC's
  story is what the canon heroes left behind.

Any lane below works in any of D1/D2/D3. Pick the pair (lane + distance) that excites you.

---

## LANE A — 「账」 The Debt Ledger

**Concept:** A merchant's son in a small border town awakens a *tool-type* martial soul the academy
writes off on sight: a **ledger book** — it shows nothing of rank or battle power. What it actually
does is *record value*: it reads the price of anything in soul terms — a beast's years, a person's
true debt, a sect's claim, a lie's cost. Society despises tool-types; the world runs on souls as
hierarchy. He starts as the shop-counter boy who knows, better than anyone, *what things are worth* —
and the story is him learning that worth and value are not the same thing.

**Engine:** Information + economy as power. He can't out-punch the strong, so he out-leads, out-
trades, out-simulates — until the ledger starts showing him prices in *human* lives and he has to
decide what he's willing to spend.

**Core question:** What do you do when you can price everything — including yourself?

**Flavor:** underdog rise, underestimated-tool, city/sect politics, sharp dialogue, consequences of
economic power in a soul world. Fresh, no rival in canon.

---

## LANE B — 「双魂」 Two in One Body

**Concept:** At the soul-awakening ceremony a child of no particular clan awakens — twice. Two souls
in one body: **his own** (a modest, ordinary martial soul he can't even name proudly) and a **guest
soul** that should have been dead for a hundred years — an old master's remnant, wearing him like a
house that was standing empty. The guest brings a ruinous, once-feared martial soul, its instincts,
its enemies, and its debts. The host must learn to *share* a body with a tenant who is stronger,
older, and losing patience.

**Engine:** Two-protagonist-in-one — internal dialogue as real conflict, not metaphor. Growth is
negotiation: when do they cooperate, when does the guest seize control, whose life is this?
Each has their own goal; they cannot both win forever.

**Core question:** If someone else's power is keeping you alive — how much of yourself do you pay for it?

**Flavor:** identity drama, possession horror turned into partnership, earned power-shares, a hidden
history to unfold, high stakes for failure. Risk: needs discipline to not become melodrama — that's
an architect challenge we can beat.

---

## LANE C — 「守」 The Last Guardian of a Dead Village

**Concept:** As a child, the OC's mountain village — a tiny keeper-shrine community that "fed" a
forest spirit-beast for generations in exchange for peace — is destroyed in a single night. Not by
beasts alone. Someone *used* the village as a lever against the beast, and it burned. The OC is the
only survivor, orphaned of a family and a duty in one night, and their awakened martial soul is the
shrine itself: a **guardian-type** that carries the village's bound spirits — the dead speak through
it, protect through it, and *mourn* through it. Every ring they take is a new voice in the shrine.
They are not the strongest; they are the one who was left to keep watch.

**Engine:** Duty vs. self. The dead ask for the thing the living should avenge. The forest beast is
not the enemy and not the ally. The one who ordered the burning is still alive, still powerful, and
has no idea the village left a witness. Canon-distance D1 fits this lane naturally — the story owns
its region, its beast, its villain.

**Core question:** What do you owe the people who are already gone?

**Flavor:** grief as strength, found family, a living landscape, a patient revenge that could corrupt
the protector into what he protects against. Deepest emotional well of the three; slow-burn power
with a haunting cost.

## What I still need from you once you pick a lane

1. **Canon distance:** D1 / D2 / D3 (above).
2. **Name feel:** Chinese-name protagonist; any preference for the given name's meaning
   (e.g., 守-ish/账-ish tones) — or I propose names once the lane is set.
3. **Tone dial:** light-adventure → mixed → darker. (Recommended: mixed.)
4. **Romance:** present or absent in early arcs (both old projects handled this carefully — we
   choose consciously, not by accident).
5. **Scale ambition:** village→region story, or continent-scale destiny. (Both fine; affects pacing.)

Or **bring your own seed** — a soul concept, an image, a name, a "what if" — and we build around it.
No wrong answers; this is the fun part. Pick the lane and we lock the bible next.
