# Chapter 06 — The Hem Road

The hem road was not a road, it was what was left when forest decided not to be forest. It was a strip of hard ground between trees and tilled ground. Feet and cart rims and weather had beaten it flat, grass grew up its middle and was beaten down again. Villages knew it, hunters knew it, no one owned it. That was why his father had said hem, and north, and never in trees.

He walked it north because honest ground asked no questions and led where he needed.

First morning was cold and thin, mist sat low over fields, sun had not cleared ridge yet. Air smelled of damp earth and distant smoke, ledger was there, like floor under thoughts, honest and whole, thirty. Stature grew larger, hair showed grey with frost tint, amber eyes turned ice-amber, hands became claws, nails sharpened like daggers. Faint grey fur showed on arms, cold air left his mouth, wind curled around his feet. At eleven, possession held whole morning and asked for more.

Frame had arrived as freight across a season, that freight now walked with purpose. Height taller than village boys same age, shoulders broader, wrist no longer knew sprain, back carried winter wood without complaint. Nose stood at door each morning and read whole day from air, amber in eyes caught firelight. Grey tint showed in hair when sun hit it, village dogs stopped lifting heads when he passed. Wood passed him at peace when he sat bracken in possession. Mother's coat, rebuilt as road coat with pelts sewed into hem, weighted when lifted. He knew better than to count it in front of her.

The hem road taught walking in a different alphabet, it was not ridge trails following. It was flat, and flat has its own lies, feet want to fall same way each time. Same way is how blisters come, he had learned at nine that walking was not about going faster. It was about ground not knowing you passed, at mastery now, it was limb. He could walk whole day and at evening his feet did not know they had walked.

That was because footwork was one line now, not three, walking and running and mountain stride had all been footwork. Each had hit a hundred at eight, nine, ten, they did not go to High alone. They fused into one quiet step, now his feet threw no dust even carrying weight and road coat. That was why he could pass a field of sleeping geese without one head lifting. Which fool masters so many step techniques separately, they fuse.

Hunter's sense walked with him, six years woodcraft behind father had become reading. Nose read whole day before sun cleared ridge, ears heard dogs hearts at fence. Snow got age and direction via nose, eye found weakness in fence where board would give. He did not name it aloud. Ridge had taught it as work, not as numbers, and body kept it as work.

Stillness sat with him at noon. He could sit hem road edge whole short hour, spear across knees, counting cart ruts and wind and field birds, without fidget boy's blood owes. Body outlasted boy in it, something old and wolfish held sitting boy could not. That was ghost line at work, patient, willing to endure attrition until prey tires and exposes weakness.

Soul power flowed where eye went, not where hand went. He had learned it second midwinter when power finally obeyed sight. Now power went where eye went, like water finding low ground, that made mending easier, observation walked with it. Hunter's eye and tallykeeper's counting had fused, eye found weakness, counting kept tally without meaning to.

Body arrived where mind went first, spear point leading by hand's width was not trick but training. He had learned it winter of eight with father's spear. Second spear had stood against burned cedar through whole first hunt. Now on shoulder, oiled and balanced, it was more companion than weapon, point led, body followed, not other round.

Reading and understanding walked at villages, villages wrote prices on boards, he read them without stopping, he understood without asking. He had been tallykeeper whole adult life in another world, here that became clear mind counting without meaning to, steps. Coins in hem, days to city, season to city if honest. Father said hem roads around forest skirts season to Shrek City. Same autumn boy with Spirit Eyes walks into forest far east, two roads meeting, he did not count aloud. Ledger counted.

Second day he passed first hem village, forty roofs like his own ridge, smoke from chimneys low, children chasing chickens. Women carrying water, men mending roofs before winter, he stopped at well because rope was frayed. "Frayed since spring," the woman at the well said. Well needed rope more than he needed hurry, he spliced it because hands knew work. Point leading, thrust recover low guard, boar point leads moving thing hand's width. Hands knew work because spear flow taught hands to lead. Woman at well gave him water and half flatbread for it. She did not ask where boy his age learned to splice rope so it held. Village knows when not to ask.

He ate flatbread walking and watched village children chase dogs that did not chase him, that was new and honest. Dogs smelled something old on him now, not wolf, not boy, but both, grey line gave vitality recovery predator frame. Dogs knew predator frame and gave it space, he liked that better than barking because barking wastes air.

Third day he passed second village, sixty roofs, larger, with small shrine and market board. He read board without moving lips, prices for grain, for salt, for iron, he understood. He had read many boards now, fluent reading came from years of boards and father's tally marks. He did not show it because showing reading at eleven invites questions.

He found work at edge of village, old man had fence leaning, post rotted at base. "Rotted at the base," the old man said. "Whole fence leans on it." He set post straight with one lift because flowing body made it easy. Not show, just point leading, weight through legs, not back. Old man watched him set post straight with one lift and did not ask how boy his age did it. Village knows when not to ask, and Ye Cang knew when not to answer. Old man let him sleep in loft for it, loft smelled of hay and supper below, warm and honest. He paid with mended fence and straightened gate, not coin.

Fourth day was river day, hem road met small river that cut across fields, no bridge, only ford with stones. Water cold, ankle deep, clear, he crossed without getting hem wet because quiet step taught where stones held. On other side he sat and watched water for hour because water teaches patience too. Patience as limb was not only sitting, it was also watching without needing to act, river was honest teacher.

He saw tracks at river edge, deer small morning, wolf larger night. He read them because hunter's eye and beast sense walked together. He did not follow because he was not hunting today, he was walking. But he noted tracks because tallykeeper counted without meaning to, that was clear mind at work.

Fifth day was wind day. Wind from north sharp, carrying smell of forest and snow even though snow had not come yet. He pulled road coat tighter, coat weighted with pelts sewed into hem, mother's work. He remembered her hands sewing, not counting cost in front of her. He carried that weight as promise, not burden, because promises weigh differently than pelts.

He passed third small hamlet, only fifteen roofs, no market board, only well and shrine, children staring at spear. He nodded once, as father had nodded once when boy read wind right, and that nod held more weight than other men's speeches. Children did not follow, he liked hamlets better than villages because they asked less and gave more quiet.

At noon fifth day, he sat hem road edge, spear across knees, counting cart ruts and wind and field birds without fidget. Cart ruts told story, one cart heavy left wheel wobbling passed morning, one cart light fast passed yesterday. Field birds told wind change before air changed, he counted because clear mind counted without meaning to. That was not numbers prose may never state, just tally.

Sixth day he met hunters, two men with bows, one with dog, coming south. They looked at his frame and spear and amber eyes and grey tint hair when sun hit. They nodded, as hunters nod to hunters, he nodded back, they did not ask about rings. He did not show them, to world yellow hundred-year, true color only Ledger if any.

"Road ahead is soft after rain," one hunter said. "Walk the edge." He thanked them with clear speech. Plain speech grown into clear speech at a hundred, grown mind's plain speech six years local tongue saying what needed saying no more. They went south, he went north, both honest, both keeping hem.

Seventh day he reached edge of forest skirt, forest stood east dark even at distance. Great Star Dou Forest far east, but this was smaller wood, still beast country. Hem road kept west of trees, as father said hem north never in trees. He obeyed because father had walked hem roads and knew. He walked west edge where fields met trees, where hard ground was honest, where no one owned it, and that honesty mattered more than speed.

Night seventh day he found shed at edge of field, empty, roof whole. He sat back to wall, spear point leading, and let pool run. Pool was dark dense now, water of deeper well fed by bloodlines and years, reborn mind wasting no hour and no motion, method running while he carried water, while he sat black pools, while he slept, where other souls worked one hour his worked every one and it compounded.
