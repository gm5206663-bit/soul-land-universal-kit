# Chapter 20 - The Answer Behind the Door

Lan Xuanyu sat very still.

The dormitory was quiet enough that he could hear his own breathing, quiet enough that the faint hum of the academy walls sounded louder than Qian Lei's occasional mutter from the next bed.

He should have been sleeping.

He had just passed Teacher Ji's test. He had eaten until even the canteen aunties stared. He had escaped the lesson that had trapped most of the Elite Junior Class until their faces were pale and their stomachs were empty.

By every visible rule, he had won.

His soul power read Rank 13.

Lan Xuanyu stared at the number again until his eyes hurt.

It did not change.

Rank 13.

Not Rank 14.

He had checked twice, then a third time because some part of him insisted that a test instrument could make a mistake if one looked at it long enough with enough hope.

The instrument did not care about hope.

After a while, he put it away and sat cross-legged on the bed.

Gold and silver revolved inside him.

They had always been difficult. Even when they obeyed, they obeyed like two people agreeing not to fight only because someone stronger was watching. The gold-patterned Blue Silver Grass and the silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass were both his, but the energies they carried were never truly friends.

Tonight, something had changed.

The difference between them had lessened.

It was not obvious on the surface. If a teacher examined only his hands, his rings, his soul power, perhaps nothing would seem special except the worse number. Yet in the deepest part of his cultivation, Lan Xuanyu could feel the gap between the two currents had softened by a tiny measure.

A little less repulsion.

A little more space to breathe.

Nana had once told him that a Soul Master's intuition during cultivation could be more accurate than a shouted answer in class. Sometimes, a moment of insight came as a feeling before it came as words.

Lan Xuanyu trusted that memory.

If soul power dropped by one rank, did that mean it was definitely bad?

His first answer was yes.

His second answer was fear.

His third answer was a tiny light in the middle of the vortex.

He nearly opened his eyes.

Instead, he forced himself to keep meditating and looked inward more carefully.

There, where gold and silver circled around each other, there was a dot that had not existed before. It was so small that he almost missed it. The size of a needle tip. A flicker. A grain of color hidden in a place that should have held only gold, silver, and the faint whitish blur made when the two brushed too close together.

This was different.

Colorful.

Too small to name.

Too clear to deny.

Lan Xuanyu's breathing slowed.

Was it from the collision?

From the moment his hands had touched under the Lava Hound's fireball?

From the rainbow light that had swept everything away?

He did not know.

He knew one thing very clearly.

He could not ask the wrong person.

Teacher Mu might care. Teacher Ji might know many frightening things about spirit beasts, rings, and ancient history. The academy had instruments, teachers, files, and rules. But this was not a normal question.

Why did my gold and silver energies collide into a rainbow?

Why did a colored dot appear in my vortex?

Why did my soul power drop?

Why do I think the drop may not only be damage?

Those questions were not for a classroom.

They were not for the teacher who had just turned a lesson into a battlefield without moving from the front of the room.

They were not even for Qian Lei and Liu Feng, although they were his roommates and had fought beside him.

Lan Xuanyu opened his eyes.

Qian Lei was sprawled across his bed, exhausted from the last few days of strange summons and school pressure. Liu Feng sat near the wall, polishing his White Dragon Spear with the serious expression he gave to anything that could make him faster.

Both of them had trusted him.

Both of them knew parts of his secret that ordinary classmates did not know.

They still could not know this.

Lan Xuanyu lowered his head and looked at his own hands.

Teacher Nana.

The thought came as soon as he allowed himself to stop circling around it.

If he could ask Nana, she might understand. She had understood too many things before adults around him could name them. She had seen his gold and silver problem, warned him about danger, taught him to trust his body's signs, and told him not to be careless with the conflict inside him.

But Nana was on Heaven Dou Planet.

He could not stand in the dormitory and call loudly across space.

The Gate of Summoning could reach Dong Qianqiu.

Dong Qianqiu had a storage device.

Lan Xuanyu sat up straighter.

A letter.

He could write a letter.

He would not say the question aloud where the dorm walls might keep it. He would not ask the academy. He would write everything he could, fold it, seal it, and ask Dong Qianqiu to carry it to Teacher Nana the next time Qian Lei summoned her.

The idea made his heart beat faster.

It also made him feel more afraid, because the idea was possible.

Lan Xuanyu climbed down from his bed as quietly as he could.

"What are you doing?" Liu Feng asked without lifting his head.

"Writing something," Lan Xuanyu said.

Qian Lei turned over with his face half buried in the pillow. "If it is an apology to Teacher Ji for escaping early, write one for me too. Mine will say I am sorry I was born too slow to leave class before lunch."

Lan Xuanyu almost laughed.

He did not.

"Sleep," he said.

"Cruel dorm head," Qian Lei muttered.

Liu Feng looked up once. His eyes paused on Lan Xuanyu's face, sharp enough to notice more than Qian Lei did even when tired. But he did not ask.

Lan Xuanyu was grateful for that.

He took paper from his desk and began to write.

He did not write everything.

Some things had no words yet.

He wrote that the gold and silver Blue Silver Grass had touched during danger. He wrote that a rainbow-like force had appeared. He wrote that afterward, his soul power had fallen from Rank 14 to Rank 13. He wrote that the conflict between the two energies felt a little less severe. He wrote about the tiny colorful point at the center of the vortex.

He did not write Teacher Ji's name more than necessary.

He did not write that he was afraid.

The trembling of the characters on the paper already said that.

When he finished, the first half of the noon rest had passed.

His stomach growled.

For a moment, fear lost to hunger.

Lan Xuanyu folded the letter carefully and hid it close.

Then he went to the canteen.

Only a few Elite Junior Class students were there.

The trays were full.

The canteen had prepared food for thirty children who had been forced through Teacher Ji's lesson, but Teacher Ji had not released thirty children.

Lan Xuanyu looked at the food.

The food looked back.

His unhappiness retreated with admirable speed.

Eat first.

Think after eating.

Under the stunned eyes of the few students who had escaped early or barely passed before lunch, Lan Xuanyu ate as if the academy had set a separate test for him and the prize was every steamed bun in sight.

By the time more students dragged themselves in, he had already built a small empty-plate wall.

Yan Shuo arrived with Song Yichen and Luo Haoran near the end of the proper lunch period.

Luo looked as if Teacher Ji had removed part of his soul and replaced it with questions about spirit-beast classifications.

Song was steadier, but even his shoulders carried the dull heaviness of a child who had learned that fear could be taught in a calm voice.

Yan Shuo's hair looked slightly more disordered than usual. He held his tray with both hands and moved with the careful restraint of someone trying not to show that his stomach had begun negotiating with his dignity.

He saw Lan Xuanyu's empty plates.

Lan Xuanyu saw him seeing them.

Yan Shuo's mouth opened.

Then closed.

Then opened again.

"Did Teacher Ji dismiss you," Yan Shuo asked, "or did you defeat the canteen?"

Qian Lei, who had finally arrived in a state of visible spiritual collapse, pointed at Lan Xuanyu. "He defeated us all. I thought Teacher Ji was the demon. I was wrong. The true demon is a hungry control-type Soul Master."

Lan Xuanyu swallowed before answering. "Eat. Afternoon class is soon."

"Listen to him," Luo said, sliding into a seat. "He speaks with the authority of six people's lunch."

Lan Xuanyu smiled a little.

It felt almost normal.

Yan Shuo sat across the aisle, not close enough to press and not far enough to pretend they were strangers. That had become their shape in Heaven Luo Academy: near, but with doors between some things.

He looked at Lan Xuanyu once more.

There was curiosity in his eyes.

There was no demand.

Lan Xuanyu lowered his head and ate another mouthful.

He could live with curiosity.

He could not live with the wrong question out loud.

Afternoon class proved that Teacher Ji's lesson did not end when the morning did.

Many students returned pale, hungry, and quieter than they had been before. Some had passed only after being corrected so sharply that every word looked newly dangerous. Some had not eaten enough to complain. Even Jin Xiang, whose pride usually announced itself before he did, sat as if the chair might throw him into the sky if he moved too much.

Teacher Ji looked perfectly rested.

That was the most frightening thing about him.

Lan Xuanyu hid his secret behind obedience.

Yan Shuo hid his hunger behind a notebook full of ring colors, special meanings, and warnings not to decorate what should be precise.

No one in the room knew that one student was carrying a folded letter against his heart.

No one in Dorm 336 knew that one student's soul power had fallen.

No one in the academy record room wrote anything about a rainbow.

That night, Dorm 333 summoned Dong Qianqiu again.

The Gate opened with its familiar uncertainty, silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass steadying what Qian Lei alone could not hold properly. The air changed. Cold arrived. Dong Qianqiu appeared as if she were very tired of being pulled into other people's problems and also very willing to solve them if the problem was interesting enough.

Qian Lei looked hollow before the assessment even began.

"I should charge delivery fees," he said weakly. "Summoning, fighting, message service. This is no longer a martial soul. This is a business."

Dong Qianqiu glanced at him. "A poor business."

Liu Feng coughed once, suspiciously close to laughter.

Lan Xuanyu did not laugh.

He waited until the practice assessment was over and the moment was narrow enough to be private without becoming suspicious. Then he took out the folded letter.

"Can you give this to Teacher Nana?" he asked.

Dong Qianqiu looked at the letter, then at him.

"You are very troublesome," she said.

Lan Xuanyu accepted the judgment. "Please."

She took it.

The paper disappeared into her storage device.

For some reason, seeing it vanish made the matter feel more real than writing it had.

"I will bring it," Dong Qianqiu said.

Then the Gate closed.

Qian Lei collapsed backward with a groan. "I have decided that in my next life, I will awaken a martial soul called Sleeping Money. First soul skill: no one can summon me."

Liu Feng looked at Lan Xuanyu. "What was in the letter?"

Lan Xuanyu's fingers curled.

"A question for Teacher Nana."

"About your martial soul?"

Lan Xuanyu paused.

Liu Feng did not miss it.

Qian Lei, exhausted as he was, stopped groaning.

Lan Xuanyu said, "Something I need to ask her first."

The room was quiet.

Then Liu Feng nodded.

"Fine. But if it affects the team, tell us when you can."

Qian Lei lifted one hand from the bed. "Agreed. Also, if Teacher Nana writes back with secret ways to make my Gate less tiring, copy that part for me."

Lan Xuanyu felt warmth rise under the fear.

"If there is such a part," he said, "I will."

There was no such part.

For three days, there was only waiting.

Waiting made everything louder.

Lan Xuanyu cultivated whenever he could. The missing rank sat behind every breath like a debt. Rank 13 was not a disaster to most children. For him, it felt like falling down a mountain he had climbed with his fingernails.

His soul power had never been easy.

Others improved through effort and resources. He improved too, but slowly, strangely, with gold and silver refusing to settle. Every little rise had cost him. Losing one rank felt like watching a month of hunger, meditation, sweat, and teacher scolding vanish into a burst of beautiful color.

Beautiful.

That was the worst part.

The rainbow had not felt wrong.

Terrifying, yes.

Wrong, no.

On the first day, he nearly asked Qian Lei to summon Dong Qianqiu again immediately.

He did not.

On the second day, he nearly looked for Mu Zhongtian.

He did not.

On the third day, the reply came.

Dong Qianqiu's expression when she arrived through the Gate suggested that carrying letters between planets had not improved her opinion of Dorm 333.

"Teacher Nana said you should read it alone," she told Lan Xuanyu.

Qian Lei put both hands over his chest. "Do I look like someone who survives enough summoning to read other people's letters?"

Dong Qianqiu looked at him.

"No."

"That hurt more than the Soul World." 

Liu Feng dragged Qian Lei back before he could say more.

Lan Xuanyu took the letter.

His hands were steadier than when he had written his own.

He waited until Dong Qianqiu returned and the Gate closed. Then he climbed into bed, pulled the curtain slightly, and opened Nana's reply.

The first line made his chest loosen.

She did not scold him for hiding it from the academy.

She did not say the drop meant his cultivation was ruined.

She wrote that his situation might be a Self Spirit Fusion technique.

Lan Xuanyu read the words twice.

Self Spirit Fusion.

The golden-patterned and silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass might be able to fuse.

Nana's explanation was careful, but the meaning was clear enough for his heart to speed up. The two energies in his body likely shared a homologous origin, but their attributes conflicted so sharply that they could not get along. When real danger pressed them together, the barrier between them had broken for a moment.

That break had produced the rainbow.

That break had taken his soul power down one rank.

That break might also be the beginning of a solution.

Lan Xuanyu read on.

Nana said that being able to use such a fusion at just over Rank 10 was already incredible. The drop in soul power was probably a change caused by the fusion, not simple damage. The lessened conflict and colorful point could mean the two forces had begun to fuse in a small way. If they could truly get along better, the future might become stronger rather than weaker.

But cultivation speed might be affected.

The sentence made Lan Xuanyu's mouth tighten.

Of course.

Nothing about him was ever simple.

Nana's suggestion was to cultivate first and see whether returning to Rank 14 became faster. If recovery was fast, then the change might be good. If he used the fusion again, he had to tell her what changed afterward.

The last part was the heaviest.

The conflict between the gold and silver energies was still his greatest hidden danger.

This fusion might become the way to resolve it.

Lan Xuanyu lowered the letter.

Behind the curtain, the dormitory sounded ordinary. Qian Lei complained in a whisper that Liu Feng's training notes were blocking the lamp. Liu Feng told him that if his eyes could not survive notes, they did not deserve to guide a Gate of Summoning. Qian Lei declared that friendship had ended and would resume after breakfast.

Lan Xuanyu almost smiled.

His world had not changed on the outside.

Inside him, the tiny colorful point waited.

He closed his eyes.

Gold and silver revolved.

The rank he had lost still hurt.

But fear no longer sat alone.

There was an answer behind a door he could not open yet.

For now, he would cultivate.

Harder.

More carefully.

And he would get back to Rank 14 before any teacher's record noticed the missing step.

Three dormitories away, Yan Shuo was also awake.

He was not thinking about rainbows.

He was thinking about balance.

Not the grand kind adults put into speeches. The physical kind. The kind that decided whether a child wearing a practice harness slammed shoulder-first into a padded wall or corrected his center before the instructor shouted.

The Duo Mecha Master introductory room smelled of clean metal, old padding, and the faint sourness of too many students discovering that a close-body machine did not forgive pride.

Yan Shuo stood in a half-frame rig that wrapped around shoulders, waist, knees, and wrists. It was not a real mecha. It was a training interface that punished sloppy movement by pulling the student's limbs a finger-width out of line.

A finger-width was enough.

A finger-width made fire wasteful.

A finger-width turned a rescue angle into a collision.

A finger-width made Song's shield edge too close and Luo's interruption lane too narrow.

The instructor had said, "If you cannot make your own body honest, do not ask metal to obey you later."

Yan Shuo liked that sentence less after falling twice.

He liked it more after he understood why.

His Fire Phoenix wanted to rise. His shoulders wanted to flare. Dawnfeather Mantle wanted to answer pressure with a wide feathered spread because wide had once meant safe.

The rig disagreed.

Wide was not always safe.

Wide could blind an ally.

Wide could cook the space Luo needed to pass through.

Wide could rebound from Song's shield and snap heat back into Yan Shuo's own wrist.

So he practiced narrow.

Copper-red warmth gathered beneath his skin at the lowest output he could manage. Pale-gold motes followed, not as sparks thrown into the air, but as tiny points clinging to the edges of the motion. White-gold feather lines traced the places where his shoulders wanted to overreach.

The rig tugged.

Yan Shuo breathed out and pulled the flame back into line.

Warm illumination touched the inside of the practice frame.

Not a flare.

A guide.

"Again," Song said from outside the marked circle.

He did not sound like he was giving an order. He sounded like the shield wall he became in combat: present, practical, there because someone had to be.

Luo lay on a bench with one arm over his eyes. "If he does it again, I am going to start seeing little gold dots when I sleep."

"Good," Yan Shuo said without looking away from the target line. "Then dodge them."

"I prefer dreams without homework."

"You joined the wrong class."

That made Song huff once.

The rig pulled. Yan Shuo corrected late. The practice frame clicked red.

Failure.

He released the flame before it could widen.

No one applauded.

No one gave him a number.

No one said the Dawnflame Kite's true age or the first ring's private growth aloud, because those were not things the academy's basic practice room measured.

It measured whether his shoulder turned too early.

It measured whether heat left the safe boundary.

It measured whether he could stop being dramatic long enough to be useful.

Yan Shuo reset his stance.

Dawnflame Kite pulsed once inside him, warm and bright together.

Again.

The next attempt passed.

A month began that way.

Not with a tournament.

Not with a new ring.

Not with a teacher announcing that anyone had become a different person overnight.

The month began with children being too tired to boast properly.

Elite Junior Class did not slow down because Teacher Ji had frightened them. If anything, the schedule tightened around the fear. Morning lessons carried sociocultural theory, spirit-beast classifications, Federation basics, battle history, mecha terminology, and questions that looked harmless until a teacher asked for dates, causes, and consequences in the same breath.

Afternoons bled into practical drills.

Evenings were stolen by secondary professions.

Warship-piloting students disappeared into simulator cabins to drive Soul Guidance cars, then more complicated models, then routes with traffic patterns designed by adults who had apparently never loved children. Lan Xuanyu and Qian Lei came back from those lessons with the stunned expressions of people who had discovered that high Spiritual Power did not make a vehicle less willing to crash.

Duo Mecha Master students learned different pain.

They learned posture.

They learned frame alignment.

They learned that a machine close to the body magnified every lazy habit. They learned that battle armor was not decoration and that later, if they ever deserved it, the metal would not save them from having poor control.

Liu Feng threw himself into the route like speed had offended him personally.

Yan Shuo watched him from across practice divisions sometimes.

Liu Feng's line was thin, sharp, and hungry.

Yan Shuo's line was brighter, heavier, and more dangerous to allies if he stopped thinking.

Different problems.

Different routes.

That was becoming easier to accept.

Lan Xuanyu's route was different again.

Yan Shuo saw the public surface of it: the appetite, the late nights, Qian Lei's complaints, Liu Feng's sharper focus, the way Dorm 333 moved more like a team now even when they pretended not to. He saw Lan Xuanyu studying between mouthfuls and walking into simulator lessons with the quiet face he used when pressure had already been folded and put away somewhere private.

Yan Shuo wondered.

He did not push.

Pushing did not make a friend's door open.

It only proved one did not respect the door.

So he worked on his own.

His father sent a message reminding him that secondary-profession costs did not stop at tuition. Maintenance fees, materials, time, spare parts, future armor interfaces, simulator access — every route had teeth. Yan Ke's words were practical rather than discouraging. A lantern did not tell travelers not to walk. It showed where the ground broke.

Lin Mei's message was shorter.

Do not mistake a smaller flame for a weaker one. If it lands where you choose, it is stronger than a wide one that lands everywhere.

Yan Shuo read that sentence three times.

Then he copied it into his notebook under Teacher Ji's line about meaning.

The two sentences did not like each other.

That made them useful.

On the training floor, Dorm 336 changed in small ways.

Song learned to leave half a step more space on Yan Shuo's right when the Mantle's white-gold feather edge appeared. Not because Yan Shuo needed permission, and not because Song controlled him, but because a shield that knew its fire user's boundary could make the fire safer and stronger.

Luo learned that if he cut across the lane after the pale-gold motes tightened, he was safe.

If he moved before that, he deserved every lecture he received.

"I do not deserve lectures," Luo said after one near miss.

"You do," Song and Yan Shuo answered at the same time.

Luo looked wounded. "Team betrayal."

"Team education," Yan Shuo said.

The next pass was better.

Yan Shuo's flame did not grow taller.

It grew cleaner.

The copper-red center struck where he meant it to strike. The light-lined edge trimmed the output before it became waste. Recoil spread across shoulder and back rather than snapping into wrist and elbow. His breathing recovered faster because he stopped spending heat to prove he had heat.

No formal test recorded it.

His latest Spiritual Power number remained the one from the Chapter 13 recheck. His rank remained the one the academy already knew.

Yet when he woke each morning, his body remembered a little more.

A month of that mattered.

Lan Xuanyu's month mattered too.

He cultivated whenever he could.

At first, Rank 13 sat on him like a wet blanket. Every meditation began with the memory of loss. But after several days, he noticed the recovery was faster than he feared.

He had already walked this road once.

His meridians had already widened.

The step back to Rank 14 did not feel like climbing an entirely new mountain. It felt like crossing a path after a storm and finding that the stones, though wet, were still there.

The tiny colorful point did not grow.

Lan Xuanyu checked often.

It remained the size of a needle tip, unchanged by the soul power that slowly returned around it. That made him uneasy, but not panicked. Nana had told him to observe. So he observed.

Dorm 333 practiced privately outside normal lesson times.

They did not tell others why their coordination improved.

They did not announce that Lan Xuanyu's gold and silver Blue Silver Grass could change Liu Feng's spear or steady Qian Lei's summoning. They did not speak of Nana's reply, or Dong Qianqiu's delivery, or the possibility that Lan Xuanyu's own two spirits could fuse.

They simply trained.

Qian Lei still complained.

He complained while improving.

"I used to be a genius with Spiritual Power," he said one night, lying on the floor after a practice summon. "Now I am a genius floor decoration."

Liu Feng stepped over him. "Then decorate quietly."

"Cruel speed-type."

Lan Xuanyu offered Qian Lei a hand.

Qian Lei took it and groaned upright.

Their formation changed because Lan Xuanyu stood in the middle now in truth as well as name. Liu Feng trusted the timing of the grass. Qian Lei trusted the moment Lan Xuanyu told him to wait. Lan Xuanyu learned how much he could support without spending too much soul power, and how much he must hide even from people who trusted him.

Leadership, he discovered, was not only giving orders.

Sometimes it was swallowing a secret until it became light enough not to choke on.

Outside Dorm 333, the class changed in its own way.

No one had time to fight the old casual fights. Elimination hung over them like a blade with a school emblem engraved on it. Even Jin Xiang's provocations became shorter because training stole the breath needed to be properly annoying.

Ye Lingtong did not come to ask Lan Xuanyu what he had meant that day outside the cafeteria.

Lan Xuanyu noticed.

So did Yan Shuo.

She studied hard. She trained hard. She moved with Lu Qianxun's group and carried her pride like armor that had been dented but not discarded.

Once, in a corridor after theory class, Yan Shuo saw her pause as Lan Xuanyu passed.

For half a breath, it looked as if she might speak.

Then Qian Lei said something foolish about simulator steering, Liu Feng told him his hands had betrayed the concept of direction, and the moment vanished.

Ye Lingtong walked on.

Yan Shuo did not know what question lay between them.

He only knew there was one.

He had enough respect for questions now not to chase every one.

The midterm exams arrived after a month that felt longer than some years.

Lan Xuanyu woke before the bell.

He sat up quietly and checked his soul power one more time.

Rank 14.

The relief that went through him was so strong that he had to close his eyes.

At least this would not draw attention.

At least the academy's test would not ask why a student had gone backward during a month when everyone was supposed to improve.

At least one danger could stay hidden.

The colorful point remained unchanged.

Tiny.

Silent.

Waiting.

Lan Xuanyu washed his face and prepared for the day.

Qian Lei emerged from his blanket like a summoned creature with poor compatibility.

"If the first exam is sociocultural theory," he said, "I am optimistic. If the first exam is Teacher Ji asking questions before breakfast, bury me with dignity."

"You have dignity?" Liu Feng asked.

"I have potential dignity. It has not awakened yet."

Lan Xuanyu laughed this time.

He was still afraid.

He was also Rank 14 again.

Both things could be true.

In Dorm 336, Yan Shuo tied his sleeves with more care than usual.

The academy uniform looked the same as yesterday. The hallway looked the same. Song's expression was as steady as ever. Luo looked as if he had personally been betrayed by the concept of exams.

But the air was different.

A month of learning and resources had led to this day. The academy would measure what could be measured, score what could be scored, and then decide whose progress mattered enough to keep investing in.

Yan Shuo flexed his fingers once.

Copper warmth answered beneath the skin.

Pale-gold brightness flickered and settled.

He did not let the Mantle form.

Not yet.

Today was not a day for showing fire because his nerves wanted reassurance.

It was a day for spending only what each test required.

"You look serious," Luo said.

"It is an exam."

"Yes, but you look Teacher-Ji serious. I object."

Song adjusted the strap on his wrist guard. "Do not object too loudly. The academy may add an objection section."

Luo went pale. "I retract everything. I have never had an opinion."

Yan Shuo smiled.

The smile steadied him more than the flame had.

They walked to the examination hall with the rest of the Elite Junior Class.

Thirty children moved through the corridor in uniforms too neat for the amount of pressure they carried. Some whispered dates and theory points. Some rolled shoulders as if the physical tests could be intimidated first. Some said nothing.

Lan Xuanyu entered with Qian Lei and Liu Feng.

For a moment, his eyes met Yan Shuo's across the hall.

Yan Shuo lifted two fingers in a small greeting.

Lan Xuanyu nodded back.

There were doors between them.

There was also still a bridge.

The sociocultural test came first.

Paper, screen, silence, questions.

For Lan Xuanyu, it was almost restful compared with everything else. Strong Spiritual Power made memory clearer. Spirit-beast knowledge from home made some questions feel like old family conversations with sharper scoring. He moved through the exam steadily, careful not to waste time worrying about the afternoon.

For Yan Shuo, the test was not effortless, but Teacher Ji had changed the way he read questions. He no longer trusted a familiar word to mean what he wanted it to mean. Thousand-year. Ten-thousand-year. Ferocious Beast. Orange-gold. Million-year uncertainty. He read the edges before answering.

Once, a question asked about why ring color could not be treated as decoration.

Yan Shuo nearly smiled.

Meaning, not decoration.

He wrote a precise answer.

No flame appeared.

None was needed.

By the time the sociocultural exam ended, the room seemed to breathe again.

Then the teachers led them toward the physical data test area.

The machines waited in clean lines.

Strength.

Speed.

Spiritual Power.

Soul Power.

Simple words.

Dangerous words.

A month of hidden changes, public growth, private fear, improved teamwork, route choices, and swallowed questions would be pressed into numbers where numbers could be taken.

Lan Xuanyu looked at the strength apparatus and felt his blood stir faintly, as if remembering Lava Hound heat and metal walls before they had even begun to close.

Yan Shuo looked at the testing order and felt Dawnflame Kite pulse once inside him, copper-red warmth threaded with pale-gold light.

No one had called his name yet.

No one had measured what the month had done to him.

The teacher's voice rang through the hall.

"Next, Lan Xuanyu."

Yan Shuo stood with the rest of the class and watched his friend step forward.

Some answers came in letters.

Some came in numbers.

Some, Yan Shuo was beginning to understand, came only when pressure closed in from both sides and a person decided what shape they truly had.

The walls of the apparatus began to move.
