# Chapter 32: The Years That Learned to Wait

Time did not leap.

It accumulated.

It arrived in mornings when the practice room floor was still cold and Yan Shuo's bare feet found the same stance mark before his mind fully woke. It arrived in evenings when Song Yichen's shield arm trembled but did not drop, and Luo Haoran's laughter came too thin because he had spent the last hour learning that a wrong step against speed was not embarrassing; it was expensive. It arrived in the cafeteria, where Qian Lei complained about food with the solemn grief of a child betrayed by portion sizes, and Liu Feng ate quickly because every spare breath looked like something he intended to sharpen.

It arrived in the way Lan Xuanyu stopped having holidays.

After the match the board owed, Heaven Luo Academy did not announce a single simple first place that satisfied every mouth.

Teachers disliked giving children sentences they could turn into banners, but children made banners out of anything. Within three days the Elite Junior Class had divided the result into phrases short enough to throw across a hallway.

Dorm333 had the forbidden answer.

Dorm336 had the field answer.

Dorm333 was the team that could make adults go silent.

Dorm336 was the team that could be sent back onto the field tomorrow and trusted to stand.

Neither phrase was complete. Both were true enough to change training.

Mu Zhongtian wrote the official record in colder language. Dorm333 retained pressure-stage leadership due to exceptional adaptive combat and the confirmed existence of an unclassified high-risk technique. Dorm336 received direct-combat recognition under repeatable academy-safe restrictions. Further ranking comparisons would be handled by category until additional data could be collected.

The students translated that as: do not let Lan Xuanyu put his hands together, and do not let Yan Shuo lower his shoulders.

Yan heard the second half from Jin Xiang first.

"They say if you lower your shoulders, someone is going through a wall," Jin Xiang announced one morning with the satisfaction of a person delivering important military intelligence.

Yan looked up from fastening his wrist guards. "Who says that?"

"Everyone."

"Everyone is lazy."

"Everyone is accurate."

Luo Haoran leaned over from the next bench. "I support everyone. Everyone has excellent survival instincts."

Song Yichen tightened the strap on his forearm shield and said, "If everyone had excellent survival instincts, they would stop standing in straight lines in front of him."

Jin Xiang considered this, then moved half a step sideways.

Yan stared at him.

"What?" Jin Xiang said. "Improvement begins with listening."

Luo laughed so hard that Teacher Mu assigned him additional footwork.

The class laughed too, but less loudly than before. They laughed around the knowledge that jokes now bent around real danger. Three years earlier, a rumor could decide a child's place for a week. Now a rumor became a training note, and a training note became a survival habit.

That was the first thing time taught them.

It taught them that childhood did not vanish all at once.

It became supervised.

The first months after the final exam were organized into schedules so dense they seemed designed by adults who had declared sleep a philosophical disagreement. The Elite Junior Class did not receive a normal holiday. It received thinner lessons, longer practice slots, rotating Soul World entries, theory reviews, body-conditioning blocks, Spiritual Power exercises, and punishment assignments disguised as opportunities.

Dorm333 vanished into two different kinds of misery.

Ji Hongbin owned Lan Xuanyu's body, soul skills, movement, basics, and every hesitation that could be beaten into correction before a real enemy found it. Yin Tianfan owned the other abyss, the one people did not describe in the cafeteria because even Qian Lei stopped joking when Lan walked back from it pale and smelling faintly of metal, cold sweat, and something too much like artificial starlight.

Yan did not know the exact method.

He knew Lan's hands sometimes shook before picking up chopsticks.

He knew Lan could smile at Qian Lei's complaints while his eyes failed to focus on the table edge.

He knew Liu Feng watched Lan the way fast people watched a crack in ice they could not cross quickly enough.

He knew Qian Lei talked more on the days he was most frightened.

And he knew Lan Xuanyu always said, "I'm fine," in different shapes.

Yan hated all of them.

Not Lan. Never Lan.

He hated the shapes.

One evening, after Ji Hongbin had kept Lan in the training ground until the corridor lights dimmed to night mode, Yan found him outside the side entrance. The academy's high windows glowed above them, too bright and too far away. Lan stood with one hand against the wall, head bowed, breathing carefully through a face that still looked young and already looked tired.

Public Yan Shuo stopped three steps away.

Private Yan Shuo'er wanted to cross the remaining distance without thinking.

The difference between those two wishes had become sharper over the months. The public boy mask knew where people might be watching. The private girl beneath it knew when someone she loved in a way she did not yet have language for was hurting.

Yan chose the space between them.

"You missed dinner," he said.

Lan turned his head. His eyes cleared slowly, then warmed. "Teacher Ji says missing dinner builds character."

"Teacher Ji says everything builds character. One day a chair will fall on you and he will call it furniture-based education."

Lan laughed once. It was quiet and rough, but it was real.

Yan held out a wrapped bun.

Lan stared at it as if it were a divine artifact. "Where did you get that?"

"Stole it from Qian Lei."

"Really?"

"No. If I stole food from Qian Lei, we would be discussing my funeral."

Lan accepted the bun with both hands. His fingers brushed Yan's glove.

The contact lasted less than a breath.

Yan's fire noticed anyway.

It was not the pressure from Lan's hidden bloodline. It was not the strange cold-warm heaviness that sometimes made Dawnflame go still and Dawn-Iron settle deeper along his bones. This was smaller, more embarrassing, and somehow harder to command. A warmth rose under Yan's collar, not battle heat, not soul power, only the private self reacting before the public mask gave permission.

Lan looked down at their hands too.

For a moment neither of them spoke.

Then Lan said, "Your hand is warm."

Yan withdrew first because the corridor was public and because breathing had become complicated. "I have a Fire Phoenix martial soul. Congratulations on your observation skills. Teacher Ji will be proud."

Lan took a bite of the bun to hide a smile. "He would say I noticed too late."

"He would be right."

"You sound like him."

"Take that back."

Lan's smile softened.

The tiredness did not leave his face, but it stopped owning all of it.

Yan leaned against the opposite wall and watched the corridor corner. "I won't ask."

Lan's fingers tightened around the bun.

"Ask what?"

"The unsafe parts."

Lan was quiet long enough that the night ventilation hummed between them.

"I don't always know which parts are unsafe," he said at last.

That was more honest than fine.

Yan turned the words over carefully. In public he could be a rival, a monitor, a boy with a Fire Phoenix and a second ring that made classmates step aside. In private, under the collar, Yan Shuo'er carried a name Lan did not know and a body-truth the world had been kept from touching. She knew something about not knowing which part of a secret was the dangerous edge.

"Then say what you can," Yan said. "And when you can't, say that."

Lan looked at him.

"That's allowed?"

"It is the rule."

"Whose rule?"

"Mine."

Lan's eyes curved. "Class monitor rules are getting strange."

"They improve when applied to difficult students."

Lan finished the bun slowly, as if food could be made to last longer by treating each bite politely. When he was done, he folded the wrapper into a square so neat it looked like an apology.

"Then you have to follow it too," he said.

Yan's chest tightened.

"Follow what?"

"Say what you can. When you can't, say that."

The public corridor suddenly felt full of eyes even though no one stood there.

Yan Shuo'er smiled through Yan Shuo's face.

"Fine," he said. "But you first."

"That's unfair."

"I know."

Lan laughed again, and this time it came easier.

Neither of them called it a promise.

It became one anyway.

The next day, Ji Hongbin made Lan repeat body-skill coordination drills until his legs looked offended by existence. Yin Tianfan took him after that. Qian Lei loudly declared that if Lan died from training, Dorm333 would haunt the teachers as a group. Liu Feng told him ghosts probably needed more Spiritual Power than he could spare.

Yan heard the joke, saw Lan laugh, and did not ask about the unsafe parts.

Instead, he trained.

Dorm336's schedule changed after Chapter31 in a way the academy did not announce with speeches.

Teacher Mu stopped treating them like a team waiting for the next ranking arrangement and began treating them like a tool the academy had not finished naming. Direct-combat stability. Repeatable pressure. Anti-summon response. Anti-speed response. Fire-center formation. Defensive lane creation. Interruption timing. Controlled high output.

The phrases sounded professional on paper.

On the floor, they sounded like Song's shield striking the ground again and again until the practice mats split at the seam.

"Earlier," Yan said.

Song's arm trembled. "I moved before Luo called."

"You guessed before he confirmed. That is not the same as early."

Luo, lying on his back after being clipped by a foam projectile he had absolutely deserved, lifted one hand. "As the person who confirmed too late, I agree with the monitor's criticism and reject its emotional tone."

"Stand up," Song said.

"Emotionally or physically?"

"Both."

Luo stood.

At the start, Song had been a wall Yan could hide behind when hiding was useful and leap around when patience failed. Luo had been a warning bell with legs, fast enough to find trouble and clever enough to explain afterward why trouble had started it.

Time made them harder to simplify.

Song learned that a shield did not only stop things. It invited attacks into shapes. He could turn a beast's shoulder by half a palm-width and make Yan's flame arrive where bone was already wrong. He could angle a strike into the floor without spending all his strength. He could hold back just enough that the opponent believed the line was opening, then close the opening around Luo's cut.

Luo learned that speed was not only arriving first.

Sometimes speed was making another person look first.

Sometimes it was a step that made Liu Feng's spear-line, in practice footage, feel possible to interrupt instead of impossible to admire. Sometimes it was silence. Sometimes it was letting Song take the obvious pressure while Luo's Threadwind Mantis edge touched the place where a command would pass and made the command late.

Yan learned the hardest lesson.

He learned to wait while teammates moved.

Dawnflame hated waiting at first. The fire-light kite inside him wanted sky, wanted shoulder, wanted the warm clean line where a strike became a wingbeat. Dawn-Iron was less impatient. The roc's presence settled through Yan's breastbone, spine, hips, and feet like a mountain teaching a bird that height meant nothing if the body shattered on landing.

Feather over bone.

Bone under fire.

Fire held until the field gave permission.

Lin Mei approved.

"You make approval sound like punishment," Yan told her after one evening when she had made him repeat the same opening step eighty times because the seventy-ninth had been almost correct and almost was apparently an insult.

Lin Mei looked at the scorched practice mark. "Approval is not praise."

"That explains my childhood."

Yan Ke, sitting near the wall with a tablet full of expenses, did not look up. "Your childhood is currently eating my retirement plan."

Yan lowered his stance again. "You don't have a retirement plan."

"I did before you became talented."

Lin Mei's mouth moved very slightly.

That was her version of laughing while holding a training rod.

The family humor helped because the family numbers did not.

Rank twenty-three had looked impressive on a report.

Rank twenty-four looked expensive.

Rank twenty-five looked like a second set of food suppliers, a private agreement with a medicinal kitchen, and Yan Ke learning that some invoices could make a grown man sit down without being asked.

Rank twenty-six arrived with a fever that was not illness. Yan slept for fourteen hours afterward and woke hungry enough that Lin Mei quietly added another recovery rule to the wall chart: if the body asks, feed it before asking why.

Rank twenty-seven did not arrive like a flame rising.

It arrived like weight sinking.

Dawn-Iron's frame thickened beneath the Fire Phoenix. Yan could feel the difference when landing from a burst step. Before, the body accepted impact. Now it answered it. Knees, hips, spine, shoulders, and the hidden root of wings that did not yet exist all spoke one sentence more clearly: again.

Teacher Mu watched the test footage three times and said nothing for so long that Luo became nervous.

"When teachers are silent," Luo whispered, "are they impressed or planning murder?"

Song said, "Both are possible."

Yan kept his eyes on the replay. "If they were planning murder, Teacher Ji would already be explaining why it improves fundamentals."

Across the room, Ji Hongbin sneezed.

No one admitted fear.

No one laughed where he could hear.

Rank twenty-eight came with control problems.

The public mask had always been more than clothes. It was posture, heat, voice, face, how long Yan let people look, how much warmth stayed under the skin instead of blooming through the air. As a child, the mask had been a coat against weather. As the Fire Phoenix foundation deepened, it became a coat worn near a furnace.

Some mornings, Yan woke with red-gold warmth threading through his hair before he pulled it back under the ordinary cut and dulling clip. Some evenings, the line of his collar felt too tight because the private body beneath the public shape was changing in ways no academy form had space to name.

Lin Mei noticed before Yan said anything.

She always did.

"The mask is pulling," she said.

Yan sat very still in the family practice room while his mother adjusted the heat-dulling clasp at the throat. Yan Ke had already sealed the windows and set the privacy field. The apartment felt smaller whenever they worked on the parts of life that could not be reported honestly.

"It is not supposed to hurt," Lin Mei said.

"It doesn't."

Yan Ke looked up.

Yan corrected, "Not much."

Lin Mei's fingers paused.

"Not much is not the same as no."

"It only complains when I use the frame too long."

"Then we change the frame of the mask, not the truth of the body."

The words struck deeper than the clasp.

Private Yan Shuo'er looked down at her hands. They were still narrow, still callused, still strong. Public Yan Shuo could close them into fists and make classmates see a talented boy with a Fire Phoenix. The world had accepted that shape because her parents had built it carefully and because children believed records until records failed them.

But her body was not a record.

It was growing.

She hated being afraid of that.

Lin Mei's hand settled on the back of her head, firm and warm.

"The mask protects you," her mother said. "It does not get to punish you."

Yan Ke's voice was quieter. "If it starts to, we change the protection. We do not change you."

Dawnflame stirred under Yan's ribs, a small bright ache.

Dawn-Iron held steady, not a cage, not armor, only structure.

Yan breathed until the warmth stopped trying to escape through pride.

"Lan asked me to follow my own rule," she said.

Lin Mei did not ask which rule.

Yan Ke did, because fathers sometimes stepped directly into dangerous places while carrying paperwork. "Which rule?"

"Say what you can. When you can't, say that."

Yan Ke's expression changed in a way that made him look both pleased and wounded.

Lin Mei finished adjusting the clasp.

"That is a good rule," she said.

"It is annoying."

"Many good rules are."

Yan Shuo'er touched the collar. The public shape settled around her again, looser than before.

Safer.

Not honest.

Not false enough to survive forever.

The months kept moving.

The Elite Junior Class shrank.

No one liked to call it shrinking. Teachers said selection. Reports said optimization. Parents said opportunity when speaking to other parents and worry when speaking after doors closed. The children said names.

A seat empty after the first year.

Another after winter.

Two more when the combined pressure of Soul Power, Spiritual Power, body tests, Soul World runs, theory grades, and team results proved that talent was not the same as lasting.

Yan remembered every empty seat because a class monitor had no right to forget names just because the academy did not print them on the next roster.

Lan remembered too.

That surprised no one who knew him and would have surprised many people who only saw the rankings.

Sometimes, after a student left, Lan became quieter for a day. Not useless. Never useless. He would still solve theory questions, still adapt mid-drill, still tell Qian Lei to stop making that expression before Teacher Ji saw and added exercises. But quiet settled around him like frost.

Yan learned not to try melting all of it.

Fire could comfort.

Fire could also ruin the shape of something that needed to remain cold until it understood itself.

So he sat beside Lan when the cafeteria was noisy and said nothing until Lan chose to speak.

The first time, Lan only said, "Do you think they hate us?"

Yan did not pretend not to understand.

"Some of them. For a while."

Lan looked at the table.

"That is honest."

"You asked me."

"What if they should?"

Yan's fingers tightened under the table.

Across the cafeteria, Qian Lei was loudly arguing with Luo about whether a summoned beast counted as a pet if it tried to bite you. Liu Feng listened with the expression of someone deciding whether friendship required intervention. Song ate steadily, eyes flicking between his teammates and the room. The class kept breathing around empty seats.

"They can hate the exam," Yan said. "They can hate the teachers. They can hate us if they need a face for it. That does not make you guilty for surviving the class you were put in."

Lan's lashes lifted.

"You make monitor speeches even when sitting down."

"You ask depressing questions during lunch."

"Fair."

Lan's shoulder brushed his.

It might have been an accident.

Yan did not move away.

Neither did Lan.

No one said anything about it, because they were children and classmates and rivals and boys in the eyes of the room, and because some truths did not survive being named too early.

The brush of shoulder lasted until Qian Lei declared that if a summon bit you, that was proof of emotional investment.

Luo said, "By that logic, Teacher Ji loves everyone."

Half the table choked.

Lan laughed so suddenly that his shoulder bumped Yan's again.

This time it was definitely not an accident.

Yan looked down at his tray until the warmth in his face became manageable.

Dawnflame chirped silently inside him with an emotion that felt suspiciously like smugness.

Yan told it to be quiet.

It did not listen.

By the end of the first full year, the academy had learned to watch Dorm336 differently.

At first, observers had looked at Yan.

That was natural. Fire drew eyes. A Fire Phoenix drew more. A Rank twenty-eight two-ring child with a deep-purple second ring and a direct-combat result against Dorm333 made adults fold their arms and pretend they were not recalculating budgets.

Then Song started turning matches before they became matches.

Then Luo started making fast opponents late.

Then Yan stopped needing to rescue mistakes that no longer happened.

A team that survived because its strongest member carried everyone was frightening.

A team that improved until its strongest member could wait was worse.

Ji Hongbin said that in a teacher meeting.

He did not say worse with disapproval.

Yin Tianfan leaned back in his chair, laughed, and asked whether anyone wanted to trade students.

No one answered him.

Teacher Mu wrote another note.

The note did not reach students, but training changed again.

Dorm336 began receiving mixed-objective fields: protect a target while being hunted, break a control formation without destroying the hostage markers, cross a terrain zone where Yan's fire could solve the obvious problem but only if Song and Luo solved the actual one first. Dorm333 received fields that looked impossible until Lan found the hinge and made the impossible embarrassed. Lu Qianxun's team received more direct power-pressure work, and Lu met it with the grim satisfaction of someone who would rather be crushed by a mountain than ignored by one.

Ye Lingtong grew sharper.

Chang Jianyi grew quieter.

Jin Xiang grew taller and claimed this improved his wings by increasing dramatic presence.

Huang Qifan claimed his Battle Bear had always been dramatic.

Children grew around pressure because pressure was what the academy had given them instead of a normal childhood.

Yan grew too.

Rank twenty-nine came near the seventeenth month after the field owed to Dorm336.

It did not arrive loudly.

That frightened the adults more.

The test room was sealed under a higher privacy level than before. Mu Zhongtian stood beside the measuring console. Ji Hongbin stood at the back with his arms folded. Lin Mei watched from the family side of the authorization line. Yan Ke had three signed forms open and the expression of a man who had begun suspecting paper could smell fear.

Yan stood in the center circle under the public file.

Yan Shuo.

Fire Phoenix.

Two rings.

No third-ring registration.

No special reveal.

The soul-power test rose through familiar pressure and stopped where Yan already knew it would.

Rank twenty-nine.

Not the wall yet.

The step before it.

The room did not exhale.

Spiritual Power came next.

Cool stimulus touched the inside of Yan's skull. The old Spirit Connection road opened. Once, Spiritual Power had felt like a number adults used to decide whether children were promising. Now it felt like weather behind the eyes: pressure, distance, the ability to know where heat wanted to go before it became flame.

The number settled.

Three hundred ninety-two.

Still Spirit Connection.

Close enough to make Ji Hongbin's brows move.

Mu Zhongtian checked the equipment because teachers were human and sometimes human beings responded to extraordinary children by blaming machinery.

The number did not change.

"Record it," Ji said.

Mu's fingers moved over the console. "Public summary?"

Ji looked at Yan.

Yan looked back.

There were many numbers that could hurt if written in the wrong place.

The public class already knew enough. Rank twenty-nine was impossible to hide if training continued. Spiritual Power details could be summarized. Soul-spirit private maturity did not belong to hallway mouths.

"Soul Power only," Ji said at last. "Spiritual Power: internal instructor file. Soul-spirit compatibility: protected consultation."

Yan did not thank him.

He inclined his head because public Yan Shuo was a student and a monitor and knew how to accept a useful boundary without making it sentimental.

Private Yan Shuo'er felt relief anyway.

Then Dawnflame moved.

It was not an awakening.

Yan knew the difference now in the way one knew the difference between a door and the wind under it.

Dawnflame Kite rose inside the first ring like a bird pressing its breast against shell. Fire-light feather lines trembled along Yan's shoulders, contained by discipline and the dull clasp at the throat. The public yellow ring did not blaze purple. The public file did not rewrite itself. No full wings unfolded. No sky accepted him.

But in the private ledger of living things, the first soul spirit had grown.

Seven hundred twenty years.

High deep-yellow.

Still waiting.

Dawn-Iron Phoenix Roc answered beneath it with a deeper pressure that made Yan's bones remember cliffs and landing stones. The second foundation had thickened too, not by show, not by a new ring, but by surviving repetition until structure became instinct.

Two thousand fifty years.

Deep purple.

Still waiting.

Feather over bone.

Bone under fire.

Fire at the edge of a door.

Yan swallowed.

Lin Mei saw.

Yan Ke saw.

Ji Hongbin saw enough to stop pretending this was only a good test.

"No further high-output training," Ji said.

Yan blinked. "For how long?"

"Until the consultation."

"Which consultation?"

No one answered too quickly.

That was how Yan knew adults had already begun preparing for a door they had not told him about.

The first file arrived that night.

It did not come through the academy student system. It arrived through a protected family-channel relay and required Lin Mei's signature, Yan Ke's signature, Mu Zhongtian's academy authorization, and a Spirit Pagoda consultant code that Yan had only seen twice before.

The apartment windows were sealed again.

The practice room lights were lowered.

Lan Xuanyu was somewhere across the academy being dragged through another layer of training that would leave him smiling too softly tomorrow morning. Song and Luo were in Dorm336, probably arguing over whether Luo's new timing drill counted as progress or a personal threat. The class slept, studied, complained, or pretended not to worry about the next ranking list.

Public Yan Shuo sat at the family table.

Private Yan Shuo'er felt her heart beat too loudly under the collar.

Yan Ke opened the file.

The first page was mostly warnings.

High compatibility risk.

High transformation risk.

Rare flame-bird soul spirit.

Not recommended without senior protection.

Not recommended without containment.

Not recommended for ordinary third-ring acquisition.

Yan read each line once.

Then she saw the name.

Purple Flame Eidolon Bird.

Estimated age: six thousand years.

Dawnflame went utterly silent.

Dawn-Iron settled so heavily that Yan felt the floor through his bones.

Lin Mei did not speak for a long time.

Yan Ke's face had gone pale in the very quiet way it did when fear became arithmetic and arithmetic failed.

On the screen, the bird's recorded silhouette burned violet at the edges, not like a torch, not like ordinary fire, but like the memory of a flame seen through closed eyes.

Yan touched the collar at his throat.

The public mask held.

The private body beneath it listened.

Somewhere inside the Fire Phoenix, a door breathed.

Not open.

Not yet.

But for the first time, the thing waiting on the other side had a name.
