# Chapter 43: Thirty Seconds of Green

The first lesson Lan Xuanyu had learned from Yin Tianfan was that an opportunity which looked like mercy was probably bait.

The second lesson was worse.

Sometimes bait was still the only road forward.

False magma pressed around his shoulders. It did not burn the way true magma should have burned, but heat still found every place fear had already opened. It pushed at his skin. It dragged at his breathing. It demanded that his soul power answer every moment and reminded him that the pool had lied only about death, not about pain.

Qian Lei floated beside him with both cheeks puffed full of air and eyes wide enough to reflect red light.

Liu Feng looked thinner in the magma. The White Dragon Spear was held close to his body, its pale line distorted by waves of heat. His usual sharpness had been folded inward. No unnecessary movement. No wasted breath.

Above them, two mountains fought.

The Ground Fire Scarlet Dragons had lost their human targets.

Lan had known that hiding would not end the danger. A target disappearing did not make a monster gentle. It only changed where the violence went. But even he had not expected the valley to become so direct so quickly.

The first crash rolled through the false magma like thunder through water.

Qian Lei jerked.

Lan caught his wrist.

Do not rise yet.

He did not say it. He could not spare the air.

Qian Lei understood anyway. Terror had made him loose before. Now terror made him obedient.

A second crash came, followed by the deep tearing roar of a creature injured badly enough to feel rage beneath instinct. The red light above them broke apart into wild shadows. Something enormous hit the shore. Scales scraped stone. Heat surged in ripples.

Then the fire changed.

Lan felt it before he saw it.

The false magma around them trembled with a pressure that did not belong to the two Scarlet Dragons. It was not cold like his own ice. It was not the wild furnace heat of the simulated beasts. It was cleaner, higher, and sharper, a red-gold command passing through the valley's fire veins like a note struck on a bell.

Qian Lei's eyes widened underwater.

Liu Feng glanced toward the side ridge through the wavering red.

Lan knew that direction.

Dorm336.

Yan Shuo.

Lan did not know Yan's private burdens. He did not know the shape of the secrets behind the public file. But he knew the public Fire Phoenix had stood on the ridge, three purple rings burning under Shrek's eyes, and had not been ordinary for a single breath since the scan began.

Now the valley remembered that too.

Above the pool, one of the Ground Fire Scarlet Dragons roared and spat flame toward the side fissure.

The flame did not travel straight.

It bent.

Not as if seized by an invisible hand. Not as if some impossible divine authority had descended. It bent the way inferior fire bent when a higher flame told it the air was no longer entirely its own.

The fireball struck the rock short of the fissure mouth and burst outward in a ragged fan. Red fire scattered. A thread of red-gold burned through it and did not scatter at all.

The Scarlet Dragon recoiled.

Lan's pupils tightened.

The Ground Fire Scarlet Dragons were terrifying. Their bodies were huge, their scales thick, their claws strong enough to break stone. But their fire was not sovereign fire. Their dragon blood was not the blood of a true Dragon monarch. They were powerful simulated fire-dragon beasts, not an existence that could stand above a true awakened Fire Phoenix and command it to bow.

Yan's pressure did not make their mass vanish.

It did not turn their claws harmless.

It did something almost as important.

It made their fire hesitate.

It made their instincts doubt.

Lan counted heartbeats.

Yin Tianfan's voice seemed to laugh somewhere in the back of his mind.

A commander who only moves when calm is a commander who dies while arranging his manners.

Ji Hongbin's voice followed, colder and heavier.

But a Soul Master who moves before calculation is only feeding the enemy.

Lan had been beaten by both kinds of lesson until calm was no longer a virtue. It was a scar.

He pulled Liu Feng and Qian Lei lower.

Only when his lungs began to ache did he let the three of them drift upward.

Their heads broke the surface in the shadow of heat haze.

Air entered like fire.

Qian Lei gasped once and clapped both hands over his mouth as if the breath itself might betray them.

Liu Feng's eyes immediately went to the battlefield.

Lan's went to the Dragons.

The two Ground Fire Scarlet Dragons were no longer hunting.

They were destroying.

One had blood-dark cracks across the side of its neck where the other's jaws had found purchase. The other dragged one foreclaw a little lower than before, several talons hanging at angles no living creature should tolerate. Their scales, which had seemed like red-black armor when aimed at students, now tore under equal strength. Each collision removed pieces of certainty from the world.

And every time their flame swelled too near the side fissure, a red-gold pressure pressed back.

It was not loud.

It was not wasteful.

That made it more frightening.

A child's flame would flare and disappear. A clever student's flame would dodge and snatch points. Yan Shuo's Fire Phoenix pressure sat inside the valley's heat like a quiet throne, forcing the simulated beasts to spend strength resisting a hierarchy they did not have the purity to overturn.

One Dragon snapped toward the fissure, furious at being checked.

The other slammed into it before it could commit.

Territory. Pain. Phoenix pressure. Lost human targets.

The instincts tangled.

The impact opened another wound.

A tail swept through the place where Lan's team had stood not long ago.

A claw hammered downward.

Stone burst.

The impact sent shards skipping across the pool and droplets of false magma into the air.

Lan ducked back under with the others before a falling stone could strike Qian Lei's head.

This time Qian Lei did not need to be pulled.

He sank first.

A moment later he looked at Lan with a face that clearly asked whether Shrek Academy had any intention of leaving children alive.

Lan almost wanted to know the same thing.

But the answer did not matter.

Alive was not something Shrek Academy handed over.

Alive was something they had to keep stealing.

They rose again several breaths later.

The Dragons had become uglier.

The later-arriving Ground Fire Scarlet Dragon lunged forward and bit down on the first Dragon's forelimb. Bone cracked loudly enough that even Dorm333 heard it through the roar. The first Dragon smashed its head sideways with a force that twisted the other's neck. For an instant, the later Dragon staggered with its head tilted wrong, its pupils burning with pain and fury.

The first Dragon had won that exchange.

It had also lost the use of one claw.

Then a thin line of red-gold fire cut across the ground between the side fissure and the battling monsters.

Not an attack meant to kill.

A boundary.

The later Dragon's flame aura rolled into it and collapsed inward, dirty red fire guttering as if swallowed by cleaner heat. The Dragon's head jerked toward the fissure with rage and uncertainty.

The first Dragon seized the hesitation.

Its wounded claw scraped across the other's shoulder, tearing scales that had already loosened under their own fighting.

Lan's eyes narrowed.

Not yet.

But closer.

Blood flowed in smoking streams across the ground. Fireballs came slower now. The Dragons were still terrifying, but their strength no longer looked endless. Their attacks had begun to carry delay. Their turns dragged. Their breathing was ragged enough to change the rhythm of the battlefield.

And their fire was no longer fully theirs.

Qian Lei swallowed air too quickly.

Lan pressed a hand against the back of his neck and pushed him down before panic could make him stare too long.

They submerged again.

Heat closed over them.

Far from the pool, behind basalt that had been cracked open by pressure rather than kindness, Dorm336 was not hiding like prey.

Dorm336 was holding a line.

Yan Shuo stood at the narrowest place of the fissure with his wings half-spread and three purple rings dimmed to a controlled, dangerous glow. The Fire Phoenix behind him did not roar at full strength. It did not need to. A true Fire Phoenix did not need to scream at every lesser flame to prove it was fire.

Ultimate Fire gathered along his palms and wing edges in thin red-gold threads.

The threads ran into the cracks Luo Haoran indicated.

They did not explode.

They did not waste heat against stone.

They entered the false geothermal seam and turned the seam into a pressure channel.

Outside, the Ground Fire Scarlet Dragons were enormous.

Inside the fissure, their fire was smaller.

That difference mattered.

Yan was not a low-ranked ordinary fire Soul Master trying to endure a fire beast's territory. He was Rank39 before eleven, a full awakened Fire Phoenix Soul Elder with three purple rings, three perfectly compatible and fully absorbed soul spirits, and a foundation whose combined soul-spirit age had already crossed ten thousand years. Dawnflame Phoenix Kite, Dawn-Iron Phoenix Roc, Purple Flame Eidolon Bird — none of them were being fused into the full trump card now, but their existence sat under his martial soul like roots under a tree.

He did not need the full tree to prove the roots were deep.

Song Yichen's Stoneplate Tortoise shield braced against the physical shock wave. The shield did not pretend to defeat a Scarlet Dragon. It made a safe angle for the team to stand inside while Yan's pressure traveled outward.

Luo Haoran knelt with one hand to the stone and the other pointing by inches.

"Left seam. Two breaths. Not more. If you overfill it, the ceiling splits."

Yan shifted the fire.

The fissure wall glowed.

Outside, a Dragon's fireball bent short and burst against empty rock.

Song's teeth flashed in a grin despite the sweat on his face. "That one hated that."

"It should," Luo said. "Its fire got told to sit down."

Yan's mouth curved faintly.

The Fire Phoenix inside him approved of the phrasing.

Then the Dragon's body struck the ridge.

Stone screamed.

Song's shield buckled inward a handspan.

Yan stepped back and caught the edge of the shield with one wing, using Fire Phoenix body strength rather than a flashy skill to keep the formation from folding. Even with bloodline suppression, even with Ultimate Fire, mass remained mass. A mountain did not become harmless because its flame bowed.

That was why this was not a solo flight.

That was why Song and Luo mattered.

"Status," Yan said.

Song spat dust. "Still alive. Irritated. Respectfully requesting fewer mountains."

Luo's eyes were tight. "The other Dragon hit it. They're turning again. Your pressure is making them hesitate whenever they try to choose us. It is destabilizing their fire rhythm and their target choice at the same time. They hate it. They also hate each other."

"Good."

"Good?" Song repeated. "Our plan is to make two Scarlet Dragons hate everything?"

"No," Yan said. "Our plan is to make sure we are not the easiest thing to hate."

Luo's mouth twitched. "Strategic insult redirection."

"Put it in the report."

This time Song almost laughed.

Then a sound came through the stone that made even Yan's Fire Phoenix pause.

Not a Dragon's roar.

Something thinner.

Sharper.

A bird-type killing intent flashed through the battlefield, so concentrated that it felt absurd inside a valley of giant reptiles. It was not higher than the Fire Phoenix. It was not nobler. It was a knife, not a crown. But a knife did not need to be royal to be terrifying when pointed at an eye.

Yan's wings lifted half an inch.

Not fear.

Recognition.

The Purple Flame Eidolon Bird's presence stirred under his third ring, predatory and curious, but Yan held it down. This was not his summon. Not his pact. Not his route.

"What?" Song asked.

Yan's eyes stayed on the fissure mouth.

"Something changed outside."

"Another Dragon?"

"No. Smaller. Faster. A bird-type killing line."

Luo's face tightened. "Dorm333?"

Yan did not know the mechanics.

He had seen Lan Xuanyu disappear into impossible magma. He had seen Qian Lei's gate open before. He knew only public things: Lan could find openings other people missed; Qian Lei's summoning could turn nonsense into disaster; Liu Feng could make one line of movement count where others needed ten.

That was enough.

"Hold the seam," Yan said. "Do not break formation."

Song stared. "You think they are attacking?"

"I think Lan waited for something."

Luo exhaled. "That's usually dangerous for everyone except his target."

Yan's red-gold fire thinned, becoming less a wall and more a pressure that kept the Dragons' flame rhythm broken without drawing their bodies straight into the fissure.

The Fire Phoenix inside him wanted to rise.

Not because it was helpless below.

Because it knew it could contest the sky.

Yan kept it grounded.

The trump card remained untouched.

The team route stayed intact.

In the magma pool, Lan surfaced for the fourth time and decided that waiting had reached its edge.

The two Scarlet Dragons collided again.

The later Dragon, neck twisted from the previous blow and flame rhythm broken by red-gold pressure, still managed to bite down on the first Dragon's injured forelimb. The first Dragon roared and drove its skull into the side of the other's neck. The impact was terrible. The later Dragon's head lurched sideways. Its body stumbled. The first Dragon's broken foreclaw hung uselessly, but its eyes still burned with enough fury to scorch courage from a child's bones.

Both monsters were badly hurt.

Both still lived.

Their fire had been checked.

Their bodies had not.

That was the narrow place.

Yan's suppression had made the impossible battlefield weaker.

It had not placed a blade inside the Dragon's eye.

Lan leaned close to Qian Lei.

"Second soul skill."

Qian Lei's pupils shrank, then brightened.

"Now?"

"Prepare now. Wait for my word. Target the eyes."

Qian Lei looked at the Dragons.

Looked back at Lan.

A smile spread across his face despite the false magma, the danger, and the fact that his hair was plastered to his forehead like a drowned rat.

"Boss," he whispered, "you are really not a good person."

Liu Feng stared at him.

Qian Lei added quickly, "I mean that with admiration. Tremendous admiration."

Lan ignored him.

There were moments when correcting Qian Lei cost more than letting him live with his own mouth.

"When they finish the next exchange," Lan said. "Not before. You only get thirty seconds."

Qian Lei's expression changed.

Fear did not disappear, but something steadier rose under it. His second soul ring began to shine, a light different from the uncertain opening of his first gate. Replicate was not the long gamble of calling into the unknown. This was memory held in a ring, an imprint prepared because even unreliable people could learn to prepare.

Liu Feng drew a breath.

"If it misses?"

"Then we go back under and run when the pool changes," Lan said.

Qian Lei's mouth twitched. "That's a terrible backup plan."

"It's the only one."

"I hate how often those are the same thing."

Lan raised one hand slightly.

On the shore, the Dragons met again.

The first Dragon lunged with its damaged body, jaws wide, trying to crush the neck it had already injured. The later Dragon twisted away at the last instant and rammed shoulder-first into its opponent's chest. Yan's red-gold pressure struck their fire a final time through the seam, and both beasts' flame auras stuttered in the same blink. For that blink, both heads were low. Their wounds opened. Their eyes were exposed, unfocused from pain, rage, and disrupted instinct.

The world narrowed to one line.

Lan's hand cut down.

"Now! The later one first!"

Qian Lei's second soul ring flashed.

A Gate of Summoning opened above his head, smaller and sharper than the first-skill gate, as if it had no patience for ceremony.

Green light erupted.

It was so small compared with the Scarlet Dragons that for half a heartbeat the mind refused to measure it as a threat. A streak. A sliver. A feathered blade of jade flashing out of the gate with a shriek that cut through the Dragons' roars and the pool's hissing heat alike.

The Emerald Demon Bird had arrived.

Not as a pet.

Not as an obedient soldier.

As hunger given wings.

Qian Lei pointed with two shaking fingers.

"There!"

The green light vanished.

No, not vanished.

Moved.

Lan's eyes barely tracked it. Liu Feng, whose whole life had been built around speed, went still with shock.

The Emerald Demon Bird crossed the distance in a flash and struck the later Dragon's exposed eye.

There was no contest of strength.

No heroic clash.

A mountain could crush a needle under ordinary circumstances. But if the needle found the eye before the mountain closed, strength became irrelevant.

The later Ground Fire Scarlet Dragon screamed.

The sound was different from its earlier roars. Those had been anger. This was injury entering a place scales could not guard. Its body convulsed, claws tearing trenches in the ground as the Emerald Demon Bird drove inward with a ferocity completely out of proportion to its size.

Qian Lei's face went pale.

"I told it the target," he whispered. "I didn't tell it to be that vicious."

"Can you call it back?" Liu Feng asked.

Qian Lei gave him an offended look despite his trembling. "Do I look like I can negotiate with that thing?"

Lan did not look away.

The first Scarlet Dragon had frozen for one fatal breath.

Even monsters understood surprise.

Then instinct returned. The first Dragon opened its mouth and tried to spit a ball of fire toward the later Dragon's head, not to save it, but to burn whatever had invaded it.

The fireball formed badly.

Its flame had been fighting Yan's red-gold pressure too long. It was still deadly, but no longer clean.

The Emerald Demon Bird tore free before the fire landed.

Green crossed red.

The fireball exploded against the later Dragon's skull, and the Dragon's enormous body collapsed, still twitching, its roar turning into a broken gurgle swallowed by the heat.

The first Dragon snapped its eyes shut and released a furnace-like wave from its scales.

Lan's heart tightened.

It was injured, not stupid.

The red-gold line from the fissure thinned rather than flared.

Yan was not trying to steal the kill.

He was keeping the battlefield's fire from becoming whole again.

The Emerald Demon Bird did not care about any of that.

It slammed into the closed eyelid with a sound like jade striking iron.

The green light bounced back, feathers smoking.

Qian Lei flinched as if the impact had hit him.

The Scarlet Dragon howled.

Its eyelid had not yielded completely, but a torn gash opened across it. Beneath, the eye ruptured from the force.

"Again," Lan said under his breath.

He did not know whether the bird could hear him.

The bird did not need to.

The Emerald Demon Bird beat its wings once, shaking sparks away. Its feathers flashed bright green through red fire. Then it shot forward a second time, through the cracked heat, through the broken lid, into the wounded eye.

The first Dragon's entire body arched.

For a moment the valley seemed to hold its breath.

Then the Ground Fire Scarlet Dragon fell.

The impact sent a wave through the false magma high enough to wash over Lan's face.

He pulled Qian Lei and Liu Feng under by instinct, not thought.

Heat and bubbles swallowed them.

When they surfaced again, neither Dragon was standing.

Qian Lei stared until his eyes watered.

Liu Feng stared too.

Lan stared longest.

He had planned the opening. He had ordered the target. He had believed the Emerald Demon Bird was the best choice because thirty seconds demanded speed, and weak points mattered more than size.

Even so, belief and witnessing were not the same thing.

A thousand-year soul beast had just killed two Ground Fire Scarlet Dragons because Qian Lei had carried the right imprint, waited for the right moment, and attacked the only place a giant could not armor.

And because the battlefield had been made into that moment by more than one team.

Lan's gaze flicked once toward the side fissure.

Red-gold light faded there, restrained before it could become boast or waste.

Yan had not needed to kill the Dragons to prove the Fire Phoenix's weight.

The Dragons had spent the last breaths of their lives proving it for him.

Lan turned slowly toward Qian Lei.

Liu Feng did the same.

Qian Lei lifted his chin with heroic calm that lasted almost one full second.

"What are you looking at me for?" he said, voice cracking. "I am also frightened."

Liu Feng blinked.

Qian Lei coughed and tried again. "I mean, obviously this proves I am the hidden strongest combatant of our elite junior class. Please remember to be respectful in the future."

"You can't control it," Liu Feng said.

"Details."

"You were shaking when you pointed."

"A refined summoner must tremble in harmony with fate."

Lan almost laughed.

Almost.

Because Qian Lei's ridiculousness did not erase the truth.

His first soul skill had been unreliable because it opened toward too many possibilities. But once the second soul skill fixed an imprint, that uncertainty transformed. Thirty seconds was short. Thirty seconds was also more than enough for the right creature at the right target.

"Your second skill is stronger than you pretend," Lan said.

Qian Lei's expression changed at the praise.

Then he looked away, ears reddening. "Only when the first summon gives me something worth copying. And don't forget who helped me summon this thing in the first place. Without your enhancement, I might have copied a lizard with ambition."

Liu Feng said, "That sounds like something you would do."

"I object to how accurate that is."

Lan glanced at the gate above Qian Lei.

The Emerald Demon Bird returned as a green streak, emerging from the ruined head of the second Dragon and circling once over the fallen bodies. It did not look satisfied in the way a person looked satisfied. It looked as if the world contained meat, speed, and insufficient fear.

For one instant, its gaze turned toward the pool.

Then toward the side fissure.

The green bird's killing intent sharpened.

From the fissure, a red-gold Phoenix pressure rose by one quiet degree.

Not attack.

Warning.

The Emerald Demon Bird was savage, not foolish. Its body was a blade, but a blade did not mistake a furnace-crown for prey without paying for it. The moment lasted less than a breath, but every sensitive thing in the valley felt the hierarchy inside it.

Qian Lei stopped breathing.

Liu Feng's spear hand tightened.

Lan felt cold despite the heat.

The bird's eyes were too sharp. Too alive. It was a replicated summon, not a friend. The connection that brought it here did not make it gentle.

Then the thirty seconds ended.

The green figure folded into light and vanished back through the gate over Qian Lei's head.

The gate closed.

Qian Lei exhaled all at once.

"If I ever say I want to keep one of those as a pet," he said, "please remind me that I enjoy having a head."

Liu Feng gave him a sideways look. "You just claimed to be the hidden strongest."

"Strong people can have healthy respect for birds that eat brains."

"That is the first wise thing you've said all day."

Qian Lei opened his mouth to protest.

The pool changed.

It began as a pulse beneath their feet.

The false magma, which had been bearable only because Lan's water and ice control bent around its deception, suddenly grew hotter. Not gradually. Not naturally. It was as though the simulation remembered what magma was supposed to be and decided to become honest.

Lan's expression sharpened.

"Out. Now."

Golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass shot from his hand and wrapped around Liu Feng's waist.

Liu Feng did not ask why. The White Dragon Spear thrust downward into the pool, and his body surged toward the shore. Lan shoved from behind, using the vine as both anchor and line. Qian Lei yelped as another strand wrapped him and dragged him through the heat.

"Warn me before pulling!"

"I did," Lan said.

"A word and a vine are different kinds of warning!"

The false magma surged harder.

Liu Feng struck downward again. Momentum lifted all three of them. Lan twisted in the air, caught Qian Lei before he could tumble head-first into a hot stone shelf, and shoved him toward solid ground. Liu Feng landed first, slid, then drove his spear into the earth to stop himself from pitching back into the pool.

Lan came down beside him on one knee.

Qian Lei sprawled on his back, gasping dramatically enough to suggest that the ground should apologize for not being softer.

Behind them, the pool began to boil.

The two Ground Fire Scarlet Dragon bodies lay ahead like fallen hills.

Lan pulled Qian Lei up and moved toward them, not because he wanted to be near corpses, but because the shore behind them was no longer safe.

Then the bodies faded.

Not rotted. Not burned. Simply loosened into red light, edges breaking apart until two enormous monsters became scattered particles and vanished into the simulation's air.

In their place, a door appeared.

Red light gathered upright before them, tall enough for even a Dragon to have noticed. Within it, a broad hand made of condensed light extended and curled its fingers.

Come.

Qian Lei stared.

"Is that an invitation or a threat?"

"At Shrek?" Liu Feng said. "Both."

Lan looked behind them.

The pool erupted.

False magma surged up the shore, no longer patient, no longer pretending to be a hiding place. The wave rolled toward them with enough heat to make the air scream.

"Door," Lan said.

No one argued.

The three of them ran.

Lan entered first because he had already decided that if the door killed them, at least the decision would be his. Liu Feng followed half a step later, spear ready. Qian Lei stumbled through last, muttering something about Shrek Academy's unhealthy obsession with dramatic entrances.

The red light swallowed them.

At almost the same moment, the fissure holding Dorm336 changed its answer.

Luo felt it first.

His hand jerked off the floor as if the stone had bitten him.

"Shift!"

Song did not question him. The Stoneplate Tortoise shield slammed outward, widening the usable gap by inches as fragments fell from above.

Yan's wings opened, not fully, but enough to draw heat away from Song's back and Luo's exposed side. Red-gold fire that had pressed outward through the seam folded back around Dorm336 like a cloak being pulled home. Purple flickered once along the wing edge and disappeared before it could become wasteful. The Fire Phoenix inside him had no room for grandeur here. Only command.

The fissure's red flow changed direction.

For several breaths, Yan's pressure had used the false geothermal current as a channel.

Now the simulation was closing the channel.

"Back?" Song shouted.

"No back," Luo said. "The route collapsed. Forward, left, now!"

"Left is a wall!"

"It wasn't before!"

That was not comforting.

Yan moved anyway.

He drove one palm toward the left wall, not striking with enough force to shatter the whole chamber, only sending a compressed Fire Phoenix pulse through the crack Luo indicated. The stone did not explode. It peeled open along a line already waiting inside it.

Red light leaked through.

Not magma.

A door.

A hand of light extended from it, beckoning with the same unreasonable calm as every door Shrek had offered them so far.

Song stared. "I hate that I am relieved to see a mysterious red hand."

"Complain after entering," Yan said.

He shoved Luo through first.

Luo did not waste time pretending dignity mattered. He dove.

Song followed with his shield raised behind him.

Yan entered last, folding his wings as the fissure ceiling cracked and a tongue of false magma swept through the space where his back had been.

Red light closed.

The barren valley disappeared.

Lan Xuanyu hit grass.

For one bewildering instant, the softness offended him.

After so much stone, ash, heat, and death, grass felt like a lie of a different kind.

He rolled once, came up on one knee, and lifted both hands. Golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass wrapped around Liu Feng and Qian Lei before he fully understood where they were.

Liu Feng landed in a crouch, spear already pointing toward the nearest tree.

Qian Lei landed face-down.

"I have defeated two Dragons," he said into the grass. "If the grass attacks me, I surrender."

The grass did not attack.

It smelled alive.

Lan slowly took in the surroundings.

They were still in a valley, but not the one they had survived. Tall trees grew thickly around them, their canopies layering green shade over warm sunlight. Vines hung from branches. The air carried moisture instead of ash. Life aura filled each breath so strongly that his exhausted meridians seemed to loosen on their own.

No Ground Fire Lizards.

No Scarlet Dragons.

No burning stones falling from the sky.

That made Lan more cautious, not less.

"Stay alert," he said.

"I am alert," Qian Lei mumbled without lifting his face. "I am alert at ground level."

Liu Feng nudged him with one foot.

"Up."

"The ground and I are discussing my future."

Lan heard movement before Liu Feng could reply.

Not from the forest.

From the air to their right.

Red light opened again.

All three boys turned at once.

Qian Lei scrambled upright so quickly that grass stuck to his cheek.

A second door formed several dozen meters away, its edge angled as if it had been cut from a different corridor of the simulation. Luo Haoran tumbled out first and rolled twice before stopping beneath a low shrub. Song Yichen followed with his shield over his head. Yan Shuo stepped through last, wings half-open, copper-red hair leaking a brief red-gold shimmer before he restrained it.

For a moment, both teams stared at each other.

Qian Lei's mouth fell open.

"They're alive?"

Liu Feng said nothing, but his eyes changed.

Lan felt something loosen in his chest that he had not given himself permission to feel during the fight.

Yan saw them too.

His gaze found Lan first, then moved to Qian Lei, Liu Feng, the green grass under their knees, and the absence of Dragons. His expression did not soften completely. Public Yan Shuo did not have that luxury in front of Shrek's eyes. But his shoulders dropped a fraction.

Alive, that fraction said.

Lan gave the smallest nod.

Alive.

Song lay on his back for two breaths and then lifted one hand weakly. "If anyone asks, that was controlled entry."

Luo, still under the shrub, said, "Your shield bounced off a root."

"A controlled root."

Qian Lei pointed at them. "How did you survive?"

Song pointed back without sitting up. "How did you survive?"

Qian Lei opened his mouth.

Then stopped, because any honest answer began with hiding in magma and ended with a murderous green bird entering a Dragon's eye, and he suddenly understood that he did not sound more reasonable than they did.

Before either team could cross the distance, light rose between them.

It was not a wall of force in the usual sense. It looked more like clear glass made of air, a translucent partition cutting the valley into separate assessment pockets. The trees beyond it remained visible. The other team remained visible. Sound dulled, as if the simulation wanted to allow proof of survival but not conversation.

Yan reached toward it and stopped before touching.

Lan did the same from the other side.

A shimmer passed between their fingers, several meters apart.

Not hostility.

Boundary.

Luo pushed himself to his feet and narrowed his eyes at the partition. "Separate scoring spaces."

Song finally sat up. "You can tell that from a glowing wall?"

"I can tell it from the fact that Shrek never gives one test when it can give three."

Yan looked beyond the barrier at Dorm333.

Qian Lei was gesturing wildly now, probably explaining something that made Liu Feng look increasingly unwilling to be associated with him. Lan stood slightly apart, still watching the forest, still calculating. Even exhausted and covered in false magma residue, he looked more awake than he should.

Yan did not know the full mechanics of what had happened in the pool.

He knew the result.

Dorm333 had found a road through a place everyone else should have avoided, and Qian Lei had released something sharp enough to finish what fire, claw, collision, and Phoenix pressure had opened.

That was enough to make the Fire Phoenix in his chest quiet for one strange breath.

On Dorm333's side, Lan lowered his hand from the barrier.

"Their result is being kept separate," he said.

Liu Feng nodded slowly. "So the qualifier isn't over?"

Qian Lei looked betrayed by the trees. "If it isn't over, I will file a complaint with every plant here."

Footsteps answered him.

Liu Feng moved first, White Dragon Spear snapping into place.

Lan's golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass flashed, wrapping Liu Feng's waist with one strand and Qian Lei's body with another. Qian Lei's Coin of Summoning appeared in his palm out of pure habit, the beginnings of a gate flickering uncertainly.

A figure emerged from the forest.

Tall, slender, smiling as if she had not just watched children hide in magma while Dragons ate each other alive.

Ling Yiyi.

Qian Lei almost collapsed from relief.

"Sister Ling! Please tell me the trees are not the third phase."

Ling Yiyi's smile deepened. "Relax. If the trees wanted to test you, you would know."

Qian Lei looked at the trees with renewed horror.

Lan did not release the grass immediately.

Ling Yiyi noticed and seemed pleased rather than offended.

"Very good reaction speed," she said. "Even after changing environments, you prepared formation first instead of assuming safety."

Liu Feng lowered his spear a little, but not fully.

Lan withdrew the Blue Silver Grass from Qian Lei and Liu Feng only after confirming that no other aura moved nearby.

Across the partition, another figure of light appeared before Dorm336. It looked like Ling Yiyi at first glance and not exactly like her at second glance, as if the simulation had produced an assessment projection rather than a person. The projection raised one hand. Yan, Song, and Luo became still, listening to words Dorm333 could not hear.

Ling Yiyi followed Lan's glance.

"Team Dorm336's survival path has been recorded," she said. "The academy does not ignore a team that finds a valid route, especially not a route that alters a fire-dragon battlefield. Their assessment is being handled separately for now."

Qian Lei blinked. "So they count too?"

"Of course."

"Then are we first or are they first?"

Liu Feng closed his eyes briefly.

Lan said, "Qian Lei."

"What? This is important."

Ling Yiyi laughed softly. "The qualifier's detailed results will not be announced here. You will learn them later. For now, I am here to speak with the three of you."

Qian Lei straightened immediately. "If it is praise, I am ready. If it is another Dragon, I am absent."

"All of you performed excellently," Ling Yiyi said. "In a certain sense, your qualifier has already ended."

"In a certain sense," Lan repeated.

Ling Yiyi's eyes curved. "You dislike that phrase?"

"It usually means the important part has not ended."

"A cautious answer." She nodded. "Good. Shrek prefers students who listen to the parts between words."

Qian Lei leaned toward Liu Feng and whispered, "I listened to the part where she said excellently."

Liu Feng whispered back, "That is because it was the easiest part."

Ling Yiyi looked at Qian Lei.

The smile remained, but something in her gaze sharpened.

"Qian Lei."

Qian Lei froze.

"Ah? Yes? Present. Innocent. Mostly."

Lan had a sudden feeling that the chapter of danger had changed its shape.

Ling Yiyi said, "I have good news for you. If you are willing, you may be admitted to Shrek Academy in advance."

The valley became very quiet.

Even Qian Lei had no words.

Then his face emptied, filled, emptied again, and finally settled into a kind of stunned disbelief so complete that Liu Feng reached out as if to make sure he was still breathing.

"Early... admitted?" Qian Lei said. "Me?"

"You," Ling Yiyi confirmed. "Your martial soul has been recognized by the academy. If you accept, you can register as a new student of Shrek Academy's outer court without waiting for the following assessment stages. One of the thirty places can be yours."

Qian Lei's lips parted.

No joke came out.

For once, the boy who could complain while being dragged out of false magma had been struck silent by something larger than fear.

Lan looked at him and felt surprise first.

Then envy.

He did not like the envy, but it existed, so he acknowledged it and put it where it belonged. Qian Lei's summoning had always looked unreliable to others. He had been mocked for it, doubted for it, carried and carrying in uneven measure. Today, in thirty seconds of green light, that martial soul had revealed a door even Shrek could not ignore.

He deserved joy.

Liu Feng's eyes were complicated too, but the first thing in them was happiness.

"Fatty," Liu Feng said softly, "you did it."

Qian Lei's eyes reddened at the edges.

Then vanity rushed in to save him from sincerity.

"I knew," he said faintly. "I always knew Shrek Academy had excellent taste. It only took them one qualifier and two dead Dragons to notice."

Ling Yiyi smiled. "Then your decision?"

Qian Lei swallowed.

The word Shrek had weight.

It was not merely an academy. It was every story told to children who dreamed of soul rings and battle armor and names that did not disappear. It was the place adults spoke of with reverence even when trying not to. It was a future so large that an ordinary family could spend three generations pointing at it.

Qian Lei was only twelve.

Twelve was old enough to want glory.

Young enough for glory to feel like the whole sky.

"I accept," he said, too quickly and too honestly. "Of course I accept! Sister Ling, thank you! I accept!"

He bowed twice in a row, nearly hitting his own knees.

Liu Feng laughed despite himself.

Lan smiled.

Across the partition, Yan could not hear the words clearly, but he saw Qian Lei bowing, saw Ling Yiyi's attention fixed on Dorm333 rather than Dorm336, saw Lan smile with a complicated edge.

Song, on the other side, rubbed ash from his jaw. "Why do I feel like something dangerous just happened even though no one is roaring?"

Luo watched the partition. "Because the adults are smiling."

Yan said nothing.

A battlefield announced danger with heat and teeth.

An academy announced it with offers.

Ling Yiyi waited until Qian Lei's excitement rose high enough to make him forget the ground beneath it.

Then she turned to Lan Xuanyu and Liu Feng.

A trace of regret entered her expression.

Lan saw it.

His smile faded.

Ling Yiyi said gently, "There is one thing I must tell you. The academy's following assessments are arranged for teams of three. If Qian Lei registers in advance and leaves your team structure, the two of you may not be able to continue with the later tests."

Qian Lei's bow stopped halfway.

Liu Feng went still.

Lan did not speak.

The life-filled valley suddenly seemed much colder than the false magma pool.

Ling Yiyi's voice remained soft.

"I am sorry. These are the rules."

Qian Lei slowly straightened.

The joy on his face had not vanished yet.

It simply had nowhere safe to stand.
