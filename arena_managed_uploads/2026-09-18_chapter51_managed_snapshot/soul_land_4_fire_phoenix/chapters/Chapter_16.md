# Chapter 16: The Door That Answered

The third fireball came lower than the first two.

Lan Xuanyu saw that in the instant before it crossed the melting ice thorns. The hundred-year Lava Hound had learned from Liu Feng's redirection. Its head dipped, its shoulders rolled, and the fireball did not fly toward the spear this time.

It flew toward Qian Lei.

Qian Lei's Summoning Money hung between his hands, trembling with the half-formed light of another gate. His face was pale from strain and hope. He had finally reached the point where his first soul skill could answer again.

If he moved now, the gate would collapse.

If he did not move, the fireball would reach him.

Lan Xuanyu moved first.

Silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass snapped from his left hand. Frost burst beneath his feet, slick and thin, giving him the speed he could not afford to waste. The fireball filled his vision. Heat washed over his skin. His remaining spirit power was already low, and his chest hurt from breathing smoke and cold together.

He did not dodge.

His shoulder struck Qian Lei's side and threw him away from the path of the fireball.

The explosion caught Lan Xuanyu instead.

For a moment there was only orange light, heat, and the hard feeling of the ground leaving his feet.

He flew backward.

Qian Lei shouted his name.

Lan Xuanyu's back hit something that was not stone.

The Gate of Summoning stood behind Qian Lei, half-open, its frame trembling like a door made from water and old coins. Lan Xuanyu crashed against it shoulder-first. Pain burst across his ribs. Instinct drove his left hand out.

Silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass wrapped around the gate frame.

The half-formed doorway froze.

Not in ice.

In attention.

Lan Xuanyu's fingers tightened before he understood why. The silver lines on the Blue Silver Grass brightened. A strange pull came through his palm, not like ordinary soul power and not like the cold he used in battle. It felt far away. It felt deep. It felt as if something on the other side had turned its head.

The Gate of Summoning stopped shaking.

Qian Lei stared.

"It stabilized?" he blurted.

Lan Xuanyu did not answer. He could not. His left hand seemed to have become part of the doorway, and through it came a breath of air that did not belong to the Lava Hound arena.

Cold air.

Clean air.

Then someone stepped out.

She was not a spirit beast.

She looked like a girl not much older than them, with long dark-blue hair and a white dress that made the colosseum's smoke and fire seem suddenly clumsy. Her eyes opened on the Lava Hounds, the cracked ground, Qian Lei's stunned expression, Liu Feng gripping his blood-slick spear, and Lan Xuanyu with silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass still wrapped around the gate.

For one heartbeat, everyone stared.

Then a Lava Hound leaped.

The girl raised her hand.

An ice wall erupted from the ground.

The hound struck it and bounced back with a howl. Frost crawled across the stone under its paws.

Qian Lei's mouth opened.

"Beautiful," he whispered, then seemed to remember that terror and business existed. "No, wait, summoned beauty! I summoned a beauty!"

The girl's gaze turned to him.

It was colder than the ice wall.

"Where is this?"

"Soul World!" Qian Lei said quickly. "My Gate of Summoning brought you here, so technically you are my—"

A shard of ice formed beside her hand.

Qian Lei changed direction with the survival instinct of a boy who had lived with bad luck for years.

"—honored temporary ally! Very honored. Extremely temporary if you want."

Liu Feng, pale and still panting, looked from the girl to Lan Xuanyu's left hand. "She came after you touched the gate."

Lan Xuanyu heard him, but his attention was split. The connection through the silver-patterned grass was still there, faint and alive. The girl's aura brushed against his senses like cold water over metal.

She looked at him next.

Her expression changed a little.

"You," she said. "Your aura pulled me."

Lan Xuanyu frowned. "I did not know it would."

"Clearly."

Another fireball formed behind the ice wall.

The girl's feet left the ground by less than a handspan as cold gathered around her. Two soul rings rose from beneath her.

Yellow.

Purple.

Qian Lei made a sound that was almost worship.

"A two-ring Spirit Master," Liu Feng said, voice tight.

The girl did not bother explaining. Ice gathered in her hand and stretched into a dark-blue spear. She moved before the next fireball launched.

The spear pierced the first Lava Hound through the shoulder and pinned it to the ground in a burst of frost. She spun, white dress flashing between fire and smoke, and an ice ring opened beneath the nearest pack. Ten-year Lava Hounds skidded, howled, and froze at the paws. Ice blades followed, quick and precise.

Dorm 333's desperate arena changed shape.

Not safe.

But possible.

Lan Xuanyu forced himself upright. His left hand still held the gate, and his right hand gathered golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass close to his body without releasing it. He did not understand the girl. He did not understand the doorway. He understood only that she was fighting and that fighting alone in this arena was how Liu Feng had nearly been swallowed.

A hundred-year Lava Hound broke through the frost on her left.

Lan Xuanyu's silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass moved.

It wrapped around the girl's waist and pulled.

She turned her head sharply, but the grass dragged her just outside the hound's bite. The hound's teeth closed on empty air. At the same instant, something passed through the grass connection.

Dark-blue light spread along the silver-patterned strands.

The girl's eyes widened.

The ice spear in her hand brightened until the air screamed around it.

She thrust.

The spear split into a line of cold that punched through the charging Lava Hound and drove frost across the ground behind it. The beast dissolved into light.

Lan Xuanyu nearly fell to one knee.

The girl landed lightly, one hand touching the grass around her waist.

"Your grass strengthens me," she said.

Qian Lei looked ready to faint from joy. "My summon and Xuanyu's grass are compatible! This is destiny! This is wealth!"

"It is not your destiny," the girl said.

"Shared destiny?"

"No."

"Team destiny?"

"Fight."

Qian Lei fought by closing his mouth, which at that moment was the most useful thing he could do.

Lan Xuanyu pulled the grass taut again. Liu Feng drew in a breath, ignored his injured hands, and took position at Lan Xuanyu's right rather than racing away alone. The girl became the blade of ice. Lan Xuanyu became the line that kept the blade from being surrounded. Liu Feng cut off stragglers when his body could bear it. Qian Lei protected the gate and tried not to offend the person who had come through it.

The Lava Hounds still had numbers.

They no longer had control.

Ice walls rose and collapsed. Ice blades flashed low through legs. The dark-blue spear struck eyes, joints, throats, and open mouths before fire could gather. When a hound tried to blindside the girl, Lan Xuanyu pulled her away. When she needed more force, the silver-patterned grass tightened and that dark-blue bridge appeared again, making her ice denser and sharper.

The last hundred-year Lava Hound tried to leap over the frost line.

The girl looked up.

An ice spear rose from below and met it in midair.

The beast shattered into light before it touched the ground.

Silence fell over the colosseum.

Then the arena dissolved.

Qian Lei caught his Summoning Money with both hands and hugged it against his chest.

"We won," he said, reverently. "We actually won."

A Soul World prompt announced the reward in its flat voice.

One Soul coin returned.

One Soul coin gained.

Qian Lei's eyes shone as if someone had handed him a kingdom.

Lan Xuanyu swayed.

The girl glanced at him once more before the gate's pull returned around her.

"If this happens again," she said, "explain properly."

"Absolutely," Qian Lei said. "I am excellent at—"

She vanished through the gate.

"—explanations," he finished weakly.

Liu Feng looked at Lan Xuanyu.

His hands were no longer bleeding in the same way now that the scenario had ended, but he kept flexing them as if the pain had taught his fingers a lesson.

"It changed when you touched the gate," he said.

Lan Xuanyu looked at his left hand.

The silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass had already withdrawn, but the sensation of distant cold had not fully vanished.

"Then we test it," he said.

Qian Lei looked up from the reward coin. "Again? Right now? We just survived!"

"That is why we test it now," Lan Xuanyu said. "Before we decide it was luck."

Qian Lei stared at him as if friendship had become an expensive disease.

Then he looked at the extra coin.

His expression became solemn.

"For science," he said.

Liu Feng snorted despite himself.

They returned to the counter.

One Soul coin left Qian Lei's hand again.

He watched it go with less grief this time, because possibility had begun whispering interest.


Dorm 336 did not see any of it.

Their stone yard was busy teaching them humility with teeth.

Yan Shuo's world had narrowed to three things: Song Yichen's shield line, Luo Haoran's ankles, and the exact height at which Needleback rats liked to leap when they thought a child's attention had gone upward.

The last Ironhorn ram snorted from across the yard. Its horn was cracked from Song's plate. Its left foreleg trembled from Luo's cuts. Its flank carried two dark scorch marks where Yan Shuo had placed dawnfire precisely instead of angrily.

It was still standing.

So were they.

That felt like an achievement and an accusation.

"No wide fire," Song said, breathing hard.

Yan Shuo wiped dust from his mouth. "I know. The rats use the blind edges."

"I was reminding Luo."

Luo, who had been halfway into a grin, froze. "I am extremely reminded."

The ram charged.

This time Song did not wait for it at the center. He stepped half-left and made his Stoneplate Tortoise armor show the beast an angle. Luo's Threadwind Mantis light cut low, not at the leg, but at the rat wave trying to follow the charge. Yan Shuo let Dawnflame Kite's pulse settle behind his ribs.

Not more.

Cleaner.

Heat crossed the ground in a narrow crescent. It did not become a wall. It became a boundary the rats chose not to cross until Luo had already sealed the next gap.

The ram struck Song on the slant.

Stone rang.

Song slid.

Yan Shuo moved through the space behind the shield, Dawnfeather Mantle folding over his shoulder. No full burst this time. No expensive sunrise. He used the remaining heat like a hand on the beast's balance, striking the scorched flank exactly where the previous burns had made it flinch.

The ram twisted.

Luo cut.

Song drove forward.

The Ironhorn ram dissolved into light.

For three breaths, none of them celebrated.

Then the last Needleback rats scattered, and the stone yard began to break apart around them.

The Soul World voice announced their result.

Scenario cleared.

One Soul coin returned.

One Soul coin gained.

Luo sat down immediately on the vanishing ground. "I am going to become a civilian. Civilians have chairs."

Song's stone plates faded from his arms. His face looked calmer than his breathing sounded.

"You would overextend while sitting."

"I would sit aggressively."

Yan Shuo laughed, then stopped because laughing pulled at the sore line under his ribs.

The pain was not full pain. Thirty percent still became a teacher if it knew where to stand.

His shoulder remembered the stone scrape. His thigh remembered the rat teeth. His lungs remembered the moment he had almost chosen a stronger burst because it would have felt good to be the answer.

Dawnflame Kite pulsed quietly.

Not proud.

Not frightened.

Awake.

Yan Shuo flexed his fingers. The low-output Dawnflame Form had already withdrawn, but the places it had touched remained warm: shoulders, upper back, elbows, knees, breath. Something in his body kept replaying the last charge from three angles at once.

Song's shield.

Luo's cut.

His fire.

No voice told him what to learn.

The lesson settled anyway.

A team opening was not a chain.

It was a road wide enough for power to arrive without burning the people beside it.

The stone yard vanished.

Dorm 336 returned to the training-ground counter with dust still in their hair and a Soul coin heavier than it had been before.


Dorm 333 returned to the colosseum.

The gates opened.

Lava Hounds poured out again.

Qian Lei raised his Summoning Money with almost religious seriousness.

"Come," he whispered. "Come, beautiful wealth. Come, stable future."

Lan Xuanyu ignored the words and waited for the gate.

When the Gate of Summoning opened, he stepped forward at once. Silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass wrapped around his left hand and caught the frame deliberately this time.

The same distant cold answered.

The gate steadied.

Qian Lei's face went still.

The dark-blue-haired girl stepped out again.

This time she did not look confused first.

She looked annoyed.

"You again."

Qian Lei almost cried. "It worked. It really worked. Same summon, same beauty, same—"

Ice formed beside her hand.

"Honored ally," he corrected instantly.

The girl looked at Lan Xuanyu. "What is happening?"

"We are trying to understand," Lan Xuanyu said. "This is Soul World on Heaven Luo Planet. Qian Lei's first soul skill opens a Gate of Summoning. When my silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass holds the gate, it seems to connect to you."

Her eyes sharpened at the words Heaven Luo Planet.

"Heaven Luo?"

"Yes."

"I am on Heaven Dou Planet."

For a moment, even the Lava Hounds seemed less important.

Qian Lei's expression became too large for his face.

"Interplanetary summoning," he breathed. "I can summon across planets. I, Qian Lei, have—"

"You have a mouth," the girl said.

Lan Xuanyu tightened the grass around the gate as the first hounds neared. "Can you fight with us again?"

She looked at the approaching beasts, then at the silver-patterned grass.

"Wrap it around my waist."

Lan Xuanyu obeyed.

The grass circled her waist. Dark-blue aura flowed along the connection, subtler at first than during the first fight, then stronger as her own soul power responded. She looked down at it with a seriousness that made Qian Lei finally stop posing.

"My power is denser," she said.

"Then we use it," Lan Xuanyu replied.

The second fight was cleaner.

Not easy.

Cleaner.

Lan Xuanyu knew not to waste ice on every hound. Liu Feng knew not to race to the far wall. Qian Lei knew that his most useful contribution after summoning was to protect the time they had bought and stop insulting their ally. The girl knew where the grass would pull and how much force Lan Xuanyu could give through it before his face lost color.

The first group of ten-year Lava Hounds fell faster.

The first hundred-year beast died under a dark-blue spear strengthened through the silver grass.

The second slammed into an ice wall and found Liu Feng's White Dragon Spear waiting at its side.

The third tried to break the connection by circling wide.

Lan Xuanyu changed his footing, pulled the girl away from its path, and laid frost under its paws. She turned the pull into a spin and drove ice blades upward through the beast's throat.

Again, the colosseum dissolved.

Again, the reward voice spoke.

Qian Lei held two coins now and looked as if he had personally invented dawn.

"Stable," he whispered. "Repeatable. My first soul skill is repeatable."

Liu Feng looked at Lan Xuanyu. "Your grass makes it repeatable."

Qian Lei clutched the coins to his chest. "Our skill. Our skill is repeatable. Team property. Very team property."

The girl had not returned through the gate yet. She stood with one hand still on the silver grass at her waist, studying Lan Xuanyu as if his existence were a puzzle that had interrupted her day.

"What is your name?" Lan Xuanyu asked.

She did not answer immediately.

Qian Lei leaned forward. "Mine is Qian Lei, future greatest summoner in the federation, and this is—"

"I did not ask you."

Qian Lei leaned back.

Lan Xuanyu almost smiled despite his exhaustion. "Lan Xuanyu."

The girl's gaze paused on him.

Before she could speak, Qian Lei recovered enough to boast.

"Remember the name Qian Lei too. Without my Gate of Summoning, none of this interplanetary greatness could happen. Heaven Dou Planet, Heaven Luo Planet, all through me! Perhaps next time I can charge appearance fees."

The cold around the girl sharpened.

"Next time," she said, "try silence."

The gate took her back before Qian Lei found a response.

For several seconds, Dorm 333 stood in the training-ground hall outside the dissolved arena and listened to Qian Lei's future fortune collapse into one offended breath.

Liu Feng spoke first.

"You annoyed our strongest ally into leaving."

"She was already leaving," Qian Lei said weakly.

Lan Xuanyu looked at the counter, then at the coins.

The thought had already arrived.

Qian Lei saw it and stepped back. "No. Absolutely not. We tested it twice. The scientific method is satisfied."

"We need to know what we can do without her," Lan Xuanyu said.

"We can rest. Rest is something we can do without her."

Liu Feng flexed his fingers around the White Dragon Spear. "He is right. If she leaves in a real fight, we still have to survive."

Qian Lei looked betrayed by both teammates and economics.

A coin went back to the counter.


The third entry felt different before the gates opened.

Lan Xuanyu's body had learned too much too quickly. The Soul World restored ordinary injury at the end of a cleared scenario, but it did not erase everything. There was a deep tiredness beneath the surface now, a place behind his bones that did not refill just because the arena reset.

He knew it.

He still stepped forward.

This time Qian Lei did not open the gate.

This time three hundred-year Lava Hounds emerged without the cover of the ten-year pack.

They were larger, smarter, and angry in a way the earlier swarm had hidden. Fire rolled from their backs. Their paws left black marks on the arena floor.

Liu Feng lifted his spear.

Qian Lei swallowed.

Lan Xuanyu looked at his hands.

Silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass had strengthened the girl.

His gold-patterned Blue Silver Grass had always felt different.

Ye Lingtong had been suppressed by him before. The girl had been strengthened. What about Liu Feng?

There was no time to think gently.

The first Lava Hound charged.

"Liu Feng," Lan Xuanyu said, "come here."

Liu Feng did not argue. That alone showed how much the first two fights had changed him.

Lan Xuanyu grabbed his arm.

Gold-patterned Blue Silver Grass wrapped around Liu Feng's wrist.

White light burst from the White Dragon Spear.

Liu Feng's eyes widened.

The spear seemed to wake. Scales along its shaft brightened. The dragon-headed tip hummed with sharper force, and Liu Feng's body leaned forward as if speed had found a spine.

"Go," Lan Xuanyu said.

Liu Feng went.

This time his speed did not scatter away from the team. Lan Xuanyu's grass held a line to him. The White Dragon Spear struck the leading Lava Hound's shoulder and drove it sideways with far more force than before. Liu Feng landed, twisted, and used the pull of the grass to avoid the second beast's bite.

Qian Lei stared. "You can strengthen him too?"

Lan Xuanyu's face tightened. "Maybe."

Maybe was not enough.

The three hundred-year Lava Hounds attacked together.

Ice spread under their feet. One slowed. Another leaped over it. Liu Feng intercepted, spear flashing white-gold where the grass strengthened him. The impact threw him backward, but Lan Xuanyu pulled him clear before the third hound's fireball struck.

The explosion shook the arena.

Lan Xuanyu felt the last of his ordinary strength sliding away.

His right hand clenched.

The lesson Nana had left inside him waited in his muscles and bones: not complete, not safe, not something he fully controlled. A golden dragon shadow had once been more feeling than technique, more instinct than skill.

Soul World was not the real world.

It copied the real body. It also allowed a child to fall, fail, and return with a cost that was not death.

If he was ever going to test the unfinished movement under controlled danger, this was the place.

Lan Xuanyu inhaled.

Gold-patterned Blue Silver Grass wrapped around his right arm.

A low dragon cry seemed to pass through the arena.

Golden light rose from his body.

Qian Lei's mouth opened, but no words came out.

Liu Feng felt the change and threw himself aside without being told.

The three Lava Hounds lunged.

Lan Xuanyu moved.

Golden Dragon Soar was not complete.

It did not make him a dragon. It did not give him endless strength. It lifted his body in a fierce, arcing strike, golden light gathering at the point of impact like a young dragon throwing itself through a storm before its wings had learned the sky.

The first hound met the blow head-on and shattered into light.

The second was caught by the edge of the strike and slammed into the wall. Liu Feng followed with a White Dragon Spear thrust strengthened by the fading grass connection.

The third fireball burst against ice that Lan Xuanyu barely managed to raise.

Heat swallowed him.

He came through it coughing, golden light fraying around his arm.

The remaining Lava Hound leaped.

Qian Lei, who had no spear and no ice and no useful summon, threw a loose piece of arena stone at its eye.

The stone missed.

The hound turned its head anyway.

That tiny turn was enough.

Lan Xuanyu stepped in, right arm heavy as if the bones had filled with metal, and drove the last fragment of Golden Dragon Soar into the beast's chest.

The arena broke into light.

Dorm 333 stood in the white space after victory, all three boys too tired to pretend they had meant every part of it.

Qian Lei looked at the coins they had gained and then at Lan Xuanyu's face.

"I am happy," he said carefully, "but I am also frightened by your training ideas."

Liu Feng lowered the White Dragon Spear. He was staring at his own hand now.

"When you held me," he said, "my spear became stronger."

Lan Xuanyu nodded once.

His stomach felt hollow. His limbs felt restored on the surface and empty underneath.

"We keep that quiet first," Liu Feng said.

Qian Lei immediately hugged his coins closer. "I can be quiet. I am famous for silence."

Liu Feng looked at him.

"I am famous in advance," Qian Lei amended.

Lan Xuanyu tried to answer.

His stomach growled loudly enough that even the fading arena seemed to hear it.

Qian Lei's eyes moved from Lan Xuanyu's face to his stomach.

"Food first," he declared. "Secrets after food. Possibly more secrets during food. Definitely no more spending before food."


Far away from the training ground, Tang Le stopped singing in the middle of a note.

The studio fell silent around him.

Le Qingling looked up from the side with immediate alarm. She had learned that Tang Le forgetting the world was usually either artistic or impossible.

This did not look artistic.

Tang Le's hand had lifted toward his chest.

For a moment, his eyes seemed to look through walls, through city lights, through the ordinary distance between one place and another. A faint golden disturbance had passed somewhere beyond sight. It was small compared with the storms that lived in forgotten parts of him, but familiar enough to make his heart answer before memory could.

"Tang Le?" Le Qingling asked softly.

He blinked.

The studio returned: equipment, staff, waiting silence, a schedule that could not understand why one note had broken.

"I'm fine," he said.

It was not entirely true.

It was the only answer he had.

The feeling was already fading, but it left behind a question shaped like a child and gold light.

Tang Le lowered his hand.

"Again from the last line," he said.

No one in Heaven Luo Academy's training hall heard the pause.

No child in Soul World knew he had felt anything at all.


On Heaven Dou Planet, cold mist faded from a quiet room.

The dark-blue-haired girl opened her eyes.

Her white dress had not burned. Her hair had not been touched by smoke. Soul World had returned her body cleanly to where it belonged.

Her expression remained cold.

It also remained thoughtful.

"Qianqiu?" Nana's voice came from nearby.

Dong Qianqiu turned.

Nana stood by the window, silver hair loose over her shoulders, gaze calm enough that the room seemed quieter around her. The school outside continued its normal rhythm. None of the children passing in the distance knew that a gate in Soul World had just reached across planets.

"Teacher Nana," Dong Qianqiu said, "something strange happened in Soul World."

Nana listened without interrupting.

Dong Qianqiu described the Gate of Summoning, the boy with too many words, the Lava Hounds, the silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass, and the boy whose aura had pulled her through the gate. She described how his grass had wrapped around her waist and made her ice power denser. She described Heaven Luo Planet.

At first Nana's expression did not change.

Then Dong Qianqiu said the silver-grass boy's name.

"Lan Xuanyu."

The name entered the room like a small bell.

Nana's eyes softened before she could hide it completely.

"Say that again," she said.

Dong Qianqiu watched her teacher carefully. "He said his name was Lan Xuanyu."

Nana was quiet for a long moment.

There were many things she could not say. Many memories that were not whole enough to trust. Many rules around her, after the pirate warship incident, that made every movement outward dangerous for people who had not chosen danger.

But a gate had reached across distance.

A silver-patterned martial soul had found resonance with an ice path on another planet.

"It may be a kind of spatial-frequency resonance," Nana said slowly. "Not ordinary summoning. More like two compatible points answering through the Soul World system and that gate."

Dong Qianqiu frowned. "Then it was not the fat boy who pulled me?"

"His skill opened the door," Nana said. "But the person who let the door find you may have been the boy with the silver-patterned grass."

"Lan Xuanyu."

Nana looked toward the window again.

For a moment she seemed very far away.

"If you are summoned again," she said, "ask him carefully. Confirm what you can. Do not force the gate. An unstable crossing can be dangerous."

Dong Qianqiu nodded.

"Teacher," she asked, "do you know him?"

Nana's fingers rested lightly against the window frame.

Outside, Heaven Dou's sky was clear.

"I may," she said.

That was all.

The rest stayed behind her eyes.


Yan Shuo first heard Qian Lei before he saw Dorm 333.

"I am telling you, this is the beginning of a financial era!"

"Lower your voice," Liu Feng said.

"I am whispering historically."

"You are shouting historically."

Yan Shuo turned from the training-ground counter with a cup of water in both hands. Dorm 336 had already finished its first review. Song Yichen stood beside him with his sleeves still dusty. Luo Haoran leaned against the wall in a way that suggested his legs had resigned from academy service.

Across the hall, Dorm 333 emerged from the pod area.

Qian Lei looked reborn. Liu Feng looked quieter than usual. Lan Xuanyu looked calm, which meant nothing, because Lan Xuanyu could look calm while standing in the path of disaster.

Then Yan Shuo saw how much Lan Xuanyu was eating from a snack pack one of the assistants had shoved into his hands.

That meant something.

"Did they pass?" Luo asked.

"Looks like it," Song said.

Yan Shuo started toward them, then slowed.

Not his team.

Still his friend.

There was a difference between distance and absence.

Lan Xuanyu saw him and lifted one hand in greeting without stopping his chewing.

Yan Shuo looked at the food, then at Qian Lei's shining eyes, then at Liu Feng's thoughtful silence.

"You look," Yan Shuo said, "like someone won money and lost common sense."

Qian Lei clutched his Soul coins. "I gained common sense. It is coin-shaped."

Liu Feng said, "He spent most of it immediately testing whether a miracle would happen twice."

Yan Shuo's eyebrows rose.

Lan Xuanyu swallowed. "We passed. It was difficult."

That was Lan Xuanyu's way of saying the ground had nearly eaten them.

Yan Shuo did not ask for the private details. Partly because Liu Feng's eyes had sharpened when Qian Lei opened his mouth. Partly because the training hall was full of other students. Mostly because friendship did not mean grabbing every closed door by the handle.

"Dorm 336 passed too," Yan Shuo said.

Qian Lei looked at the cup in Yan Shuo's hand. "Then why do you look like the coin bit you back?"

"Rats," Luo called from behind him. "Many rats. Philosophical rats."

Song closed his eyes briefly. "They were not philosophical."

"They taught me the meaning of pain. That is philosophy."

Lan Xuanyu's mouth twitched.

For a breath, they were only children in rough academy clothes, dusty from training worlds and hungry in ways adults would turn into reports later.

Then Lan Xuanyu's gaze moved past Yan Shuo.

Ye Lingtong stood near the corridor entrance, talking with another girl from class. She looked over because Qian Lei's whispering had failed to remain any kind of whisper. The moment her eyes met Lan Xuanyu's, guilt and stubbornness both crossed her face.

Lan Xuanyu looked down at his left hand.

Yan Shuo noticed the motion.

He did not know about the gate.

He did not know about the girl from Heaven Dou Planet.

He did not know about the golden strike that had made Tang Le stop singing or the quiet room where Nana had heard Lan Xuanyu's name.

He only knew that Lan Xuanyu had the expression he wore when a question had become too important to leave alone.

"You are going to do something troublesome," Yan Shuo said.

Lan Xuanyu finished the last bite of food.

"Maybe just ask a question."

"Those are worse."

Qian Lei immediately straightened. "What question? Is it profitable? Is it about summoning? If it is about summoning, I should supervise as the injured financial party."

Liu Feng caught his sleeve.

"No."

"You do not even know what he is asking."

"That is why no."

Lan Xuanyu stepped toward Ye Lingtong.

Yan Shuo stayed where he was.

A few weeks ago, he might have followed out of habit. A few months ago, he might have thrown a joke in front of the awkwardness because silence made him uncomfortable. Today, his shoulder still remembered the Stoneplate Tortoise shield. His ribs remembered Luo's elbow. His fire remembered waiting for the road the team made together.

Not every opening belonged to him.

That did not make him smaller.

It made the world bigger.

Dawnflame Kite stirred softly inside him, actual living age of 248 years, yellow tier, bright and young and not finished, its warmth settling under his breath. The official paperwork somewhere still carried the original one-hundred-year acquisition record. Paper lagged behind living things. Bodies did not.

Yan Shuo drank his water.

Across the hall, Lan Xuanyu called Ye Lingtong's name.

Qian Lei stopped trying to escape Liu Feng's grip.

Song opened one eye.

Luo whispered, "This feels like another scenario."

Yan Shuo watched his friend step toward a question no simulation prompt had prepared.

"Then," he said, "do not spend a coin on it."
