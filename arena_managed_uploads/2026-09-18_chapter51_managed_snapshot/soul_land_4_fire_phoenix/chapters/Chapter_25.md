# Chapter 25: The Second Weight

Ye Lingtong did not sleep well.

Sleep should have come easily after the Maze. Her body had been exhausted, her meridians still remembered the strange collapse that had stolen her strength, and her mind had gone over the battle so many times that even anger should have run out of breath.

It had not.

Every time she closed her eyes, she saw Lan Xuanyu's face in the corridor, pale and fierce in a way that did not match the boy she remembered from the elementary academy. She heard Lu Qianxun's Golden Lion Roar fill the passage. She felt her own Celestial Body martial soul answer, then fail, as if a hand had reached through her bones and told her power to kneel.

Then Liu Feng's spear came.

Ye Lingtong's hands tightened around the edge of her blanket.

Dorm 316 had taken fourth.

Fourth.

It was not last. It was not shameful for an ordinary class. In the Elite Junior Class, with Lu Qianxun's Golden Lion King, Chang Jianyi's control, and her own strength, fourth felt like being told the world had been counted incorrectly and that she was expected to accept the number.

She had also watched Lan Xuanyu after the pods opened.

He had looked tired enough to fall over.

That made her anger worse, because a person who defeated you should at least look as if defeating you had been simple. Lan Xuanyu looked as if he did not know what he had done either.

That was the part Ye Lingtong hated most.

When she reached the canteen the next morning, the noise inside reached her before the smell of breakfast did.

"Lan Xuanyu, you have to explain!"

"How could you eat all of it? What are we supposed to eat?"

"Did you hide some in a Soul Guidance storage device? No one can eat that much alone!"

Ye Lingtong stopped at the door.

For a moment, the Maze disappeared.

The canteen plates were empty.

Not almost empty. Not picked over. Empty. The covered trays that should have held enough breakfast for the whole Elite Junior Class looked as if a swarm of spirit beasts had passed through and politely taken the crumbs too.

Lan Xuanyu stood in the center of more than ten students, face red with embarrassment, mouth opening and closing without producing an answer that satisfied anyone.

Qian Lei was not beside him.

Liu Feng was not beside him.

For some reason, that made Lan Xuanyu look smaller.

Chang Jianyi stood near the front of the crowd, frowning. He saw Ye Lingtong and explained before she asked.

"When we arrived, all the food was gone. He said he accidentally ate everything because he did not eat dinner last night."

"All?" Ye Lingtong looked at the trays again.

Food for thirty people.

Lan Xuanyu's mouth twitched. "I... I was really hungry."

Someone immediately said, "That is not hungry. That is robbery."

"Or storage theft."

"Search his device!"

The words pushed toward Lan Xuanyu from every side.

Ye Lingtong's chest tightened before she decided what to do. Yesterday, she had wanted to beat him. Yesterday, his roar had made her collapse. Yesterday, Liu Feng's spear had left a shadow inside her pride.

But this was not a battle.

This was a crowd.

"Enough," she said sharply. "Are you going to make him vomit it out? If he does, will you eat it?"

Several students froze.

Lan Xuanyu looked at her with such surprise that Ye Lingtong almost regretted helping him.

Then another voice cut across the canteen, steadier and lower.

"Move back two steps."

Yan Shuo had arrived with Song Yichen and Luo Haoran.

His hair was still slightly damp from morning washing, and his academy uniform sat too neatly for someone who had clearly hurried after hearing the noise. His eyes moved once over the empty trays, once over Lan Xuanyu's embarrassed face, once over the crowd.

He did not ask how.

He did not ask why.

He said, "Teacher Mu is coming?"

Lan Xuanyu nodded quickly, grateful for a question he could answer. "I already called him."

"Then everyone waits." Yan stepped to the side instead of between Ye Lingtong and Lan Xuanyu. He did not steal the defense she had already given. He simply made a line with his body and voice. "No one searches another student's device without a teacher. No one blocks the canteen door. If more food comes, injured students and those with morning medication eat first."

Luo Haoran leaned close to Song and whispered, "He has canteen battle formation rules."

Song whispered back, "You complain less when you are fed."

"I have not been fed. That is the problem."

The humor did not erase the irritation in the room, but it made several students remember that they were hungry children rather than judges. The circle around Lan Xuanyu loosened.

Ye Lingtong glanced at Yan Shuo.

A month earlier she might have found his calm annoying. Today it was still annoying, but useful. That made it more annoying.

Lan Xuanyu stood in the newly opened space and wished he could become very small.

He had woken before dawn.

The nausea from the warship had disappeared, but hunger had taken its place so suddenly and completely that thinking became difficult. It was not the ordinary hunger he knew after cultivation or a hard training day. It was bright, sharp, and everywhere. His stomach felt empty enough to echo. Even his meridians seemed to be asking for food.

So he had waited at the canteen door.

The moment the staff opened it, he entered.

One plate became two. Two became four. The food tasted better than usual because all food tasted better when the body shouted for it. He ate until the shaking inside him softened, then ate because the softness had not reached his chest yet, then ate because stopping seemed impossible while another tray remained.

Only when the next students arrived did he understand what he had done.

It had not even made him feel full in the way it should have.

That was the worst part.

Mu Zhongtian arrived with several chefs behind him, each carrying fresh dishes. The canteen exhaled like a single large creature.

"All right," Mu Zhongtian said. "Eat first. We will handle the rest after breakfast. Lan Xuanyu may have something wrong with his body. Xuanyu, come with me. I will take you for a check."

"Yes, Teacher."

Lan Xuanyu escaped the crowd at once.

Yan Shuo had already shifted aside, leaving him a path to the door without making the movement look like rescue. Their eyes met for half a breath.

Lan Xuanyu's expression said, I do not know how to explain this.

Yan's small nod said, Then do not explain it here.

Lan Xuanyu followed Mu Zhongtian out.

The round vice president stood just beyond the door.

Most students were too busy staring at the new dishes to notice him. Yan noticed a teacher at the edge of the scene, nothing more. He had no reason to walk closer, and Mu Zhongtian's body blocked the corridor well enough.

Outside, Lan Xuanyu's stomach turned the moment he saw Yin Tianfan's smile.

"Teacher."

"Your condition is normal," Yin Tianfan said, as if normal children often ate breakfasts meant for a class. "You used up too much energy yesterday. Your meridians also need a great deal of replenishment. This sort of thing is expected. Next time, do not eat in the canteen. Come to my office for meals. Three meals. Do you understand?"

Lan Xuanyu nodded quickly.

Mu Zhongtian looked at Yin Tianfan, then at Lan Xuanyu, and the envy in his eyes was almost harder to hide than worry. Food from this vice president's office was not ordinary food. It was not something a student could simply buy by being hungry.

Yin Tianfan lowered his voice slightly. "After school, come over. Eat first, rest for an hour, then class. Danger is not something you avoid by closing your eyes. You face it until your body remembers how not to be afraid."

Lan Xuanyu's face paled for reasons that had nothing to do with breakfast.

"Yes."

Inside the canteen, Mu Zhongtian's extra dishes had restored order faster than any lecture could have. Hungry students were practical. Anger waited better after porridge.

Yan Shuo did not sit until the last tray reached the last table.

Luo watched him return and pushed a bun toward him. "If you become any more responsible, Teacher Mu will stop doing his job and make you do it."

Yan took the bun. "Then eat before that happens."

Song looked toward the door. "Lan Xuanyu did not look sick."

"Not all sickness looks the same," Yan said.

Luo's eyes sharpened. "You think it is sickness?"

Yan broke the bun in half. Steam rose between his fingers. "I think Teacher Mu took him away, and that is the part we are allowed to know."

Luo sighed. "Your rules ruin gossip."

"Good."

Across the canteen, Ye Lingtong ate without tasting much. Chang Jianyi spoke quietly beside her, but her attention kept turning toward the empty doorway.

Lan Xuanyu was strange.

He had always been strange.

But now the whole class had seen it.

---

The full Maze results were posted before the morning lessons ended.

Mu Zhongtian did not allow the class to swarm the screen at once. He made them stand in team order, which would have worked better if every team had not tried to look around every other team.

First place: Dorm 333.

No one was surprised now.

That did not make it easier to accept.

Second place: Dorm 336.

A different murmur crossed the room.

Yan Shuo stood still. Song Yichen's shoulders rose with badly hidden pride. Luo Haoran grinned until Song's elbow reminded him that grinning too widely invited attention.

Third place belonged to a team that had avoided the worst collisions and preserved points well enough to make half the class mutter about luck.

Fourth place: Dorm 316.

Lu Qianxun read the line without changing expression.

Ye Lingtong's jaw tightened.

Chang Jianyi's eyes moved, not to the rank, but to the names above them. Dorm 333 first. Dorm 336 second. The class monitor's team had not stolen the champion position, but it had not fallen away either. The old simple shape of the class had broken into three pressures: the miracle team, the steady monitor team, and the Golden Lion King team that had been defeated but not dismissed.

Mu Zhongtian let them look long enough for the lesson to sink in.

Then he said, "Results are not decorations. They are records of what happened. If you dislike them, improve the next record."

Jin Xiang lowered his head.

Several students lowered theirs too.

"Lan Xuanyu's meal arrangement will be handled separately for the time being," Mu continued. "Because he affected everyone's breakfast, he will eat under teacher supervision as a consequence. No one needs to argue about this again."

Punishment was a useful word.

It made anger neat.

The class accepted it much faster than it would have accepted special care.

Yan Shuo heard the word consequence and looked once toward Dorm 333. Liu Feng's face remained still, but Qian Lei's eyebrows jumped in a way that said he knew more than the public explanation and hated keeping his mouth closed.

Yan looked away first.

Some secrets were protected by lies.

Some were protected by everyone choosing not to pull on a loose thread.

---

Lunch made Lan Xuanyu understand that some punishments were fragrant.

Yin Tianfan's office had a dining table that Lan Xuanyu had not noticed the night before because he had been too busy learning that space could hate him. Now the table held eight dishes and a soup. The bowls were not huge, but every dish carried a warmth that ordinary canteen food did not have. Meat sliced thin enough to shine. Vegetables with pale spirit-energy vapor. Broth clear as amber and strong enough that one mouthful seemed to run all the way to his fingertips.

Lan Xuanyu forgot fear for three breaths.

Then he remembered the simulator.

The chopsticks trembled.

Yin Tianfan smiled. "Eat. If you want to survive training, learn to love food properly."

Lan Xuanyu did not need to be persuaded twice.

The first mouthful spread through his body like a door opening. The strange emptiness in his meridians eased. The hunger did not vanish, but it became organized, as if the body finally recognized material it could use without shouting.

By the time he finished, his face had color again.

"Thank you, Teacher." He bowed deeply.

"Eating matters," Yin Tianfan said. "Growing matters. But food without pressure makes a fat child, not a capable one. Rest for an hour. Then we continue."

Lan Xuanyu's gratitude faltered.

"Continue..."

"Of course."

Yin Tianfan's smile grew wider.

Lan Xuanyu suddenly understood that Teacher Ji's lessons had not been the worst thing in the world. They had only been honest about being terrible.

The simulator was worse because it waited after soup.

---

Days began to divide the class in new ways.

In public, Lan Xuanyu had been punished.

In reality, he disappeared at mealtimes and after school, returned tired, and tried to attend lessons with eyes that sometimes focused half a breath too late. His written work remained neat for a while, then began to show small mistakes that made teachers pause. Qian Lei joked louder. Liu Feng trained harder. Dorm 333 became lower profile than ever, not because the class forgot them, but because every time someone remembered them, the question of first place returned.

No one knew what to do with a miracle that looked sleepy.

Yan Shuo saw only the public edge.

He saw Lan Xuanyu come back from lunch with better color and leave after school with a face that said he was walking toward something unpleasant by choice or order. He saw Qian Lei bite back words so often that the effort nearly counted as cultivation. He saw Liu Feng's spear practice become sharper, as if every secret he could not say had been poured into speed.

Yan asked Lan Xuanyu once, in the corridor between classes, "Are you eating enough now?"

Lan Xuanyu stared at him for a moment, then laughed weakly. "Too enough."

Yan did not move aside immediately.

"That is food," he said. "I asked whether you are all right."

Lan's smile became smaller.

For a moment, Yan thought he would give the same soft answer adults liked because it made worry go away without actually solving anything. Lan had always been good at being obedient when he thought obedience would protect someone else's feelings.

Then Lan glanced toward the end of the corridor and lowered his voice.

"I am being trained," he said. "Teacher Yin is very strict."

"The round vice president?"

Lan's eyes widened with brief horror. "Do not call him that where he can hear."

"Then he should not be round in public."

A real laugh escaped Lan before he pressed it down.

That laugh told Yan more than the words did. Lan was tired, not broken. Hiding, not gone. Still Lan.

"There is a simulator," Lan admitted after a pause. "It makes me dizzy. Afterward I get hungry. Very hungry. I cannot explain all of it. Some of it is teacher stuff."

Teacher stuff was a wall, but it was not the same wall as silence.

Yan accepted it because accepting a boundary was different from pretending not to see the bruise around it.

"Teacher Nana's catching game was kinder?" he asked.

Lan considered that seriously. "Teacher Nana was not kind. She just smiled while being impossible."

"That sounds kinder than dizzy."

"A little," Lan said.

The corridor bell chimed once, warning them that lingering would soon become lateness.

Yan stepped aside, but not before saying, "If an adult tells you not to say something, then do not say it. But do not become a sleepy ghost with meal privileges and expect me not to notice."

Lan's expression softened.

"I will say what I can," he promised.

"Good. Then eat too enough."

This time, when Lan laughed, it stayed.

Dorm 336's route did not become easier because Dorm 333 became strange.

If anything, it became heavier.

Second place in the Maze gave them pride and pressure in equal measure. Other teams now treated them as a wall to measure against. Teachers asked cleaner questions. Mu Zhongtian corrected Yan's formation calls in front of the class because monitor did not mean mistake-proof. Ji Hongbin, when he passed through their combat theory session, stared at Yan's stance long enough to make Luo whisper that the Demon King had smelled breakfast fear on him.

"Your shoulders are ahead of your waist," Ji said.

Yan froze.

"If you build battle armor on that habit later, the armor will magnify the mistake. Fire children like to think attack begins from the hands. Winged martial souls like to think movement begins from the back. Both are wrong if the feet have not agreed."

He moved on after saying it, leaving Yan with a correction that hurt more because it was useful.

Close-body mecha interface lessons became less like play and more like being told by machines exactly where the body lied.

Harnesses measured force through shoulders, spine, hips, knees, and ankles. Balance rigs punished overbright flame with jolting feedback. Armature frames did not care that Yan Shuo's Fire Phoenix felt noble; if his back took recoil before his stance was ready, the frame dragged him half a step off line.

Again.

Again.

Again.

He did not work twice as hard for ordinary output.

That would have been simple and insulting.

She worked because every correct repetition sank deeper than it should have. Breath changed stride. Stride changed heat. Heat changed how Dawnflame Kite settled under her ribs. The yellow ring did not glow every moment, but it was never gone. It lived in the way her shoulders stopped jumping before her feet, in the way the pale-gold edges of flame folded back instead of spilling, in the way hunger after training felt less like emptiness and more like construction.

Something was building.

It pressed behind Rank 19 like a door with weight on the other side.

At first, Yan told no one.

Then the door began to answer during sleep.

She woke three mornings in a row with her back warm and her palms curled as if gripping invisible feathers. Dawnflame Kite's small presence was restless, not frightened, but crowded. During meditation, her soul power reached the same point again and again, touched a thin barrier, and returned denser instead of moving through.

On the fourth morning, Lin Mei looked at her over breakfast and said, "How long?"

Yan nearly dropped her spoon. "What?"

"The wall."

Yan Ke lowered his cup slowly.

Yan looked between her parents and considered lying for approximately the time it took her mother's eyebrow to rise.

"A few days," she said. "Maybe longer. It does not hurt."

"Walls often do not hurt until children headbutt them," Lin Mei said.

Yan Ke put the cup down fully. "We inform the academy."

"I can stabilize first."

"Informing is not surrendering," her father said. "It is how we make sure the next door has a handle."

Yan wanted to argue.

The warmth under her shoulders shifted, and the Dawnflame Kite tucked itself closer as if it agreed with the adults.

Traitor, she thought.

The little warmth did not apologize.

---

News of Lu Qianxun's breakthrough came one month before the end-term test.

It did not arrive as a trumpet, because academies did not officially trumpet children. It arrived as a registration update, a training-room whisper, a Golden Lion King aura that felt fuller when Lu walked into class, and the unmistakable sight of two rings during a supervised drill.

Yellow.

Yellow.

Two rings under Lu Qianxun's feet.

The class went quiet in a way it had not gone quiet for weeks.

A Soul Grandmaster.

Lu Qianxun stood taller without trying. His pride had not disappeared after the Maze; it had folded inward and sharpened. Now it unfolded again, edged by the second ring's authority. The Golden Lion King pressure behind him felt more complete, as if the phantom mane had found another layer of voice.

Several students looked from him to Yan Shuo before they could stop themselves.

Lu noticed.

Yan noticed too.

For a moment, the old question returned in a new shape. Lu had broken through first in public. Yan remained the monitor, the first raw-score student from the midterm, the Fire Phoenix with one ring and one plus sign. Which mattered more now: the second ring or the whole foundation that had not yet crossed?

Children loved crowns because crowns were easier than uncertainty.

The class tried to place one on Lu's head before the day ended.

Lu accepted the attention as if it had always been delayed, not lost.

Yan did not contest it.

That annoyed Luo more than if Yan had sulked.

"You are letting them talk," Luo said that evening in the training room.

"They are allowed to talk."

"They are saying he is strongest now."

"He has two rings now."

Song, polishing one edge of his practice shield, looked up. "That does not answer Luo's complaint."

Yan stood inside the close-body frame with the straps open around his shoulders. Without the machine active, it looked harmless. He knew better.

"Lu Qianxun's breakthrough is real," Yan said. "If we pretend it is not, we learn wrong. If I rush because other people talk, I also learn wrong."

Luo crossed his arms. "And if you are already at the wall?"

Yan's silence answered too quickly.

Song set the shield down.

Luo's eyes widened. "You are at the wall."

"Close," Yan said.

"How close?"

The training room door opened before Yan had to answer.

Mu Zhongtian entered with a tablet in one hand and the expression of a teacher who had already received three reports and disliked the same thing in all of them.

"Close enough," Mu said, "that your parents and the academy have already scheduled a Spirit Pagoda consultation."

Luo's mouth opened.

Song's did not, but his eyes brightened.

Yan looked at the tablet. His heartbeat changed.

Not fear.

Not exactly excitement.

A second ring was not an ornament. A second soul spirit was not a prize one grabbed because a rival had crossed first. The first time he had stood in a Spirit Pagoda selection room, he had learned that compatibility could look like risk and that a living soul spirit did not become easy just because adults approved the paperwork.

Mu Zhongtian's gaze softened by the smallest amount.

"You will not force the breakthrough in training tonight. You will sleep. Tomorrow, you will attend the consultation. If the evaluators say wait, you wait."

Yan inhaled.

The wall inside him pressed back.

"Yes, Teacher."

Luo looked personally offended by the word sleep.

Song picked up his shield again. "We should still review formations without him."

"You should," Mu said. "If your monitor gains a second ring and the rest of you forget how to stand, I will count that as collective stupidity."

Luo straightened. "We will be intelligent, Teacher."

Mu stared at him.

"We will try," Luo corrected.

---

The Spirit Pagoda consultation room smelled the same as Yan remembered.

Clean air. Metal. Quiet money. A faint medicinal trace under the filtration system, as if risk had been washed from the walls many times and never fully left.

The appointment tablet did not show a second name.

That was the point.

It showed **Yan Shuo**, Heaven Luo Academy Elite Junior Class student, Fire Phoenix martial soul, rank nineteen pressing the second bottleneck. It showed academy authorization, guardian consent, Spirit Pagoda consultation status, and a confidentiality flag for a high-risk purple soul-spirit match.

It did not show Yan Shuo'er.

Her parents had not survived six years of caution just to let one formal room become a public reveal before she could truly protect herself. The truth remained where it had always lived: in her own bones, in Lin Mei's eyes, in Yan Ke's hands checking the collar clasp twice before they left home.

Mu Zhongtian had signed the academy privacy line for the second-ring consultation and then stopped at the outer door when the inner fusion chamber closed. He knew the school needed a record. He did not need every reason a family kept a child safe.

No one in this room announced the private name.

No one asked the wrong question.

Yan Shuo'er stood under the public file and breathed as herself.

That was the difference today. Not a reveal. Not enough strength to throw the mask away. Only enough honesty, inside the body, to keep a new soul spirit from building itself on a lie.

Lin Mei stood on her left. Yan Ke stood on her right. The evaluator stood before the console and treated the student in front of her as the file required: Yan Shuo, Fire Phoenix, academy-approved, legally present.

Yan's fingers touched the small dull-black heat clasp behind the collar. She did not unclip it. The clasp was not a treasure and not a cultivation seal. It only helped keep outer warmth close, flattened the red-gold glimmer that sometimes caught in her hair, and reminded the world to see a pretty, lively boy with neat records instead of looking longer.

Today, the outside still needed that.

Inside, the bones did not.

She let her shoulders settle without performing the practiced boyish angle. She let her breath fall lower than the public stance liked. She did not bloom. She did not show the room the full Fire-Phoenix brightness her parents feared. Under the loose uniform and clipped collar, the change was almost invisible.

To the martial soul, it was the difference between a door and a wall.

Yan Ke noticed first, because fathers who built safeguards also knew when one was being trusted not to become a cage. His mouth tightened. Lin Mei only brushed two fingers against Yan's wrist and withdrew.

The evaluator checked the first soul-spirit screen.

Deep yellow.

Not the plain yellow of the original public paperwork. Not purple. Deep yellow, dense and warm, the color of a first ring whose living soul spirit had kept growing privately while the official record stayed exactly one hundred years.

The evaluator's eyes narrowed with professional interest, not recognition. "Student Yan Shuo. Public academy record: Fire Phoenix martial soul, Rank 19 pressing the Rank 20 threshold. Latest academy Spiritual Power record, eighty-eight. First soul spirit, Dawnflame Kite. Original public paperwork exactly one hundred years. First ring yellow category, current response deep yellow. Dawnflame Form stable at low output. We will recheck Spiritual Power before binding. Correct?"

"Correct," Yan said.

The recheck placed a cool sensor crown against her forehead.

She breathed through the familiar discomfort. Spiritual Power testing always felt as if someone had knocked on a door inside the head and waited politely but firmly until the whole house answered.

The number rose.

It passed eighty-eight.

It slowed.

It stopped at one hundred and twelve.

Lin Mei did not smile, but one tight place around her mouth loosened.

The evaluator made a note. "Spirit Connection Realm. Improved control foundation. Suitable for a true purple second soul spirit if compatibility is exact. Not suitable for choosing by pride."

Yan looked at the second projection.

Purple.

Not yellow deepening toward purple. Not a near-threshold gamble. Purple from the beginning, rich enough that even the projection seemed heavier.

A roc stood on a projected cliff. It was larger in spirit than in size, compact but not small, with iron-grey wing bones visible beneath dark feathers and earth-brown talons sunk into stone. Its back was strong, but the eye did not stop there. The whole bird looked built around the place where wing became shoulder, shoulder became rib, rib became breath, and breath decided whether weight was a burden or a beginning.

Its record line glowed purple.

One thousand two hundred years.

Yan Ke drew in a breath too quickly.

The evaluator looked at him. "That is why we are speaking slowly."

"I appreciate slow speech," Yan Ke said. "It gives panic more structure."

Lin Mei did not take her eyes off the roc. "Explain why a one-thousand-two-hundred-year purple soul spirit is still on an academy-family consultation list."

"Because its problem is not color," the evaluator said. "Its problem is awakening."

She enlarged the inner diagram. The roc's skeleton appeared in lines of iron-grey and earth-brown. At the center of the breastbone, under the folded wings, a dim ember-like point slept without flame.

"Ironwing Mountain Roc," she said. "One thousand two hundred years. Purple before fusion. Metal-earth structural bird-line soul spirit. It has a dormant skybone core and a faint ancient phoenix-branch response, but the response has never awakened. Its mountain weight wants to root. Its sky instinct wants to rise. The dormant phoenix branch wants both bone and feather to answer one center. Ordinary winged martial souls wake the sky but cannot carry the mountain. Earth and metal martial souls carry the weight but deaden the wing-line. Pure fire scatters it. Pure light thins it. Most absorbers would receive a purple ring, yes, but the best part would stay asleep or twist into crude heaviness."

The projection changed again. Failed compatibility traces appeared and vanished. Some ended in collapsed wing-lines. Some in over-hardened backs. Some in spiritual rejection warnings.

"Then why show it to us?" Yan Ke asked.

The evaluator's gaze shifted to Yan. "Because Dawnflame Kite proves the earlier file was not exaggerating. Your first soul spirit was not perfect because it was expensive or famous. It was perfect because its flaw matched what your Fire Phoenix, body, temperament, and unusual living compatibility could solve. Dawnflame Kite gave feather and fire-light. This roc can give bone, wing root, earth, metal, and ground-to-sky order. More fire would make you louder. More light would pull you away from your martial soul. A defensive shell would teach your body to hide. A speed-flight spirit would promise the sky before your body could pay for it. This one gives the missing foundation."

She paused.

"But only if it evolves during absorption. If it remains merely Ironwing Mountain Roc, you should not take it."

The words landed harder than any boast.

Not take it unless it evolved.

Not settle for purple color.

Not call an asleep trait a perfect ring because the number looked impressive.

Yan looked at the roc.

It did not preen.

It waited like a cliff waiting for weather.

Dawnflame Kite stirred inside her. The little fire-light bird was not afraid. It was awake in a way she had only felt before dangerous drills: feathers close, warmth ready, light thin and exact.

Attentive.

The evaluator touched another control. "One more matter. Do not let any presentation habit guide this fusion. Skybone alignment runs through heel, hip, spine, rib, shoulder, wing root, and breath. The body knows its correct load line better than paperwork does. If you force a trained outer posture into the fusion, the roc will build on the wrong frame."

Yan Shuo'er's throat tightened.

For years, the mask had protected her. Short hair. Loose shirts. Controlled gait. The practiced shrug that made classmates see a boy who was a little too pretty and very strict about rules. She did not hate the mask. It had given her room to grow. It had kept stares ordinary. It had let her become monitor, teammate, friend, rival, child.

But a shield was not supposed to become the skeleton.

Lin Mei's hand found her shoulder. "The cover is for outside."

Yan Ke's voice came quieter. "Not for inside."

Yan breathed.

The Fire Phoenix in her chest settled, proud and young and suddenly very still.

"Run compatibility," she said.

The room changed.

Projection became pressure.

Yan released her martial soul under instruction. The Fire Phoenix did not appear in full; it rose as a close red-gold outline behind her, juvenile and incomplete, brighter inside than the public mask usually allowed but still held tight enough that the room saw discipline before beauty. Her deep-yellow first ring circled beneath her feet. Dawnflame Kite's presence stirred in the spiritual space, copper-red at the center, pale gold at the edges, awake and ready.

The Ironwing Mountain Roc lowered its head.

Not submission.

Measurement.

The pressure touched her feet first.

That surprised her even after all the evaluator's warnings.

She had expected shoulders. Back. Wing roots. The roc began from the floor, from the place every leap had to repay. It pressed through soles, ankles, knees, hips, waist, ribs, shoulder blades, and finally the base of the neck. At each point the public stance tried to answer first: narrower, flatter, safer, trained to make observers stop looking.

Yan let those answers pass.

Not outside.

Inside.

The true body-line answered beneath the mask: not weaker, not softer, simply more correct.

The roc's mountain weight sank.

Its sky instinct pulled.

The sleeping ember at the breastbone trembled.

Pain came next.

It was not burning pain. Burning pain was old. This was shape pain, the feeling of a frame being asked to admit every place it had compensated for fear. Feet. Knees. Hips. Ribs. Shoulders. Back. Breath.

Yan nearly stepped back.

Dawnflame Kite moved.

The little fire-light bird drew warm feather-lines along the places where structure should hold without becoming a cage. Copper-red heat thinned into channels. Pale-gold motes touched the iron-brown pressure and did not turn it into light. They marked the boundary so the new weight would not grind against the old bond.

Young.

Proud.

More cost.

More speed when fed correctly.

More consequence either way.

Deep purple.

Not revealed. Not free of the public name. Different in the frame. Her back no longer felt like a place that wanted wings. Enough for now.

Rank 23.

Not Rank 20.

It only waited under the name.

End-term decides records, not rumors.
