# Chapter 47 — Five Hundred and Five

Mu Zhongtian wanted answers.

He had first place on the ranking screen, a number that made no normal sense, and two conscious members of Dorm333 standing in front of him pretending that silence was a form of student virtue.

"Twenty thousand three hundred and forty-one," he said. "Qian Lei, Liu Feng, do you understand what number that is?"

Qian Lei nodded with great seriousness. "Champion number."

"That is not an explanation."

"A very beautiful champion number?"

Liu Feng stepped half a pace forward before Teacher Mu's expression could darken further. "Teacher, Xuanyu arranged the final part. He told us the details should wait. There are still later tests."

Mu looked at Ji Hongbin.

Ji's face was stern, but he did not scold them.

"The score is public," Ji said. "The method is not. Let Xuanyu wake first."

Qian Lei immediately looked wronged and relieved at the same time, which was a complicated expression only he could perform naturally.

Before Mu could try another angle, heavy footsteps came quickly from the far end of the corridor.

Yin Tianfan arrived almost at a run.

"Deep meditation?" he demanded. "Xuanyu entered deep meditation?"

Ji nodded. "Yes."

Yin's eyes brightened at once. "And Spirit Sea? His last test was four hundred ninety-nine. He is going to break through from Spirit Connection into Spirit Sea?"

"Most likely."

"Good!" Yin slapped his palm, then remembered the corridor and lowered his voice only slightly. "Good. My disciple knows how to turn pressure into growth."

Qian blinked. "Vice President, this is your quiet voice?"

"Compared to my heart, yes."

Ji's brow twitched. "Keep it down."

Yin waved that away, but his next question was practical. "When is the next round?"

"Three days later," Mu said.

Yin glanced toward Dorm333's closed door. "Then he should have enough time. His cultivation is still low, so a first deep meditation shouldn't last too long. And he must wake to claim the Lucky Wheel reward."

At the words Lucky Wheel, Qian Lei's entire spirit seemed to stand up.

"Reward," he said softly, as if greeting an old friend he had not yet robbed.

Mu gave him a warning look. "Dorm333 earned first place. Each of you will have a chance to spin the Lucky Wheel once. Do not turn this into a performance."

Qian placed a hand on his chest. "Teacher Mu, I would never disrespect destiny."

Liu said, "You would bargain with it."

"Respectfully."

Yan Shuo stood a few steps away, still keeping the corridor clear. Song Yichen and Luo Haoran were behind him now; someone had clearly dragged them out after the ranking news reached Dorm336. Song's hair was flattened on one side. Luo looked awake only because shock was stronger than sleep.

Yin finally turned his full attention to them.

"Second place," he said. "Nine thousand eight hundred and forty-six."

Song straightened.

Yin's mouth curved. "Do not puff up. Compared to twenty thousand, it is lower. Compared to normal students, it is enough to keep teachers awake."

Song's puffing stopped halfway. "Vice President, your praise bites."

"Good praise should leave marks."

Yan answered calmly, "Dorm336 survived as a team."

Yin's eyes sharpened with interest.

That answer was better than pride.

"Remember that," he said. "Shrek likes monsters. It dislikes monsters who cannot tell who helped them stand."

Yan did not smile, but Song and Luo both looked different after the words. Not rewarded with an object. Not congratulated by ceremony. Simply seen in the right place, at the right moment, by an adult whose opinion mattered.

That was enough for now.

Ji Hongbin ended the corridor gathering before it grew teeth. "Qian Lei, Liu Feng, return inside and watch Xuanyu. Yan Shuo, do not stand guard until you collapse. Rotate and recover. Dorm336 will be called for a short report later. Your score is not ordinary, and the academy will not train you as if it is ordinary. But no shouting outside this door."

"Yes, Teacher," Yan said.

No one called it a reward.

No one opened a second pink door.

The ranking itself had already changed their weight in the academy.

That felt more real than a speech.


Lan Xuanyu woke when night had returned to the room.

At first, he did not open his eyes.

He did not need to.

The room was there inside his mind: Qian Lei sprawled over his bed with one arm hanging down, Liu Feng sitting upright in meditation, the cooled food box on the table, the faint dust near the window, the corridor beyond the door with its quiet breathing and distant footsteps. His perception did not replace sight or hearing. It slipped between them and made the world arrive with edges he had never felt so clearly before.

Then he looked inward.

Gold and silver moved through his body with a clarity that startled him. Meridians, bones, the places where the two bloodline powers brushed close without devouring each other — all of them felt more distinct. The vortex at his core turned naturally, steadier than before.

Spirit Sea.

The gate at four hundred ninety-nine had opened.

Lan opened his eyes.

Qian Lei's eyes snapped open almost instantly.

For one heartbeat Qian stared at him.

Then his face lit up.

"You're awake!"

Liu Feng opened his eyes and stood at once. "Xuanyu."

Lan blinked, letting ordinary vision settle over the new perception. "How long?"

"Almost two days," Liu said. "Teacher Ji said it was deep meditation."

Qian leaned in with grave accusation. "You slept through champion glory. This is irresponsible leadership."

"Champion?" Lan repeated.

Qian drew himself up, holding the moment as long as his patience allowed.

"Dorm333. Heaven Luo qualifier champion. Twenty thousand three hundred and forty-one."

Lan stared.

He had expected high.

He had not expected the number to survive the system like that.

Liu's voice softened. "It counted the Ground Fire Scarlet Dragons."

Lan understood.

Then Qian added, "Dorm336 is second. Nine thousand eight hundred and forty-six."

Lan's expression changed again.

"Second," he said.

Not surprise in the same way.

Recognition.

Yan's team had not merely endured. Song's shield, Luo's readings, Yan's Fire Phoenix pressure, the decision not to abandon the team — Shrek's ranking had given that route weight the whole class could see.

"Good," Lan said quietly.

Qian squinted. "You sound like you expected them to matter."

"They did matter."

Liu nodded. "Yan kept the corridor clear when we went for teachers."

Lan looked toward the door.

His new perception could not read thoughts. It could, however, make him more aware of absences and traces. He imagined Yan standing outside, not entering, not demanding answers, only making sure no one disturbed a state that might help Lan wake stronger.

Warmth moved through him.

"We should tell the teachers," Liu said.

Qian said, "And eat. He has missed too many meals to remain a reliable captain."

Lan tried to stand. The room briefly arrived too intensely — bed edge, floor, Qian's movement, Liu's soul power, corridor breath. Liu shifted closer, but did not grab him.

Lan steadied himself. "I'm fine. Just not used to it."

"Breakthrough inconvenience," Qian said. "I look forward to suffering it one day."

They left quietly.

A door cracked open almost at once. A student's whisper began, "Lan Xuanyu woke—"

"Back inside," a calm voice said from down the corridor. "He just woke from cultivation. Do not crowd him."

Yan Shuo stepped out from the stairwell shadow.

He had clearly been resting nearby rather than standing there all day. His jacket was loose over his shoulders, hair not perfectly tied, eyes still carrying fatigue from his own team's ordeal. But when he spoke, the corridor obeyed.

Lan stopped.

For a moment, public words had to do too much.

"You woke," Yan said.

"I woke," Lan answered.

"Spirit Sea?"

"I think so."

Yan's eyes softened by a fraction. "Good."

Lan said, "Congratulations. Second place."

Yan looked at him. "Congratulations. Champion."

Neither asked for hidden details.

Neither offered what could not be spoken in a corridor.

It was not distrust. If anything, it was the opposite: years of being each other's first real friend had taught them when a question would only press on an adult-built wall. Lan knew Yan had changed since childhood — more restrained, more dangerous, more beautiful in a way the public mask could barely contain — and Yan knew Lan was carrying things he could not safely put into words. They still understood enough to stand there without turning silence into rejection.

Qian, naturally, could not let silence become too meaningful.

"We have established that both teams are expensive now. Please remember this when distributing food."

Yan glanced at him. "If value depended on noise, you would bankrupt the academy."

"That is almost praise."

"It is not."

Liu said, "We are going to Teacher Yin."

Yan nodded. "I will tell Teacher Mu that Lan woke. Rest properly after eating."

The last words were directed at Lan.

They sounded like an order because Yan was class monitor.

They felt like concern because Yan was Yan.

Lan nodded and went on.


Yin Tianfan had food ready before Lan arrived, as if he had planned to win an argument with nourishment.

"Eat first," he said. "Test after the first bowl. If you collapse on my table, Old Ji will accuse me of starving you."

Qian immediately raised his hand. "Guardians of deep-meditating champions also require food."

Yin pointed to a side plate. "Eat. If your mouth slows, it counts as progress."

Qian obeyed with no further complaint.

Mu Zhongtian arrived soon after Yan's message. Ji Hongbin followed not long later. Once Lan had eaten enough to stop looking hollow, Yin pushed the testing device toward him.

Lan placed his hand on it.

The number climbed smoothly past five hundred and stopped.

505.

SP505.

"Spirit Sea," Yin said, satisfaction rolling through his voice.

Ji Hongbin nodded. "Just over the threshold. Good. Now stabilize it. A gate opening does not mean the road is finished."

Qian whispered, "Teacher Ji can turn miracles into homework."

"Correct," Ji said.

Qian straightened. "I support homework spiritually."

Mu Zhongtian ignored him and spoke to Lan. "The academy has upgraded food and recovery support for the teams that advanced. Six teams from our Elite Junior Class entered the top hundred, not five. Your team is champion. Dorm336 is second. Lu Qianxun's team is ninth. The president is already preparing reports and resources."

This was how second place changed things.

Not with a sudden treasure chest.

With schedules altered, teachers assigned, food upgraded, recovery checked, reports written, and eyes that no longer passed over Song and Luo as Yan Shuo's background.

Lan understood that better than a speech.

Qian asked carefully, "Does upgraded food mean all six teams eat the same?"

"It means all six teams get better support," Mu said. "It does not mean you steal Yan Shuo's portion."

"I had not planned that exact crime."

"Good."

Yin leaned forward, no longer willing to wait. "Now, Xuanyu. Tell us how twenty thousand points happened."

The room quieted.

This was not a corridor. These were his teachers. Strategy needed secrecy from other teams, not from the people responsible for keeping them alive.

So Lan told them.

False magma. Heat. The two Ground Fire Scarlet Dragons injuring each other. The impossible timing under their bodies. Qian Lei's Replicate preserving the Emerald Demon Bird summon. The eyes and brain as weak points. Liu Feng's positions. The red door after the kill. The character test that followed.

He kept to what belonged to Dorm333.

When Dorm336 came up, he did not invent details. He only said what could be seen from the result and from public battlefield logic: Yan's Fire Phoenix must have affected the inferior dragon-fire rhythm, but Song and Luo's survival meant the team had done more than hide behind power.

Ji Hongbin listened with a darkening face.

Yin Tianfan listened with shining eyes.

Mu Zhongtian looked as if every answer created three new reports.

When Lan finished, Yin slapped his shoulder. "Good! Calm, adaptable, and brave. You used terrain, enemy injury, teammate ability, and timing. That is command."

Ji Hongbin said, "It was also a shortcut."

Yin's smile vanished. "Old Ji."

"Do not twist my meaning," Ji said. "The tactic was excellent. But the score came from special conditions: injured targets, a rare summon, weak-point timing, and courage under extreme risk. Xuanyu, your Soul Power is still Rank20. Your body still has limits. Your foundation must rise, or Shrek will punish you for mistaking one brilliant answer for complete strength."

Lan lowered his head. "Yes, Teacher Ji."

Yin snorted. "You speak as if a battlefield waits for foundation to become perfect."

"And you speak as if cleverness can replace it."

"Cleverness saved them."

"Cleverness also gets commanders expelled when they forget principles."

The room became cold in a way that had nothing to do with element.

Qian's eyes widened.

Liu Feng went very still.

Yin Tianfan's expression changed. The joke in his face disappeared, leaving an old scar behind.

"No need to taunt me," he said quietly. "I was expelled, yes. I did not graduate. So what? I still relied on my own ability and commanded a fleet later. I studied in Shrek's Space Command System for years before special circumstances ended it."

Ji Hongbin's anger faded into something more complicated.

"That incident was not entirely your fault," he said. "Your command talent was real. Many of us thought you could enter the inner court and become a Federal fleet commander one day."

Yin waved him off. "No use talking about it."

Then he looked at Lan.

"Since the topic has reached here, I should tell you why I was fired. Not so you pity me. So you learn the lesson before Shrek teaches it harder."

His eyes shifted to Qian and Liu.

"What are you two standing here for? Planning to listen to an old man's embarrassing story for free? Old Ji, shouldn't they train?"

Qian looked betrayed. "History is education."

Ji said, "So is training."

"Training hurts more often."

"Move."

Liu stood immediately. Qian tried to take one more bun from the plate. Ji looked at him. Qian put it back with the expression of someone returning a treasure to its tomb.

Before leaving, Qian turned. "Teacher Ji, Vice President... you were both Shrek students?"

The question hung in the room.

Ji Hongbin answered plainly, "I graduated from Shrek's outer court. I did not achieve much. I disgraced my alma mater."

Qian forgot the bun.

Liu also looked startled.

Yin's face had gone still again.

Ji opened the door. "Out."

When Qian and Liu were gone, Mu Zhongtian hesitated, then left as well.

Only Lan and Yin remained.

Yin looked at the testing device still showing 505.

"Five hundred and five," he murmured. "Good. A mind that can hold more should also be made to understand more."

He leaned back.

For once, there was no performance in his voice.

"Back then, I was determined to enter Shrek's inner court..."
