# Chapter 45 — Thanks, and the Score Not Yet Shown

Lu Qianxun's hand remained between them.

For a breath, Lan Xuanyu only looked at it.

The real training-ground room was brighter than the volcanic valley had been, but the light here felt thinner. The simulation cabins stood in rows behind them, quiet now, with their transparent covers open and their indicator lamps returning to harmless blue. Students who had been dragged out of a world of meteor rain, burning plains, Ground Fire Lizards, and Scarlet Dragon roars still sat or stood in uneven clusters, their faces pale from exhaustion and their eyes unable to stop measuring the two teams that had returned last.

Dorm333.

Dorm336.

Those two labels had already begun to change shape in everyone's minds.

One had vanished beneath a false magma pool and returned after the impossible roar of dying Dragons.

The other had found a thermal fissure where there should have been only fire and stone, then forced a route open under the pressure of the same final battlefield.

Different answers.

Both alive.

Both recorded.

Lan could feel the room's attention pressing against the side of his face. He had never enjoyed that kind of pressure. In class, he was usually the one yawning quietly, listening with sleepy eyes, answering only when asked, and then doing the work that had to be done. Even after the last few years of change, even after he had slowly become the center of Dorm333, he still did not naturally stand under everyone's stare.

But Lu Qianxun's hand did not tremble.

The boy had lost. He had been eliminated earlier. His Golden Lion King pride should have made this moment difficult.

Yet he still came.

Lan raised his own hand and clasped Lu's.

"You're welcome," Lan said. "At that time, if your team hadn't held them for a while, we wouldn't necessarily have had the chance to reach the next step either."

Lu Qianxun's fingers tightened once, then released.

His expression changed very slightly.

It was not flattery. Lan had not said it to comfort him. Lu could hear that.

Chang Jianyi, standing half a step behind Lu, pushed his glasses up by habit though the lenses were clean. Ye Lingtong looked at Lan without speaking. Her lips pressed together, and for a moment the coldness she usually used as a shell did not fully cover the turmoil beneath.

Lu asked, "How did you think of it?"

Lan blinked.

"The magma pool?"

"Yes." Lu's voice was lower than usual. "We saw you go toward the Ground Fire Scarlet Dragon. At first I thought you wanted to hide under its body. That was dangerous, but not impossible. But after that, you disappeared. When your team did not come out, I could not understand where you had gone."

Several students nearby unconsciously leaned closer.

Mu Zhongtian noticed and frowned, but he did not immediately stop them.

Ji Hongbin stood behind the crowd with folded arms, eyes narrowed. Yin Tianfan was beside him, smiling with that dangerous expression that always meant he was collecting evidence for later training.

Lan felt all of that.

So he chose each word carefully.

"I did think of hiding near the Dragon first," he said. "The assessment's second phase forced the soul beasts to prioritize human targets. If we could enter the blind spot under a Scarlet Dragon's body, its own size and the confusion of the battlefield could cover us for a short while."

Lu nodded. "But that was not enough."

"Not enough," Lan agreed. "The two Dragons' bodies were too large. Their steps shook the ground. Even if they could not see us for a breath or two, the shock alone could crush the wrong position. Then I remembered the first creatures we saw."

"Ground Fire Lizards," Chang Jianyi murmured.

Lan looked at him and nodded. "Yes. Ground Fire Lizards. They came out of the magma pool."

A few students exchanged looks.

Jin Xiang, standing farther away with his teammates, looked even more unhappy. He had been eliminated early by the test's anti-flight and human-priority pressure. Hearing Lan speak calmly about using the environment only made his defeat feel rougher.

Lan continued, "If that pool had been real lethal magma, weaker Ground Fire Lizards could not have used it as casually as they did. It had heat, pressure, and concealment, but it was not the same as actual magma. In a simulation, danger can be true in effect without being identical in structure."

Lu's eyes sharpened.

"So you judged that it was a false pool."

"A false pool with real enough punishment if we made a mistake," Lan said. "We still needed water and ice protection, breath control, and timing. If we entered too early, the lizards or Dragons might notice. Too late, and there would be no chance."

"And after that?" Lu asked.

Lan paused.

Qian Lei's shoulders, behind him, tightened almost imperceptibly.

Liu Feng also glanced at Lan.

The room had become too quiet.

Lan smiled faintly, sleepy and harmless at first glance. "After that, we hid. The Dragons fought. We were lucky to last until the assessment changed again."

Lu stared at him.

Lan stared back with innocent blue eyes.

Qian Lei nearly ruined everything by looking as if he had swallowed a very expensive secret and it was refusing to go down.

Yin Tianfan's smile widened.

Ji Hongbin's eyelid twitched.

Lu Qianxun understood enough.

Lan had explained the thought process that mattered for battlefield judgment. He had not explained everything. He had not said how the two heavily injured Ground Fire Scarlet Dragons finally died. He had not said what Qian Lei's second soul skill had done. He had not said why the final score might become strange.

That was not cowardice.

That was opponent awareness.

They might meet again in later rounds.

Lu felt the weight of that restraint more clearly than if Lan had boasted.

"So it was like that," Lu said slowly.

Ye Lingtong's gaze lowered for a moment.

The false magma pool.

The Ground Fire Lizards.

The blind spot under a Dragon's body.

Lan had seen all of that while running for his life beneath meteor rain and soul-beast pursuit. She remembered the boy from years ago, the one who had tried to speak with her privately after discovering that his golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass might react to her. She remembered being pulled away before they could finish the conversation. At the time, it had seemed like an ordinary missed moment between classmates.

But perhaps some missed moments were doors.

And once closed, they did not wait forever.

She then remembered another test, another danger, another time Lan Xuanyu had lured away the Three-Eyed Witch Ape to protect her. He had not made a speech then either. He had simply acted.

Guilt came quietly, without drama.

It did not mean she hated Lu's team. It did not mean she wanted to abandon the companions beside her. Lu Qianxun and Chang Jianyi had fought hard, and she had chosen to stand with them. The Elite Junior Class did not exist for people to change teams every time regret appeared.

But she could still recognize that she had misunderstood Lan for a long time.

She could still feel the ache of that recognition.

Across from them, Lu Qianxun looked at Lan as if seeing a different opponent than the one who had sat sleepy-eyed through years of lectures.

"I always thought I was calm enough," Lu said. "At least among our age group, I thought few people could judge a battlefield more clearly than I could."

No one laughed.

Lu had earned the right to say that much. His team had charged into the second phase, had drawn fire, had endured a Golden Lion King center's burden under pressure most teams could not face at all.

"But in the qualifier," Lu continued, "I was still looking at strength first. You were looking at rules, terrain, target priority, false danger, and timing."

Lan shook his head. "Strength matters too. Without enough strength, a plan can't survive its first step."

"It matters," Lu said. "But it was not the only answer."

His gaze shifted.

It moved to Yan Shuo.

That movement changed the circle of silence again.

Yan stood with Song Yichen and Luo Haoran not far away. Song's shoulder had been checked and wrapped by a teacher's quick treatment. Luo's face still held the pale, thin look of overused sensory focus. Yan himself stood straight because he was Yan Shuo, class monitor, and because his pride would not allow him to slump in front of the class. Yet those close enough could see the exhaustion beneath the controlled posture.

The copper-red in his hair was muted under restraint, but not dull. Even without releasing his martial soul, there was something bright and dangerous in him now that had not been there in the same way before the cocoon months ago. Everyone in the room knew the broad facts by now. Rank39. Spirit Sea. Three purple rings. true Fire Phoenix. Ultimate Fire.

And now, a final-survivor route that had forced even fire-dragon-type beasts to hesitate.

Lu's expression became more complicated.

"Dorm333 was not the only answer," he said.

Yan met his gaze calmly. "No."

The single word did not boast.

It also did not retreat.

Lu said, "Your team found a route too."

Song muttered, "Found, held, regretted, held again."

Luo said softly, "And reported accurately afterward. That part is important."

Song looked at him. "Only you would call surviving Dragons 'reporting accurately.'"

"Without the report, no one knows what nearly killed us."

"The Dragons. The answer is the Dragons."

Yan's mouth moved by a fraction, almost a smile.

The slight exchange loosened the air, but not by much.

Lu looked back at Lan.

"If we are able to continue participating in the later rounds," he said, "let us cooperate. If the rules allow it, our teams can become allies."

The room stirred.

This was not the Lu Qianxun most of them knew.

He was proud. He was direct. He did not usually lower his head first. Even after Yan Shuo became class monitor through strength and consistency, Lu had remained the strongest rival-team leader in the class, someone whose Golden Lion King pressure made weaker students subconsciously straighten.

For that Lu to ask Lan Xuanyu for alliance openly was not a small matter.

Lan was genuinely startled.

Lu saw it and added, "This is not about the class monitor position; not the class monitor position at all. Yan Shuo is the class monitor. That does not change. I am talking about later-round cooperation between teams. If there is a situation that needs battlefield judgment, you lead the analysis."

A second stir moved through the nearby students.

Lu Qianxun asking Lan Xuanyu to lead tactical analysis.

Not politely listening.

Lead.

Lan did not answer immediately.

His first thought was not triumph.

It was risk.

Cooperation could help. Lu Qianxun and Ye Lingtong were both powerful offensive-type Soul Masters, and Chang Jianyi's control could stabilize a battlefield. In a large-scale assessment, an alliance between Dorm333 and Lu's team could create possibilities Dorm333 alone could not force.

But cooperation also meant exposure.

Qian Lei's summon secret.

Liu Feng's growth route.

Lan's own support effect.

And if Dorm336 also advanced, there was Yan's route to consider. Yan's public strength was obvious, but the exact shape of his control, his cost, and the way Song and Luo built a safe line around him did not need to be handed to every future opponent.

Lan glanced at Yan.

Yan did not speak for him.

That, too, was trust.

The class monitor could have stepped in. He could have turned Lu's proposal into a class-level matter. He could have claimed command because his public strength and position were higher.

Instead, Yan only watched Lan, as if waiting to see what choice he would make.

Lan thought for a moment.

"If the later rules allow cooperation," he said at last, "and if cooperating doesn't violate the assessment requirements, I have no objection. But there are things each team can't reveal before the next round."

Lu nodded without hesitation. "Of course."

"Then," Lan said, "we can help each other when there is a reason to."

Lu's shoulders loosened slightly.

"Good."

Yan said then, "If it becomes a class-wide survival situation, I will judge it as class monitor. If it becomes a team-alliance situation, each captain keeps responsibility for his own team."

Lu looked at him.

For a moment, the two strongest public leaders in the class faced each other.

Golden Lion King pride.

Fire Phoenix restraint.

Neither flared.

Neither submitted.

Lu said, "Fair."

Yan nodded once.

That simple exchange did more to settle the room than any teacher's speech could have done.

Mu Zhongtian quietly exhaled.

The Elite Junior Class had taken a terrible blow today. Many teams had been eliminated quickly. The students were tired, embarrassed, frightened, excited, resentful, and curious all at once. If Lu's pride had turned bitter, if Yan's authority had pressed too hard, if Lan had boasted, the room could have split into ugly fragments before the rankings even appeared.

Instead, the three centers had each held a different line.

Lu admitted what he had seen.

Lan accepted without exposing everything.

Yan framed the boundary without crushing either side.

Ji Hongbin's eyes remained stern, but a trace of satisfaction moved through them.

Then Qian Lei sniffed.

Everyone near him turned.

He immediately looked offended. "What? My emotions are complicated. Historic people have complicated emotions."

Liu Feng said, "Historic fool."

"Secret!" Qian hissed. "Do you want the entire class to call me that?"

Several students who had already heard enough of the earlier blurt looked at him with renewed interest.

Qian Lei clutched his chest as if arrows had pierced him.

Yan's gaze shifted to him.

The faint warmth that had eased around the class monitor's eyes vanished.

Not anger at Qian.

Anger for him.

Lan noticed immediately.

He had already guessed Yan would understand part of it. Qian had said too much in the open. Liu had said a little more. Ling Yiyi's earlier non-answers, Qian's swollen eyes, Liu Feng's protective silence, and Lan's own expression all pointed in the same direction.

Yan did not know the private words inside the Shrek pocket.

He did not know every sentence Ling Yiyi had used to separate Lan and Liu from Qian.

But he knew enough.

Shrek had given Qian Lei something real enough to hurt, then placed his team on the other side of the choice.

Yan's fingers curled once at his side.

No flames appeared.

The room did not heat.

But Lan, standing nearest, saw Yan's pupils seem to sharpen, the copper-red under restraint catching one thread of red-gold light before being pressed back down.

"They gave you certainty alone," Yan said quietly, "and made your teammates the price?"

Qian Lei's mouth opened.

For once, no joke came out first.

Liu Feng's jaw tightened.

Lan answered instead, because Qian had already bled enough pride today.

"It was probably a test," Lan said. "Not just of his martial soul. Of his choice."

Yan looked at him.

"I know."

The words were calm.

Too calm.

Yan's gaze moved past Lan toward the place where Ling Yiyi had stood before leaving the immediate center of the room to speak with another Shrek staff member near the record console. He did not raise his voice. He did not accuse Shrek Academy in public like a child who did not understand rules. But his tone carried a clean edge.

"A test can still be cruel."

The sentence landed softly.

It landed everywhere.

Qian Lei stared at him.

Liu Feng looked away for a second, blinking too hard.

Lan felt something in his chest shift.

He had told himself the same thing already: Shrek's method was intentional. It had meaning. It had tested the thing Qian most needed to prove. Lan could accept that.

But accepting a test did not require pretending it had not hurt.

Yan had seen the hurt and named it.

Not loudly.

Not romantically.

Not with any secret that should not exist in this room.

Simply because Lan and Dorm333 mattered to him.

Ling Yiyi, at the edge of the console, turned her head as if she had heard.

For an instant, her smile was unreadable.

Then she looked away again.

Yan did not pursue it.

Lan said, "Qian passed."

"I know," Yan said again.

This time his voice softened.

"That does not mean he should be laughed at for it."

Qian Lei's eyes went red again with terrible speed.

"Class monitor," he said, voice shaking, "from now on, when I become famous, I will definitely remember that you had taste."

Song coughed. "He is recovering."

Luo nodded. "Speech volume rising. Tears still unstable."

"Can Dorm336 stop analyzing my tears?" Qian demanded.

"No," Song said. "It distracts from my shoulder."

The surrounding students finally laughed, but it was gentler now.

Lan glanced at Yan.

Yan looked back.

The exchange lasted only a moment.

Thank you, Lan did not say.

You're welcome, Yan did not answer.

The words were unnecessary.

Ye Lingtong saw it.

She did not fully understand what she saw, only that there was a quiet line there, one that had not existed in the class at the beginning. Lan Xuanyu, who had once seemed distant because he was always tired. Yan Shuo, who had become class monitor through bright, impossible pressure. Two different centers, not colliding today, but aware of each other.

Her fingers tightened at her side.

Regret did not give her the right to resent either of them.

So she swallowed it and stood beside her own team.

That was also a choice.

Mu Zhongtian clapped his hands once.

"Enough. All of you, stop standing around like the simulation cabins will give you extra points for looking pitiful. Medical checks are finished for the worst cases. Return to your dorms, eat, rest, and do not spread half-understood rumors before the official rankings are released."

Qian Lei immediately raised his hand.

Mu Zhongtian stared at him. "What?"

"Teacher Mu, if rumors are fully understood, can they be spread?"

"No."

"What if they praise me?"

"Especially no."

Qian Lei sighed. "Shrek first took my early admission, now Heaven Luo takes my fame."

"You refused the first one yourself," Liu Feng said.

"That makes it worse."

Yin Tianfan laughed once. "If you still have strength to complain, you have strength to write a reflection."

Qian Lei went rigid.

"Teacher Yin, I suddenly feel extremely faint."

"Then write it while faint."

Lan finally smiled.

It was small, but real.

Yan saw it and the last hard edge in his expression eased.

Then the teams began to disperse.

Dorm336 did not leave immediately. Ji Hongbin called Yan, Song, and Luo aside for a few brief questions. None of them were detailed enough to expose their route before official analysis, but each question carried weight.

"How many times did you channel through the fissure?"

"Three controlled pressures and one emergency interruption," Yan answered.

"Full integration?"

"No."

Ji Hongbin's eyes sharpened with satisfaction.

No.

That answer mattered.

If Yan had needed his full trump state simply to affect the Ground Fire Scarlet Dragons' flame rhythm, then his public strength would have looked impressive but shallow. If he could do it without that, under team protection, through terrain, with cost and control, then the feat was repeatable enough to matter and limited enough to study.

A monster with limits was not less frightening to Shrek.

It was more valuable.

"Song," Ji Hongbin said.

Song straightened despite his shoulder. "Teacher."

"Your shield line held physical shock that your rank should not normally tolerate. Why?"

Song glanced at Yan, then at Luo.

"Because I did not try to stop the whole impact," he said. "Luo called the angle. Yan's fire pressure made the Dragon's flame side recoil first, so the body line shifted. I only anchored the part that would have broken the crack."

Ji Hongbin nodded minutely.

"Luo."

"Teacher."

"Sensory overuse?"

"Yes. The heat made false lines. I had to separate real stone pressure from flame turbulence. Next time I need a shorter call structure or I will slow the team down."

"Good," Ji Hongbin said.

Good, not because Luo had overused himself.

Good because he knew exactly how.

Lan heard fragments as he passed with Qian and Liu. He did not slow down, but he understood enough to feel a faint admiration.

Dorm336's route had not been simply Yan being strong.

That made it harder to dismiss.

It also made Yan's refusal in the Shrek pocket easier to understand.

A team route was not a phrase.

It was three people nearly burning together and still returning together.

Lu Qianxun watched them as well. His eyes moved from Song's bandaged shoulder to Luo's pale face, then to Yan's steady back.

He had asked Lan for cooperation because Lan had shown him a different way of reading battle.

But if later rounds widened, he would also have to consider Yan Shuo.

The class monitor was not merely the strongest student by raw public data. He was someone who had chosen his team when Shrek had offered a cleaner individual road.

Lu did not like admitting admiration easily.

Today had been full of unpleasant education.

"Let's go," he said to Ye Lingtong and Chang Jianyi.

Chang nodded.

Ye followed.

As they walked out, Ye turned once more.

Lan Xuanyu had already reached the door with Qian Lei and Liu Feng. Qian was talking again, hands moving as he described how his heart had been stabbed by opportunity. Liu Feng looked annoyed, but the annoyance had softened into companionship. Lan listened with that quiet smile that made him seem ordinary until one remembered the false magma pool.

Yan Shuo stood a little farther back, still answering Teacher Ji, his posture controlled and bright even in exhaustion.

Ye lowered her eyes.

There was no use envying paths already taken.

There was still a next round if they qualified.

If they did, she would fight properly.

Not to undo regret.

To be worthy of the road she was on.


Dorm333's room felt much smaller after the simulation.

That should have been impossible. It was the same room, the same three beds, the same shelves, the same traces of school life scattered in ways that would have made a stricter dorm inspector sigh.

But after a valley where Dragons could step on your heartbeat, a dorm room became something almost fragile.

Qian Lei entered first and collapsed face-first onto his bed.

"I am dead," he announced into the blanket. "Do not disturb my funeral. Donations may be placed beside my pillow. Preferably Shrek badges. Purple if possible."

Liu Feng closed the door behind them and leaned his White Dragon Spear carefully against the wall. "You are not dead."

"My future fortune died."

"You refused it."

Qian rolled over dramatically, clutching his chest again. "Because I am loyal! Why does everyone keep saying that like it makes the loss smaller?"

Lan sat on the edge of his own bed.

The moment he did, fatigue climbed up from his bones like water filling a room.

During the assessment, he had not dared to fully feel tired. There had always been another choice to make: run, hide, freeze, breathe, wait, support, pull Qian back, judge Ling Yiyi, speak carefully, return, answer Lu. Now that the door was closed and no teacher's eyes were on them, his body finally remembered that he had been under pressure for too long.

Liu Feng noticed. "Xuanyu?"

"I'm fine," Lan said.

Qian Lei lifted his head immediately. "You always say that before doing something terrifying."

Lan blinked. "Like what?"

"Like jumping into magma."

"It was false magma."

"It looked very sincere!"

Liu Feng sat down as well. His expression had quieted. "Our results should not be bad."

Qian Lei turned his face toward him with suspicion. "Not bad?"

"Who else could do what we did?" Liu Feng said. "Even if no one knows the last part, the assessment should know. We lasted until the end. We killed things that were far beyond normal student targets. We passed the Shrek test. Our ranking should be high."

Qian's expression trembled between pride and grief.

"High ranking," he murmured. "Early admission. High ranking. Early admission. Why must a human heart contain so many contradictions?"

Lan laughed softly.

Qian looked at him. "Do not laugh. You are the captain. You should comfort the wounded."

"Liu Feng already comforted you."

"He used facts. I need emotional compensation."

Liu Feng said, "You want praise."

"And possibly snacks."

Lan reached over and patted Qian's shoulder. "Then listen carefully. Today, you were not foolish."

Qian froze.

Liu Feng also looked at Lan.

Lan's voice remained light, but his eyes were serious. "You wanted the offer. That was normal. Anyone would. It was real, and it was precious. You refused because you did not want to leave us behind. That is not foolish."

Qian's mouth tightened.

Lan continued, "A person who knows something is valuable and still chooses his friends is not a fool. He is just... expensive."

For a moment, Qian stared.

Then he burst out, "What kind of comfort ends with me being expensive?"

Liu Feng's lips twitched.

Lan said, "A wise and greedy noble. Very expensive. Very loyal."

Qian sat up with great dignity. "That is better. Continue."

"No."

"Captain!"

Liu Feng finally laughed.

The sound was short, almost rusty from exhaustion, but it broke something. Qian began complaining again. Lan answered occasionally. Liu Feng inserted sharp comments at exactly the worst moments. The three of them sounded, for a little while, like the assessment had been a strange nightmare instead of a battlefield that had almost separated them.

Then Qian suddenly lowered his voice.

"You two have to keep it secret."

The air changed.

Lan and Liu Feng both knew what he meant.

Qian stared at his knees.

"The offer," he said. "Don't tell everyone the details. I already said too much outside. If people know exactly what I gave up, some will praise me, some will call me stupid, and some will ask why I did not take it. I don't want to hear it."

Liu Feng's expression softened.

"We won't say it."

Lan nodded. "We won't."

Qian let out a breath.

"Teacher Ji might ask. Teacher Yin might ask."

"Safety and assessment reports are different," Lan said. "But we do not need to spread it among classmates."

"And if Teacher Yin says I have to write a reflection?"

Liu Feng said, "Then write one sentence: I was tempted and still chose my team."

Qian considered it.

"Too noble. Teacher Yin will think I copied it."

Lan said, "Then add: I also regret the lost resources every time I breathe."

Qian pointed at him. "That is authentic."

They laughed again, quieter this time.

Outside the window, the academy grounds were returning to their normal rhythm. Somewhere in another dormitory, students were probably arguing about the qualifier. Somewhere else, teachers were waiting for Shrek's ranking system to finish processing scores across Heaven Luo Planet. The entire planet's candidates had entered the same kind of nightmare in different ways, and only the first hundred teams would continue.

Dorm333 did not yet know where they stood.

Dorm336 did not know either.

Lu's team did not know whether their earlier elimination had still earned enough points.

Jin Xiang's team, eliminated so quickly, probably believed they had no hope at all.

No one knew.

That uncertainty made the room feel suspended.

Qian lay back down, but this time he did not bury his face. "Do you think Yan Shuo's team will rank high?"

Liu Feng answered before Lan. "They should. They lasted until the end."

"Not just lasted," Lan said.

Qian turned his head. "What?"

Lan leaned back against the wall. "Their route was different from ours. We used concealment, timing, and your summon for the final kill. Yan's team used pressure and terrain to survive in a place where the fire itself should have belonged to the enemy. Shrek will value that."

Qian's brows drew together.

"So his score could be high too."

"Yes."

Qian sighed deeply. "Why is everyone around me becoming valuable? It increases my social pressure."

Liu Feng said, "You like valuable things."

"Only when I own them."

"You do not own Yan Shuo."

"I can admire from a safe distance."

Lan smiled but did not join that joke.

He thought of Yan's face in the training room, the way those restrained eyes had sharpened when he understood what Shrek had done to Qian. Yan had not known everything, yet he had seen enough. He had called it cruel.

Lan had already accepted the logic of the test. He still did.

But Yan's reaction remained warm in a place Lan had not expected.

Not soft.

Yan Shuo was not soft in battle. The Fire Phoenix did not survive by being soft.

But warmth did not need to be weakness.

Lan closed his eyes for a moment.

The image that came first was not the training room.

It was red fire bending.

Not his fire.

The memory came from reports and fragments, from what he had seen before entering the false magma pool, from what he had inferred after Dorm336 returned. The Ground Fire Scarlet Dragons' flames had been terrifying. Their bodies had remained lethal. But fire was not only heat. Fire had quality. Fire had hierarchy.

Yan's flame had not been bigger.

It had been higher.

That difference mattered.

Lan opened his eyes.

Qian was still talking. Liu Feng was answering less and less. The rhythm of the room was slowing.

"Meditate?" Liu Feng asked.

Qian groaned. "Can grieving geniuses sleep instead?"

"You slept in class for years," Liu said. "Meditate."

"Xuanyu sleeps in class."

Lan said mildly, "I also cultivate."

"That is unfair."

"Shrek likes unfair tests," Liu Feng said.

Qian opened his mouth, then closed it.

"Too soon," he said with dignity.

The three washed quickly, ate the food delivered by the academy canteen, and cleaned up enough that the room no longer looked like a battlefield's afterthought. The food tasted better than it should have. Even Qian, who claimed his heart was too wounded to chew, finished faster than either of the others.

Afterward, they sat cross-legged on their beds.

The first cycle of meditation was usually familiar. Breath. Soul Power. Spiritual focus. Let the mind settle into the body's pattern. For Lan, cultivation had always been strange compared with others. His formal Soul Power rank was low, but his body and bloodline did not behave like they belonged to a simple Rank20 Soul Grandmaster.

Tonight, the moment he closed his eyes, the qualifier returned.

Not as fear.

As pieces.

Ground Fire Lizards crawling from false magma.

Meteor rain forcing movement.

Human-priority rules turning beasts toward them.

Lu Qianxun's Golden Lion King roar buying time.

Liu Feng flashing across impossible gaps under golden-patterned support.

Qian Lei's Gate of Summoning trembling like a mouth that might bite the summoner first.

A green shadow too fast for thought.

A Dragon's eye.

A command: target the eyes.

The sound of something enormous collapsing.

Then a red door.

A lush valley.

Ling Yiyi's smile.

Qian's face when temptation became pain.

Yan's quiet sentence: a test can still be cruel.

Lan's breathing slowed.

He did not chase the thoughts away. Ji Hongbin had taught them that fighting experience only became cultivation when the mind could digest it. Yin Tianfan had taught him that fear, pressure, and decision were all forms of data if one survived long enough to review them.

So Lan reviewed.

The false magma pool had not been luck alone.

The water element around him had not merely blocked heat. It had shaped layers: cooler outside, stable inside, shifting boundary against simulated pressure. After gaining his second ring, his silver-patterned Blue Silver Grass had changed more than its appearance. Fire had entered his control, but fire had also forced him to understand water more clearly. Water did not only extinguish. It carried, separated, cooled, concealed, and delayed.

His golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass had also changed.

When he supported Liu Feng and Qian Lei, the consumption should have been crushing. Instead, after his bloodlines had undergone that strange fusion-like balancing during his breakthrough, the energy in his body had become thicker, steadier, more willing to flow in two directions at once. The support he gave was not only Soul Power. It carried bloodline strength.

That was dangerous.

That was useful.

That was still not something he could casually explain.

He was officially Rank20.

Only Rank20.

But inside, his strength did not feel like a simple number.

For Yan Shuo, Spirit Sea was already a public fact, written into the scan with a number so high that teachers could barely pretend not to be shocked. For Lan, SP499 was different. It was not a height already reached. It was a cliff edge under his toes. One more step, and the sea would open.

He had been standing at that edge before the qualifier.

Today, Shrek had pushed him.

Lan's awareness sank deeper.

The room faded.

Qian Lei's breathing became distant.

Liu Feng's Soul Power circulation became a faint line beside him, sharp and thin like a spear seen through mist.

Lan felt his own two bloodline currents.

Gold.

Silver.

They did not collide now.

Not at this moment.

They circled each other like two great forces that had learned, temporarily, to share one sky.

When he breathed in, warmth gathered under his skin.

When he breathed out, coolness spread through his meridians.

The golden side carried strength, pressure, body, command.

The silver side carried elements, change, distance, flow.

Between them was the strange fused center he still did not fully understand.

He did not force it.

He remembered Nana's lessons without naming them aloud even in his own drifting thoughts: do not let one side crush the other; do not fear the contact; guide, rotate, return.

The silence deepened.

At some point, Lan no longer knew whether he was sitting on his bed or floating in dark space.

A huge roar crossed the emptiness.

Not the Scarlet Dragons.

Something older in feeling, too vast for a student's body to contain.

Then came the memory of space again: a golden figure walking where no one should walk, warships like fragile toys before him, dragons of light descending with each impossible step. Lan had seen that power once, but memory never held it steadily. It always broke into fragments: a song, a figure, a stomp, golden dragons falling like judgment, warmth spreading through fear.

The Ground Fire Scarlet Dragon's footsteps overlapped with it.

Boom.

Boom.

Boom.

Each step struck his heart.

In the simulation, he had hidden beneath danger.

In memory, danger became rhythm.

Lan followed the rhythm downward.

The gold and silver currents inside him thickened.

His soul power turned viscous, as if each thread had absorbed a drop of bloodline light. The two Blue Silver Grass martial souls seemed to breathe with him, leaves and vines unseen but present in the dark. Fire, water, ice, strength, support, command, and restraint folded into one another without words.

His mind emptied.

The day's calculations dissolved.

The fear dissolved.

Even Shrek's door, Qian's tears, Lu's hand, Yan's eyes, and the waiting rankings became distant ripples.

Lan Xuanyu forgot to be tired.

Then he forgot to be awake.


The room passed through evening without him.

Qian Lei was the first to notice something strange, though not because he was observant in a noble way.

He was hungry again.

"Xuanyu," he whispered at first, because Liu Feng had threatened him earlier about disturbing cultivation. "Xuanyu, if you are not eating, can I take your extra bun?"

No answer.

Qian waited.

The bun waited.

His conscience struggled.

Liu Feng opened one eye from the opposite bed. "Don't."

"I haven't done anything."

"You were thinking loudly."

"That is discrimination against hungry people."

Lan still did not move.

Liu Feng's expression changed. He stood quietly and approached Lan's bed.

Lan sat cross-legged, hands resting naturally, breathing so evenly that it felt almost unreal. A faint layer of light moved beneath his skin, not bright enough to illuminate the room, but enough that Liu Feng stopped before touching him.

Qian also climbed down, hunger forgotten.

"Is he asleep?"

"No," Liu Feng whispered.

"Cultivating?"

"Yes. But deeper than usual."

They waited a little while.

Lan did not wake.

Meal time came. They went to the canteen after hesitating for a long time, leaving only after agreeing twice that one of them should run back immediately if anything changed. They brought food back for Lan, but he still did not wake.

The room darkened.

The academy quieted.

Qian tried meditating, partly out of envy and partly because he hoped loyalty might produce sudden enlightenment. Liu Feng tried as well, more seriously. Neither entered any mysterious state. Qian fell asleep once, denied it, then fell asleep again.

Lan remained still.

Through the night, the faint light under his skin continued to circulate.

It was not violent.

It was not weak.

It was like a tide gathering far from shore.


Early morning came with pale sunlight sliding through the window.

Qian Lei woke with his hair flattened on one side and his first instinct pointed toward breakfast.

Then he saw Lan.

Still sitting.

Still breathing evenly.

Still surrounded by faint, dignified undulations that made the air near his bed feel quietly different.

Qian's eyes widened.

He slapped a hand over his own mouth before the shout escaped.

Liu Feng woke at the movement and followed his gaze.

For a moment, neither of them spoke.

A day and a night.

Lan Xuanyu had entered deep meditation after the qualifier and had not woken once. His life force looked stable. His breathing was calm. His soul power fluctuated faintly, but there was no sign of pain.

That did not make it ordinary.

It made it more extraordinary.

Qian pointed at Lan, then at the door, then at himself, then made a helpless expression that asked five questions at once.

Liu Feng nodded.

Do not disturb him.

Find a teacher.

Qian moved with exaggerated care, as if the floorboards might shout. Liu Feng opened the door without letting it creak. The two slipped out into the corridor, still in yesterday's clothes, faces pale with worry and excitement.

Behind them, Lan Xuanyu sat alone in the morning light.

Gold and silver moved beneath his skin like two distant currents finding the same sea.

The qualifier's score had not yet been shown.

But Shrek's test had already changed them.
