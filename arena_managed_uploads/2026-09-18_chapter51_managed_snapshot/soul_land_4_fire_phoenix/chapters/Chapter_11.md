# Chapter 11: The Tenth Name

Nana opened the door before the bell finished ringing.

Eight people stood outside the Lan apartment.

The woman in front had worry written so clearly across her face that it nearly counted as identification. Yun Yan's hand lifted, lowered, lifted again, and finally reached for Nana's wrist before remembering too late that Nana did not like being grabbed.

"Nana," Yun Yan said, voice tight. "You made me search everywhere. Why did you come to Heaven Luo Planet without telling anyone?"

Nana stepped back only half a step, not enough to invite them inside.

"Why did you find me?"

Behind Yun Yan stood men in neat silver uniforms. A golden sword insignia shone over each left chest, stamped with the Federation emblem. Their boots were quiet on the corridor floor. Their eyes were not.

One middle-aged man stepped forward.

"Lady Nana," he said, polite but hard beneath the politeness. "Please return with us to Heaven Dou Planet for investigation."

Invisible pressure rolled into the doorway.

Nana looked at him.

The pressure broke.

The man groaned, staggered back three steps, and blood ran from his eyes, nose, ears, and mouth.

Weapons rose behind him with metallic clicks.

"Stop!" Yun Yan threw out both arms. "All of you, stop!"

Nana's face did not change.

The corridor became so quiet that even the apartment seemed to be listening.

"What if I do not want to leave with you?" Nana asked.

The middle-aged man wiped blood from his chin. Shock had replaced command in his eyes. He had been sent because the central investigation team had seen a satellite fragment: a silver-haired woman holding a child above Horizon Building, a dark blue light thrown into the sky, and a terrorist battleship vanishing into fire. He had known she was strong.

Knowing was different from having his spirit nearly collapse because she glanced at him.

"Please do not misunderstand," he said, this time with real respect. "You left Heaven Dou Planet during your observation period. But you also saved many people in Zi Luo City and recovered part of the Federation's reputation after a terrible incident. We need you to return and cooperate with the investigation."

Nana's eyes cooled at the word cooperate.

Yun Yan spoke quickly, before the corridor could break again. "Nana, please. You are still under supervision. You worked so hard to receive legal Federal status. If you refuse now, your citizenship may be affected. It might even implicate your student."

Nana's gaze moved.

Yun Yan swallowed, then forced herself to continue. "If you stay here against the order, people will keep coming. Lan Xuanyu is still a child. His parents are ordinary citizens. Do you want his peaceful life to become tied to official conflict?"

The name did what pressure could not.

Nana was silent.

For several years after waking, the world had passed around her like scenery beyond glass. Research rooms. Tests. Yun Yan's worried voice. Questions she could not answer because memory had been cut away from her like a shadow at noon.

Then there had been a boy with clear eyes and blue-silver grass.

Lan Xuanyu smiled when he called her Teacher Nana. He cried when he was afraid. He stood before his mother even though the cannon in front of him could have erased his small body. He was warm, close, familiar in a way no memory explained.

If she stayed, she could protect him.

If she stayed wrongly, she might bring wolves to his door.

"I will leave," Nana said at last.

Yun Yan exhaled so sharply it almost became a sob.

"But I have two requests."

"Say them."

"First, I will say goodbye to my student."

"Of course," Yun Yan said at once.

"Second, his body needs nourishment. Precious ingredients must be delivered to him every day. They must be no lower in grade than dragon tendon."

Yun Yan turned toward the injured middle-aged man. This was no longer her authority.

The man nodded without hesitation. "The War God Temple can arrange it. Miss Nana, you need not worry."

Nana gave a small nod.

"Come tomorrow," she said.

Then she closed the door.

The apartment fell quiet again.

Nana stood behind the door for a long time.

When she imagined Lan Xuanyu coming home, hugging her leg, crying and refusing to let go, something in her chest hurt in a way no attack had managed.

She was not afraid of the people outside.

She was afraid she would not leave if he asked her to stay.

So when Lan Xuanyu came home that evening, he found Nana's room empty.

"Teacher Nana?"

He searched the bedroom first because the smell of her always lingered there, clean and cool, like snow before dawn. The bedding was neat. The drawers were empty. The little items Nan Cheng had bought for her were gone or folded away as if no one had lived there at all.

"Teacher Nana!"

He ran to the training room.

Empty.

The bathroom.

Empty.

The living room answered with silence.

Then a gentle hum sounded.

Light unfolded in the air, and Nana's image appeared before him.

"Xuanyu," the projection said softly. "When you return, the spirit mark I left will activate. Teacher has to go away."

Lan Xuanyu froze.

Nana's projected eyes were warm and red at the edges.

"I wanted to wait for you. But if I saw you, I might not be willing to leave. Teacher liked you very much from the first time I saw you. I wanted to stay and watch you grow, but I have matters I must handle."

"No," Lan Xuanyu whispered.

The projection could not hear him.

"Listen to your daddy and mommy. Cultivate according to what I taught you. Continue practicing the technique. When your soul power reaches the point I told you, you must call Teacher. No matter what happens then, Teacher will come to find you."

Lan Xuanyu shook his head. Tears had already gathered before he understood they had fallen.

"You are brave," Nana continued. "That day, you stood before your mother. You are already a man. Men can cry, but they must still move forward after crying. This is only a temporary farewell. We will meet again."

Her hand lifted in the projection, as if she wanted to touch his face.

"Goodbye, Xuanyu."

The light vanished.

Lan Xuanyu lunged forward and caught only air.

"Teacher Nana!"

At the Heaven Luo space center, Nana paused before boarding.

Yun Yan looked at her nervously. "What is it?"

Nana turned her head toward Zi Luo City.

She could not hear him with her ears.

She heard him anyway.

After one breath, she lowered her eyes.

"Nothing."

The spacecraft doors closed behind her.

Lan Xuanyu was sad for a week.

Sad was not the same as quiet. He still answered Nan Cheng. He still went to school. He still ate, though his appetite became a duty instead of a festival. But every time he passed the room where Nana had stayed, his steps slowed. Every time training became difficult, he looked toward where her hand should have corrected him.

Nan Cheng did not try to make him forget.

She only stayed near him.

Every day, precious ingredients arrived through channels she did not dare question too deeply. The packages came with clean seals, official handling, and prices so far beyond ordinary family meals that Yan Shuo, if he had seen the bills, would have declared arithmetic a criminal organization.

Lan Xuanyu did not see the bills.

He saw a path.

Teacher Nana had left.

Teacher Nana had promised to return when he was strong enough to call.

So he trained.

He trained until the emptiness stopped being a hole and became a direction.

Yan Shuo learned that Teacher Nana had left three days after it happened.

Lan Xuanyu told him in the courtyard after school, not in a long message and not with adults nearby. He stood with his schoolbag straps held too tightly and stared at the paving stones between them.

"Teacher Nana went back to Heaven Dou Planet," Lan Xuanyu said.

Yan Shuo had prepared three jokes on the walk over, because jokes were what he used when the world became too heavy.

All three died before reaching his mouth.

"Will she come back?"

Lan Xuanyu nodded, but the nod looked like something he was holding together by force. "I have to get stronger first."

"Then you will."

"En."

Yan Shuo kicked a small pebble, watched it hop twice, and hated that he could not punch distance.

"If getting stronger is the rule, then you are good at rules when you like them."

Lan Xuanyu looked up.

Yan Shuo continued, because stopping now would make feelings attack from the side. "You do homework when Teacher Qiu asks. You practice when Teacher Nana asks. You eat more than a storage device with legs. If Heaven Dou Planet runs away, you can probably chase it by eating."

Lan Xuanyu's mouth twitched once.

It was not a smile, not fully.

But it was the place where a smile might grow later.

"You are still bad at comforting people," Lan Xuanyu said.

"I know. I am training."

"Do you need a teacher?"

"No. Adults would make it homework."

This time Lan Xuanyu did smile, faint and tired.

Yan Shuo stored the sight like a victory too small to announce.

He did not ask why Nana left. He did not ask what she had taught. He did not ask what stronger meant.

Not knowing was still something he had to train with.

Three months passed with Zi Luo City wearing its scars more quietly each week.

Horizon Building remained behind barriers for a long time. The black ribbons came down, but people still lowered their voices when they passed news screens showing updates about the City of Sin investigation. Parents held children's hands tighter near crowded buildings. Schools added evacuation drills that no one joked about the first time.

By the third time, Huang Qifan joked that if they evacuated much faster, they would graduate by running out of the academy.

Gong Yinghao assigned him two laps for recovering his courage incorrectly.

Ye Lingtong disappeared from school for most of that period.

At first the rumor was that she had transferred. Then someone said her father had been arrested. Someone else said her family had moved to Heaven Luo City. No one knew, which meant everyone spoke with great confidence.

Yan Shuo listened once, then stopped.

He remembered Ye Lingtong crying without crying when classmates laughed. He remembered her father's uniform on news stills, reduced by a star. He remembered Lan Xuanyu saying he had been scared.

Rumors had become less fun.

Instead, he trained.

Lin Mei did not suddenly become gentle because terrorists had attacked the city. If anything, she became more exacting.

"Fear made you hold the mantle closer," she said during one evening drill. "Good. Now learn to do it without fear."

"I liked it better when fear was bad," Yan Shuo muttered.

"Fear is information. Panic is waste. Pride is expensive. Again."

"Mother, your sentences are getting shorter."

"Again."

After the first ring, every part of cultivation became more expensive.

Food vanished faster. Sleep became less negotiable. His meridians held more soul power, but filling them was like pouring water into a cup that kept becoming wider. Dawnfeather Mantle did not only ask for soul power; it asked for breath, nerves, shoulders, bones, balance, courage, and the tiny judgment that decided whether a flame should spread or fold inward.

Yan Ke recorded the cost with a father's sigh. "Compared with before the first ring, your body's demand is in a different category. This is not a small cup anymore."

Yan Shuo, chewing through the third bowl of dinner, raised his chopsticks. "Then the engine is bigger, not weaker. This is fair."

"Your stomach agrees too enthusiastically."

The strange thing was that the cost did not slow him the way adults expected.

The Dawnflame Kite steadied with him through the months. It remained recorded at exactly 100 years in the original acquisition paperwork, no matter how alive its warmth felt inside his ribs. Some days its response came like a wingbeat before his thought. Some days it sulked hotly when he refused to flare. Some days, after long school drills, he fell asleep before dinner and woke with the sense that the small bird had tucked itself around his tired soul instead of the other way around.

His body learned while he was awake.

Then it kept learning while he slept.

A wrong step left soreness in the ankle; the next morning, the same ankle turned half a finger-width earlier. A burst of anger made the mantle flare loose; by evening, his breath found the place where anger became fuel instead of smoke. Pressure from Gong Yinghao's shout, Lin Mei's rod, the memory of a falling panel, and Lan Xuanyu's quiet sadness did not vanish. It sank into him, broke apart, and returned as instinct.

More demand.

More absorption.

More pressure.

More shape.

By the end of the third month, Gong Yinghao called for a safety-room check because the academy training logs no longer matched Yan Shuo's reactions.

The machine gave **Rank 15 / Level 15**.

Gong Yinghao made him test again.

Rank 15.

Yan Shuo stared at the number, then at his teacher. "If I say the machine likes me, will you make me test a third time?"

"Yes."

"The machine hates me."

Gong Yinghao still made him test a third time.

Rank 15.

The Spiritual Power cap came next. Yan Shuo sat beneath it with his palms on his knees, breath low, shoulders wide, warmth folded close. The first number climbed past the old wall of forty without hesitation and stopped at **54 points**.

Gong Yinghao stopped writing for half a breath.

Lin Mei's eyes sharpened.

Yan Ke forgot to look worried.

"Spiritual Power 54," Gong Yinghao said slowly. "Spirit Connection Realm. Newly entered."

Yan Shuo blinked. "New realm?"

"New responsibility," Lin Mei said.

That was worse, because it sounded like homework with a ceremonial hat.

The holiday did not make the growth gentler. It made the training less interrupted.

Lin Mei added one thing she had refused to treat as optional any longer: soul-spirit fusion form practice.

It was not a martial soul fusion skill. There was no second person and no miracle name in the sky. It was also not a forbidden trump card to keep sealed until death. Dawnflame Kite was his first soul spirit. His first ring had grown from it. Dawnfeather Mantle already carried its feathers. Letting bird-soul, ring, mantle, body, and Fire Phoenix beat in the same rhythm was not strange.

For Yan Shuo, after months of hidden foundation, pressure, recovery, and co-growth, it became natural.

The hard part was not entering.

The hard part was entering cleanly.

The first deliberate entry came with a soft click inside his ribs, like a lock realizing the key had always been warm. Copper-red fire and pale gold light unfolded across his arms, shoulders, and upper back. A narrow wing-frame formed behind him, not full wings and not true flight, but a living outline of where wings would one day belong. His feet grew lighter. His spine straightened. His perception widened until he could feel the angle of air around his shoulders and the heat left by Lin Mei's moving rod.

Dawnfeather Mantle no longer lay on him like armor.

It entered form with him.

Feathers layered over muscle and breath, flexible where ordinary shields were stiff, hungry where ordinary first rings were blunt. It caught force, guided it through his shoulders, and returned it through his feet. The yellow ring's old acquisition paperwork still began at 100 years, but no one in the training room was foolish enough to call the result ordinary.

The Dawnflame Kite had grown too.

Not because an academy form had magically checked it. Yan Ke's old legal file still wrote exactly 100 years because no Spirit Pagoda retest had updated the paperwork. But he had stopped writing only one number in his private notes.

Acquisition paperwork: 100 years.

Private actual living age for soul spirit and first ring: actual living age of 203 years, yellow tier.

That second number was not a new ring. It did not change the color of the first ring. It did not let Yan Shuo pretend he had found a second soul skill. It meant the living soul spirit and the ring born from it had truly grown past the ordinary newly crossed 100-year state. Its fire-and-light output had thickened. Its emotions no longer arrived like random sparks. It tucked itself into his pulse, corrected the angle of the mantle, and understood the difference between play, practice, and danger before Yan Shuo found words for it.

The first ring had grown through the soul spirit.

Dawnfeather Mantle had grown through the first ring.

Yan Shuo's body had grown around both.

Lin Mei watched three entries, then said the word Yan Shuo had been waiting to hear.

"Perfect compatibility."

Yan Shuo immediately ruined the dignity of the moment by grinning so hard his cheeks hurt.

Then he crashed into a padded wall because joy made the wing-frame pull faster than his feet.

"Again," Lin Mei said.

Yan Shuo lay upside down against the mat. "Mother, I have discovered that birds are arrogant."

Inside his chest, the Dawnflame Kite pulsed with offended heat.

"Good," Lin Mei said. "Now teach this arrogant bird that your body is the nest, not luggage."

During the one-and-a-half-month holiday, Dawnflame Form became casual, not careless. By the second week, Yan Shuo could enter within one breath. By the fourth, he could enter, move, stop, and release without falling over or scorching the mat. Low-output form could stay with him through ordinary drills, footwork, and short school-level exchanges. High-output form was different: brighter wing-frame, sharper perception, faster pounce, heavier drain. That still emptied him quickly and demanded food, sleep, and silence afterward, three things he respected in that order only when half-dead.

But form entry itself was no longer a miracle.

It was part of him.

The day before school reopened, the final home-and-school joint check recorded what the past months had built.

**Rank 16 / Level 16.**

Yan Shuo stared at the number.

This time he did not joke immediately.

The Fire Phoenix warmth under his skin felt larger than his chest, not wild, not gentle, but alive and waiting for him to be worthy of it.

The Spiritual Power test followed.

The number rose to **61 points**.

Spirit Connection Realm.

Not Lan Xuanyu's impossible height. Not a realm he mastered perfectly. But no longer the first shallow pool of childhood awareness.

Gong Yinghao wrote the result down with a seriousness that made the room feel smaller. "Yan Shuo. Rank 16 / Level 16. Spiritual Power 61 points. Spirit Connection Realm. First ring stable. Dawnfeather Mantle improved. Dawnflame Form stable. Soul-spirit compatibility perfect. Dawnflame Kite's response is far above its acquisition paperwork. Formal soul-spirit retest pending."

Yan Shuo tried to look mature. "So I may use it normally?"

"You may enter low-output form normally," Gong Yinghao said. "It is part of your first ring and soul spirit. But high-output wing-frame bursts are still not toys. If you show off, I will personally make your next month very educational."

Yan Shuo swallowed. "I fear education."

Yan Ke, standing near the door, finally remembered how to breathe. "Congratulations. Your grocery budget is terrifying."

Lin Mei did not hide her smile. "Now that you are stronger, your mistakes will be faster."

Yan Shuo looked at his parents. "Other children get congratulations."

"Congratulations," Yan Ke said. "Your mistakes will be faster and more expensive."

The next day, Gong Yinghao kept Yan Shuo after class.

"Next semester," he said, "Heaven Luo Academy will open an Elite Junior Class. Zi Luo Branch may recommend ten students for assessment. I have put your name forward."

Yan Shuo blinked.

"My name?"

"Yes."

"As in, my name, not Huang Qifan's name accidentally wearing my face?"

"Your name."

Yan Shuo's chest warmed so quickly he had to press one hand to his ribs.

Gong Yinghao saw it. "Control."

The warmth folded down.

"Yes, Teacher."

"This is an assessment, not acceptance. Most candidates will be older than you. Some will have higher soul power. You are being recommended because your martial soul, measured Spiritual Power, control improvement, and conduct during the Horizon emergency show potential. Potential is not achievement."

Yan Shuo nodded hard enough to make his hair bounce. "Potential is homework waiting for a uniform."

Gong Yinghao paused. "That is not how I would phrase it, but yes."

Across the academy, Qiu Yuxin kept Lan Xuanyu after class.

She explained the same Elite Junior Class with far more smiles.

It would gather the most outstanding students from affiliated academies. It would use advanced curriculum and the best teachers. The candidates had to be under ten and recommended by their schools. Ten applicants from Zi Luo Branch would take the assessment. Overall quality mattered, not only soul power.

"I signed you up," Qiu Yuxin said. "Work hard during the holiday. If you enter, your future path will be much broader."

Lan Xuanyu thought of Nana's projection.

He thought of the promise to grow strong enough to see her.

"Thank you, Teacher Qiu. I will work hard."

Outside the classroom, a voice waited like an old thorn.

"Lan Xuanyu."

He turned.

Ye Lingtong stood there.

She looked paler than before. The sharpness in her eyes was still present, but it had lost some of its easy shine. Her uniform was proper. Her chin was lifted. Her hands were clenched at her sides.

"You are back," Lan Xuanyu said, surprised despite himself.

"I came to tell you something." Ye Lingtong stepped closer. "I will definitely find a way to defeat you. I will win."

Lan Xuanyu's brows knit together. "Is there anything else?"

Her expression cracked for half a breath.

"You did not even ask where I was."

"Why does it matter to me?" Lan Xuanyu asked. He did not say it cruelly. That almost made it worse. "You always pick fights with me. I am going home."

He walked away.

Ye Lingtong stared after him, eyes stinging.

Yan Shuo came around the corner holding a stack of forms and stopped when he saw her.

For a dangerous moment, both of them looked as if they wished the hallway were wider.

"You came back," Yan Shuo said.

"Obviously."

"People said you changed schools."

"People talk too much."

"I noticed."

Her eyes flicked to the forms. "Elite Junior Class?"

"Teacher Gong gave them to me."

"Then I will beat you too."

Yan Shuo considered the sentence. "Do you have a list?"

"What?"

"People to beat. Lan Xuanyu, me, maybe Huang Qifan if he breathes wrong. A list helps with organization."

Ye Lingtong's mouth trembled between anger and something not anger.

"You are annoying too."

"I am training."

She turned away sharply before he could see whether her eyes were wet.

Gong Yinghao's voice came from behind them. "Lingtong. Come here. I need to talk to you about next semester's assessment."

Ye Lingtong wiped her face with one fast motion and walked toward the teacher.

Yan Shuo looked after her and did not joke again.

That evening, Lan Xiao called home after nearly fifty days without contact.

The communication was rough with distance, but his voice reached Nan Cheng and Lan Xuanyu like rain after a long heat. He said the expedition had gone smoothly. They had found another planet, larger and resource-rich, suitable for humans, with air and pressure close to the parent planet. The return journey would take one and a half months.

Nan Cheng cried and scolded him in the same breath.

Lan Xuanyu shouted that Daddy had to bring gifts.

Lan Xiao laughed, promised to come home, and left the line with love because interstellar communication time was never long enough for families.

The holiday lasted one and a half months.

Lan Xuanyu set goals like tying knots in a rope: reach Rank 14, raise gravity training, refine footwork, strengthen his body, control water better, practice the technique Nana left him. The cultivation room at home could not do everything, but he squeezed everything it could do from it. Expensive ingredients continued arriving. Nan Cheng watched him eat and train and wondered when childhood had become so serious.

Yan Shuo's holiday looked less expensive and smelled more like sweat.

No daily rare ingredients arrived for him. No silver-haired teacher left interplanetary promises. His equipment still tore. His father still counted costs. His mother still tapped the wrong shoulder with a padded rod until he fixed it.

But the Elite Junior Class form lay on the table, and every time Yan Shuo saw his name printed among the candidates, his chest tried to become a bonfire.

So he learned to make it a lamp.

The night before school reopened, Lan Xiao came home.

Lan Xuanyu reached him before the door finished sliding open. He leapt like a small black shadow, clung to his father's neck, and made Lan Xiao stagger back laughing.

"You grew heavier!"

"Daddy!"

Nan Cheng ran out, saw her husband's untrimmed beard and tired eyes, and began complaining before tears could take the lead.

Lan Xiao smiled with every part of himself that was not exhausted.

"Where else could I go? I only have one home."

That night, Lan Xuanyu did not meditate.

He ate with his father.

Warmth filled the Lan home in a way even precious ingredients could not imitate.

The new school year began under crowded skies.

Soul Guidance flying cars gathered around Heaven Luo Academy's Zi Luo Branch until even adults muttered that children caused more traffic than battleships. Lan Xiao personally sent Lan Xuanyu to school, still thinner from travel but bright-eyed and promoted for his expedition's success.

"Second grade," he said, looking at his son. "Work hard."

"I will. Daddy, watch me enter the Elite Junior Class."

"I will watch."

Across the gate, Ye Feng arrived with Ye Lingtong.

Lan Xiao straightened by habit, then paused when he saw the epaulet.

Two stripes. Three stars.

The same as his own.

Ye Feng noticed his glance and smiled bitterly. "No need to call me senior officer now. Congratulations on your promotion."

Lan Xiao hesitated. "What happened?"

Ye Feng looked toward the academy entrance, where children were disappearing into the building. Ye Lingtong had already hurried after Lan Xuanyu's back.

"If you have time," Ye Feng said, "coffee?"

Lan Xiao agreed.

In the cafe, Ye Feng spoke of the Horizon Building tragedy without dressing it up. Months of preparation. A trap that caught bait. A larger trap hidden behind it. Hostages. Bombs. A city held by the throat. Hundreds dead or injured despite the final miracle no report could properly explain.

"The military protected me from worse," Ye Feng said. "Demotion is already a mercy. I will lie low for a few years and try to break through. If I reach Titled Douluo, perhaps things will change."

Lan Xiao listened, then offered what comfort he could.

Ye Feng set down his cup.

"Your Xuanyu is extraordinary."

Lan Xiao's fingers stilled.

"Children change quickly," he said carefully.

"Not like that." Ye Feng's gaze sharpened. "I reviewed the match with Lingtong. His two hands are different. The suppression he caused is not normal. Lingtong's Celestial Dragon is a real dragon-type martial soul. If your son's mutation can restrain it, then his spirit may be related to a stronger dragon type, or something equally unusual."

Lan Xiao neither confirmed nor denied.

Ye Feng continued, "I can help him enter the military system. If groomed properly, he might even have a chance toward the War God Temple."

The name carried weight.

Lan Xiao refused at once.

Ye Feng frowned. "You do not need to answer so quickly."

"I do," Lan Xiao said.

He thought of a baby found in an egg, of fabricated birth records, of investigations a military path would invite. He also thought of Nan Cheng crying over eight months of absence and Lan Xuanyu clinging to his neck the night before.

"My son will follow the academy path. His mother and I cannot bear to send him away. Family being together matters more to us."

Ye Feng looked at him for a long moment.

Perhaps before Horizon, he would have called that shortsighted.

After confinement, after thinking of his own wife and daughter through locked doors, the word no longer came as easily.

"If you change your mind, find me," Ye Feng said.

Then he left.

At school, the Elite Junior Class assessment pulled three younger children out of the new-year ceremony.

That was the first visible butterfly everyone could point to.

Lan Xuanyu from Class B.

Ye Lingtong from Class A.

Yan Shuo from Class A.

Seven older students from the third grade waited with them near the gymnasium doors. Some stared openly. A few had the look of children who had been told they were excellent for years and were now insulted by sharing a line with younger candidates.

Yan Shuo noticed and whispered, "If staring added points, third grade would win already."

Lan Xuanyu's mouth twitched.

Ye Lingtong glared. "Be serious."

"I am serious. I am calculating enemy scoring methods."

"You talk too much."

"That is not a scored category yet."

Ye Lingtong reached toward Lan Xuanyu's sleeve, perhaps to pull him back into the old argument, perhaps because her hand moved faster than her pride could think.

Lan Xuanyu stepped aside.

"Teacher Qiu," he called immediately, face innocent enough to make Yan Shuo suspicious on principle, "she tried to grab me."

Ye Lingtong froze with her hand still in the air.

Gong Yinghao's face tightened.

Qiu Yuxin glanced back, one arm already moving protectively around Lan Xuanyu's shoulder. "Lingtong, no trouble before the assessment. Xuanyu, boys should not fuss over every little thing."

Lan Xuanyu looked even more wronged.

Yan Shuo raised both hands. "I saw nothing. My eyes are saving strength for the test."

Gong Yinghao gave him a look.

Yan Shuo lowered his hands.

The gymnasium had been changed for the assessment. Devices lined one side of the arena: soul-power apparatuses, Spiritual Power testers, recording stands, medical monitors, and ten silver simulation pods waiting like sealed eggs. The martial stage defenses shimmered faintly beneath the floor, ready to contain children's mistakes before mistakes became hospital visits.

A smiling middle-aged man walked forward.

"Teachers may leave the gymnasium," he said. "Leave the children to us."

Qiu Yuxin squeezed Lan Xuanyu's shoulder.

Gong Yinghao looked once at Ye Lingtong, once at Yan Shuo. "Do not let pride answer questions for you."

"Yes, Teacher," Yan Shuo said.

The teachers left.

The ten candidates stood straighter.

"My name is Mu Zhongtian," the examiner said. "I am a teacher from Heaven Luo Institute. Today you will undergo a series of assessments: basic soul-power testing, Spiritual Power testing, and a comprehensive test of inner quality. Being recommended means your branch school considers you excellent. It does not mean you will enter. If none of you meet the standard, none of you will be accepted. The Elite Junior Class would rather remain empty than admit those who are merely adequate."

His gaze passed over them.

Pressure came with it.

It was not killing intent. It was not the heavy wrongness of the Horizon day. It was a teacher's deliberate weight, enough to make young shoulders reveal what they were made of.

Several older students stiffened.

One released his martial soul before catching himself.

Ye Lingtong's body tightened as Celestial Body light gathered under her skin.

Yan Shuo felt heat rise instinctively toward his shoulders.

Close.

Hold.

Not fear.

Not pride.

His breath lowered. The Dawnflame Kite tucked in, warm and alert. A faint copper-red shimmer touched the line of his upper back, then folded away before it could become a display.

Mu Zhongtian noticed him for half a breath and nodded slightly.

Then his attention moved to Lan Xuanyu.

Lan Xuanyu stood as if the pressure had walked past him and forgotten to knock.

He did nothing.

No tensing. No martial soul. No visible resistance.

Just clear eyes and a calm face.

Yan Shuo saw the examiner's gaze sharpen and felt, not jealousy exactly, but the familiar sensation of standing beside a door only Lan Xuanyu could open.

He pushed the feeling down before it reached his mantle.

The first test was soul power.

The older candidates went first. Two of them showed Rank 16. Five showed Rank 15 after the apparatus stabilized. Ye Lingtong stepped forward and recorded Rank 16, earning immediate attention as one of the youngest candidates and still tied with the highest group.

Then came Yan Shuo.

He placed his hand on the apparatus. The machine hummed.

"Yan Shuo," the examiner at the console announced. "Rank 16 / Level 16."

The older students no longer looked relieved.

One of them whispered, "Another sixteen?"

Yan Shuo's pride leapt up like fire seeing dry grass.

His breath caught it by the collar.

Rank 16 was his number. He had earned it through food, sleep, bruises, a larger vessel's appetite for nourishment, and a soul spirit that refused to remain a decoration. His effort had not been paying a penalty for ordinary output; it had been feeding something broader and higher-quality until the result finally became visible. It did not need to become louder just because someone else finally noticed.

Lan Xuanyu tested last.

"Lan Xuanyu. Rank 14."

The room's attention on him dimmed a little.

Rank 14 was good for their age, but among candidates for a gifted class, it did not shine like thunder. A few older students relaxed again.

Yan Shuo did not.

He had known Lan Xuanyu too long to believe the first number was the whole person.

Lan Xuanyu looked unconcerned.

Or perhaps he had simply learned not to argue with first numbers.

The Spiritual Power test began from the higher soul-power ranks.

Ye Lingtong sat first among the familiar faces. The apparatus lowered. A moment later, the examiner called, "Spiritual Power, 37."

Ye Lingtong stood, expression unreadable.

Yan Shuo knew enough to understand that 37 was not weak. He also knew she hated numbers that did not win.

Other candidates followed.

Forty-one.

Forty-four.

Thirty-nine.

Forty-eight.

Mu Zhongtian smiled when the forty-eight appeared. "Not bad."

Then Yan Shuo sat beneath the testing apparatus.

The cap felt cooler than the school device, smoother, more accurate in a way that made his scalp prickle. Warmth wrapped around his head. The Dawnflame Kite stirred, curious at the sensation, and for one breath its wingbeat lined up with his pulse.

Not danger.

Not game.

Enter cleanly, not loudly.

Hold shape.

The number appeared.

"Yan Shuo," the examiner said, glancing once at Mu Zhongtian. "Spiritual Power, 61 points. Spirit Connection Realm."

Mu Zhongtian's smile changed.

Not the polite approval he had given forty-eight.

Real interest.

"Fire Phoenix. One ring. Rank 16. Spiritual Power 61 at this age." He looked at the note beside Yan Shuo's name. "Dawnflame Form already stable? Perfect compatibility?"

The examiner nodded. "First-soul-spirit fusion form. Perfect compatibility record. The basic assessment does not retest soul-spirit or soul-ring age. No second ring. No extra soul skill."

Yan Shuo stood very still. The Dawnflame Kite pressed warm pride through his ribs, and the hidden shape inside him pressed back, turning that pride into a cleaner flame before it could spill through his shoulders.

Mu Zhongtian nodded once. "Very good. Use it cleanly."

Very good.

Yan Shuo stepped down with a straight face and an inward cheer loud enough to need supervision.

Then Lan Xuanyu sat.

The apparatus activated.

One breath passed.

Two.

The examiner's expression changed.

"Spiritual Power..."

He stopped.

Mu Zhongtian walked over.

The number on the screen made the air different.

Ninety-nine.

"Lan Xuanyu," Mu Zhongtian said after a pause, "Spiritual Power 99. Spirit Connection Realm."

For a moment, no one spoke.

Spirit Origin Realm was where children began: zero to fifty, the first shaping of awareness. Spirit Connection Realm began at fifty, where mind and desire aligned enough for Spiritual Power to be used more consciously, where a child could bear more and control more than ordinary peers.

Ninety-nine was not a small lead.

It was the kind of number that made every earlier whisper about Rank 14 sound foolish.

Yan Shuo stared at Lan Xuanyu.

Sixty-one had felt like opening a door in his own head.

Ninety-nine was a second sun appearing beyond that door.

His first feeling was not anger.

It was awe so sharp it almost hurt.

Then came envy, honest and hot, because envy was also information if he did not let it become smoke.

Then came a fiercer joy, because Lan Xuanyu being impossible did not make Yan Shuo smaller. It only proved that the road ahead was wider and stranger than adults admitted.

Then came worry, because Lan Xuanyu's numbers had always come with shadows adults refused to name.

The heat in Yan Shuo's shoulders trembled.

He breathed it down.

Lan Xuanyu stood from the device looking more puzzled than proud, as if he had opened a door and found everyone else staring at the hinges.

Ye Lingtong's face went pale in a different way.

Mu Zhongtian did not praise too much. Good examiners knew that raw numbers were not the entire assessment. But his eyes returned to Lan Xuanyu again and again.

"Next," he said, "the comprehensive inner-quality assessment. You will enter the simulation pods. The simulated world will be a survival environment. Once eliminated, your assessment ends. Your reactions, decisions, martial soul usage, cooperation, courage, judgment, and adaptability will all affect your final score. You may encounter other candidates. You may cooperate or compete. Do everything you can to survive."

Ten silver pods opened.

Yan Shuo looked at them and whispered, "They look like eggs for people."

Lan Xuanyu, still dazed from the Spiritual Power result, glanced over. "Don't say that."

"Why?"

"It sounds strange."

Yan Shuo had no idea why Lan Xuanyu looked especially uncomfortable, so he chose the safest answer.

"Then they look like shiny soup coffins."

"That is worse," Ye Lingtong snapped.

"I am improving in the wrong direction."

A staff member pointed Yan Shuo toward a pod near the side. He stepped in and immediately felt the transparent gel close around his ankles like a muddy pond pretending to be technology.

"This is disgusting," he said with great sincerity.

"Turn around and sit," the examiner instructed.

Yan Shuo obeyed. The gel rose around him. His body wanted to flare fire against the strange contact, but the pod was not an enemy. It was a device. Devices were expensive. Burning expensive devices created adult paperwork, and adult paperwork created consequences.

He breathed.

Heat folded close.

Across the row, Lan Xuanyu entered his own pod. Ye Lingtong stepped into another with her chin high. The older candidates vanished one by one behind transparent lids.

A metal mask descended over Yan Shuo's face.

His last clear sight of the gymnasium was Lan Xuanyu's profile through silver light, calm and small and carrying a number that did not feel small at all.

The Dawnflame Kite stirred inside him.

Yan Shuo entered Dawnflame Form at its quietest level before the mask finished descending: boy, ring, bird-soul, mantle, Fire Phoenix, and the patient inner force that had been changing him since before he knew his own name. Copper-red warmth settled over his shoulders without flaring.

If the forest demanded more, he would rise from there.

Then the hum deepened.

Mu Zhongtian's voice came through the pod.

"Assessment begins."

The world dropped away.
