# Chapter 51 — The Cost of Quiet

Qian Lei's eyes lit as if Lan Xuanyu had handed him a treasure map.

"That's right," he said. "That actually makes sense. I feel it too. When my Spiritual Power improves, summoning becomes easier. Even without your help, my summons are stronger than before now. It should be the overall increase in my cultivation."

He sat straighter beneath the tree roots, face suddenly full of heroic determination.

"I have decided. Within one year, I will reach five hundred Spiritual Power and Rank30 Soul Power."

The declaration lasted beautifully for two breaths.

Then reality arrived.

Qian's shoulders sank. "But Spiritual Power is not something I can raise just because I decide to. Meditation is slow. Repeated use is painful. Continuous use of the Gate... really..."

Liu Feng looked at him with no sympathy. "You are lazy."

Qian's head snapped up. "Me? Lazy?"

"If you cannot overuse the Gate in the real world, come into Soul World and practice. Use it again and again."

Qian stared at him as if Liu had suggested he voluntarily hug a Ground Fire Scarlet Dragon.

"Do you know what Spiritual Power overdraft feels like?" Qian said. His voice lost some of its joking edge. "It is not only tiredness. Your head feels like it is splitting open. Your whole body becomes weak, like it does not belong to you. You cannot lift your hands properly. You feel as if you are dying while still being awake enough to complain about it."

Liu did not answer immediately.

Qian continued, quieter now, "When I summon, I consume more Spiritual Power than Soul Power. If I become too weak, the Gate may not answer properly. Worse, a summoned beast might not listen and could devour me instead. You do not rely on Spiritual Power the same way, so of course you can say it easily."

The forest was quiet around them.

Lan watched Qian's expression. The complaint was real this time. Not cowardice. Experience. A summoner who opened a door to unknown beasts learned early that weakness was not only pain. Weakness was an invitation.

Liu Feng's tone softened by a small amount, but not enough to let Qian escape.

"Because it hurts, it is effective. If you had forced yourself harder before, maybe you would already be near Spirit Sea."

Qian opened his mouth.

Closed it.

For once, no argument came out.

That was how Lan knew Liu had struck the truth.

Qian hated the weakness after overusing Spiritual Power. He hated the splitting pain, the empty limbs, the feeling that the Gate might turn against him if he reached for it while hollow. So he always stopped before the worst edge. Sensible, sometimes. Limiting, always.

Lan patted his shoulder.

"If we remained only in the Elite Junior Class and fought together all the time, your current ability would already be enough for many situations. But we are trying to stand firmly in Shrek. Not as a rescued team. Not as people who only survive when the test happens to suit us."

Qian looked at him.

Lan said, "Brother, you may have to be harder on yourself."

The words were gentle.

They still landed heavily.

Then Lan's mouth curved into a bitter smile. "At least you can choose when to be harder. I do not even have that much freedom. Teacher Ji and Teacher Yin made that decision for me long ago."

Qian stared.

Liu stared.

Then both of them laughed.

Not cruelly.

Because they had seen too much evidence.

Qian pointed at Lan. "That is true. There were days you returned to the dorm looking as if Teacher Ji had squeezed your bones and Teacher Yin had used the rest for spare parts."

Liu added, "Sometimes he fell asleep before reaching the bed."

"Once on the floor," Qian said, suddenly excited. "Do you remember? We found him in the morning. I thought he had been assassinated by homework."

Lan looked away. "I remember you standing over me and asking whether dead captains still owed meal money."

"I was confirming inheritance procedures."

"You were stealing my bun."

"Only because you were not actively defending it."

Even Liu smiled then.

The laughter did not remove the pressure. It made it bearable.

Liu Feng looked down at his right arm. The sleeve hid the silver crescent patterns, but all three of them knew they were there. The Silver Moon Wolf Right Arm Bone had changed him. Not only his strength. His understanding.

"We should all work harder," Liu said. "If we want a place in Shrek, we have to eat hardship. Before, everyone in the Elite Junior Class trained hard, so it was difficult to feel the difference. But after the qualifier... I understand it more."

His fingers flexed once.

"We became champion, so we received rewards. Because of those rewards, my strength rose again. The stronger someone is, the more chances he gets to become stronger. If that keeps happening, the gap only grows. Heaven Luo Academy is already strong on our planet, but compared with Shrek, how large is the distance?"

Qian did not joke.

Lan listened silently.

He had his own picture of distance: a figure in gold destroying what a child in a warship could not survive. A height he could not explain to Qian or Liu, because some secrets were not his alone to give away.

Power could become a mountain.

Opportunity decided who even saw the mountain clearly.

Liu's right arm had become his mountain marker.

Lan's Spirit Sea was another.

Qian's darker yellow rings were a third.

None of them were enough yet.

"Continue," Lan said.

Qian summoned again.

The rhythm of the first day settled into their bones: Gate, beast, movement, judgment, rest. They did not chase every point. They did not avoid every fight either. When they met weaker soul beasts, they dealt with them cleanly and moved on. Some were ten-year beasts that fled after one clash. Some were hundred-year beasts with strange forest instincts: clawed shapes under roots, quick shadows in branches, a hard-shelled creature that tried to ram Qian's knees and immediately regretted meeting Liu's spear.

Each encounter became a sign.

Lan adjusted direction by what answered them.

Too weak and sparse meant they might be drifting outward.

Stronger but manageable meant they were walking correctly.

A sudden gap after activity meant territory, danger, or luck pretending to be safety.

By nightfall, they judged they had advanced about twenty kilometers into the forest.

Qian was the most exhausted. Constant summoning drained him more than the fights drained Liu or Lan. He sat cross-legged beneath a large tree, face pale but determined enough that he did not complain until after sitting down.

"For the record," he muttered, "my heroic suffering is substantial."

"Recorded," Lan said.

"With praise?"

"With accuracy."

Qian sighed. "Cold captain."

Lan and Liu guarded him while taking turns resting. Soul World did not make them hungry or thirsty here; the simulation pods outside supplied nutrient fluids, keeping their real bodies fed while their minds endured the test. That solved food.

It did not solve vigilance.

The first day passed without major trouble.

Only once did the forest gather itself into real danger: a beast horde moving through the undergrowth with too many feet, too many breaths, and no interest in allowing three children to measure it properly. Lan heard it before they saw it. Liu reached the same conclusion through vibration and broken branches. Qian's face went white.

They climbed.

Fast.

High into thick branches, bodies pressed close to bark, breath held as the horde passed below in a flood of backs, horns, tails, and dark movement. The branches shook. Leaves rained down. One beast paused beneath their tree long enough for Qian to squeeze his eyes shut as if refusing to be perceived.

Then the horde moved on.

They did not follow.

Points were useless if the cost was being swallowed by a tide.

They met no other teams.

That fact should have been comforting. It was not. The Star Dou Forest was too vast. More than a hundred teams had entered, yet inside this amount of green, even that number became thin. Other teams could be ten kilometers away, or two hills over, or moving toward the core faster than Dorm333 without ever crossing their path.

Night passed quietly.

Too quietly.

On the morning of the second day, all three had recovered.

Qian stretched, looked much too pleased with having survived the night, and lifted the Coin of Summoning.

Lan raised his hand. "Wait."

Qian froze. "We are not summoning?"

Lan frowned at the forest.

Mist hung low between trunks. Leaves shone with simulated dew. Somewhere far away, a bird cried once and fell silent. The green looked calm enough to be painted.

"Yesterday was too peaceful," Lan said. "Do you feel uneasy?"

Qian blinked. "No. My perception is very sharp. I did not sense danger."

"Not danger," Liu Feng said suddenly. "Unease."

Qian turned to him. "You understand?"

Liu ignored the tone. "We want a good result, not only survival. We have to penetrate deeper and kill more soul beasts for points. Yesterday we moved too carefully. Twenty kilometers sounds far, but for the Star Dou Forest, it may still be shallow. Our points cannot be high."

Lan looked at him and nodded.

Liu continued, "And we do not know what the other teams are doing. If some teams chose risk from the start, they may already be ahead in points. Only ten teams win in the end. If we lag too much, surviving seven days may still mean elimination."

Qian stared at Liu.

Then he looked at Lan.

Lan nodded again.

"That is what I meant."

Qian slowly pointed at Liu. "How did you become enlightened?"

Liu pointed to his own head. "I learned to reflect and observe. Did you think everyone is like you?"

Qian's eyes widened. "Are you calling me foolish?"

"I am praising you," Liu said. "Foolish people have foolish luck."

"You are the foolish one!"

"Then give me some luck."

"No. My luck is private property."

Lan let them argue for one breath longer, because the bickering meant fear had not settled too deeply. Then he cut in.

"Enough. We move. Liu Feng leads and opens the path. Same formation: Liu front, Qian middle, I cover the rear. Today we increase speed. Qian, do not waste a summon immediately, but be ready to call one at any time."

Qian's hand tightened around the coin. "Ready. Reluctantly mature, but ready."

Lan's eyes remained on the deeper green.

Yesterday's caution had kept them alive.

Today, caution alone could make them lose.

Somewhere in this forest, Lu Qianxun would not be waiting politely at the edge. Somewhere, Yan Shuo would read the same rules through a different body and a different flame. Lan did not need to know Yan's position to understand that. Years of being first friends, rivals, and the kind of people who heard weight in each other's silences had taught him enough: Yan would not confuse safety with progress. He would protect Song and Luo, but he would also move.

Dorm333 could not fall behind because Lan mistook quiet for success.

"This test is not only about survival," Lan said. "It is testing whether we dare to take risks after understanding them. Courage, meticulousness, strength, adaptability... and luck. All of them matter."

Liu Feng lowered his body, White Dragon Spear angled forward.

Qian took his place in the middle.

Lan let golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass rest ready at his fingers.

The second day began not with a roar, but with a decision.

They left the safe tree behind.
