# Chapter 51 — Silver Under the Skin

No one laughed.

The silver light waited in Liu Feng's hands, quiet as a drawn blade.

Ji Hongbin stepped forward.

His expression had returned to the hard calm of a teacher facing danger that could not be removed, only crossed. He did not reach out to take the Soul Bone from Liu Feng. He did not soften his voice.

"Focus," he said. "Release your Martial Soul. Inject your Soul Power into the Soul Bone, then follow the feeling it gives you. Do not fight blindly. Feel the change. There will be pain. You must endure it."

Liu Feng nodded.

"Yes."

The word was short.

His fingers tightened around the Silver Moon Wolf Right Arm Bone.

A pale light rose across his knees. The White Dragon Spear appeared horizontally before him, slender and bright, its body reflecting the silver glow in the room. It had followed Liu Feng through failure, speed, impact, and Lan Xuanyu's support. Now it lay across his legs like a road waiting to be sharpened.

Liu Feng closed his eyes.

He drew in a breath.

Soul Power moved from his body into his hands.

The answer came instantly.

Pain.

Not warmth. Not resistance. Not the slow pressure Lan had imagined a man-made Soul Bone might give.

A sharpness bit through Liu Feng's right palm as though an invisible blade had closed around his bones. His shoulders jerked. A sound escaped his throat before he could stop it, low and rough, cut off between his teeth.

Qian Lei flinched.

Lan's fingers tightened at his sides.

The silver arm bone dissolved.

One moment it lay in Liu Feng's hands. The next, it broke into a brilliant stream of silver light and drilled straight into his right arm.

Liu Feng's entire body bowed forward.

His right arm swelled.

Not gradually.

It expanded as if something adult and violent had been forced beneath a child's skin. The sleeve over his arm tore apart with a dry ripping sound. Silver rays sprang across the exposed flesh, leaping, cutting, bouncing from wrist to elbow to shoulder like trapped moonlight searching for a way out.

The arm became thick, almost the size of an adult's.

Too large.

Too bright.

Too sharp.

Sweat broke across Liu Feng's forehead in large drops. His lips pressed together until they lost color, but a tremor ran through his body again and again, impossible to hide.

Qian Lei's face had gone white.

"Liu Feng..."

"Quiet," Yin Tianfan said.

It was not harsh.

It was necessary.

The silver light on Liu Feng's arm suddenly flared. Blood pricked through in small spots, red against the white-silver glare.

Liu Feng's breathing broke.

For an instant, the thought in his face was terrible and clear.

Cut it off.

Let the arm go.

Anything but this.

Ji Hongbin's voice landed beside him like a weight.

"Stay conscious. Focus. A Soul Bone that crosses your current rank will not submit because you wish for it. If you want to use it, overcome it first."

A stream of hot air descended from above Liu Feng's head.

Lan felt it wash across the room, steady and controlled. It did not crush the silver light. It did not seize Liu Feng's Soul Power and pull the fusion forward by force.

It only held him.

Held the shaking body.

Held the wavering mind.

Held the boy at the edge where pain tried to make him let go of himself.

Ji Hongbin was not fusing the Soul Bone for him.

Lan understood that immediately.

If a teacher forced the process, perhaps the bone could be pressed into the arm. Perhaps the danger could be reduced. But then the Silver Moon Wolf Right Arm Bone would not fully accept Liu Feng. It would become something attached, not something won.

This was Liu Feng's road.

The teachers could guard the cliff.

They could not walk it in his place.

Liu Feng's trembling eased by a fraction.

His eyes stayed shut.

His mouth was still pressed into a hard line, but his breathing began to find rhythm again.

Yin Tianfan watched with a grave expression.

"Two thousand years," he said quietly. "Even refined by the Spirit Pagoda, it is still not gentle."

Mu Zhongtian's hands had clenched without him noticing.

None of them moved to interfere.

The silver rays coiled tighter.

The pain changed.

Lan could tell from Liu Feng's face.

At first it had been a clean cut, a sudden severing sharpness. Now it seemed to drag. Liu Feng's brows knotted, and his right arm twitched as if a blunt knife were taking it apart piece by piece and rebuilding each piece before moving on to the next.

More red spots appeared beneath the silver.

Qian Lei swallowed.

For once, he did not joke.

He stared at Liu Feng with both hands folded into fists at his sides, eyes wet with anger at something he could not hit.

Lan looked at him, then back to Liu.

Elite Junior Class students were called talented because adults liked neat labels. Talent looked simple on paper: rank, Spiritual Power, Soul Rings, test results.

But no one reached this room only by being labeled.

Liu Feng trained every day to control speed that could throw his own body out of line. He repeated movements until narrow paths became instinct. He took the dangerous opening when Lan called it, again and again, even when his attack power lagged behind his courage.

Qian Lei complained, joked, avoided seriousness whenever he could, and still trained his Spiritual Power because unreliable summoning terrified him more than he admitted.

Lan himself had spent years balancing things inside his body that should not have coexisted.

Everyone was working hard.

That did not make pain fair.

It only made enduring it possible.

Another silver pulse struck Liu Feng's right arm.

His body pitched forward.

Lan stepped half a pace before he asked.

"Teacher Ji," he said, "can I help him? My Martial Soul supports him. It may guide and stabilize the fusion."

Ji Hongbin did not turn his head immediately.

His eyes remained on Liu Feng's arm.

Yin Tianfan looked at Lan.

They both knew what Lan meant. With Lan Xuanyu's golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass, Liu Feng's battle power changed as if a missing piece had been returned to his body. His speed became less fragile. His White Dragon Spear became sharper. His whole person seemed to find a line that had been waiting for him.

A Soul Bone fusion was not a battle.

But it was still Liu Feng facing a force that needed to recognize him.

"Go," Ji Hongbin said.

Lan moved at once.

He crouched beside Liu Feng, raised his right hand, and released golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass.

The slender grass coiled around Liu Feng's swollen right arm.

Gold met silver.

The reaction struck back through Lan's hand so sharply that his breath caught.

It felt as if the Blue Silver Grass had been wrapped around a spinning saw.

Cut.

Cut.

Cut.

The silver light did not know Lan. It did not care that he had come to help. It only recognized contact as another thing to slice open.

Lan steadied his mind.

Bloodline power surged from deep within him, warm and heavy, different from ordinary Soul Power. The golden patterns on the Blue Silver Grass brightened. A faint golden halo spread outward, not blazing, not violent, but carrying a dignity that made the silver rays hesitate.

The slicing pressure weakened.

Liu Feng inhaled sharply.

The White Dragon Spear across his knees rang.

It was not a metal sound.

It was clear, high, and alive, like a dragon's voice heard through a small body that had waited too long to sing.

The spear dissolved into white light.

Qian Lei's eyes widened.

The white light rushed toward Liu Feng's right arm, circled once around the golden Blue Silver Grass, and then settled into a tiny white dragon shape coiling along the swollen limb. Its eyes opened within the light. It leaned against the golden-patterned grass as if recognizing support, then turned toward the silver crescent glare as if challenging it to become part of the same path.

Liu Feng's body changed immediately.

The tremor did not vanish, but it stopped ruling him.

His shoulders lowered.

His forehead relaxed.

A faint white halo rose from his skin and joined the silver-gold-white spiral around his right arm.

The silver rays became gentler.

Not obedient.

Not harmless.

But no longer wild.

Lan kept his hand steady.

The feedback still cut at him in thin echoes. Each silver flicker that struck the Blue Silver Grass sent a sting through his palm and into his arm. He did not withdraw.

This was Liu Feng's fusion.

Lan could not bear it for him.

But he could give him the line.

Ji Hongbin watched, and beneath his stern expression something like shock passed through his eyes.

Second ring.

Lan Xuanyu was only second ring.

Yet the golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass had suppressed the violence of a two-thousand-year Soul Bone enough for Liu Feng's own martial soul to answer joyfully. This was not simple amplification. It was guidance, suppression, compatibility awakening — something that ordinary teacher explanations could not easily hold.

Ji Hongbin's gaze moved briefly toward Lan's two yellow rings.

Hundred-year rings.

They had become yellow without the Spirit Ascension Platform.

Even after a year of cultivation, ordinary Soul Rings did not simply improve in cultivation age by themselves. Lan's public explanation had been plausible enough for students. It was not enough for a teacher who knew how systems worked.

Mutation?

Bloodline?

Something deeper?

Ji Hongbin did not ask.

Not now.

Liu Feng needed silence.

Half an hour became a narrow bridge.

No one in the room truly relaxed during it.

Qian Lei stood so still that Lan almost forgot he was capable of motion. Mu Zhongtian watched the flow of silver and white with the tense pride of a teacher seeing pain become growth. Yin Tianfan paced once, stopped, then forced himself to remain still. Ji Hongbin's support never wavered.

Lan's Blue Silver Grass stayed wrapped around Liu Feng's arm.

At first, the silver light fought every breath.

Then it began to turn.

Not away.

Inward.

The swollen arm slowly shrank. The adult-like thickness receded. The tearing pressure beneath the skin softened into a pulse. One by one, the silver rays stopped leaping and sank into the flesh, leaving thin lines behind like moonlight drawn under the surface.

Liu Feng's breathing deepened.

The White Dragon Spear's small dragon-shape circled once more, then returned into white light and settled back into his martial soul.

The golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass loosened.

Lan withdrew it slowly.

His own palm still tingled as if fine blades had brushed it.

Liu Feng's right arm had returned to its normal size.

Only the sleeve was gone, torn away by the fusion. Across the skin of his arm lay silver crescent patterns, faint but clear, like tiny moons sleeping beneath the surface. They did not look painted. They looked grown there.

Liu Feng let out a long breath.

His eyes opened.

For an instant, silver flashed in them.

The room seemed sharper around him.

Not louder.

Sharper.

As if a blade that had always been thin had finally found an edge.

Qian Lei exhaled so hard he nearly bent over.

"You scared me to death," he said, voice rougher than usual.

Liu Feng looked at him.

His face was pale. His clothes were soaked in sweat. But the corner of his mouth lifted by the smallest degree.

"I am not dead."

"That is exactly the minimum standard I was hoping for."

Yin Tianfan gave Qian Lei a look.

Qian Lei shut his mouth, but this time the silence was full of relief rather than fear.

Ji Hongbin stepped closer and looked Liu Feng over.

After a moment, he nodded.

"Your Soul Power rose. Almost Rank30 now."

Liu Feng blinked.

"Almost?"

"Rank29," Ji Hongbin said. "Not far from the next threshold."

Rank29.

Lan felt happiness for him first.

Then, beneath it, something else.

A small ache.

Not jealousy sharp enough to shame him. Not resentment. Just the familiar weight of watching someone step forward in Soul Power while his own path stayed strangely slow.

He was Rank20.

Still Rank20.

His Spirit Sea had opened, his control had changed, his martial souls had evolved, his command had carried them through impossible places — and yet his Soul Power climbed as if each step asked a question no teacher could answer.

Lan lowered his eyes for a breath.

Then Liu Feng turned toward him.

There was sweat on his face and silver still faintly bright beneath his skin.

"Thank you," Liu Feng said.

Lan looked up.

"You endured it yourself."

"You helped me endure it correctly."

That answer left Lan with no simple reply.

Ji Hongbin's voice cut through the moment before it could become too soft.

"Go wash up. Then familiarize yourself with the Soul Bone as quickly as possible. The next qualifier begins in two days. Before then, you must understand its effects well enough to use it as a trump card if necessary."

Liu Feng straightened despite exhaustion.

"Yes."

Yin Tianfan added, "Do not play with it. Do not show off. Do not cut academy furniture to test whether the crescent edge is sharp."

Qian Lei looked personally accused. "Why did you look at me? It is Liu Feng's arm."

"Because if anyone says the words 'test target,' it will be you."

Qian considered denying it.

Then wisely chose survival.

"I will support responsible familiarization."

"Good," Ji Hongbin said. "Start by supporting silence."

Liu Feng rose carefully.

His right arm moved a little stiffly at first, then steadied. He looked down at the silver crescent patterns again, not with greed, but with the serious expression of someone receiving a weapon that would demand equal seriousness from him.

A Soul Bone had not made him complete.

It had given him a sharper question.

Could he bear the answer?

The next two days did not belong to celebration.

They belonged to preparation.

Qian Lei's turn came almost immediately. Mu Zhongtian brought him to Heaven Luo City's Spirit Pagoda division to process the reward card that increased the cultivation age of all his current Soul Rings by five hundred years. Qian tried to look brave before leaving, then asked twice whether the procedure involved needles, three times whether it involved pain, and once whether the Spirit Pagoda offered snacks afterward for spiritually exhausted geniuses.

Mu Zhongtian told him to get in the vehicle.

Qian went.

When he returned, his face held the complicated expression of someone who had suffered less than he feared and still wanted credit for courage. Lan did not ask for exact numbers. No teacher gave them. What mattered was that the reward had gone through the proper Spirit Pagoda procedure. Qian's current rings had truly gained their promised years.

They were stronger.

He was not suddenly reliable enough to stop training.

Qian understood that part even if he complained loudly about it.

Liu Feng spent the two days learning the weight of silver beneath his skin.

He did not use Silver Edge carelessly.

Under supervision, he learned how the right arm answered when Soul Power moved through it, how the crescent sharpness wanted to form, how quickly it consumed him, and how easily excitement could make the edge unstable. The White Dragon Spear accepted the new line with a clarity that made Liu Feng train until his face went pale more than once.

Ji Hongbin stopped him each time.

A trump card that broke the user was only a prettier failure.

Lan meditated.

Again and again, he settled his breathing and entered the quiet inner place where his newly opened Spirit Sea stretched wider than before. It still felt unfamiliar. Not fragile, exactly, but newly formed — a vastness that needed shape. If he pushed too greedily, his thoughts scattered. If he forced control, the sea resisted.

So he stabilized.

He did not chase a number.

No one told him his Spiritual Power had risen beyond SP505. No measurement confirmed anything. The only certain fact remained the one he had already earned: Spirit Sea.

His Soul Power remained Rank20.

That truth sat beside him every time he opened his eyes.

Slow.

Too slow.

But not stopped.

He remembered Nana's instruction.

Water and fire.

Balance and collision.

He remembered Yin Tianfan's story.

Victory and principle.

He remembered Liu Feng's arm swelling under silver pain, and Qian Lei leaving for the Spirit Pagoda with fear hidden under noise.

Everyone had a road.

His road was simply stranger.

Outside Dorm333's training, Heaven Luo Academy did not sleep easily.

The news that could be public moved through teachers faster than students. The exact details remained protected, but some things could not be hidden from the shape of preparation: Dorm333 had received real prizes; Liu Feng's right sleeve stayed covered afterward; Qian Lei walked like a person carrying secret treasure; Lan Xuanyu spent long hours in meditation rather than boasting.

Dorm336 remained its own pressure.

Yan Shuo did not come to pry.

That was almost more noticeable than if he had.

When their paths crossed once in a corridor, Yan's gaze paused briefly on Liu Feng's right arm, then moved to Lan's face. Publicly, he was only the class monitor, calm and sharp-eyed, with no wings released and no flame shown. But Lan still felt the weight behind that look: not ordinary curiosity, not childish envy, but evaluation from someone whose own foundation had forced Shrek to open a separate file.

Yan did not ask.

Lan did not explain.

The rules between them remained full of doors neither could open in public.

Yan only said, "Two days is short. Don't waste recovery pretending it is training."

Liu Feng looked at him in surprise.

Qian Lei muttered, "Why does that sound like advice and a threat?"

Yan's mouth curved faintly. "Because you need both."

Then he walked on, Dorm336's Song Yichen and Luo Haoran falling into step behind him, their own second-place route still carrying Shrek's quiet attention.

Lan watched them go.

No one in Heaven Luo's final list was ordinary anymore.

But the world did not become fair because many people were special.

It became sharper.

Two days passed quickly.

Too quickly.

On the morning of the next qualifier, the simulation building filled again.

This time the air felt different before anyone entered the pods.

The first qualifier had been terrifying because no one knew what Shrek would throw at them.

Now they knew enough to be afraid properly.

Heaven Luo Academy's six shortlisted teams gathered under teacher supervision. Six, not the neat number some old expectation might have assumed. Dorm333 stood at the center of too many glances. Dorm336 stood not far away, second-place quiet wrapped around them like heat under stone. Lu Qianxun's team was there too, repaired by rest but changed by elimination and analysis. The remaining teams kept their voices low.

No one looked at Lan Xuanyu's trio the same way as before.

Twenty thousand three hundred and forty-one points.

Numbers could become rumors even when details stayed protected.

Every student present had seen the Ground Fire Scarlet Dragon. They had felt the ground shake. They had seen fireballs erase formations, anti-flight meteors tear the sky, and human-priority rules punish every easy thought.

Ground Fire Lizards could not make that score.

No amount of ordinary survival could make that score.

Lu Qianxun stood across the room, arms folded, gaze steady on Lan's team. There was no hatred in it now. Only calculation, respect, and the unwillingness of a rival to lie to himself.

"They killed the Dragons," someone whispered behind him.

No one asked which dragons.

Everyone knew.

Lu did not turn around.

"That is the only explanation," he said quietly. "The points could not come from lizards."

The whispering stopped.

Qian Lei leaned closer to Lan and Liu.

"I suddenly miss when people underestimated us."

Liu Feng flexed the fingers of his right hand once beneath his sleeve.

A faint silver line flickered and vanished.

"They will not make that mistake again."

Lan looked at the simulation pods waiting ahead.

His Spirit Sea was calm.

His Soul Power was still Rank20.

His reward card had not been used.

Qian's rings had been strengthened, but reliability still had to be earned.

Liu's right arm carried a silver edge, but a trump card was not a plan by itself.

Across the room, Yan Shuo's eyes met his for one brief moment.

Class monitor.

Second-place survivor.

Fire Phoenix file that no serious Shrek observer could treat as ordinary.

Then the teachers called them forward.

The next round was waiting.

Lan stepped toward the pod.

Together did not mean the road became gentle.

It only meant no one entered it alone.
