# Chapter 49 — No Pattern Underfoot

The pink light did not feel like a door.

It felt like being folded.

For one instant, Lan Xuanyu could still sense the training ground behind him: Mu Zhongtian's controlled breathing, Ling Yiyi's smiling attention, Qian Lei's excitement beside his left shoulder, Liu Feng's steady quiet beside his right.

Then the world bent.

Light closed over his eyelashes. His feet lost the ground. The soft pink radiance wrapped around the three of them from all directions, gentle enough to look harmless and strange enough to make every instinct in Lan's newly opened Spirit Sea wake at once.

He did not reach out.

He only breathed.

Together, he had said.

So together they passed through.

When the light opened again, there was no training ground.

Above them was space.

Not the painted ceiling of a simulation room. Not the blue sky of Heaven Luo Academy. An immeasurable darkness spread overhead, so deep that even the stars looked like thoughts too distant to touch. They hung there in countless bright points, some sharp and some soft, some near enough to seem like they were watching, some so far away that Lan's mind could not decide whether they were real or only part of the Soul World's illusion.

Below their feet was the opposite of darkness.

Color.

A vast wheel filled the world beneath them. It was so wide that its far edge vanished into the horizon of light. Red, gold, green, blue, violet, white, black, silver, and colors Lan did not have names for spun beneath the transparent surface where their feet hovered. The wheel turned so quickly that no section could be fixed by sight. Every time Lan thought he had found a pattern, the colors blurred and dissolved into another stream.

Pink halos drifted around them like falling petals that refused to fall.

Qian Lei stared downward with his mouth slightly open.

"This," he whispered, "looks expensive."

Liu Feng's hand moved toward his spear case out of habit, then stopped when he remembered this was a reward space, not a battlefield.

Lan did not relax.

Shrek reward space was still Shrek space.

If there was a rule, there was a test hidden somewhere inside the rule.

Ling Yiyi's voice sounded from all directions at once, gentle and clear.

"You may now move around the wheel and choose your own spot. When the wheel stops and your choice locks in, your reward will appear. After you stop, you may not move again."

Qian Lei's eyes brightened so fast that Lan almost saw greed become physical.

Lan spoke before Qian's foot could twitch.

"Wait."

Qian froze with one knee half-bent. "I was only preparing a heroic first step."

"That is exactly why I said wait."

Liu Feng nodded silently.

Lan looked toward the empty air where Ling Yiyi's voice had come from. "Sister Ling, can the three of us stand on the same spot?"

"No," Ling Yiyi answered. Her tone carried a smile. "Only one person may choose a spot."

Qian's expression collapsed. "There goes the safest plan."

"It was not the safest plan," Liu Feng said.

"It was the most unified plan."

"It was you trying to make Xuanyu choose for all three of us."

"Unity has many definitions."

Lan ignored them for the moment. "Then what kinds of rewards are on the wheel? Are the colors connected to reward types?"

There was a small pause.

Then Ling Yiyi said, "I don't know."

Qian looked personally betrayed by the sky.

Lan blinked. "You don't know?"

"This is the Lucky Wheel," Ling Yiyi said. "Naturally, it depends on luck. After you enter, do not stop moving unless you want to choose. The moment you stop, your position is fixed."

The pink halos gathered.

Lan had time to say only one thing.

"Don't stop."

Light swept under them.

The three of them dropped.

Their feet landed on the surface of the giant wheel without a sound.

At once, Lan felt the difference between seeing it from above and standing upon it. The wheel was not merely spinning beneath them. It seemed to pull at balance, perception, and desire all at once. Colors raced under the soles of his shoes. A faint pressure rose from below as if countless possible rewards were breathing beneath a thin layer of glass.

Qian Lei wobbled.

Liu Feng caught his sleeve.

"Move," Lan said.

The three of them stepped forward together.

The surface did not resist. It felt smooth, but not slippery; solid, but not quite floor. Every step landed on color that vanished before the next step arrived. The wheel spun too fast for ordinary eyes, and the direction of its rotation seemed to change whenever Lan tried to decide whether it moved clockwise or counterclockwise.

"Captain," Qian whispered, "is it possible the best prize is under my foot right now?"

"If you stop to test it without instruction, I will let Liu Feng drag you by the collar."

Liu Feng said, "I am ready."

"Both of you lack trust."

"We have experience," Lan said.

Qian sighed heavily, but he kept walking.

Lan let their rhythm settle.

Step.

Step.

Step.

The wheel blurred beneath them. Pink halos crossed his vision. Overhead, the boundless starry space made their small line of three seem almost laughable. They were children walking on a giant wheel under a false universe, trying to pick prizes from a school that liked to hide knives inside smiles.

But Lan's mind was clearer than before.

Spirit Sea did not make everything easy. It did not turn the world into obedient lines. If anything, it made him more aware of how much he could not understand.

Still, his perception had changed.

He narrowed his eyes, softened his breathing, and allowed a thread of Spiritual Power to spread downward.

Not force.

Listening.

The wheel answered with confusion at first.

Too many colors. Too much movement. Surging light, rotating pressure, strange echoes sliding past each other so quickly that his mind almost reached for them and lost them at the same time.

Lan slowed his thoughts.

Yin Tianfan had taught him never to stare only at what an enemy wanted him to see.

Ji Hongbin had taught him that foundation mattered more than one clever moment.

Yan Shuo had said: Don't let a reward scatter your team.

Lan's breathing became even.

He did not try to seize the whole wheel.

He sensed one passing current.

Then another.

A sweet freshness brushed past from somewhere below, light and clean, like new leaves after rain or pure soul power washed of impurities. It made people instinctively want to step toward it.

Another current followed, fierce and sharp, with an ominous edge. It was not evil exactly. It was danger refined into an object: claw, bone, fang, weapon, something that could cut or bite if mishandled.

Then there was a heavy resistant feeling, as if the wheel itself were saying no. It was not frightening, but it pushed at the mind. Lan sensed it only for a breath before it spun away.

There were also blank places.

No aura.

No sweetness, no danger, no resistance. Empty patches sliding underfoot as if they were ordinary color with nothing hidden there.

Lan's brows tightened.

The auras did not stay attached to the colors his eyes saw. Or if they did, the speed and shifting layer of the wheel made the connection almost useless. By the time he sensed something and matched it to a place, that place was already gone.

This was luck.

But not pure luck.

A person with stronger perception could at least know that the wheel was complicated.

Knowing it was complicated did not mean controlling it.

Qian Lei whispered, "Captain, did your face just become the face you use before cheating a test?"

"I am observing."

"That is what people say before cheating a test."

Liu Feng glanced at Lan. "Can you tell anything?"

"Only categories," Lan said. "Maybe. Not exact rewards."

Qian immediately leaned closer, still walking. "Categories are good. Tell me which category contains money."

"I didn't sense money."

"Then the wheel is flawed."

Lan almost smiled, but he forced himself back to focus.

They could not walk forever. Ling Yiyi had said to move around and choose. The wheel might allow only a limited path. Even if it did not, hesitation was also a choice, and Shrek rarely rewarded hesitation without purpose.

Who first?

Qian Lei was the most restless. He had also given up the most obvious personal certainty before this reward. The wound of Ling Yiyi's early-admission offer had not disappeared just because he joked. If he waited too long, his greed and anxiety would tangle together and make him worse.

Let him draw first.

Let him receive something that belonged to him.

Lan did not think that because Qian was weak.

He thought it because a team did not only arrange positions by power. Sometimes it arranged them by heart.

"Qian Lei," Lan said, "choose a spot first."

Qian's eyes widened. "Me?"

"You. Stop when I say."

"Wait, do I prepare a prayer?"

"No."

"A slogan?"

"No."

"A personal declaration to the wheel?"

"Definitely no."

Qian clasped both hands anyway and looked at the colors under his feet. "Great Wheel of Shrek, please compensate a loyal summoner who refused temptation and suffered greatly."

Liu Feng said, "You suffered for one day."

"Deep suffering has no clock."

Lan's perception followed the wheel.

A fresh aura was not under Qian's foot. It was ahead — no, behind — no, offset by the wheel's strange pull.

Four steps away.

Lan did not know whether four steps meant anything.

But the current was clean, sweet, and alive.

"Stop," he said.

Qian froze.

The moment he stopped moving, the surface beneath his feet flashed.

A purple halo rose from the wheel and wrapped around him from ankle to waist, then to shoulders. Qian's mouth opened again, but no complaint came out this time.

A pleasant female voice sounded in the starry space.

"Reward: cultivation age of all current Soul Rings increased by five hundred years."

For a breath, none of them moved.

Then Qian Lei made a sound between a gasp and a strangled shout.

"Five hundred? All rings?"

The purple halo shimmered around him.

He tried to hop.

His feet did not leave the wheel.

Qian's upper body performed the celebration his legs were forbidden to complete. "Five hundred years! Did you hear that? Five hundred years! My Gate of Summoning loves me again!"

Liu Feng stared at the purple light with rare open surprise.

Lan was stunned too.

Soul rings were not coins. Their cultivation age was foundation, power, quality, and skill depth. Even a fixed increase could mean very different things depending on compatibility and the soul master's martial soul, but for Qian Lei the effect might be unusually meaningful.

His summoning was strange.

Sometimes unreliable.

Sometimes terrifying.

The strength of the rings supporting it mattered: opening speed, stability, duration, quality of summoned beasts, the strain of maintaining a gate, maybe even how cleanly Replicate could imprint a prior summon. Five hundred years to every current ring was not merely a number. It was time compressed into permission.

It might not make Qian reliable overnight.

Nothing was that merciful.

But it could give him a wider floor beneath his talent.

Qian looked at Lan with shining eyes. "Captain, if this is because of you, I forgive you for all future training."

"You cannot forgive training you have not done."

"I can prepare forgiveness in advance."

Lan looked down.

The purple halo beneath Qian had come after the fresh aura shifted.

Four steps.

The fresh aura had not been under him when Lan gave the order. It had moved there after he stopped.

So the wheel did not simply freeze the current spot.

It resolved.

Maybe by inertia.

Maybe by delayed aura movement.

Maybe by a mechanism meant to punish overconfidence.

Lan kept walking with Liu Feng while Qian remained locked in place behind them.

Qian called after them, "Choose well! I need teammates worthy of my enhanced future greatness!"

"Be quiet," Liu Feng said.

"I am encouraging you."

"Quietly."

Lan glanced at Liu Feng.

Liu's face had already returned to calm, but his fingers had tightened slightly. He was happy for Qian. He was also human. A reward like that appearing first made the next choice heavier.

Lan understood.

He felt it too.

The wheel was no longer abstract.

It could give something real.

Something life-changing.

And it could give something unknown.

"Liu Feng," Lan said.

"Mn."

"Your turn."

Liu did not ask why.

He did not bargain.

He only adjusted his breathing and kept his eyes forward.

Lan followed the auras again.

Freshness had moved away. A resistant patch spun past too quickly. Then that fierce, ominous current appeared at the edge of his perception.

It was not close.

Seven steps.

Lan remembered four.

If the wheel used offset, maybe seven would matter. If it changed by person, maybe it would not. If it deliberately responded to expectation, any pattern he built might already be useless.

But the fierce aura did not feel bad for Liu.

It felt dangerous.

Liu Feng's whole path was a blade drawn through danger.

His White Dragon Spear, his speed, his willingness to take the narrow line when Lan called it — these were not gentle things. A fierce reward might cut him if unsuitable, but a Shrek reward wheel should not hand poison as first-place reward without warning.

Probably.

Lan almost heard Yin Tianfan laugh at the word.

He made his choice anyway.

"Stop."

Liu Feng stopped.

The wheel changed.

Lan felt it clearly this time. The rotation beneath Liu's feet suddenly accelerated, not everywhere, but in the layer of aura itself. The fierce current that had been seven steps away shot forward as if pulled by an invisible hook and landed directly beneath him.

Dark red light mixed with purple and silver.

The pleasant female voice rang out again.

"Reward: Spirit Pagoda-made two-thousand-year right arm Soul Bone, containing one Soul Skill."

The starry space seemed to go silent.

Qian Lei's celebration died mid-breath.

Liu Feng stared at his own right arm.

Lan's pupils contracted.

Soul Bone.

For children from Heaven Luo Academy, those two words had a weight even greater than most people outside the Soul Master world could understand.

A soul ring was the road every Soul Master had to walk. Ten levels, one ring, one skill. The path was cruel, but at least there was a path.

A Soul Bone was different.

It was extra.

In ancient times, Soul Bones appeared only under rare special conditions from soul beasts. Countless Soul Masters could live their entire lives and never see one, much less own one. Unlike soul rings, which vanished with the Soul Master's death, Soul Bones could remain, could be passed down, could become clan heritage or battlefield treasure. A single piece could change a person's combat system.

Head.

Torso.

Left arm.

Right arm.

Left leg.

Right leg.

Six main body parts, six possible foundations of extra power.

Each Soul Bone strengthened its body part and came with a Soul Skill of its own. To a Soul Master, that meant not just a stronger arm or leg, not just a sturdier body, but an additional ability beyond the strict limit of soul rings.

An extra card.

An extra life.

An extra answer in a place where one answer could decide everything.

In recent millennia, the Spirit Pagoda's research had made man-made Soul Bones possible. That did not make them cheap. It did not make them common. The cost of creating them remained astronomical, and ordinary Soul Masters could only look at them in catalogues, reward lists, or dreams they were too embarrassed to speak aloud.

And now Liu Feng had drawn one.

A two-thousand-year right arm Soul Bone.

With one Soul Skill.

Liu Feng's right hand trembled once.

Only once.

Then he clenched it slowly.

His White Dragon Spear was not in his hand, but Lan could almost see the line from shoulder to wrist, from wrist to spear, from spear to the sudden acceleration that had carried Liu through so many dangerous moments.

Right arm.

For Liu Feng, that was not random.

Qian Lei finally found his voice.

"Soul Bone," he said weakly. "A Soul Bone. Liu Feng, you drew a Soul Bone."

Liu looked up.

"I heard."

"You are too calm. If I drew a Soul Bone, I would be spinning."

"You cannot move after stopping."

"Emotionally spinning."

Liu lowered his gaze to the light beneath his feet. The halo held him gently but firmly. He could move his upper body, but his feet stayed fixed, just as Ling Yiyi had warned.

"If I move," Liu asked, "will I lose it?"

Lan answered before Qian could test the universe. "Do not move."

"I will not."

Qian leaned forward as much as the lock around his own feet allowed. "Can you at least look happier? My heart needs confirmation that the team is grateful for destiny."

Liu's mouth moved by the faintest degree.

"I am happy."

"That is your happy face?"

"Yes."

"Then your sad face must be invisible."

Lan watched them and felt something loosen in his chest.

There was envy in the air. Of course there was. Children, Soul Masters, competitors — how could there not be? Qian's ring-age increase had been enormous. Liu's Soul Bone was even more shocking. The wheel had placed treasure beneath their feet one by one, and each treasure belonged to one person.

This was exactly the kind of moment that could scatter a team.

Not because any of them were bad.

Because difference created weight.

One person received something obvious and precious. Another person waited. A third might receive something less clear. Then hearts began making accounts.

Lan remembered Yin's words.

A commander who treats allies as disposable obstacles may win one battle and ruin an army.

He also remembered his own answer.

If I ever treat either of you as disposable, you should hit me.

Lan breathed out.

"Qian Lei's reward improves our summoning foundation," he said. "Liu Feng's Soul Bone will improve our attack and mobility once it is integrated. Both are team gains."

Qian blinked.

Liu looked at him.

Lan kept his eyes on the wheel. "Do not start comparing whose reward is worth more. We still have the next selection stage. If one of us grows, all three of us have more ways to survive."

Qian's expression shifted.

For once, he did not immediately joke.

Then he said, softer, "I know. I just... Soul Bone."

"I know," Lan said.

Liu nodded. "If it suits me, I will use it well. For the team."

"Not if," Qian said at once. "It must suit you. If Shrek gives you a right arm Soul Bone that does not suit your spear, I will file a complaint with the wheel."

"You are still fixed in place."

"My spirit is mobile."

Lan smiled faintly.

Then the smile faded.

His turn.

The wheel continued to spin beneath him.

Qian's fresh aura had been four steps away.

Liu's fierce aura had been seven steps away.

Four.

Seven.

If he continued the sequence, perhaps ten.

But the thought was too clean.

Too tempting.

Shrek would not make the Lucky Wheel solvable by adding three.

Unless it did.

Unless it wanted him to think it would not.

Unless the pattern changed because each person had different affinity.

Unless the auras were only bait for people with enough Spiritual Power to become arrogant.

Lan's mouth tightened.

This was exactly the sort of trap he would design if he wanted to test a young command-type student who had just entered Spirit Sea.

Give him enough information to form a theory.

Make the first two results support the theory.

Then see whether he trusted the theory more than uncertainty.

He could almost hear Yin Tianfan approving.

He could also hear Ji Hongbin asking whether his foundation justified the conclusion.

Lan kept walking.

Step.

Step.

The auras spun beneath him: fresh, fierce, resistant, blank, fresh again, something cold, something heavy, blank.

His Spirit Sea perception reached down, but the wheel no longer felt like something he could read. It felt like something reading him back.

Greed?

Fear?

Expectation?

Calculation?

Team responsibility?

Lan did not know whether the wheel had that much intelligence or whether his own mind was giving meaning to pressure.

It did not matter.

He had to choose.

If his reward was worse than theirs, the team still had two major gains.

If his reward was better, he must not let it become a wall between them.

If his reward was strange, he would understand it later.

Luck was part of battle too.

A commander could arrange many things.

Not all.

Lan slowed by half a step, then steadied himself.

There was a blank place beneath the blur, an empty aura sliding past. It seemed worthless. Or unreadable. Or protected from perception.

The fierce aura was far away. Freshness was farther. The resistant feeling had just vanished.

No pattern.

No guarantee.

Underfoot, the wheel's colors became a river.

Lan stopped.

For an instant, nothing happened.

Then his body sank.

Not physically, not the way a foot sank into mud. His whole perception dropped as if the wheel beneath him opened downward and pulled his senses into its center. Color rose around him. The starry sky stretched. Qian and Liu's voices became distant.

A current rushed across the wheel.

Lan felt it approach from far away.

At least ten steps.

Maybe more.

So there had been a pattern.

Or there had been no pattern, and the wheel had chosen a result that mocked the pattern.

He could not tell.

The aura that arrived beneath him was not fresh.

It was not fierce.

It was not resistant.

It was almost blank — but not empty. More like a closed door in a silent wall. There was no fragrance, no blade, no pressure that announced treasure. Only depth, waiting, and a faint sense that something inside would become whatever the person brought into it.

The pleasant female voice sounded for the third time.

"Reward: one access opportunity to the Elementary Spirit Ascension Platform."

Light flashed.

The sinking sensation vanished.

Lan stood still.

Qian Lei stared at him.

Liu Feng stared too.

Then Qian said carefully, "Captain... you drew a place?"

Lan did not answer immediately.

Elementary Spirit Ascension Platform.

The name carried Spirit Pagoda weight. He had heard words about the Spirit Pagoda from teachers, classmates, and public knowledge — Soul Spirits, simulated environments, cultivation support, expensive systems tied to deep research and soul beast data. But the exact meaning of Spirit Ascension Platform was not something he had studied in detail yet.

A platform.

Ascension.

Spirit.

Access once.

It did not sound as immediately solid as Liu Feng's Soul Bone.

It did not sound as immediately measurable as Qian Lei's five-hundred-year ring increase.

But Shrek's Lucky Wheel would not place a worthless reward beside those two.

Lan looked down at the blank light beneath his feet.

He had stopped on no aura.

No aura had given him a door.

Qian's face became solemn in the exaggerated way he used when trying not to panic. "Is this good? It sounds good. It has four words. Important things usually have many words."

Liu Feng said, "Spirit Pagoda access should not be cheap."

"Neither is a restaurant bill after training, but that does not make it a Soul Bone."

Lan raised his head. "I don't know exactly what it is."

Qian immediately looked offended on his behalf. "The wheel should include instructions."

"It probably expects us to ask."

"That is inconvenient design."

The wheel answered them with light.

All three halos brightened at once.

The colorful surface beneath their feet dissolved.

The starry sky folded inward.

Pink radiance swept over them again, and in the next breath they were standing back beside Ling Yiyi on the clean training-ground floor.

The sudden stillness felt strange.

After the spinning wheel, ordinary ground seemed too honest.

Qian Lei looked down at his own feet and tested a careful shuffle.

He moved.

"Freedom," he whispered.

Liu Feng flexed his right hand again, then quickly stopped as if afraid the promised Soul Bone might vanish if he looked too eager.

Lan turned toward Ling Yiyi.

"Sister Ling," he asked, "what is the Spirit Ascension Platform?"

Ling Yiyi's smile did not answer the question directly.

It became the sort of smile adults used when a child had received something valuable and did not yet know how valuable it was.

"Your rewards will be delivered to your academy later," she said. "Liu Feng's Soul Bone will follow the proper handling procedure. Lan Xuanyu, Qian Lei, the two of you will need to go to the Spirit Pagoda to collect your rewards."

Qian lifted both hands. "Collect? Does that mean mine is not increasing now?"

"Correct procedure is required," Ling Yiyi said. "A reward that changes soul-ring cultivation age is not a candy you eat in a corridor."

Qian slowly lowered his hands. "That sentence hurt, but I respect it."

Lan asked, "Should we collect immediately?"

Ling Yiyi shook her head.

"I suggest you wait until after the following qualifier stage. The Spirit Ascension Platform may require quite a lot of time. If you enter hastily or leave too soon, you will not gain the full value of the opportunity."

Lan absorbed every word.

After the following qualifier stage.

Not now.

Not a shortcut before the next test.

A future opportunity, valid and real, but not something to be swallowed greedily the moment it appeared.

That distinction mattered.

It mattered for him.

It mattered for the team.

It mattered for every deleted wrong path that tried to turn future reward into current strength before the story had earned it.

Qian looked tragic. "So my soul rings must wait?"

"They must be processed properly," Ling Yiyi said.

"Can I at least tell them good news?"

"If you have a method to speak with your rings, you may try after leaving."

Qian looked thoughtful.

Liu Feng said, "Do not."

"You do not know what my martial soul needs emotionally."

"I know what our room needs at night. Silence."

Ling Yiyi's gaze moved to Lan again.

"Remember, rewards are only rewards. They are not admission. They are not the end of the road."

Lan nodded. "I understand."

"Do you?" Ling Yiyi's eyes curved. "Many students become less steady after receiving something good than after suffering something bad."

Lan thought of Qian's purple halo, Liu's Soul Bone, his own blank-door reward.

He thought of Yin Tianfan standing alone over a clean simulation field with no allies left.

He thought of Yan Shuo's corridor warning.

"Then we will be careful," Lan said.

Qian opened his mouth.

Lan looked at him.

Qian closed it again, then said with great effort, "Carefully happy."

Liu Feng nodded. "That is acceptable."

Ling Yiyi laughed softly.

"Good. Then I look forward to your next performance."

Her figure began to fade.

The pink light around her thinned into petals, then threads, then nothing.

A moment later, Ling Yiyi was gone.

The training ground remained.

Mu Zhongtian stood not far away, still silent.

For several breaths, none of the three boys spoke.

The Lucky Wheel had given each of them something different.

A summoner's foundation.

A spearman's bone.

A captain's door.

Qian Lei finally exhaled so dramatically that it sounded like air escaping a damaged balloon.

"I wanted treasure," he said. "We got trouble-shaped treasure."

Liu Feng looked at his right arm. "Good trouble."

"Easy for you to say. Your trouble is a Soul Bone. Mine requires going to the Spirit Pagoda and trusting procedure."

"Mine also requires procedure."

"Yes, but yours has the words Soul Bone. That automatically improves the procedure."

Lan smiled.

Qian immediately pointed at him. "And Captain's reward is mysterious. Mysterious rewards are usually either extremely powerful or extremely troublesome. With Captain, probably both."

"Maybe," Lan said.

He did not pretend certainty.

The Spirit Ascension Platform might become an answer later. It might become a risk. It might help his soul rings, his strange martial souls, or something he did not yet understand. It might take time he did not know how to spare.

But that was later.

Now, they were still three boys who had not entered Shrek Academy yet.

They still had to pass the next selection.

Rewards could strengthen them.

Rewards could also tempt them into thinking the road had already accepted them.

Lan turned to Qian and Liu.

"We wait until after the qualifier stage," he said. "No rushing. No arguing with teachers. No trying to secretly collect early."

Qian looked wounded. "Why did you look at me for all three sentences?"

"Because Liu Feng would not secretly collect early."

Liu Feng nodded.

"He might silently collect early," Qian argued.

"I would not."

"See? Too calm. Suspicious."

Lan's smile became a little deeper.

Then he looked at both of them seriously.

"Qian Lei, your reward matters. Liu Feng, yours matters too. Mine may matter later. But none of them changes the order. We entered together. We draw separately because the rule forced it. We keep moving together."

Qian's joking expression faded.

Liu Feng straightened.

Lan did not raise his voice.

He did not need to.

"The next stage will not care how happy we are tonight. If anything, Shrek will make it harder because we ranked first. So we treat the rewards as preparation, not victory."

Qian was quiet for a breath.

Then he grinned.

"Preparation can still be celebrated with food."

Liu Feng said, "You always return to food."

"Food is also preparation."

Lan looked at Mu Zhongtian.

Their teacher's eyes were complicated: pride, worry, disbelief, and the exhaustion of an adult who had just watched three children obtain things that would make half the academy lose sleep.

Mu Zhongtian finally spoke.

"Exit the Soul World first. Details can wait until formal delivery. And Qian Lei—"

Qian stood at attention. "Yes, Teacher Mu."

"Do not attempt to negotiate with your soul rings tonight."

Qian's face fell. "Why does everyone know me so well?"

"Because you are loud," Liu Feng said.

The training ground began to fade.

Lan felt the simulation release them.

Pink light, colorful wheel, starry sky — all of it retreated into memory.

But the feeling under his feet remained.

No pattern.

No guarantee.

A blank place that became a door.

Perhaps that was luck.

Perhaps that was Shrek.

Perhaps that was simply the road ahead.

Lan closed his eyes as the Soul World dissolved.

When he opened them again, the simulation cabin lid was rising.

The real ceiling of Heaven Luo Academy came into view.

No stars.

No wheel.

Only the next stage waiting somewhere beyond tomorrow.

And three rewards that had not yet become power.
