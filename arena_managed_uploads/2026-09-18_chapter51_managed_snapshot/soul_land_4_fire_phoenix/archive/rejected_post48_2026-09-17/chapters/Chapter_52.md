# Chapter 52 — The Forest Counts Separately

The question did not disappear just because no one dared ask Lan Xuanyu directly.

How had they killed the Ground Fire Scarlet Dragons?

It moved through the lineup in pieces: a glance toward Liu Feng's right sleeve, a look at Qian Lei that carried both envy and suspicion, a longer stare at Lan Xuanyu's back. Those who had survived the first qualifier had seen the Dragons. They had seen the fireballs, the cracked stone, the bodies thrown aside by heat and mass. They knew what their own attacks had felt like against that pressure.

Lan's Martial Soul Fusion technique?

Impossible.

The technique was strange and strong, and everyone in the Elite Junior Class knew that Lan Xuanyu could turn another person's ability into something sharper than it should have been. But Lan himself was still only Rank20. No one with sense believed a Rank20 child's fusion technique alone could injure something like a Ground Fire Scarlet Dragon.

Then what had happened?

No answer came.

Only the score remained.

Twenty thousand three hundred and forty-one.

First on Heaven Luo Planet.

Perhaps first across the entire federation qualifier stage.

That number had already passed beyond the students. It had moved into teacher reports, academy meetings, and adult smiles that were too controlled to be casual. Heaven Luo Academy had gained an honor large enough that even students could feel the building around them stand a little straighter. Rumor said the academy president had already given instructions to prepare for the next Elite Junior Class.

A result like this could become policy.

A result like this could become funding.

A result like this could make adults believe the impossible was worth training for again.

Lu Qianxun's team had placed ninth.

That was not weak.

Ninth among a hundred advancing teams was enough for pride, enough for a report, enough for a rival to keep his head raised. Ye Lingtong and Chang Jianyi stood beside Lu with the quiet stiffness of people who knew they had lost, survived, and still earned the next gate.

But ninth could not stand beside first and pretend the light was equal.

When the teams lined up, Lu Qianxun brought Ye Lingtong and Chang Jianyi forward without hesitation.

He stopped behind Dorm333.

No one ordered him to do it.

That made the movement louder.

Lan noticed, but did not turn immediately. Qian Lei did turn. He looked back, saw Lu's team behind them, then looked ahead again with a face fighting hard not to become smug.

Liu Feng's right hand remained relaxed by his side.

The sleeve over that arm was neat today. Too neat, perhaps. Anyone who had seen him before could tell he was guarding something, but no one saw silver. No one saw the crescent patterns under cloth.

Six teams from Heaven Luo Academy had gathered.

Six teams, not five.

The old expectation would have been cleaner. Three people per team, five teams, fifteen pods. But this year had not stayed clean. Dorm336 had survived with 9,846 points and second place, and no serious teacher could pretend that did not change the count. Yan Shuo stood with Song Yichen and Luo Haoran not far away, public identity calm, class monitor presence as steady as a drawn line.

No flame showed.

No wings unfurled.

He did not need them to make space around him.

Still, today even that presence did not pull command away from Lan. This round's question was not who burned brightest. It was who could read rules, terrain, greed, danger, and people quickly enough to keep a team alive for seven days.

Yan's gaze crossed Lan's for a moment.

No challenge.

Recognition.

Lan looked away first because the lineup ahead had begun to settle, and because questions between them never fit properly in public.

Behind the main ranks, Jin Xiang stood with Tang Yuege and Li Pengbin.

Jin Xiang's eyes were fixed on Lan Xuanyu.

Regret had an ugly taste.

He had not understood it when Lan first arrived at the Elite Junior Class. Back then, provoking Lan had felt easy, even natural. Lan's Soul Power had not looked frightening. His temperament had not been sharp in the way Lu Qianxun's was sharp or overwhelming in the way Yan Shuo's public presence could become. Jin Xiang had thought there was room to test, to mock, to stand above someone before knowing what he was looking at.

Years had passed with that distance left in place.

Now the distance looked stupid.

Lan Xuanyu was not simply strong.

If strength were all that mattered, many people in this lineup could comfort themselves. Qian Lei and Liu Feng had not seemed overwhelmingly strong at first glance. Qian Lei's summoning was unreliable enough that jokes clung to him even when he was serious. Liu Feng had speed, but speed without a path could become a way to lose faster.

Under Lan's command, those pieces had become first place.

Not just survival.

First.

Even the other teams had benefited from Lan's warning in the first qualifier. Without his shout to separate, without his reading of the Ground Fire Scarlet Dragon's targeting, how many of Heaven Luo's teams would have reached the top hundred at all?

Jin Xiang knew the answer was ugly.

Lu Qianxun had already expressed goodwill. He had made it clear that if cooperation became possible in this next qualifier, he was willing to let Lan Xuanyu take the lead. Others clearly thought the same. No one wanted pride badly enough to throw away a commander who had proved he could find the hidden road inside a deadly test.

Only Jin Xiang felt embarrassed to approach.

That embarrassment followed him into the lineup and sat heavily on his shoulders.

Ji Hongbin stood before them.

His gaze swept across the six teams. It paused on Dorm333, moved across Lu's team, touched Jin Xiang's lowered expression, and did not skip Dorm336. When his eyes passed Yan Shuo, neither teacher nor student needed to say aloud that command and combat talent were not the same category.

"The qualifier will start soon," Ji Hongbin said in a deep voice. "I do not need to say much. Display your strengths as much as possible. If this round gives you a chance to work together, let Lan Xuanyu take overall command."

A small stir moved through the students.

For Lu Qianxun, it was confirmation.

For the other teams, permission.

For Jin Xiang, rescue.

Teacher Ji had said it. Then following Lan would not look like begging for a place. It would look like obeying instruction.

Jin Xiang suddenly found Ji Hongbin far more adorable than usual.

Overall command was good.

Very good.

Mu Zhongtian did not let the students' expressions wander for long.

"Time," he said sternly. "Enter the simulation pods."

Eighteen pods opened.

The sight made the six-team change impossible to ignore. One by one, students stepped into the waiting chambers. Qian Lei muttered something about hoping this round did not begin with falling rocks, poisonous fog, or a teacher's smile. Liu Feng ignored him. Lan lay down and let the pod close over him.

Connect.

Soul World.

Darkness shifted.

Then the familiar simulated world formed around consciousness like water closing over a stone.

A pleasant voice rang from the sky.

"Welcome to the Shrek Academy Heaven Luo Planet qualifiers."

Lan opened his eyes inside the simulated staging space and listened.

He did not allow himself to relax just because the voice sounded gentle. The previous qualifier had taught him that Shrek's words were knives wrapped in silk. Every rule had meaning. Nothing was casual. Nothing was decorative. If the announcement said something, it wanted them to act, fear, misunderstand, or survive by understanding.

"The rules of the qualifiers are as follows. First, work in teams. After entering, each team will be transferred to a random corner of the Soul Beast Forest. The venue of the qualifiers is the simulated Star Dou Forest that once existed on the Mother Planet. What you need to do is go deep into the Star Dou Forest and do your best to hunt high-level soul beasts. The final score is calculated by accumulated hunting points. Each team will be scored separately. Whoever completes the final kill receives the points."

Qian Lei's eyes moved quickly.

Final kill.

Lan stored the phrase.

"Second, killing is allowed between teams. Killing members of another team grants half of the victims' accumulated points. The final score of the killed team will be the remaining half."

This time, even Qian did not make a sound.

Team killing.

Half.

The rule changed the forest before they even saw it.

"Third, the test will last for seven days in Soul World. Scores of the various teams will be calculated after the test ends. Prepare to enter. Countdown."

Seven days.

Separate teams.

Random corners.

Go deep.

Final kill.

Kill teams for half.

Lan repeated the structure silently and felt it arrange itself like pieces on a board.

"Ten, nine, eight..."

The countdown began.

Lan glanced left and right only once.

Qian Lei's round face had lost some color, but his eyes were alert. Liu Feng stood straight, right arm quiet, gaze already carrying the sharpened look that had remained after the Soul Bone fusion.

This was not the first qualifier.

That was danger.

This was danger with rules that invited people to betray each other.

"Three, two, one. Begin."

Light fell.

It covered them from above, white and weightless.

The world blurred.

Lan felt his body lose direction. Sky and ground rotated through each other. For a breath there was no up, no down, no forest, no teammate, only the spinning sensation of being taken apart by light and carried elsewhere.

Then fresh air struck his face.

Lan dropped into a crouch by instinct.

His hands were ready before his eyes fully focused.

Green surrounded him.

Not the green of academy gardens or roadside trees.

This was old green, heavy green, green layered upon green until distance became a wall. Towering trunks rose around them, so thick that several adults might not have encircled one. The canopy above locked together high overhead, blocking most of the sky. Vines hung like ropes and snakes. Ferns, moss, roots, and strange broad leaves crowded the ground. There was no road, no path, no sign that humans belonged here.

The forest did not feel empty.

It felt like it was listening.

Qian Lei appeared beside him with a confused expression, blinking at the surrounding plants as if hoping a signboard might appear and apologize.

Liu Feng was already turning, sharp eyes scanning gaps between trunks, high branches, vine shadows, and the dark spaces beneath exposed roots.

Lan stood slowly.

He reached out, took Qian Lei by the shoulder, and pulled him between himself and Liu Feng.

"Here," he said.

Qian obeyed instantly.

Whatever jokes he had saved for the forest died under the weight of the trees.

Lan gestured toward a nearby trunk so broad it looked less like a tree and more like a wall grown from the ground. They moved to it together. Lan took one side. Liu Feng took the other. Qian stood protected between them, hands already twitching toward the familiar preparation of his summoning.

Only after their backs had something solid behind them did Lan breathe fully.

"Which direction do we go?" Qian whispered.

The whisper was wise.

The forest swallowed loudness badly.

Lan shook his head. "Don't rush. We analyze first."

"Good," Qian said at once. "Analysis is excellent. I strongly support analysis before being eaten."

Liu Feng did not smile, but his shoulders eased by a fraction.

Lan looked through the trees.

The announcement replayed in his mind.

"The previous qualifier was also a test of comprehensive qualities," he said. "This one is longer. Seven days means the first requirement is not killing. It is living here for seven days."

Qian nodded quickly.

"Living is good. I vote for living."

"Second," Lan continued, "the announcement said to go deep into the Star Dou Forest. Shrek's announcements are not meaningless. Going deeper should mean higher-level soul beasts and higher possible scores."

Liu Feng said, "So staying at the edge may be safe, but the score will be low."

"Probably." Lan's eyes remained on the vegetation. "Third, each team is scored separately, and the final kill receives the points. That means if multiple teams fight the same soul beast, the one who finishes it gains the score. Cooperation is possible, but it is unstable."

Qian's face wrinkled. "And killing teams gives half their points."

"Yes." Lan's voice lowered. "That rule is the most dangerous. It means the longer a team survives and accumulates points, the more tempting they become to others. If we are killed by another team after working for days, half our effort becomes theirs."

Liu Feng's gaze sharpened. "But the announcement did not mention losing points if a soul beast kills us."

Lan looked at him and nodded.

That was why Liu Feng was easier to command after the first qualifier. He did not only move quickly; he listened to the same knife-edge inside a rule.

"Right. The killed team's final score becomes the remaining half only when another team kills them. The rule did not say soul beasts take points from us. That means, at the worst moment, dying to a soul beast is better than letting another team kill us."

Qian stared at him.

"You mean..."

"If there is absolutely no way out," Lan said calmly, "even suicide inside the simulation would be better than handing another team half our accumulated score."

Qian's mouth opened.

Closed.

Then he nodded with unusual seriousness.

"I understand. I hate understanding it, but I understand."

The three of them stood silent for a moment while the forest breathed around them.

Go deep.

Hunt soul beasts.

Beware other teams.

Persist until the end.

Four lines. None simple.

Strangely, Qian Lei felt steadier after hearing them spoken aloud. Liu Feng did too. A simple arena had never suited Lan best. In an arena, direct power pressed everything into a narrow shape. But this was a forest, a rule system, a survival problem, a hunt, a points game, and a human trap at the same time.

Lan's judgment mattered here.

His ability to enhance mattered here.

His habit of hearing the hidden meaning beneath polite announcements mattered here.

Qian and Liu both knew it.

That knowledge loosened fear without removing caution.

Liu Feng asked, "What now?"

Lan did not answer immediately.

He looked up, trying to read the canopy. Too thick. He looked down, studying root directions and soil. Too many layers. He listened. Leaves shifted in places where no wind reached.

"We need to understand where we are first," he said. "Teacher Ji spoke about the ancient Star Dou Forest. It was once the largest soul beast forest on the Mother Planet and contained all kinds of soul beasts. But it was not one uniform place. There were regions."

Qian counted on his fingers as Lan spoke.

"Peripheral Region," Lan said. "Hybrid Region. Core Region. And at the very center, the Territory of Great Beasts."

Qian's fingers stopped at the last one.

"That name sounds like somewhere we should admire from very far away."

"With our current strength, Shrek should not throw us directly into the Core Region at the beginning," Lan said. "Not if this is meant to test choices rather than kill everyone immediately. We are most likely in the Peripheral Region, or at most somewhere near the Hybrid Region."

"Most likely," Qian repeated. "I enjoy when life depends on 'most likely.'"

Liu Feng looked at Lan. "So we test the area first."

"Yes. We explore carefully and judge the strength of nearby soul beasts. We need to know how dangerous this forest is before deciding how quickly to go deeper. Once we meet something we cannot handle, the qualifier may be over. There is no second chance. For at least the first few days, caution matters more than greed."

Qian exhaled. "Caution. Good. I have always been a cautious person."

Liu Feng finally glanced at him. "You once tried to summon faster by shouting louder."

"That was experimental caution."

"That was panic."

"Panic is caution with speed."

Lan raised one hand slightly.

Both stopped.

The forest seemed too willing to accept noise.

Liu Feng adjusted his stance. "I'll scout?"

He did not say it proudly.

He said it because he was the agility-type Soul Master. Because his speed had always been the blade Dorm333 sent forward when a path had to be found. Because now there was silver under his right sleeve, not as decoration, not as mastery, but as a fused strength that made the teachers more willing to let him step beyond the first trunk.

Lan looked at Liu's right arm for one breath.

The Silver Moon Wolf Right Arm Bone did not glow through the cloth. It did not need to. Liu Feng stood differently now — not larger, not arrogant, but sharper, more gathered around the right side of his body. The fused Soul Bone had raised his battle power, and Rank29 placed him close to the next threshold.

That made scouting safer.

It did not make it safe.

"Three hundred meters," Lan said. "No farther for the first sweep. Scout within a radius of three hundred meters and return. Do not chase anything. Do not test Silver Edge unless you must escape or survive. We need information first, not points."

Liu Feng nodded.

"Understood."

Lan turned to Qian Lei. "Prepare the Coin of Summoning. If Liu Feng encounters a soul beast and draws it back, we must be ready to fight immediately."

Qian's expression tightened. The old complaint rose to his face by habit, then faded before becoming words.

He lifted his hand.

Soul Power began to gather.

"Ready," he whispered.

Liu Feng lowered his body slightly.

For an instant, the forest reflected in his eyes as lines and openings instead of trees and leaves. His right hand flexed once beneath his sleeve. No silver crescent flew. No edge cut the air. He was not here to show a new weapon.

He was here to find the first answer.

Lan watched him step into the green shadow.

The forest counted every team separately.

So Dorm333 would have to count every breath.
