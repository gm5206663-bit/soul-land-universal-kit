# Chapter 47 — Five Hundred and Five

Mu Zhongtian looked at Qian Lei and Liu Feng for a long moment.

His expression was complicated enough that Qian Lei decided the safest expression was innocence.

He arranged his face carefully.

Then, because he was Qian Lei, he ruined it by smiling.

"Teacher Mu," he said, "why are you looking at us like that? We are still children who have just been through a terrible assessment and require gentle encouragement."

Liu Feng turned his head slightly away.

Mu Zhongtian pointed at the office corridor behind them, as if the ranking screen were still visible through walls. "Champion. Twenty thousand three hundred forty-one points. The third-place team has one thousand two hundred thirty-one. Yan Shuo's team already shocked everyone by taking second with nine thousand eight hundred forty-six. You expect me to believe there is nothing to ask?"

Qian's smile became even more painful.

"There is much to ask," he admitted. "There is also much to preserve for future victory."

"Qian Lei."

"Teacher."

"Speak normally."

"I am trying to sound reliable."

"It is having the opposite effect."

Liu Feng stepped in before Qian could defend his entire personality.

"Teacher Mu, we really cannot explain the final part before Xuanyu wakes. He arranged it. He is the captain. Some details affect later tests."

Mu Zhongtian opened his mouth, then looked at Ji Hongbin.

Ji's face was stern, but there was no anger in it.

"They are not wrong," Ji said. "The score is public. The method is not. Let Xuanyu wake first."

Qian's shoulders loosened.

Then a voice came from the far end of the corridor.

"Deep meditation?"

The voice was round, anxious, and familiar.

Yin Tianfan came almost at a run, which was a sight that made several students flatten themselves against the corridor wall. The vice president's usual manner made it difficult to forget that he was not only a teacher but also someone with a long shadow behind him. Seeing him hurry with such undisguised concern made the situation feel serious all over again.

He stopped in front of Ji Hongbin and Mu Zhongtian.

"Xuanyu entered deep meditation?"

Ji nodded. "Yes."

Yin Tianfan's eyes widened, then shone. "And Spirit Sea? He is going to break through Spirit Connection and enter Spirit Sea?"

"Most likely," Ji said. "His last measured Spiritual Power was four hundred ninety-nine. The qualifier pushed him over the edge."

Yin slapped his own palm with delight. "Good! My disciple indeed. Calm under pressure, sharp in war, and now he even knows how to turn crisis into cultivation."

Mu Zhongtian reminded him, "He is still meditating."

"I know. I am happy quietly."

Qian Lei stared at him.

Liu Feng also stared.

Yin looked back. "What?"

Qian said carefully, "Vice President, this is your quiet?"

"Compared to what I feel, yes."

Ji Hongbin's brow twitched. "Keep your voice down."

Yin waved a hand, but he did lower his voice. "Fine, fine. How long has he been in it?"

"Since last evening," Liu Feng said.

"Good foundation." Yin's eyes turned even brighter. "When is the next round?"

Mu Zhongtian answered immediately, "Three days later."

Yin's smile paused.

"Three days." He looked toward Dorm333's door. "Deep meditation duration is unpredictable. If he sleeps through it, what then? There is also the champion reward. The Lucky Wheel, right? They must claim that."

At the words Lucky Wheel, Qian Lei's entire body seemed to regain life.

"Reward?" he asked, though he had already been told once and had been savoring it ever since. "Teacher, please speak more about the wheel. Slowly. With details. Preferably including whether it gives badges."

Mu Zhongtian said, "The top three teams of the qualifier on each planet receive a chance to spin the Lucky Wheel. Shrek Academy's reward pool is not something we can predict. It may be resources, materials, cultivation assistance, rare items, or something even stranger."

Qian's eyes began to glow.

"Rare items," he whispered. "Cultivation assistance. Badges. Possibly money."

"Possibly not money," Liu said.

"Do not damage hope before breakfast."

Yin Tianfan ignored them. "Three days should be enough. Xuanyu's cultivation level is still low. First deep meditation at this stage usually won't trap him for too long."

"Do not say 'trap,'" Qian muttered.

Ji glanced at him.

Qian straightened. "I mean, yes, Teacher."

Yin looked at Ji. "You checked him?"

"Stable."

"Good. Then we wait." Yin's gaze sharpened, and the excitement in it changed shape. "And after he wakes, we ask him exactly how he earned twenty thousand points."

Qian immediately looked at the ceiling.

Liu Feng looked at the floor.

Yin noticed both and laughed.

"Interesting. Very interesting. You two really learned how to keep your mouths shut? Shrek did something useful yesterday."

Qian protested, "My mouth was already wise."

"Your mouth was a public safety risk."

"Vice President!"

Yan Shuo, still standing beside the corridor wall, said calmly, "Accurate."

Qian turned. "Second place should not join hostile commentary."

"Corridor quiet," Yan reminded him.

Qian shut his mouth with visible effort.

Yin Tianfan glanced at Yan then, properly registering his presence and the three members of Dorm336 behind him.

"Second place," Yin said. "Nine thousand eight hundred forty-six. Not bad."

Song Yichen's eyes almost popped. "Vice President, that is only not bad?"

"Compared to twenty thousand, it is not bad. Compared to normal students, it is enough to make half the teachers lose sleep." Yin looked at Yan with interest. "Fire Phoenix suppression route?"

Yan answered without embellishment. "With Song's shield and Luo's line calls."

Yin's mouth curved. "Good answer. Remember it. Shrek values monsters, but it also values whether a monster knows why he survived."

Song immediately whispered, "We survived because I am broad-shouldered and tragic."

Luo said, "We survived because you stood where I told you."

"And because I am broad-shouldered."

Yan's lips moved by a fraction. "Rest."

Song looked wronged but obeyed the spirit of the order by leaning against the wall less dramatically.

Ji Hongbin said, "Enough. Qian Lei, Liu Feng, return inside. Watch Xuanyu. Do not disturb him. Yan Shuo, you do not need to stand here all day."

Yan looked at Dorm333's door.

"If students gather, I will disperse them."

"Teachers can do that."

"Students argue less with me when they know I can make them run laps later."

Mu Zhongtian sighed. "That is unfortunately true."

Ji studied Yan for half a breath. "Then rotate. Do not neglect your own recovery. Second place also needs to remain alive for the next round."

"Yes, Teacher."

Qian and Liu returned to Dorm333.

The door closed.

Lan Xuanyu still had not moved.


Waiting felt different after the rankings.

Before, Qian Lei had been worried because their captain had not woken. After, he was worried because their captain had not woken and was now champion of the Heaven Luo qualifier and needed to wake up to enjoy being champion properly.

"Do you think he can hear us?" Qian whispered.

Liu Feng sat near the door with his spear across his knees again. "Do not test it."

"I only want to know if I should congratulate him now or later."

"Later."

"What if deep meditation records emotional support?"

"Then silence is support."

Qian considered this.

"You are becoming very dangerous with short sentences."

Liu closed his eyes.

Qian sat for a while, failed to meditate, stood, sat again, opened the food box he had brought earlier, closed it, opened it again, and finally whispered toward Lan, "If you do not wake before this bun becomes cold, I cannot guarantee its safety."

Lan did not respond.

Qian sighed and put the bun down.

"A captain who makes subordinates loyal after rejecting Shrek admission should at least wake up to supervise their sacrifices," he murmured.

Liu did not open his eyes. "He will wake."

The certainty in his voice steadied the room.

Qian stopped fidgeting for a while.

Outside, footsteps came and went at intervals. Most stopped before the door and then moved away. Sometimes a teacher's voice sounded. Sometimes Yan Shuo's quieter voice. Once Song Yichen whispered loudly enough to be heard through the wall, "If he wakes, tell him second place sends formal pressure," and Luo immediately dragged him away before Qian could answer.

The day lengthened.

Lan remained still.

At noon, Mu Zhongtian came by again, checked from the doorway without stepping too close, and left after confirming nothing had changed. In the afternoon, Ji Hongbin came. He stood for longer, eyes half closed, then nodded and left. Near evening, Yin Tianfan came with food and threatened to eat it all himself if Lan did not wake soon.

Lan did not wake.

Yin left the food anyway.

Qian watched the door close behind him and whispered, "The vice president's threats lack follow-through when it comes to Xuanyu."

"Good," Liu said.

"Yes, good. But if he threatens me, he follows through immediately. This is favoritism."

"You talk more."

Qian opened his mouth, thought about it, and decided silence might be safer.

Night fell again.

The academy quieted.

Liu Feng meditated in the chair for a while, then shifted to his bed only after Qian promised not to touch Lan, shout at Lan, feed Lan, shake Lan, or attempt to determine whether champion luck could be transferred by proximity.

"That last one was very specific," Qian said.

"I know you."

"You wound me."

"Sleep."

Qian slept.

Liu meditated.

Lan sat in the center of the quiet room, the faint light under his skin moving more clearly than before.


When Lan Xuanyu's awareness returned, it did not return all at once.

At first, there was only freshness.

Not the freshness after sleep, when the body slowly remembers itself and the mind climbs out of dreams. This was sharper, cleaner, like breathing after rain had washed dust from every leaf in the world. His body felt light and full at the same time. The fatigue that had buried itself in his bones after the qualifier had vanished as if someone had opened hidden windows throughout him and let air pass through.

He did not open his eyes.

He did not need to.

The dormitory existed around him with impossible clarity.

Qian Lei lay sprawled on his bed, one arm hanging over the side, his breathing uneven in the familiar rhythm of a person dreaming about something he would later deny. Liu Feng sat cross-legged on his bed, actually meditating, soul power moving in a thinner, sharper line that reminded Lan of a spear drawn through mist.

The blanket at Lan's side had a fold in it.

The food box on the table had cooled.

Dust floated near the window.

A faint sound came from the corridor: two students whispering, then falling silent when another presence passed. A teacher's footsteps farther away. A door closing. The soft, repeated pulse of the academy building's night systems.

Lan's thoughts extended outward.

Not far.

Not truly far.

But farther than before.

It was as if his mind had gained another sense, one that did not replace sight or hearing but slipped between them. The room's shape, the corridor's stillness, the positions of familiar life auras, the faint circulation of soul power — all of it appeared inside his perception as if drawn by invisible lines.

He looked inward.

Gold and silver moved through his body.

His meridians seemed clearer than they had ever been. His bones carried faint colour in his perception, not exactly light but distinction: warm gold, cool silver, places where the two brushed and then separated again. The tiny contacts between them were no longer frightening sparks. They were junctions. Dangerous, yes, but not chaotic while his mind remained calm.

The vortex at his core turned naturally.

Lan finally understood.

Spirit Sea.

His Spiritual Power had crossed the boundary.

Four hundred ninety-nine had been a locked gate.

Now the gate had opened, and beyond it was not merely a number higher than five hundred. It was a change in how the world reached him.

Lan opened his eyes.

In the dark room, two faint flashes seemed to pass through his pupils.

Qian Lei's eyes snapped open at once.

For one breath, he stared at Lan as if seeing a ghost.

Then his face exploded into joy.

"You're awake!"

Liu Feng opened his eyes almost at the same moment and stood so quickly his spear nearly slipped from his knees.

"Xuanyu."

Lan blinked, adjusting to ordinary sight again.

"How long?"

"Too long," Qian said immediately. "Unreasonably long. Emotionally damaging long. You owe me at least two buns and one explanation."

Liu answered properly. "Almost two days from when you first sat down. You entered deep meditation. Teacher Ji said not to disturb you."

"Deep meditation," Lan repeated softly.

The words felt strange.

He had not tried to enter anything special. He had only sat down, reviewed the qualifier, followed the pressure inward, and then the world had opened.

Qian climbed off his bed, suddenly unable to stand still.

"And that is not even the biggest news."

Lan looked at him.

Qian drew himself up, placed one hand over his chest, and spoke with a solemnity that lasted almost three full words.

"We are champions."

Lan stared.

"Champion?"

"Champion!" Qian's whisper was so fierce it almost became a shout before he remembered the hour and clapped both hands over his mouth. He lowered his voice. "Heaven Luo qualifier champion. Lan Xuanyu, Qian Lei, Liu Feng. Total score: twenty thousand three hundred forty-one."

Lan's eyes widened.

He had expected their ranking to be good.

He had not expected that.

Liu Feng's lips curved faintly. "The third-place team had one thousand two hundred thirty-one."

Lan was silent.

Then he understood the score.

The Ground Fire Scarlet Dragons.

The final weak-point kills.

Shrek had counted them.

Qian leaned closer. "Do you know what else?"

Lan glanced at him. "What?"

"Yan Shuo's team is second."

This time Lan's expression changed differently.

Not shock alone.

Recognition.

"Dorm336?"

"Second," Liu confirmed. "Nine thousand eight hundred forty-six."

Lan let out a slow breath.

Second.

That meant Shrek had not merely recorded them as surviving. It meant the fissure route, Yan's Fire Phoenix pressure, Song's shield line, Luo's perception, and the team-choice test had all been counted at a level the whole planet could see.

Good, Lan thought.

The word came quietly, but with force.

Qian peered at him. "You look less surprised by their second place than our champion."

"Their route was real," Lan said.

Qian considered that, then nodded. "Yes. Also terrifying. Song told me to pass on formal pressure if you woke. I do not know what that means, but he looked injured enough to be sincere."

Lan smiled.

Liu added, "Yan kept the corridor quiet for a long time after we went for teachers."

Lan looked toward the door.

The corridor beyond it was dark now, but his newly widened senses could almost remember the shape of someone standing there, controlled and warm and dangerous in restraint.

"I see," he said.

Qian narrowed his eyes. "That sounded like not enough words."

Lan turned back. "Thank you for guarding me. Both of you."

Qian's suspicion vanished instantly. "That is acceptable. Continue."

"No."

"Why does your praise always stop too early?"

Liu asked, "How do you feel?"

Lan flexed his fingers.

The world remained too clear.

"Very good," he said. "My Spiritual Power should have broken through. I can sense much farther and more finely than before. My body feels... cleaner."

Qian's eyes brightened. "Spirit Sea?"

"Probably."

"We need to test it. Also eat. Mostly eat. You have meditated for almost two days. If you become too hungry, your captain judgment may decline."

Lan touched his stomach.

As if reminded it existed, hunger rose with shocking speed.

Qian saw his expression and clapped his hands. "Excellent. I recognize that look. That is the look of a man who needs the special mess."

Liu said, "We should tell Teacher Yin or Teacher Ji first."

"We can do both. Teacher Yin has food."

Lan tried to stand and discovered that although his fatigue was gone, his body was not used to the new clarity. For a moment the room seemed to arrive too strongly: the bed's edge, the floor, Qian's movement, Liu's soul power, the corridor beyond the door. He steadied himself.

Liu moved half a step closer.

Qian also reached out, then stopped before touching him without permission.

Lan smiled faintly. "I'm fine. Just not used to it."

Qian nodded wisely. "Breakthrough problems. I look forward to having them."

They left the dorm quietly.

The corridor was emptier at night, but not entirely empty. A student from another room cracked his door open and immediately froze when he saw Lan standing.

"Lan Xuanyu woke," the boy whispered.

Another door opened.

Then another.

Liu Feng's expression sharpened.

Before he could speak, a calm voice came from farther down the corridor.

"Back inside. He just woke from cultivation. Do not crowd him."

Yan Shuo stepped out from the shadow near the stairwell.

He had clearly not been standing there all night. His hair was slightly loose from rest, and his uniform jacket was thrown over his shoulders rather than perfectly arranged. But the moment he spoke, the corridor remembered he was class monitor.

Doors began closing.

Qian whispered, "Your authority is convenient."

Yan ignored him and looked at Lan.

For one breath, neither spoke.

Lan could sense more now. Not secrets. Not thoughts. But the controlled rhythm of Yan's breath, the warmth held carefully behind a calm exterior, the sharp restraint that did not want to become public concern and could not help being concern anyway.

"You woke," Yan said.

"I woke," Lan answered.

"Spirit Sea?"

"I think so."

Yan's eyes softened almost imperceptibly. "Good."

Qian immediately inserted, "We are champions. You are second. We have established mutual expensiveness. Please congratulate us again in the morning when my hair is arranged for history."

Yan glanced at him. "Your hair may not survive history."

"Hostile."

Lan said, "Congratulations. Second place across Heaven Luo Planet."

Yan looked back at him.

"Congratulations. Champion."

The words were simple.

The corridor was too public for anything else.

Still, Lan felt the exchange settle somewhere warm.

Yan did not ask how they had earned the score.

Lan did not ask how much Yan had paid for the fissure route.

Not here.

Not yet.

"Teacher Yin?" Yan asked.

"Food first," Qian said.

Liu corrected, "Teacher first. Then food."

Yan stepped aside. "Go. I'll inform Teacher Mu that you woke."

Lan nodded. "Thank you. For the corridor too."

Yan's gaze held his for half a breath.

"Rest properly after eating."

Qian leaned toward Liu. "That sounded like an order."

"It was an order," Liu said.

Lan smiled and followed them down the corridor.

Behind him, Yan watched until the stairwell turned them from sight.

Then he went to inform the teachers.

No flames appeared.

No secrets were spoken.

But the class monitor's shoulders loosened for the first time that night.


Yin Tianfan received them as if he had been waiting with food, a testing device, and an argument already prepared.

"Awake?" he demanded the moment Lan entered.

"Yes, Teacher."

"Hungry?"

"Very."

"Good. Only living students are hungry. Sit. Eat first. Test after the first bowl. If you faint into my table, Old Ji will blame me for feeding you too slowly."

Qian Lei clasped his hands. "Vice President, do guardians of a deep-meditating champion receive food too?"

Yin pointed to the side table. "Eat. If your mouth slows, consider it cultivation."

Qian immediately obeyed.

Lan ate faster than he expected. The food was richer than ordinary canteen meals, filled with warm energy that spread through his body without heaviness. After the second bowl, he realized Yin was watching him with satisfaction rather than surprise.

"The academy changed the supply?" Lan asked.

Mu Zhongtian, who had arrived shortly after Yan's message, nodded. "Because of the qualifier results. The academy has decided to increase nourishment and training-resource support for the shortlisted Elite Junior Class teams."

"Five teams?" Qian asked, remembering the earlier lower ranks.

Mu corrected him, and there was pride in the correction. "Six. In this continuity, six of our teams entered the top hundred. Jin Xiang's team barely made it at ninety-ninth. Two more teams placed in the middle ranks. Lu Qianxun's team is ninth. Yan Shuo's team is second. Yours is champion."

Qian froze with his chopsticks halfway to his mouth.

"Six teams," he said. "Does that mean six times the glory or six times the competition for resources?"

"It means six teams need to stop embarrassing me before the next round," Mu said.

"That was not one of the options."

Lan listened quietly.

Six teams.

That mattered more than the number first suggested. If even Jin Xiang's early-eliminated team ranked ninety-ninth, the qualifier had eliminated enormous numbers quickly. Heaven Luo Academy's Elite Junior Class had not only produced a champion. It had placed a cluster of students into the next stage.

That would change the academy's expectations.

It would also change pressure from outside.

Yin Tianfan pushed a compact Spiritual Power testing device toward Lan after the third bowl.

"Enough. Test. Then eat again."

Lan placed his hand on the device.

The familiar process felt different now. Before, he had always reached toward the measurement as if extending a line from inside himself. This time the line was already there. His Spiritual Power moved smoothly, filling the device's sensing pattern without forcing.

Numbers climbed.

They crossed five hundred.

Stopped.

505.

Five hundred and five.

Mu Zhongtian inhaled.

Yin Tianfan laughed. "Spirit Sea!"

Ji Hongbin had entered at some point without making much sound. He looked at the number, then at Lan.

"Stabilized just above the threshold," Ji said. "Good. Do not get arrogant. Spirit Sea is a beginning, not a crown."

Qian Lei leaned toward Liu Feng and whispered, "Teacher Ji can turn any miracle into homework."

Ji looked at him.

Qian immediately straightened. "A beginning. Very educational."

Lan looked at the number again.

505.

It was only six points above the threshold.

But he could feel how different those six points were from the last step before the gate. His mind had more room now. His elemental control should become finer. His support to Qian and Liu might become steadier. His ability to sense danger, read battlefields, and coordinate timing would improve.

For him, whose Soul Power cultivation remained slow, this was not a small change.

Then he thought of Yan Shuo's SP962.

The comparison did not make him frustrated.

It made the world feel wider.

Yan had reached Spirit Sea earlier and climbed high in it through Fire Phoenix foundation, soul-spirit compatibility, and terrifying adaptation. Lan had opened his own sea through a different gate: pressure, bloodline balance, and command crisis.

Different roads.

Both real.

Yin Tianfan saw Lan's calm and looked pleased.

"Now," he said, leaning forward, "tell us how you scored twenty thousand points."

The room changed.

Qian's chopsticks stopped.

Liu Feng lowered his bowl.

Mu Zhongtian's expression sharpened.

Ji Hongbin crossed his arms.

Lan looked at the four of them.

This was not the corridor.

This was not a crowd of classmates.

These were his teachers. The details affected safety, training, and future preparation. Hiding everything from them would be foolish.

So Lan told them.

Not loudly.

Not dramatically.

He began from the false magma pool, then moved to the pressure beneath the Dragon's body, the rising heat, the two Ground Fire Scarlet Dragons injuring each other, the timing window, and Qian Lei's second soul skill. He explained that Qian's Replicate had preserved the previous Emerald Demon Bird summon imprint, allowing an instant call without the usual uncertainty. He explained the danger: the bird was not obedient in any normal sense, only pointed through timing and opportunity. He described how he had judged the eyes as the best weak point, and how the Emerald Demon Bird's speed and killing instinct had turned the two injured Dragons' own openings into death.

Qian Lei looked increasingly proud and increasingly nervous as the explanation continued.

Liu Feng remained silent, adding a few precise details only when Lan glanced at him: position, timing, how long the opening lasted, how close the shock wave came to crushing their shelter.

Lan also mentioned what he had safely inferred about Dorm336's route.

Not secrets Yan had not told him.

Only public facts and battlefield logic: that Yan's Fire Phoenix pressure had affected the Dragons' fire rhythm, that Dorm336's survival could not be explained by hiding alone, that Song and Luo's positions showed team-route geometry rather than luck.

Ji listened without interrupting.

Yin listened with shining eyes.

Mu Zhongtian listened as if every sentence was both blessing and nightmare.

When Lan finished, the room stayed silent for several breaths.

Yin Tianfan was the first to move.

He slapped Lan on the shoulder hard enough that Lan nearly spilled his tea.

"Good! Very good! You are indeed my student. Calm, adaptable, and ruthless toward the problem instead of the people. That is command. That is how a battlefield is read. You used the environment, enemy conflict, team ability, and one instant of opportunity. Excellent!"

Qian Lei straightened proudly as if the praise had included his entire ancestry.

Ji Hongbin's face darkened.

"Shortcut," he said.

Yin's head snapped toward him. "What did you say?"

"I said shortcut." Ji looked at Lan. "The score came from a special interaction: environment, two heavily injured targets, Qian Lei's rare summon, and a weak-point kill. It is brilliant, but it is not a replacement for individual cultivation. Do not become intoxicated by numbers. Your Soul Power is still Rank20. Your body still has limits. Your control still has gaps. If you think a score means you can neglect foundation, Shrek will teach you painfully."

Lan lowered his head. "Yes, Teacher Ji."

Yin Tianfan slapped the table. "Old Ji, can you not pour cold water the moment a child does something beautiful? This is not nonsense. This is battlefield intelligence. Shrek's assessment tested adaptability, courage, and judgment. He passed so well that their score system screamed."

Ji said coldly, "And if the Emerald Demon Bird had turned on them?"

Qian raised a finger, then slowly lowered it.

Yin said, "Then we teach him to calculate risk better, not scold him for calculating at all."

"I am not scolding calculation. I am scolding dependence on cleverness without foundation."

"You always say foundation as if the battlefield will politely wait for foundation to finish growing."

"And you always say battlefield as if rules, promises, and teammates are disposable pieces."

The room froze.

Qian Lei's eyes widened.

Liu Feng looked between the two teachers.

Lan's heart moved slightly.

There was something under Ji Hongbin's words.

Yin Tianfan's expression changed too. The excitement dimmed, replaced by something old and sharp.

"No need to taunt me," Yin said.

His voice was lower now.

Mu Zhongtian seemed to regret all life choices that had placed him in the same room as both of them.

Yin leaned back, but the heaviness did not leave his eyes.

"Yes, I was expelled. I did not graduate. So what? Later I still relied on my own ability and commanded a fleet. I studied in Shrek's Space Command System for years before that happened. Special circumstances, Old Ji. You know that."

Ji's brows drew together.

"I know that incident was not entirely your fault," he said. "Your personal battle power was never your strength, but your command talent was real. At that time, many of us thought you would enter the inner court. If you graduated from the inner court's Space Command System, you had a real chance to become a Federal fleet commander."

Yin waved him off, but the motion lacked its usual careless energy.

"Stop. It has been decided for years. Talking about it does not change it."

Lan looked at his teacher.

For the first time since he had known Yin Tianfan, the vice president seemed older.

Not in body.

In memory.

Yin's mouth twisted.

"Xuanyu," he said, "since the topic reached here, I should tell you why I was thrown out. You can learn from it. A commander who only learns victories will eventually die from the lesson he refused."

Qian Lei immediately leaned forward.

Yin's eyes cut toward him.

"What are you two doing? Planning to listen to this old man's embarrassing past for free?"

Qian froze.

Liu Feng sat straighter.

Yin pointed at the door. "Old Ji, they are in Xuanyu's team. Shouldn't they train?"

Ji Hongbin looked at Qian and Liu.

"Both of you, follow me."

Qian's eyes widened first. "Wait. Teacher Ji, Vice President... from what you just said, both of you were Shrek students before?"

For a blink, the room became too quiet.

Then Qian's face filled with betrayal. "But Teacher, history is educational."

"So is training."

"Training is often painful."

"So is history. Move."

Liu Feng stood at once.

Qian looked from Lan to Yin to the food still on the table.

Yin said, "Take a bun. Then leave."

Qian grabbed two.

Ji Hongbin's gaze moved.

Qian put one back with heartbreaking slowness.

Liu Feng gave Lan one last look before leaving. "Rest after this."

Lan nodded.

The door closed behind them.

Mu Zhongtian hesitated.

Yin looked at him.

"You too. Unless you want to hear an old wound and then pretend not to know it."

Mu Zhongtian sighed softly. "Call me if you need anything."

He left as well.

For a moment, only Lan Xuanyu and Yin Tianfan remained in the room.

The night outside the window was quiet.

Yin Tianfan looked at the half-empty bowls, the testing device still showing 505, and the student who had become champion while still looking too young to carry the number properly.

"Speaking of it," Yin said at last, "I am still not fully reconciled."

Lan did not interrupt.

The vice president's voice lost its joking edge.

"Old Ji was right about one thing. Back then, I was determined to enter the inner court. I was confident. Too confident. When I first entered Shrek, I was in the Space Command System. I had excellent results, and I was not this fat then."

Lan's mouth twitched despite himself.

Yin noticed. "What? You doubt your master was handsome?"

"No, Teacher."

"Your pause says otherwise."

"I was controlling my respect."

Yin snorted.

For a moment, the old liveliness returned. Then it faded again.

"I was proud. Many people praised my command talent. Some teachers liked my style; some hated it. I enjoyed strange tactics, surprise attacks, using the least cost for the largest victory. I thought as long as I won, the path was correct. If the battlefield was ugly, then tactics should be ugly enough to survive it."

Lan listened carefully.

He could feel, with his newly opened Spiritual Power, the shifts in Yin's aura: regret under pride, anger under humor, and something like shame buried so deep it had become part of the man's bones.

Yin looked at him.

"You understand that temptation, don't you?"

Lan did not answer quickly.

He thought of false magma.

Of hiding beneath a Dragon.

Of using two beasts' injuries against them.

Of telling Qian to target the eyes.

"A little," he said.

Yin nodded.

"Good. Then listen better than I did."

He leaned back and began.

"It was the final exam of the sixth semester..."
