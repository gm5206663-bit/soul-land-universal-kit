# Chapter 46 — The Sea Opens Before the Crown

The corridor outside Dorm333 was quiet enough that Qian Lei could hear his own breathing.

That made him nervous.

He had spent the previous night proving that, when forced to choose between certain Shrek glory and his friends, he could be noble. He had also spent the same night proving that nobility did not improve a person's patience, hunger, or ability to handle a sleeping captain who refused to wake up after an entire day and night of meditation.

Lan Xuanyu still sat inside the room.

Unmoving.

Breathing evenly.

Covered by that faint, dignified fluctuation of soul power and something deeper that Qian Lei could not name.

Liu Feng closed Dorm333's door with both hands, slowly enough that the latch did not click.

Qian looked at him.

Liu looked back.

For once, neither of them immediately spoke.

The morning sun had only just begun to touch the dormitory corridor. Most students were either asleep, exhausted from yesterday's qualifier, or dragging themselves toward the canteen with the grim courage of people who had discovered that Shrek Academy's preliminary tests could make breakfast feel like a reward for surviving war.

Qian lifted one finger and pointed down the corridor.

Teacher.

Liu nodded.

Qian pointed back at the door.

Do not disturb.

Liu nodded again.

Qian pointed at his own stomach.

Breakfast?

Liu's expression did not change.

No.

Qian's face collapsed silently.

They had barely taken three steps when another door opened not far away.

Yan Shuo stepped out.

He was already in uniform.

That was unreasonable. Anyone who had survived the qualifier yesterday should have looked like Qian felt: crushed, wrinkled, and morally deserving of porridge. Yan's uniform was neat, his posture controlled, and the copper-red of his hair had been restrained into a deeper shade under academy light. Yet exhaustion still showed in the thinness at the corners of his eyes and the careful way his left shoulder did not fully relax.

Behind him, Song Yichen's voice drifted from the room, rough with sleep. "If that is breakfast, bring three. If it is a teacher, pretend we died honorably."

Luo Haoran said, "We did not die."

"That is why I said pretend."

Yan ignored them both and looked at Qian and Liu.

His gaze sharpened immediately.

"What happened?"

Qian opened his mouth, remembered the silent corridor, and closed it again with great pain.

Liu answered quietly, "Xuanyu hasn't woken up."

Yan's expression changed so slightly that most classmates would have missed it.

Qian did not.

Lan probably would not have.

The line of Yan's shoulders tightened, and for half a breath the air around him seemed to remember heat without releasing it.

"Since last night?" Yan asked.

"Since meditation after we returned," Liu said. "A day and a night. His life force is stable. Soul power fluctuations are stable. But he isn't waking. We're getting Teacher Mu."

Yan glanced at Dorm333's closed door.

He did not move toward it.

That restraint mattered.

Publicly he was a boy, class monitor, and the strongest visible student in the class. He had every public reason to be concerned. He also had enough discipline not to barge into another team's room and disturb a state he did not understand.

"Go," he said. "I'll keep the corridor quiet. If anyone asks, he is cultivating and not to be disturbed."

Qian blinked. "Class monitor, your tone makes me feel like I should also not disturb myself."

"Correct."

"That was not an invitation to agree."

Liu was already moving. Qian hurried after him, but he looked back once.

Yan had stepped to the side of the corridor, not blocking Dorm333's door, not drawing attention, simply standing close enough that any sleepy, curious, loud student would meet the class monitor before reaching it.

For some reason, that made Qian breathe a little easier.


Mu Zhongtian was in the office corridor when they found him.

He had not slept well.

None of the teachers had. The qualifier's first round had been far too fierce. Too many teams had been thrown out quickly. Too many students had returned pale, angry, silent, or shaking. Heaven Luo Academy had spent years and resources building the Elite Junior Class, and the first preliminary Shrek test had still nearly torn the class apart before any official ranking appeared.

Mu Zhongtian was speaking with Ji Hongbin when Qian and Liu arrived.

That saved time.

"Teacher Mu," Liu Feng said, lowering his voice out of habit even though they were not near the dorm anymore.

Qian added, "Teacher Ji. Something might be wrong with Xuanyu."

Ji Hongbin's expression changed at once.

The lazy sternness vanished.

"What do you mean?"

"He started meditating after we returned yesterday," Liu said quickly. "He did not wake for dinner. He did not wake through the night. This morning he is still sitting there. His breathing is stable, and there are faint soul-power fluctuations, but we did not dare touch him."

Mu Zhongtian's face tightened.

Before he could speak, Ji Hongbin had already turned.

"Lead the way."

Qian and Liu ran.

Mu Zhongtian followed close behind, his anxiety showing more openly than Ji Hongbin's. Ji did not look anxious on the surface, but that only made him more frightening. His steps were controlled. His eyes were focused. He was the kind of teacher who became calmer the more serious things were.

When they reached the dormitory corridor, Yan Shuo was still there.

Two younger students from another room stood a few steps away, clearly having been turned back without making noise. One held a breakfast bun in midair as if unsure whether chewing would violate the class monitor's order.

Yan saw Ji Hongbin and Mu Zhongtian and stepped aside immediately.

"Teacher Ji. Teacher Mu. No disturbance."

Ji nodded once.

"Good."

That single word made the two bun-holding students stand straighter than a lecture would have.

Liu opened Dorm333's door.

Everyone entered except Yan.

He remained outside until Mu Zhongtian turned his head and said softly, "Yan Shuo, keep the corridor clear. No one comes in unless I call."

"Yes, Teacher."

The door closed behind them.

Inside, the room was filled with morning light.

Lan Xuanyu sat on his bed in the same posture he had held through the night. His hands rested naturally on his knees. His expression was peaceful, almost empty. Under his skin, a faint light circulated with slow rhythm, not bright enough to be called radiance, but clear enough that no one could mistake it for ordinary sleep.

Ji Hongbin lifted one hand.

Qian Lei, who had been about to whisper something, swallowed the words so hard his throat clicked.

Ji's gaze moved over Lan's body, his breathing, the small fluctuations in the air, the barely visible pulse of soul power around him.

Then Ji Hongbin slowly closed his eyes.

The room changed.

Qian Lei could not explain how.

Teacher Ji was still standing there. His robes did not move. His breathing did not vanish. His body remained in exactly the same place.

But for one impossible moment, Qian felt as if Ji Hongbin was no longer a person inside the room.

He was part of the room.

No.

Part of something wider.

The bed, the wall, the dust floating in the morning light, Lan Xuanyu's breath, the subtle movement of soul power, even Qian's own heartbeat seemed to fall under a vast stillness that did not suppress them but included them.

Liu Feng's pupils contracted.

Mu Zhongtian murmured, almost involuntarily, "Unity of Heaven and Man."

His voice held envy that he did not bother to hide.

Qian wanted to ask what that meant.

He did not dare.

Ji Hongbin stood that way for a long time.

Maybe it was only a few breaths.

Maybe it was half an hour.

Under that strange stillness, time felt unwilling to report itself honestly.

Finally, Ji opened his eyes.

He did not speak in the room.

Instead, he turned and gestured for everyone except Lan to leave.

They obeyed.

Even Qian Lei, who normally had an opinion about every arrangement in the world, moved as quietly as a thief in front of his own conscience.

The door closed again.

In the corridor, Yan Shuo looked at them but did not ask first.

Ji Hongbin's expression eased.

"Good news," he said. "Deep meditation."

Qian Lei stared at him.

"Deep... meditation?"

Mu Zhongtian let out a breath that sounded as if half his soul had returned to his body. "Are you certain?"

Ji gave him a look.

Mu Zhongtian immediately coughed. "Of course you're certain. I only meant—"

"He is in deep meditation," Ji said. "His body and mind have entered an optimal state. Soul Power and Spiritual Power are circulating together. Do not disturb him."

Qian's knees almost softened.

"So he isn't in danger?"

"Not if no fool shakes him awake," Ji said.

Qian clasped both hands behind his back. "I have never shaken anyone in my life."

Liu Feng looked at him.

Qian whispered, "Not recently."

Ji ignored that.

Mu Zhongtian's eyes were bright now. "Deep meditation at his age..."

"Rare," Ji said. "Very rare. Many Soul Masters never experience it once. Those who do often find it easier to touch that state again later. It improves cultivation, perception, and breakthrough comprehension. The higher the cultivation, the more meaningful it becomes, but even for Xuanyu now, the benefits will not be small."

Liu Feng asked, "Was it because of yesterday's qualifier?"

"Partly," Ji said.

His eyes flicked once toward Yan Shuo, who stood quietly at the side of the corridor.

"Pressure can break people. Sometimes, when the foundation is ready, pressure opens them instead. Xuanyu's previous Spiritual Power test was four hundred ninety-nine. That number is not ordinary. It is the edge before Spirit Sea. Yesterday's qualifier pushed his mind, body, bloodline, and judgment through continuous crisis. The stimulation was enough. He is breaking through."

"Spirit Sea," Liu Feng murmured.

Qian Lei's eyes widened. "Then Xuanyu will also enter Spirit Sea?"

Also.

The word slipped out naturally.

Everyone nearby understood why.

Yan Shuo's SP962 Spirit Sea result had already been one of the public shocks of the preliminary evaluation. It remained absurd. It remained far ahead by number. No one standing in that corridor could pretend otherwise.

Ji Hongbin did not allow the comparison to turn shallow.

"Do not measure monsters with one ruler," he said. "Yan Shuo's Spirit Sea is already publicly open and stable at an extremely high level for this age. Xuanyu is breaking through from the edge at Rank20 after a hidden-pressure battle. One is a blazing sea already visible; the other is a sealed gate opening in a place where even he may not understand the key. Both matter. They matter differently."

Yan lowered his eyes for half a breath.

He did not look proud.

He did not look diminished.

He only looked at the closed door.

"Will he wake soon?" he asked.

The question was careful.

Controlled.

But Lan Xuanyu's name did not need to be spoken for the concern to be obvious.

Ji Hongbin heard it.

So did Qian Lei.

"His first deep meditation should not last too long," Ji said. "But no one can force the exact time. Let him finish naturally."

Yan nodded.

Mu Zhongtian turned to Qian and Liu. "You two don't need to attend class today. Stay here and watch him. No one disturbs him until he wakes. If anything changes, call us immediately."

"Yes, Teacher," Liu said.

Qian nodded so hard his hair bounced. "Yes, Teacher. I will guard him with my life, my future fortune, and any breakfast I have not yet eaten."

Mu Zhongtian stared at him.

"Eat outside the room. Quietly."

"Understood."

Ji added, "When Xuanyu wakes, ask him how it felt. It may help both of you if you ever approach this state later."

Qian's expression became solemn. "If deep meditation can compensate me for yesterday's emotional loss, I am willing."

Liu said, "Deep meditation requires silence."

"Then the world is biased against me."

Yan said, "That was already known."

Qian looked wounded. "Class monitor, yesterday you understood my nobility."

"Nobility does not require constant narration."

"Mine does."

For the first time that morning, Mu Zhongtian smiled.

It was small and tired, but it was a smile.

Ji Hongbin did not smile.

He looked once more at Dorm333's closed door.

Then he turned.

"Come. The rankings should be released soon."

The word rankings pulled everyone's attention tight again.

Yan's gaze sharpened.

Qian Lei swallowed.

Liu Feng's fingers curled at his side.

They had survived.

They had returned.

They had been tested.

But Shrek Academy did not let effort remain vague for long.

Soon, numbers would decide who moved forward.


The office screen was already connected to the Heaven Luo qualifier result system when Ji Hongbin and Mu Zhongtian returned.

Several teachers stood nearby, pretending they were not staring at the screen every few breaths. None of them pretended very well.

The Elite Junior Class had been created because Heaven Luo Academy had hope.

Hope was expensive.

Hope required resources, teachers, time, reputation, and the willingness to place children under pressure that ordinary students would never touch. Yesterday's qualifier had shown exactly why Shrek Academy was Shrek Academy. Individual strength, luck, courage, judgment, adaptability, team trust, concealment, crisis handling, terrain analysis, and even moral choices had been compressed into one brutal assessment.

A student could be strong and still fail.

A student could be clever and still die in the wrong second.

A student could survive only because a teammate chose not to abandon them.

Mu Zhongtian rubbed his hands together without realizing it.

Ji Hongbin noticed and did not comment.

He was not calmer because he cared less. He was calmer because he had spent too many years learning that anxiety did not improve a result after the result was already written.

But even Ji Hongbin's eyes did not leave the screen.

The first row appeared.

Ninety-first to one hundredth.

Names.

Schools.

Scores.

Mu Zhongtian leaned forward so quickly the chair beneath him creaked.

Then his eyes locked on one line.

"Ninety-ninth place," he read aloud, voice rising despite himself. "Jin Xiang, Tang Yuege, Li Pengbin. Heaven Luo Academy. Total score: thirty-nine."

For a breath, the office was silent.

Then Mu Zhongtian nearly laughed.

"They got in! Jin Xiang's team got in!"

A teacher beside him exhaled loudly. "They were thrown out so early."

"Exactly!" Mu Zhongtian said. "If they still made the top hundred, then the teams that lasted longer—"

"Do not celebrate too early," Ji Hongbin said.

Mu Zhongtian's grin did not disappear. "Teacher Ji, if you forbid celebration at ninety-ninth place, you are colder than the simulation system."

"I forbid foolish conclusions. Celebration can wait until someone truly enters Shrek. This is only the qualifier."

"Only the qualifier," Mu muttered, but his eyes were bright. "Only the qualifier that eliminated most of Heaven Luo Planet."

The list moved.

Eighty-first to ninetieth.

No familiar names.

That was not bad news.

It meant the remaining familiar names were higher.

Seventy-first to eightieth.

Another Heaven Luo team appeared, one of the teams that had survived longer in the lower valley before being forced out by the second-phase pressure.

Seventy-fifth.

The office stirred.

Sixty-first to seventieth.

Another familiar group.

Sixty-seventh.

Mu Zhongtian's smile returned, broader now. "Three teams. Jin Xiang, seventy-fifth, sixty-seventh. And Lu Qianxun's team hasn't appeared. Lan Xuanyu's team hasn't appeared. Yan Shuo's team hasn't appeared."

A teacher whispered, "Six teams?"

No one answered immediately.

Six teams from Heaven Luo Academy's Elite Junior Class in the top hundred of the planet would already be enough to report upward with confidence.

But at that moment, none of them wanted only confidence.

The names continued to roll.

Fifty-first to sixtieth.

No Lan.

No Lu.

No Yan.

Forty-first to fiftieth.

No.

Thirty-first to fortieth.

Still no.

Mu Zhongtian stopped smiling.

Not because he was unhappy.

Because hope had grown too large to be held by a smile.

"Top thirty," he said softly.

Ji Hongbin sat straighter.

Twenty-first to thirtieth.

The list appeared.

No familiar names.

Mu Zhongtian's hand struck the table.

"Top twenty!"

His voice cracked slightly.

The other teachers did not laugh at him.

They were staring too.

The Elite Junior Class had been under pressure for years. Students complained. Parents questioned. Resources were consumed. Teachers endured. And yesterday, when the first teams were thrown out almost immediately, more than one person had felt ice settle in their stomach.

Now three names were still missing.

Lu Qianxun.

Yan Shuo.

Lan Xuanyu.

Eleventh to twentieth.

The list appeared.

No familiar names.

Mu Zhongtian jumped.

This time his head nearly struck the ceiling. He caught himself at the last second, both hands gripping the back of his chair.

"Top ten! Three teams in the top ten!"

His voice was no longer teacherly.

It was the voice of someone who had watched a building nearly collapse and then discovered its foundation had held deeper than anyone expected.

Ji Hongbin did not reprimand him.

His own hands had tightened on the edge of the table.

Top ten meant a different level of opportunity.

The top one hundred teams would continue to the next round on Heaven Luo Planet, but the later selection would be harsher. Only the strongest few would advance toward the Mother Planet and Shrek's true gates. A top-ten result did not guarantee entry. It did, however, mean the students had stepped into the zone where Shrek Academy could not casually ignore them.

The final list began to load.

Tenth to first.

Mu Zhongtian held his breath.

The first name he recognized appeared near the bottom of the final list.

Ninth place.

Lu Qianxun.

Ye Lingtong.

Chang Jianyi.

Heaven Luo Academy.

Total score: five hundred thirty-one.

"Ninth," Mu Zhongtian said. "Lu's team is ninth."

One of the teachers behind him whispered, "Five hundred thirty-one. For being eliminated before the end, that is already terrifying."

Ji nodded faintly.

"They carried real second-phase pressure. Golden Lion King center, direct combat, control support. Shrek counted more than survival time."

Mu Zhongtian's gaze had already moved upward.

Eighth.

Seventh.

Sixth.

Fifth.

Fourth.

No Lan.

No Yan.

The third-place line appeared.

A team from another academy.

Total score: one thousand two hundred thirty-one.

Under ordinary circumstances, that score would have been monstrous enough to dominate discussion for days.

Today, it became the comparison that made the next line impossible.

Second place.

Yan Shuo.

Song Yichen.

Luo Haoran.

Heaven Luo Academy.

Total score: nine thousand eight hundred forty-six.

The office went completely still.

Not quiet.

Still.

Even Mu Zhongtian did not speak at once.

Nine thousand eight hundred forty-six.

Nearly eight times the third-place score.

Far beyond Lu Qianxun's already excellent ninth place.

A score that should not exist for a team that, by all normal student logic, had no business remaining in a battlefield ruled by Ground Fire Scarlet Dragons.

Ji Hongbin's pupils narrowed.

"Shrek counted it," he said softly.

Mu Zhongtian swallowed. "The fissure route?"

"More than survival." Ji's voice was low. "Fire-dragon-type suppression, terrain use, shield-anchor geometry, sensory route reading, final hidden layer, and the team-choice probe. They counted the route."

Another teacher said, dazed, "Second in the planet."

No one corrected him.

Yan Shuo's public data had already been ridiculous: Rank39 before eleven, SP962 Spirit Sea, three purple rings, true Fire Phoenix, Ultimate Fire. But the ranking did something that records alone could not. It placed that power inside a team result and showed that Song and Luo's presence had not reduced it. They had made it usable under impossible conditions.

Second place was not a consolation.

It was a declaration.

Mu Zhongtian's eyes stung.

Then his gaze moved to the top line.

The golden crown icon appeared first.

For a moment, the words beside it seemed too bright.

Heaven Luo qualifier champion.

Lan Xuanyu.

Qian Lei.

Liu Feng.

Heaven Luo Academy.

Total score: twenty thousand three hundred forty-one.

No one spoke.

Mu Zhongtian's mouth opened.

Closed.

Opened again.

Ji Hongbin, who had remained steady through warship training reports, impossible student comparisons, and Yan Shuo's absurd scan data, stared at the number without blinking.

Twenty thousand three hundred forty-one.

First place.

Champion.

More than twice Dorm336's already unbelievable second-place score.

Nearly nineteen thousand points above the normal third-place score.

The other ninety-nine teams together, if judged by the visible distribution, might struggle to make the gap look reasonable.

Mu Zhongtian finally found his voice.

"Could there be a mistake?"

Ji Hongbin answered at once.

"Impossible. Shrek Academy would not make that kind of mistake."

"Then what did they do?" Mu Zhongtian said.

His voice had become hoarse.

"Yan Shuo's score I can at least begin to understand after yesterday's fragments. The teachers heard enough. Fire Phoenix pressure, final route, Shrek's probe. But twenty thousand? What did Dorm333 do inside after they disappeared?"

Ji Hongbin's eyes moved from the score to the closed office door, as if he could see across the academy to the boy sitting in deep meditation.

"Something worth more than survival," he said.

Mu Zhongtian stood up so fast the chair slid back.

"We have to ask them."

"Xuanyu cannot be disturbed."

"Qian Lei and Liu Feng can talk."

"Perhaps."

That word did not slow Mu down.

Ji Hongbin followed him.

The other teachers looked at the screen again, as if the rankings might vanish if not witnessed enough.

First.

Second.

Ninth.

Three Heaven Luo teams in the top ten.

Six Heaven Luo teams in the top hundred.

The Elite Junior Class had not merely survived embarrassment.

It had produced a result that would make the entire Heaven Luo Planet turn its head.


When Mu Zhongtian and Ji Hongbin reached the dormitory corridor again, Yan Shuo was still there.

He had not sat down.

Song Yichen and Luo Haoran now stood behind him, both looking considerably less neat. Song's hair was flattened on one side, his bandaged shoulder obvious beneath his uniform jacket. Luo looked as if he had woken from three dreams and trusted none of them.

Yan saw the teachers' faces.

He did not ask loudly.

"Results?" he said.

Mu Zhongtian stopped.

He had intended to go straight to Dorm333.

Then he looked at the three of them and realized how absurd that would be.

Second place was not something to throw over his shoulder while running past.

"Yan Shuo, Song Yichen, Luo Haoran," Mu said, voice still tight with excitement. "Heaven Luo qualifier. Second place. Total score: nine thousand eight hundred forty-six."

Song stared at him.

Luo's eyes widened.

Yan did not move for a moment.

Then Song said, very carefully, "Teacher Mu, I may still be asleep. Please tell me if this is an injury symptom."

"If it is," Luo murmured, "we are sharing it."

Yan lowered his eyes.

His fingers curled once, then relaxed.

Second.

Not a note.

Not an afterthought.

Not a sentence like they lasted long too.

Second in the entire Heaven Luo qualifier.

For half a breath, he saw again the red door in the fissure, Song's shield arm shaking under impact, Luo's voice calling the line through heat that made all lines lie, and his own flame thinning the Dragons' fire until their bodies still threatened but their instincts no longer ruled the field.

A team route.

Shrek had counted it.

"Thank you, Teacher," Yan said.

His voice was steady.

Song looked at him in disbelief. "That is all?"

Yan glanced sideways. "Should I scream?"

"A little. For morale."

Luo whispered, "Please do not. My head hurts."

Mu Zhongtian laughed once, unsteady with feeling.

"Celebrate later. Quietly. Xuanyu is still in deep meditation."

Yan's head lifted at once.

"Dorm333?"

Ji Hongbin answered. "Champion. Twenty thousand three hundred forty-one."

This time even Yan's restraint cracked.

His eyes widened.

Song made a sound that was not quite a word.

Luo went perfectly still, then said, "That is not survival scoring."

"No," Ji said. "It is not."

Yan looked at Dorm333's closed door.

The first emotion that crossed his face was not jealousy.

It was relief.

Then pride.

Then worry, because the champion of Heaven Luo Planet was currently unconscious in deep meditation and could not hear the result that would change the shape of the class.

Yan controlled all three emotions quickly, but not before Ji Hongbin saw them.

"Keep the corridor clear," Ji said. "The result will spread soon enough. Until official release reaches everyone, no shouting here."

Yan nodded. "Yes, Teacher."

Mu Zhongtian knocked softly on Dorm333's door.

Liu Feng opened it from inside almost immediately.

His face tightened when he saw their expressions.

"Teacher?"

"Come out," Mu said quietly. "Both of you. Let Xuanyu continue."

Qian Lei appeared behind Liu, hair still badly arranged and eyes instantly alert.

"Did something happen? Did Xuanyu wake? Did I miss breakfast?"

"Out," Liu whispered.

They stepped into the corridor and closed the door again.

Mu Zhongtian looked at them.

The words seemed to catch in his throat for a second.

Then he said, "Heaven Luo qualifier. Champion. Lan Xuanyu, Qian Lei, Liu Feng. Total score: twenty thousand three hundred forty-one."

Qian Lei froze.

Liu Feng froze too, but differently. His whole body went taut, like a spear drawn back before release.

Qian raised one finger and pointed at himself.

"Champion?"

"Champion," Mu said.

Qian pointed at Liu.

"Him?"

"Yes."

Qian pointed at the closed door.

"Xuanyu?"

"Yes."

Qian pointed at himself again, as if the first answer might have expired.

"Me?"

"Yes," Ji Hongbin said. "If you ask a fifth time, I will assign a written report on why champions should be able to count to one."

Qian's hand dropped.

His mouth trembled.

For a terrible moment, Liu Feng thought he might cry again.

Instead, Qian whispered, "My early admission died for a champion."

Liu Feng's eyes reddened at once.

He turned his head away.

Qian looked at him, then at the closed door, then at his own hands.

"We really..."

He did not finish.

He did not need to.

Yan stood a few steps away, and this time he did not interrupt the silence.

Some victories deserved to arrive before words.

Mu Zhongtian gave them only a few breaths.

Then the teacher in him returned.

"The second-place team only—" He stopped, corrected himself because this year was not canon's simpler board anymore. "The third-place team scored one thousand two hundred thirty-one. Yan Shuo's team is second at nine thousand eight hundred forty-six. You are first at twenty thousand three hundred forty-one. What exactly did you do in the last part?"

Qian Lei and Liu Feng looked at each other.

That look had already been decided the night before.

Liu Feng spoke first.

"Teacher, Xuanyu said not to reveal the last part before the later tests."

Qian nodded quickly, then seemed to remember dignity and slowed the nod into something solemn. "Yes. We discussed it. There are still subsequent rounds. We cannot expose ourselves too early."

Mu Zhongtian stared.

"I am your teacher."

Qian looked pained. "That is why this hurts me too."

Ji Hongbin's mouth moved almost imperceptibly.

It might have been the ghost of a smile.

Mu Zhongtian turned to Liu. "You too?"

Liu Feng lowered his head respectfully. "Teacher Mu, we will report anything related to safety. But the method and last-part details should wait for Xuanyu. He is the captain."

The word captain made the corridor quiet again.

Yesterday, Qian Lei had refused certainty because he would not abandon the team. Today, Liu Feng refused the safety of answering a teacher because the captain had set a strategic boundary before falling into deep meditation. Dorm333's unity had not ended when the offer vanished.

It had become more stubborn.

Mu Zhongtian wanted to be angry.

He failed.

Ji Hongbin did not.

"Good," Ji said.

Mu looked at him. "Good?"

"They know there are later tests. They know information has value. They know the captain's order matters even when the captain is not awake. If they had begun boasting in the corridor, I would be disappointed."

Qian's expression brightened. "Teacher Ji, does that mean no reflection?"

"No."

"I knew praise had a trap."

Ji ignored him. "You will write only what can be safely recorded. Timeline, injuries, broad categories. No detailed skill mechanics, no final execution sequence, no private test conversation spread among classmates. When Xuanyu wakes, he will decide what to include in the formal report, and I will decide whether his decision is foolish."

Liu Feng nodded. "Yes."

Qian also nodded. "Can the report mention that my restraint was heroic?"

"It can mention that your mouth opened three times and your teammates stopped disaster twice."

"That is hostile editing."

Song, who had been listening from beside Yan, suddenly whispered, "Historic. Painful. Very expensive."

Qian pointed at him. "See? The second-place team understands proper documentation."

"We also understand silence," Luo said.

Qian lowered his hand.

"I respect second place less now."

The tension broke.

Not completely.

Never completely. The numbers were too large. Shrek Academy's shadow was too close. Lan Xuanyu still sat behind the door, unreachable in a state most Soul Masters dreamed of touching once.

But the corridor breathed again.

Mu Zhongtian looked from Dorm333's door to Dorm336's three students.

First.

Second.

Ninth.

Heaven Luo Academy had waited years for even one path toward Shrek.

Now three very different paths stood in the same corridor.

Lan Xuanyu's path was hidden under sleepy eyes, command judgment, strange bloodline support, and a score no normal explanation could contain.

Yan Shuo's path was blazing and public, yet still controlled: true Fire Phoenix, Ultimate Fire, Spirit Sea, three purple rings, and a team that turned his power into a route rather than a spectacle.

Lu Qianxun's path was proud and direct, wounded by defeat but not broken, already turning toward cooperation instead of resentment.

And around them were Qian's summoning, Liu's spear, Song's shield, Luo's perception, Ye Lingtong's regret, Chang Jianyi's control, even Jin Xiang's battered but surviving entry into the top hundred.

The Elite Junior Class had not produced one miracle.

It had produced a problem Shrek would have to look at carefully.

Ji Hongbin seemed to reach the same thought.

"No one spreads details," he said. "The rankings will become public. Scores cannot be hidden. Methods can. Qian Lei, Liu Feng, return inside and watch Xuanyu. Yan Shuo, keep the corridor orderly. Song, Luo, rest. If your team is called for report, I will send word. Teacher Mu and I will handle the academy side."

"Yes," they answered.

Qian hesitated before reentering.

He looked at Yan.

"Second place," he said.

Yan looked back.

"Champion," he answered.

Qian lifted his chin. "We are all extremely expensive now."

Yan considered that.

"Then try not to damage yourself."

"Too late. My heart is already damaged."

Liu pulled him by the back of his collar before he could continue.

The door closed softly behind Dorm333's two conscious members.

Inside, Lan Xuanyu still had not moved.

Qian and Liu stood beside his bed for a moment, both looking at him in silence.

"Champion," Qian whispered.

Liu said nothing.

The word was too heavy.

Qian sat down carefully on his own bed.

For once, he did not complain about missing breakfast.

Liu took the chair near the door, White Dragon Spear across his knees, as if guarding a battlefield rather than a dorm room.

Lan's breathing remained even.

The faint light beneath his skin flowed on.

Outside, the corridor stayed quiet under Yan Shuo's watch.

Far away, the Heaven Luo rankings began spreading from screen to screen, office to office, academy to academy.

Ninety-ninth.

Ninth.

Second.

Champion.

Numbers were only numbers.

But today, numbers became doors.

And for the boy sitting in deep meditation, the first door opening was not on the ranking screen.

It was inside him.
