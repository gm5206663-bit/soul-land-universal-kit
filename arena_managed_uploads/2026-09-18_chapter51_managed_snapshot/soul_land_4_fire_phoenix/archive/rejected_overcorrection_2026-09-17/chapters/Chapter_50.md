# Chapter 50 — Where Rewards Become Weight

Rewards looked light before they were understood.

A card could fit between two fingers. A line of text could announce five hundred years as easily as it announced a meal voucher. A Soul Bone, before it entered flesh, could rest on a table like an object instead of a choice.

Mu Zhongtian destroyed that illusion before noon.

He stood in front of Dorm333 with his arms crossed, face stern and eyes unable to hide how fiercely proud he was.

"Qian Lei's reward is excellent," he said. "All current Soul Rings increasing by five hundred years is exactly the kind of foundation change a summoner needs. It may improve summoning quality, duration, speed, stability — maybe all of them. But it must be processed correctly through the Spirit Pagoda. Do not assume shouting happily will make it happen."

Qian Lei closed his mouth.

Only because the sentence had found him before he spoke.

Mu looked at Liu Feng next. "A two-thousand-year Soul Bone is even more shocking. Even man-made, even Spirit Pagoda refined, it is precious. Soul Bones strengthen the part they merge with and grant an extra skill. Ancient wild Soul Bones were rare enough to become family treasures. Modern technique made some things possible, not cheap."

Liu Feng listened with the stillness of someone afraid that moving too much would wake him from a dream.

Then Mu turned to Lan.

"Your Elementary Spirit Ascension Platform access is also valuable. Inside the Platform, a Soul Master fights simulated or emulated soul beasts and absorbs special energy after kills. That energy can improve Soul Rings or related Spirit Soul foundations until the opportunity ends or the user is eliminated. Slots are expensive and limited. Used well, it may exceed a fixed reward. Used poorly, it can be wasted."

Lan nodded.

Outwardly calm.

Inside, not entirely.

His Soul Rings were natural, self-born, and tied to the gold-silver vortex Teacher Nana had explained. They were not normal Spirit Soul rings. Publicly, he could say his first rings had been close to hundred-year level and changed after his second ring. Qian and Liu accepted that because hundred-year rings were normal enough among Elite Junior Class students, and Lan's martial soul had always been strange.

But the Platform was Spirit Pagoda technology.

Would it feed his rings?

Would it feed something else?

Would it notice the difference?

Lan kept the questions behind his eyes.

A reward did not have to be simple to be useful.

It only had to be handled carefully.

Yin Tianfan and Ji Hongbin made sure of that.

By afternoon, Dorm333 stood in Yin's office while Mu reported the details again. Yin Tianfan's first reaction to the Soul Bone was not greed. It was calculation, and the calculation was team-first rather than owner-first.

"A right arm bone," Yin said, fingers tapping the desk. "Do we assume Liu keeps it because he drew it, or do we check who fits it best?"

Qian's head snapped toward Liu.

Lan also looked at him.

Liu Feng's face changed, but only for a moment. Then he said, "If someone else is more suitable, I have no objection."

The room quieted.

Even Ji Hongbin's stern eyes softened.

Qian Lei had refused Shrek's early admission for Dorm333. That refusal had not vanished after the tears dried. It had become a measuring rod. Compared with leaving a team behind at Shrek's gate, surrendering an item if the team needed it felt smaller, even when that item was a Soul Bone.

Liu looked down. "We only received these rewards because all three of us reached first place. If the bone strengthens the team more in someone else's hand, then it should go there."

Yin Tianfan leaned back slowly.

"Good," he said.

Ji Hongbin added, "Very good."

Qian rubbed his nose. "For the record, I am moved. Also for the record, if anyone says my five hundred years should be redistributed, I will become less moved."

Liu's mouth twitched.

Lan said, "Your reward strengthens the team by making you less useless between miracles."

Qian clutched his chest. "Captain, your praise has thorns."

"Then grow armor."

Yin laughed once, then pointed at all three. "Listen well. Your team did not scatter when Shrek tempted you. Your rewards must not scatter you either. The academy will also not be stingy. If you enter the top ten in the Heaven Luo selection, I will speak to the president and scrape resources if I must. We will try to obtain two more Elementary Spirit Ascension Platform opportunities so all three of you can enter before Shrek."

Qian's grief healed instantly. "Teacher Yin, you are magnificent."

"I know."

Ji Hongbin said, "Do not promise with only your mouth."

Yin waved him off. "Heaven Luo has first across the federation. Even if Ling Tian Academy usually presses us down, this result lets us speak loudly for once. If the academy refuses to invest now, then all those meeting chairs deserve to be melted."

Mu Zhongtian's stern face nearly cracked.

Lan lowered his head, but warmth rose in him.

This was not just a reward.

It was adults deciding that a path deserved support.

Dorm333 was not the only team receiving that truth.

Across the training wing, Dorm336's three cases opened under a different kind of pressure.

Yan Shuo fastened the dark-red calibration cuff around his wrist. It did not flare. It did not feed him power. Thin gold measurement lines lit, then dimmed, recording the first breath of restrained heat that rose from his skin.

"Again," Mu Zhongtian said through the simulator speaker.

Yan released a thread of Fire Phoenix aura.

The cuff warmed and displayed three thin bands of light: output, leakage, wing-root strain.

Song Yichen stood behind an impact plate with his Stoneplate Tortoise shield braced. Luo Haoran wore pale-green reaction threads along his wrists and shoulders; the threads shifted whenever his body prepared to move before his feet did.

This was the reward that looked least like glory and most like work.

That made it real.

Yan's Fire Phoenix was already powerful. Rank39, SP962, three purple rings, true awakened Fire Phoenix, Ultimate Fire — no serious observer could pretend it was ordinary. But Shrek had not rewarded him by telling him to burn brighter. It had rewarded him by demanding that he burn exactly enough.

"Leakage high at the third breath," Luo called.

Yan reduced the output.

Song's shield caught a controlled heat-pulse through the simulator plate. The impact matrix flashed across its surface, showing where force traveled through the shield and where it would have hit Luo if Song's angle shifted badly.

Song winced. "That line would have taken my shoulder."

"It would have taken mine," Luo corrected. "Your shoulder would have remained attached. Mine would have complained to my ancestors."

"Then thank me for almost saving it."

Yan lowered his hand before the cuff reached warning red. "Again. But slower. We are not proving second place. We are making sure it can stand twice."

Song's expression changed.

Luo's too.

Second place had not become a plaque on a wall.

It had become a question Shrek expected them to answer again.

That was reward.

That was pressure.

That was natural.

Back in Yin's office, the physical Soul Bone finally arrived.

It lay above the table inside a transparent containment field: a faint silver right-arm shape, smaller than a full arm bone, with light folded inside it like a moon seen through water. Its aura was restrained, but when Lan stood close, the skin on his own right arm prickled as if cut by cold wind.

"Silver Moon Wolf Right Arm Bone," Yin said. "Spirit Pagoda-made, refined from Silver Moon Wolf bone material, around two thousand years. Attached Soul Skill: Silver Edge. Released through the right arm or conducted into a weapon, it forms a short silver crescent blade, about three inches, consuming Soul Power."

Liu Feng's eyes fixed on it.

White Dragon Spear.

Speed.

Weak attack ceiling.

Always needing Lan's enhancement to pierce what his movement reached.

A right arm bone that could conduct into his spear route was not merely suitable.

It was close enough that refusing it for abstract fairness would be foolish.

Yin looked at Ji. "It suits Liu best."

Ji nodded. "Right arm, weapon conduction, attack supplement. It matches his problem."

Lan felt Liu exhale.

Qian leaned over and whispered, "Congratulations. Your expensive arm is officially yours."

Liu whispered back, "Thank you for your professional description."

"I am generous with language."

Ji Hongbin did not let them continue. "Liu Feng. Sit. Release your Martial Soul. Inject Soul Power into the bone and follow the sensation it brings. It will hurt. You must remain focused. I can protect your safety, but I cannot fuse it for you. The Soul Bone must accept you through your own will."

Liu sat cross-legged.

White Dragon Spear appeared across his knees.

The containment field opened.

When Liu's Soul Power touched the Silver Moon Wolf Right Arm Bone, silver light exploded into his palm.

His fingers clenched.

Then his entire right arm shook.

Pain arrived so suddenly that he groaned before he could swallow it. The silver bone dissolved into brilliant rays and drilled into his arm. His sleeve tore apart. The arm swelled larger than it should have, skin pulled tight under racing silver lines. Sweat appeared on his forehead in an instant.

Qian's face went pale.

Lan took half a step forward and stopped himself.

Ji's palm hovered above Liu's head, sending a steady stream of hot stabilizing force downward. Not to replace Liu's effort. To keep him conscious enough to make that effort matter.

"Stay awake," Ji said. "Stay focused. A rank-skipping Soul Bone is not conquered by screaming. Endure it, guide it, and make it part of you."

Liu's teeth clenched hard enough that Lan heard them click.

This was not luck anymore.

This was the price that came after luck.

The Silver Moon Wolf's sharpness moved through Liu's arm like cutting wind. Blood spots appeared beneath the skin. Yin's expression turned grave despite his usual ease. Two thousand years, even refined and man-made, was still difficult for Liu's current cultivation.

Lan watched Liu tremble and remembered every time Liu had practiced speed until falling, every time he had missed a strike because his spear was fast but not strong enough, every time he had silently stood beside Qian and Lan as if being less dazzling meant being less determined.

"Teacher Ji," Lan said, "can I help him? My martial soul supports his."

Ji's eyes flicked to him.

He did not hesitate long. He knew the way Lan's golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass affected Liu Feng. "Careful. If the rejection feeds back, withdraw."

Lan nodded.

Golden-patterned Blue Silver Grass extended and wrapped around Liu's right arm.

The moment it touched silver light, Lan felt cutting pain run back along the grass into his own body. He sucked in a breath and called up the blood-vital energy inside him. Gold brightened. A faint halo spread along the grass.

The silver rays recoiled slightly.

Not defeated.

Softened.

Guided.

Liu's trembling eased by a fraction.

The White Dragon Spear across his knees suddenly gave a clear, joyful buzz. White light lifted from it like a small dragon, circling Liu's arm and pressing close to the golden-patterned grass. Silver, gold, and white wound together.

Ji Hongbin's eyes sharpened.

Lan was only Rank20.

Only two rings.

Yet his martial soul could suppress and harmonize the violence of a two-thousand-year Soul Bone enough for Liu's White Dragon Spear to respond like this.

Mutation?

Bloodline?

Something no public file explained?

Ji stored the questions for later. This was not the time.

Half an hour passed in sweat, breath, and silver pain.

Then Liu's swollen arm slowly returned to normal size.

The violent rays sank beneath his skin. When the last light faded, silver crescent-moon patterns remained along his right arm, pale and sharp under torn cloth.

Liu exhaled.

His eyes opened.

For one instant silver flashed in them, and his whole aura seemed thinner and sharper, like a spear point polished under moonlight.

Ji placed two fingers near Liu's wrist, then nodded. "Rank29. Not far from Rank30."

Qian Lei nearly jumped. "Rank29! Liu Feng, your arm came with levels!"

Liu tried to smile. It came out tired and bright. "Not enough to catch up with your mouth."

"No one catches that."

Lan looked down at his own hand.

Rank29.

Qian's rings strengthened.

Yan already Rank39, Spirit Sea far ahead, true Phoenix wings under restraint.

Lan remained Rank20.

He knew his path was unusual. He knew his command mattered. He knew his Spirit Sea had opened. But knowing did not remove the small, sour frustration that his Soul Power crawled while others leapt.

Liu noticed.

He reached out with his left hand and touched Lan's wrist lightly. "Thank you."

Two words again.

Simple.

They pulled Lan out of himself.

"Familiarize with the Soul Bone," Ji said. "Do not treat Silver Edge as a toy. In the next qualifier, it is a trump card, not a performance."

Liu nodded. "Yes."

Qian, meanwhile, was taken by Mu Zhongtian to Heaven Luo City's Spirit Pagoda division to process his ring-age reward properly.

He complained during the paperwork.

He complained during the waiting.

He stopped complaining when the Spirit Pagoda technician told him not to move, because even Qian understood that all current rings plus five hundred years was not the time to test fate's patience.

The process did not produce exact numbers for him to brag about. It did not turn his rings purple. It did not make him suddenly invincible.

It did make the yellow deepen.

It made the Gate of Summoning feel less sluggish when he touched it afterward, and it made Qian stare at his own feet with the expression of someone who had received both candy and homework.

"Still yellow," he said mournfully.

Mu Zhongtian replied, "Darker yellow. Stronger yellow. Be grateful."

"I am grateful. I simply wanted destiny to be more dramatic."

"Destiny is saving its drama for your next mistake."

Qian accepted that possibility with visible concern.

Lan spent the two days stabilizing his Spirit Sea, practicing perception until the world stopped feeling too sharp at the edges, and carrying the discomfort of slow Soul Power without letting it poison him. Liu trained carefully with the fused right arm, learning how the silver crescent patterns answered Soul Power without releasing Silver Edge casually. Qian tested summoning activation timing under supervision and discovered that improvement was real but not miraculous.

Dorm336 trained in the adjacent schedule block.

Yan's calibration cuff repeatedly warned him when Fire Phoenix output climbed faster than formation could bear. Song's impact matrix exposed recoil lines he had never realized were leaking through his shield. Luo's reaction threads caught every late call and every overcorrection.

They improved because the reward did not flatter them.

It measured them.

By the second evening, Song had stopped calling the matrix rude. Luo had stopped trying to outthink the threads and started moving with them. Yan had reduced one controlled heat layer until the cuff showed stable green for three breaths longer than before.

No new ring.

No new skill.

No public identity reveal.

Still, Shrek's special observation file received fresh data that said Dorm336's second place had not been an accident.

When the six shortlisted Heaven Luo teams gathered again, the room felt different from the one before the Lucky Wheel.

Dorm333 carried quiet rewards: Qian's darker yellow rings, Liu's covered silver right arm, Lan's still-unspent Platform access and broader Spirit Sea.

Dorm336 carried three training cases and a team observation mark that changed how teachers looked at them.

Lu Qianxun stood with Ye Lingtong and Chang Jianyi, gaze steady but thoughtful. Jin Xiang stood farther back, no longer loud.

Mu Zhongtian did not need to remind them what was at stake.

Everyone could feel it.

Six teams.

Eighteen students.

A hundred teams competing for ten places.

Shrek would not care that they were tired.

Ji Hongbin's gaze crossed the lineup. It paused on Dorm333, then Dorm336, then the rest.

"The next qualifier is about to begin," he said. "Display your strengths. If cooperation becomes possible, let Lan Xuanyu take overall command."

The words caused a stir, but not the wrong one.

Yan Shuo stood with Song and Luo. He did not look offended. Command in a forest of rules was not the same as raw combat power. A true Fire Phoenix could burn a path; Lan could often see which path should not be burned.

Their eyes met once.

Recognition, not surrender.

Then the simulation pods opened.

Eighteen lids rose.

This time, when Dorm333 stepped toward their pods, Qian Lei did not give a speech.

He looked at Liu's covered right arm, then at Lan, then down at his own darker yellow rings.

"Captain," he said quietly, "this time, let's make the rewards worth the weight."

Lan looked toward the open pod.

Yin Tianfan's line. Ji Hongbin's warning. Yan's public advice. Qian's refused temptation. Liu's endured pain. Dorm336's measured second place.

All of it stood behind them now.

"Together," Lan said.

They entered.
