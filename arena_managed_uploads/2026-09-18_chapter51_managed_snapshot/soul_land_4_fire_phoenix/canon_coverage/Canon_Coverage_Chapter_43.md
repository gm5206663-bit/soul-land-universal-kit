## 3. Repaired anti-nerf Fire Phoenix / Dragon hierarchy requirements

The prior Chapter43 underplayed Yan by treating Dorm336's fissure state as static survival framing. That is invalid.

Required repaired logic:

- Yan Shuo's martial soul is a true awakened Fire Phoenix, not an ordinary fire bird and not a weak student flame.
- Ultimate Fire is a major top-tier fire attribute, not a cosmetic effect.
- The Ground Fire Scarlet Dragons in this qualifier are powerful fire-dragon/pseudo-dragon simulation beasts, but not true Dragon monarchs and not superior full fire dragons.
- True Fire Phoenix bloodline/attribute authority should naturally suppress their fire aura, flame behavior, and instinctual confidence when Yan releases even controlled Phoenix pressure.
- Yan is Rank39 with three purple rings and three perfectly compatible/fully absorbed soul spirits: Dawnflame Phoenix Kite 3,100 years, Dawn-Iron Phoenix Roc 3,950 years, Purple Flame Eidolon Bird 6,400 years. Their combined soul-spirit age foundation exceeds ten thousand years. This does not mean perfect mastery, but it does mean the foundation is extremely high and cannot be treated as ordinary.
- Yan's baseline Fire Phoenix body and ordinary controlled release must already be exceptional. Full three-spirit integration remains a trump card and is not needed just to exert fire/phoenix suppression.
- Dorm336's fissure route should be active pressure-control, not static survival framing: Yan can press true Fire Phoenix/Ultimate Fire aura through the thermal seam, bend back inferior fire, destabilize the Dragons' fire rhythm, and make the Dragons hesitate/recoil/redirect.
- Song and Luo remain essential: Song anchors the physical shock line; Luo reads the thermal/impact seam and tells Yan where the pressure can be expressed without collapsing the route.
- Yan's suppression should contribute materially to the Dragons worsening, colliding, and staying off Dorm336/Dorm333 targets.
- Qian Lei still keeps the canon final kill because the Emerald Demon Bird has an eye/brain weak-point advantage, extreme speed, and a perfect thirty-second tactical opening after the Dragons are already badly wounded and destabilized.
- Yan should be written as capable of challenging or suppressing the Dragons' fire side without full integration, while still choosing team safety and the route's constraints over reckless direct solo combat.
