# Chapter 3: The Right Way

The word came down on the morning of the tenth day, and the word was short, the way the words from above were always short, because the rooms above did not spend language the way the pit spent language, which was by the handful and to no one.

"The hunt walks tonight," said Feng Si, standing in the doorway of the room with the lock, with the dawn bowl in his hands and the look of a man who had been awake all night with the knowing of it. "The word names the quarry and the party and the hour, and the word names you, and I have stood at nineteen years of altars and never seen a word this short about a child this new. Eat. The church does not feed its hunters late."

Luo Chen ate, because the bowl was warm and the day was long, and the hunt was the thing he had been building toward since the night of the two-skull gate, and the building of it had been done the way all his building was done — quietly, and in the dark, and without owing anyone for the materials.

At the under-gate of the altar they gave him the knife.

It was the assessor who gave it, because the assessor was the church's hand for the giving of things that mattered, and the knife was a thing that mattered — short, black-iron, bone in the handle, the bone worn smooth by the hands that had held it before his, and he knew when he took it what the wearing meant, because the church did nothing without its meaning.

"The church gives its blades a knife before it gives them a ring," she said. "The ring is the world's law, and the world's law is that the ring follows the killing hand, and no church on the continent has ever changed that law, not ours, because the law is older than the churches. What we bind, you kill. What you kill is yours, and the ring with it. Keep the knife clean."

"I will," said Luo Chen, and put the knife where he could reach it, and felt the weight of it settle against his hip the way a promise settles, which is heavier than the thing itself.

---

The party was small, because the church did not parade what it meant to keep.

The assessor rode the lead in her grey, with the bone whip at her belt and her reports already written somewhere behind her grey eyes. Behind her walked Gu Han, who was the hunt-master of this altar, a man of fifty winters with a face like a saddle and a coil of black chain at his hip, and the chain was his soul, and the rings on him stood four — yellow, yellow, purple, purple, the honest pattern of a man who had risen by the law and not around it, and he walked the way men walk who have walked into that forest four hundred times and come out of it four hundred times. Behind him came Shi Tie, the grey handler, with stakes and rope and a mountain axe at his back, two yellow rings standing on a soul of the same iron as his name, and behind Shi Tie walked Feng Si with the lantern dark, because the hunt walked dark, and at the rear of the party walked Luo Chen with the church's knife at his hip and the whole of his account in order.

The night air came down off the forest the way night air comes off deep country, cold at the edges and thicker in the middle, and the forest itself stood ahead of them the way the Evil Demon Forest stood, which was the way a closed eye stands — still, and vast, and certain, and the deeps of it were not where they walked, because no one walked the deeps, and the weight of the thing that owned the deeps stood over all of it the way a mountain stands over the ants at its foot, and the ants who lived there long enough learned to be grateful for the standing of it, because the weight kept the world out.

"The fringes," said Gu Han, without turning, the way a man speaks who has said the same words four hundred times and means them every time. "The hundred-year kinds keep the fringes. The deep country keeps itself. We walk the fringes tonight and no further, and the rule of the walk is the rule of every walk — the quarry is the boy's quarry, and no hand but his puts the quarry down. We find. We bind. We hold. He kills. If a man of this party takes the life of the quarry, the ring will not come to the boy, because the ring follows the killing hand, and the walk has been for nothing. Say you know it."

"I know it," said Luo Chen.

"Say it the way the hunt hears it," said Gu Han. "The quarry is mine. No hand but mine puts it down."

"The quarry is mine," said Luo Chen. "No hand but mine puts it down."

Gu Han turned then, and looked at him the way the forest looks, and gave a nod that was worth more than its size.

"Then we walk."

---

They found the quarry before the dawn, because Gu Han had walked these fringes for forty years and the fringes talked to him the way they talked to no one else.

"Old lair-country," he said, at the place where the trees thinned over broken stone and the ground went soft with the droppings of something heavy. "Something with weight keeps its meat here, and keeps it alone. Track's two days old at most. Dragon-stink under it — thin, but true." He looked at the assessor, and then at Luo Chen, and his saddle-face did the thing it did when the hunt came to its point. "The word named the quarry from the boy's own line, and the word was right to name it, because dragon's blood answers dragon's blood, and the ring a soul takes should be cut from the cloth the soul is cut from. What lairs here is an earth dragon — horned, grey, a hundred and twenty winters of it, armored like a kiln and twice as hot when it's cornered. Beast's young as the kind goes and lean with the hunger of it, which is why it keeps the fringes and eats what the fringes give. It will be the first of the boy's rings, and the boy will be the end of its winters. That is the arithmetic, and the arithmetic is honest."

The lair was under the broken stone, and the earth dragon came out of it the way such things come out — slowly, and then completely, and then too late to go back.

It was longer than a cart and low to the ground the way the earth-kind are low, grey-scaled, the scales ridged like roof-tiles, with a single horn standing back from its brow the way a horn stands, and four legs with purchase in them, and small eyes the color of the forest floor that went over the party the way the forest floor goes over everything — coldly, and without surprise, because the beasts of these fringes had learned what men meant the way the fringes themselves had learned.

And Luo Chen felt it the moment the beast looked at him, because the feeling was not new to him, and the feeling was the thing the crystal had seen and the assessor had written and the church had built a hunt around — the dragon's blood in him answering the dragon's blood in it, the way one struck bell answers another, and the answer was not fear, and the answer was not welcome, and the answer was the oldest thing in the forest, which is the counting of which of two dragon-things is the dragon.

"Bind it," said the assessor. "Hold it for him."

---

Gu Han's chains came out of the air the way such chains come out of the air, because a chain soul is a thing of reaching, and his first two rings were the rings of the hunt — the casting ring and the locking ring, yellow, honest, four hundred hunts old. The chains took the earth dragon around the neck and the hindquarters and pulled against the stakes Shi Tie drove with the mountain axe at his back, and the beast came up against the holding of it the way a kiln comes up against its door, and the forest shook with the striking of it, and the horn came around once and took a chain link out of the air where a man's head had been, and Gu Han was not there, because Gu Han was never where the horn was.

"Hold it!" said Gu Han, and the purple of his third ring stood up, and the chains bit deeper, and the beast went down on its belly with the weight of them, and stood up again, because a hundred and twenty winters of earth dragon does not go down on its belly for iron, and the bone whip sang once — the assessor's hand, keeping the kill honest, taking the strength out of the beast's spring without taking the life — and the beast came down again, and stayed down, and turned its small eyes to the only thing in the clearing that had not yet moved.

"Boy," said Gu Han, quietly, the way a man speaks at the point of the hunt. "The throat under the third plate is the road. The horn takes the left side. Walk the right. Kill it with the knife the church gave you, and kill it with your own hand, and remember what the word said — the ring follows the killing hand."

Luo Chen drew the knife, and walked the right side, and the earth dragon watched him come.

And the hunger stood up.

It stood up the way it had stood in the locked room and the way it had stood at the feeding, because the dragon in him knew a dragon's body when it saw one, and knew what was in that body, and wanted it the way the deep wants water — the hundred and twenty winters of it, the blood of it, the marrow of it, all of it drinkable, all of it his if he reached, and the reaching was so easy, and the knife was so slow, and the hunger said DEVOUR, said DRINK IT LIVING, said TAKE IT THE WAY YOU ARE MADE TO TAKE, and he heard it all the way the wall heard the song, completely and without owing it an answer, because he had written the order of the account in the pit and the order had not changed.

*The vessel before the ring,* he said, inside, while his feet walked the right side and the horn tracked him, slow and patient, the way the horn tracked. *The ring before the ease. The gate wants a killing done the right way — so the killing is done the right way, and the drinking waits, and the waiting is mine, and the hunger is a leash I hold, not a leash that holds me.*

The horn came.

He was where the horn had been sure he was, and then he was not, and the going of it took the skin from his shoulder and the standing of it took him low under the plates, and the earth dragon's throat was there the way Gu Han had said the road was, under the third plate, soft as the world is soft in the places the world does not show — and the church's knife went in, and the killing was not the clean thing the stories make of killing, because the beast thrashed and the knife was short and the blood was hot and there was a moment when the horn's shadow crossed him and the chains screamed with the holding of him, and then the knife went in again, deeper, and the earth dragon's small eyes went over him one last time, coldly, and without surprise, because the beasts of these fringes had learned what men meant, and the light went out of the eyes the way a lamp goes out, and the hundred and twenty winters stood up out of the body the way the word of the world says they stand.

The ring was yellow, and the yellow was the color of the dawn coming up behind the trees, and it turned over the dead beast slowly, the way a thing turns that knows its own worth, and it was beautiful the way the law is beautiful, and Luo Chen stood under it with the blood of a dragon down his front and his shoulder opened and the hunger in him silent with the astonishment of it, because the hunger had been sure it would be fed, and the feeding had come another way.

"The ring does not wait," said Feng Si, softly, at the edge of the clearing, where the party stood back, because the absorption is a solitary thing and the church knew its laws as well as the world knew them. "The hour is the outside of it. Sit under it, and take it, and we will stand the watch while you take it."

---

He sat under the ring with his legs folded and the knife across his knees, and the ring came down the way the book said the rings come down — slowly at first, and then wholly, settling onto him the way a yoke settles, and the hundred and twenty winters of the earth dragon came in with it.

The coming in was the trial the world makes of every soul that takes its first ring, and the world does not lighten the trial for the young of it, because the law does not know the young. The beast's years pressed into him the way weight presses, looking for the place where the vessel was weakest, and the vessel answered the way the vessels of his line answer — the dragon's blood in him rose up against the dragon's years the way a wall rises, and the devouring that was the soul's own birthright took the pressing of it and turned it, because that is what the devouring does with everything, and what had been a trial became a filling, and behind the gate that had stood shut since the day of his birth all the water of the feeding stood gathered — the cup's warmth, the beast's hundred years drunk in the locked room, every grain of every night — and the gate knew what stood behind it, and the gate had been waiting on the ring the way the word said gates wait, and when the ring settled and the gate opened, all of it came through at once.

The yellow ring burned itself into his soul and was his, and the first skill of his life stood up out of the ring the way the ring stands up out of the beast, because the skill is what the ring is for, and the skill of the earth dragon's ring was the earth dragon's own — the ARMOR OF ITS KIND, the grey scales rising out of his skin where he willed them, over the forearms first, and then the shoulders, and then the whole of him, the scales of a dragon standing on the body of a boy, and the forest was very quiet around the standing of it.

And behind his breastbone, in the place where the hidden thing slept, something knocked once, the way a thing knocks that has tasted dragon through him and approved, and Luo Chen closed over the knocking the way he closed over everything that was his and not the church's, and gave it to the dark, and kept it.

"Open your eyes, then," said the assessor, when the hour had gone and the watch with it, and he opened his eyes, and the party stood around him the way the party had stood, and Gu Han looked at him with the look of a man who had walked four hundred walks and seen few of them end this way.

"Stand," said Gu Han. "And let the hunt see what the ring gave."

Luo Chen stood, and willed the scales, and they rose, grey over his forearms the way they had risen, and Gu Han swung the flat of a chain link against them the way a hunt-master tests the thing he needs to know, and the chain rang off the scales the way iron rings off a kiln, and the arm under them did not move.

"Armor of the kind," said Gu Han. "The dragon's own. Good iron for the first door."

"Level," said the assessor, and the crystal of her office came out of her grey, because the church measures what it feeds, and she read him the way she had read him once before at the two-skull gate, and her grey eyes did the thing grey eyes do when the arithmetic comes out larger than the arithmetic should. "Soul Master. And thirteen — the gate held the devouring's water, and the ring opened the gate, and all the water came through. Write it, Feng Si: the feeding feeds the gate too, and the church's method is one method, not two. The boy walked in at the fullness of ten and he stands now at thirteen with one ring and the dragon's armor on his arms, and all of it in fifteen days." She looked at Luo Chen then, the way she had looked at him once before, the way the church looks at the things it means to use. "The church feeds what is useful. You are useful. Tomorrow the church begins to use you."

---

They walked back in the dawn, with the beast's hide and horn going onto Shi Tie's back the way the hunt carries what the hunt keeps, because the church wastes nothing of what its blades take, and Luo Chen walked at the rear of the party the way he had walked out, and the walking back was not the walking out, and the difference was him.

The knife rode at his hip, and the knife was his now, because the church gives its blades a knife and then the knife belongs to the blade, and that was the price of the giving, and he knew the price the way he knew everything, completely, and carried it the way he carried everything, without owing anyone for the carrying.

*Two of the four,* said the account, inside, while the dawn came up over the fringes and the weight of the deeps stood over all of it the way it stood. *The vessel was filled in the pit and the room. The ring was taken in the forest, by my hand, the right way, with the life standing at the taking and the hunger made to wait — and the waiting taught the hunger its place, and the place is under me. The method was given by the church and taken apart by me. The road out is the only one that remains, and the road out is built of the same three things the rest was built of — patience, and use, and the dark.*

And the hidden thing behind his breastbone slept with its one knock folded away, and the wall took its nightly brick from the cup that would come at dusk, and the forest kept its counsel, and the boy who walked at the rear of the hunt was thirteen levels of soul and six years of life, and the arithmetic of him was the kind of arithmetic the rooms above write short words about, because short words are what the church spends on the things it means to keep.
