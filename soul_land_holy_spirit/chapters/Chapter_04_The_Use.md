# Chapter 4: The Use

The use began the way everything the church did began — with a shape, and a word, and a place to stand in the shape.

"The church has read the report of your ring," said the assessor, standing in the doorway of the room with the lock on the morning after the hunt, "and the report has ridden up, and the word has come down, and the word says what the church does with a blade that takes its first ring at fifteen days. The church sharpens it. The church sharpens it by using it, because a blade that is kept in a box goes dull in the box, and the church does not keep dull blades." She looked at him the way she looked at everything, completely. "You will go to the beast-house. You will do what only you can do. You will come when you are called and you will speak the words when the words are spoken, and the cup will come to you every night, and that is the shape of your use, and the shape is a good one. Walk with me."

The beast-house stood behind the altar the way such houses stand, low and long and windowless, with the smell of it going up into the cold air like a thing announcing itself, and Luo Chen walked into the smell of it the way he walked into everything — with the account open and the face closed.

---

The beast-house was the church's larder and the church's ledger and the church's arithmetic, all in one long dark, because the church wasted nothing of what it took and it took everything it could.

The cages stood in two rows down the length of the house, black iron over stone, and what stood in the cages was the fringe country's tithe — beasts taken young and kept for the taking, beasts taken hurt and kept for the mending, beasts taken whole and kept for the servants' gates, and the law of the house stood over all of it the way the Emperor's weight stood over the forest.

"The law of the house," said the woman who kept it, "is that nothing in this house dies wasted, and nothing in this house lives past its use."

She was the beast-keeper, and her name was Qin Mo, and she was old the way the altar was old, with hands like cage-iron and a soul to match them, because her soul was CAGE THORN and three rings stood on it — yellow, yellow, purple, the honest pattern — and she had kept this house for thirty years and the beasts of this house knew her step the way the fringes knew Gu Han's.

"The beasts that are whole go to the gates," she said, walking the row with him at her side and Feng Si at his back, because the keeper stayed with the kept. "The beasts that are mended go to the gates behind them. The beasts that are hurt past the mending, or rotten with the taking, or too lean for the ring they'd give — those the house cannot use, because a ring out of a rotten soul is a rotten ring, and the church does not hang rotten rings on its servants. Those go to the pit's pot, and the pit eats what the beast-house spares, and that is the house's last use for them." She stopped at the row's end and turned to him, and her cage-iron hands folded themselves the way such hands fold when the arithmetic comes to its point. "But you are the house's new use, and the new use is better than the pot. What the house cannot use, you will drink — the life of it, the years of it, all of it, because your soul wastes nothing the way the pot wastes, and the church's method is one method. You will empty the cages the house tells you to empty, and you will empty them clean, and the levels will come to you the way the levels come to the devoted. That is your work. Do you know the work?"

"I know the work," said Luo Chen.

"Then the first cage is that one," said Qin Mo, "and the beast in it is past the mending, and the beast is yours."

---

The work was the feeding, and the feeding was the work, and the days took on the shape of it the way the days take on the shape of anything a man does with his whole attention.

The beasts came to him one at a time, the way the house gave them — a grey wolf with the sickness in its lungs, a boar with the leg gone bad under the taking, a long-necked thing of the fringes that had eaten what it should not have eaten and was dying of the eating — and he drank them the way he had drunk the first of them, cleanly and without waste, because the work was the feeding and the feeding was the innate, and the innate did the work the way water does the work of going downhill.

And the church watched the work the way the church watched everything it fed, because the assessor came at the turnings of the moon with the crystal of her office, and read him, and wrote.

"Fourteen," she wrote, at the first turning. "Sixteen," she wrote, at the second turning, and her grey eyes did the thing grey eyes do when the arithmetic outruns the writing of it. "Seventeen," she wrote, at the third turning, standing over him with the crystal in her hand and the whole beast-house standing quiet around the standing of it. "The devouring turns the house's waste into the blade's levels, and three turnings of the moon have carried him four levels, and the pace is the pace the crystal saw in him at the gate, and the pace is not slowing. Write this, Feng Si, because the rooms above will want the arithmetic of it: the boy's soul takes the years of beasts the way the forest takes rain, and the boy is six years old, and the second gate stands at the thirtieth level, and at this pace the boy will be standing at the second gate before his eighth winter."

And Luo Chen stood still for the reading of it with the account running behind his face, because the assessor's arithmetic was the arithmetic he had meant her to have, and the arithmetic was a leash the rooms above would hold with both hands, and he knew the way he knew everything that a leash held by both hands is a leash the holder believes in, and a leash the holder believes in is a leash that can be walked.

*Four levels in three turnings is the pace I chose to show,* he thought, while the crystal read him and the grey eyes wrote him, because the engine had measured the devouring's true pace against the pace it showed the way the engine measured everything — completely, and in the dark. *The true pace is the pace the dragon's blood gives, and the true pace is mine, and the shown pace is the church's, and the difference between the two is the difference between us. Let them have the arithmetic they can believe. The arithmetic they cannot believe, I will spend where it cannot be counted.*

---

The cup came every night the way the assessor had said the cup would come, because the church keeps its shapes, and the shape of the cup was the shape of the wall.

The cup came at dusk in the dark wood bowl, dusk-colored and warm, sweet to the nose, and he drank it the way the devoted drink, because the drinking was watched and the watched drinking was a shape, and while the warmth of it went through him the way the warmth always went, softening and loosening and laying its gentle hand on the wall of the mind, the engine went to work in the dark the way the engine had gone to work since the first grain — taking the song's measure, note by note, pull by pull, the way a man takes the measure of a lock he means to open.

And the wall stood, and the wall grew, and the night came when the wall proved itself.

It was the sixty-third cup, and the hand of the draught came to the place in the mind where the draught had always come, the place where the ease went in and the loosening began, and the hand found the place, and the hand did not go in, because the wall stood where the door had been, and the song of the draught beat against the wall the way rain beats against stone, and found no door, and went away.

And what sat in the room with the lock drank the warmth of it with the face of a child going soft in the easing of it, because the face was the shape the watchers read, and what stood behind the face watched the rain beat against the stone and wrote the last note of the song down in the dark.

*The song is mine now,* said the man inside the child, while the child's eyes went heavy with the ease of it, and the lock stood on the outside of the door, keeping what did not need keeping. *Every note of it, and every pull of it, and the way it reaches, and the place it reaches for. You have sung it at me sixty-three nights, and the singing has taught me the whole of your music, and the music cannot get in, and the music does not know it cannot get in, because the wall shows the music the shape of a door, and the shape is enough for the music, and the shape is all the music will ever see. Sing on, then. The church is paying for the church's own wall, and the wall is finished, and the church does not know it.*

---

The wyrm came to the beast-house on the first cold morning of the fourth turning, and the wyrm was the event the turning was for.

Gu Han brought it in, because Gu Han brought in what the fringes gave, and the hunt behind him carried the cage the way the hunt carries the things that matter, and the cage went down the length of the beast-house with the beasts in their rows going still around the passing of it the way beasts go still around the passing of the things they know, and Qin Mo stood at the row's end with her cage-iron hands folded, and the assessor stood beside her, because the word had come down ahead of the cage.

"An ash wyrm," said Gu Han, standing over the cage with the sweat of the walk still on him, because the wyrm was heavy and the way had been long. "Dragon-kind, and true dragon-kind, not the thin blood of the earth dragons — two hundred winters of it, and it came out of the deep-country's edge with the death-sickness in it, and the taking of it cost two men their standing and one man his hand. The poison of the sickness is in the flesh of it, and a ring out of a poisoned soul is a poisoned ring, and the church does not hang poisoned rings on its servants, and the wyrm is past the road back. The word says the house's new use takes it."

The assessor looked at Luo Chen, because the word had said what the word had said, but the word had also said the other thing, and the other thing was the thing that stood over the word.

"The word also says," said the assessor, "that dragon's blood is the blood of the deep country, and the deep country keeps its accounts the way the forest keeps them, and the devouring that drinks the dragon's blood may drink more than the blood. The word says: let him drink, and watch what the drinking does, and write what the watching sees."

They opened the cage.

The ash wyrm lay in it the way the dying lie, long and grey-scaled and folded around the sickness in it, with the dragon's shape true on it the way the earth dragon's had been true and the way no beast of the common blood had it — the line of the spine, the standing of the horn, the memory of wings in the shoulders of it — and its eyes were the color of deep water, and they went to Luo Chen the way the earth dragon's eyes had gone to him, coldly, and without surprise.

And the hidden one woke.

It woke behind his breastbone the way it had knocked once at the ring, but the knocking was not a knocking this time, because the knocking had been the taste of dragon through the ring, and this was dragon itself, dying in a cage two steps from him, and the hidden one stood up in the dark of him and looked at the wyrm the way the wyrm looked at him, and the looking was the oldest thing in the forest, and the forest was in the room.

Luo Chen walked to the cage with the account standing and the face closed, and the hunger stood up with him the way the hunger always stood, and the hidden one stood up under the hunger the way the mountain stands under the weather, and the two of them went to the wyrm together.

The drinking was the longest drinking of his life.

The wyrm's years came out of it the way two hundred winters come, slowly and then wholly, and the dragon's blood came with the years the way the blood comes with everything that has it, and the blood went into him the way dragon's blood goes into what is waiting for it — and something in the devouring answered the blood the way one struck bell answers another, because the devouring was the dragon's own art, and the art knew its own cloth, and the cloth was dragon.

The wyrm died clean, because the devouring wastes nothing, and what lay in the cage was the husk the first feeding had left and the shape of a true dragon folded around it, and no one in the beast-house spoke for the length of the house's silence.

"Write it," said the assessor, at last, and her voice was the voice of a woman writing the word that would ride up. "The devouring drank the wyrm clean and the devouring grew with the drinking, and the dragon's blood went into the boy and the boy's blood answered it, and the answer was not the answer of a common vessel. Write that the rooms above will want the knowing of it, and write that the knowing of it is the knowing of what the word has kept since the gate: the boy is the church's dragon, and the church will feed the boy dragons, because that is what the church does with the things it keeps."

And Luo Chen stood in the length of the house's silence with the wyrm's two hundred winters settling in him and the dragon's blood in his blood and the hidden one folded back down behind his breastbone with its drinking done and its silence kept, because the drinking had shown him the thing he filed deepest of all the things he filed — the wyrm's memory of the deep country, cold and vast and certain, and the weight of the thing that owned it, standing over all of it like a closed eye that was not closed.

*So that is what stands in the deeps,* he thought, while the assessor wrote and the beast-house kept its silence. *And that is what the church stands under, and that is what I stand under, and the standing under it is the price of the door this church opened, and the price is known now, and the knowing of it is mine. The account keeps it. The account keeps everything.*

---

The reading came at the dusk of it, because the church measures what it feeds, and the assessor stood over him with the crystal one more time, and the crystal read what the wyrm had done.

"Eighteen," said the assessor, "and standing firm, and the wyrm's winters are still settling, and the settling will carry him before the moon turns. Write it, Feng Si: the dragon's blood has doubled the pace, and the pace that was the pace the church believed is now the pace the church can believe twice, and the arithmetic of the boy is the arithmetic the rooms above will want to see with the rooms above's own eyes." She looked at Luo Chen then, and her grey eyes had the stillness of the word she was about to speak, because the word had come down ahead of the wyrm, and the word had waited on the drinking, and the drinking had answered. "The word says more, boy. The word says the altar has done what the altar can do, and the word says the rooms above mean to see what they are feeding. The word says you will be taken up to the main temple before the snow comes, and the main temple stands where the forest stands deepest, and the church's own eyes will read you there."

She went out with her grey stillness and her written word, and Feng Si stood a moment in the doorway of the room with the lock, with his lantern in his hand and his soft face doing the thing soft faces do.

"The main temple," he said, quietly. "I have stood at nineteen years of altars and never been called up to the main temple, and you are going up at six years old with one ring and a season of work. You understand what that is?"

"It's the church's eyes," said Luo Chen. "The church wants to see what it's feeding."

"Yes," said Feng Si. "It is the church's eyes. And the church's eyes are where the church decides what the thing it is looking at is for." He held the lantern a little higher, and looked at him the way a keeper looks at the kept when the keeping is coming to its end. "Be ready for the looking, then. And keep the knife clean."

The door closed, and the lock stood on the outside of it, and the cup stood empty at the bedside with the wall standing behind the wall of it, and the wyrm's two hundred winters settled in the dark of him like snow settling, and the account went over itself the way the account went over itself every night, because the account was the only thing in this room that was entirely his.

*The altar is done with me, and the church is taking me up to where the church decides what things are for,* he thought, while the snow's season came down over the Evil Demon Forest and the deeps of it stood over all of it the way they stood. *Good. Let the church's eyes read me. The reading will show the church the thing the church believes — a blade, sharpening, and grateful for the sharpening. And under the reading the account will read the church, the way the account reads everything — completely, and without owing anyone for the reading. The vessel is filled past the seventeenth level. The ring is mine. The method is mine. The wall is finished. And the road out is the only thing that remains, and the road out goes through the main temple, and the main temple stands where the forest stands deepest, and I am going there the way I went to everything — with the account open, and the face closed.*
