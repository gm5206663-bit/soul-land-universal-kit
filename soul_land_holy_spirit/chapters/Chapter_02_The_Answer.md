# Chapter 2: The Answer

The pit kept him for three days, and for three days the pit did not touch him.

That was the first thing the pit understood, because the pit understood such things the way the starving understand weather. The assessor's word had gone down the stairs behind the walk-in, and the word had stood over him the way a lamp stands over a thing that has been counted, and forty children who had spent their lives learning what counting meant gave the bunk by the fire its space, and watched the walk-in with the watching they saved for the things that were going away.

Kui watched him the most, because Kui had met the floor by him, and the floor had taught Kui something the pit rarely taught, which was the difference between the strong and the mean. On the third morning Kui stood over him with his bowl under his arm, the way he had stood over him once before, and held the bowl out.

"The morning's," said Kui. "I'm not hungry."

Luo Chen looked at the bowl, and at Kui, and took the bowl, because refusing would have cost Kui the only thing Kui had to spend, and he ate of it in the silence of the hall, and wrote Kui down in the place he kept the things that were his and not the church's — the second name, and the reason for the keeping of it.

The answer came that night.

---

It came down the stairs at the hour between the bells, when the pit sat between its soup and its sleeping, and the pit went still around it the way the pit went still around everything that mattered.

The assessor came first, in her grey with the bone whip at her belt, and behind her came two men in grey who carried nothing and moved the way men move who have carried things before and put them down where they were told, and behind them, at the stair's foot, stood Feng Si with a lantern and a face that had been told something and was still settling around the telling.

"The word from above," said the assessor, to the hall, and the hall bowed the way the pit bows, which is with the whole body and none of the will. "The word from above has read the measure that went up, and the word from above has answered. The walk-in is not the pit's."

The silence in the hall changed the way silence changes when a thing everyone expected and no one named has been named.

"The walk-in will take what is his and go up," said the assessor, "and the pit will remember that it held him, and the remembering will be quiet. The church feeds what is useful, and the church feeds it where it is useful, and the word from above has said where this one is useful." Her grey eyes went over the hall the way a whip goes, without haste. "The Holy Emperor's light shines on the devoted. Be devoted, and be fed."

Luo Chen stood from the bunk by the fire with his bowl in his hand, because the bowl was his and the church had said so in the rules, and he carried it to the stair, and the two grey men fell in behind him the way men fall in, and the assessor went before him with her lanternless walk, and Feng Si went up the stairs at his side with the lantern held low, and the pit watched the walk-in go up out of its life the way the pit had always watched things go — with the whole of its eyes and none of its voice.

On the stair, in the dark between the lamp's reach and the door above, Feng Si spoke, softly, the way he spoke about everything.

"The word from above gave you to my keeping," he said. "Me, of all the servants of this altar — the report was mine, and the answer came back with your name on it and my name under it, and that is a thing that has not happened to me in nineteen years. I will be your keeper and your teacher both, and you and I will answer for each other to the rooms above, and I would rather you knew that from the stair than learned it from the floor." He held the lantern a little higher, and his soft face had the look of a man holding a thing he had been handed that was worth more than he was, and knowing it. "You understand what that means?"

"It means you'll teach me what they want me to be," said Luo Chen, "and it means I'll learn it."

"Yes," said Feng Si. "It means that. Come up."

---

They gave him a room, and the room was the second thing the serial of his days understood, because the room was not the pit and the room was not a cell, and the difference between those two things was the shape of his worth to the church.

It was small and clean, cut from the stone of the altar's under-halls, with a bed that had a mattress and a lamp that burned without smoke and a door that had a lock on the outside of it. The lock was the truth of the room, and the mattress was the price of it, and Luo Chen read both the way he read everything, completely.

*They house the knife before they use the knife,* he thought, standing in the room with the door closing on him. *A box with a lock and a mattress. Good. The box is quiet, and quiet is what the work needs, and the lock is a door, and doors are the only things in this world worth knowing how they open.*

The doctrine came the first morning, because the church did nothing without its shape.

Feng Si came with the dawn bowl — broth and bread, better than the pit's, warm — and after the bowl came the words, spoken softly, the way the church spoke all its words, because the words of the church were not for the ears alone.

"The Holy Emperor's light shines on the devoted," said Feng Si. "The world outside this place does not know the light, and does not want to know it, and hates the light for the not-wanting, and the church has learned what the hated learn, which is to keep their own. You are the church's own now. The world would have given you nothing for what you are — you carry an evil soul, and the world's answer to evil souls is the pyre and the hunt, and you know it, because you walked past the world's doors to come to ours. The church is the only door on the continent that opened for you. Say it."

"The church is the only door that opened for me," said Luo Chen.

He said it the way the church wanted it said, because the church was listening for the saying, and the saying was the first layer of the wall they meant to build around him, and he knew the wall's whole architecture before the first stone of it was laid, and let them lay it, and watched them lay it, and counted every stone.

"And the devotion is repaid," said Feng Si, pleasantly. "The church feeds what is useful. You are useful. So the church will feed you the way the world never meant to — the method, the hunt, the gate, the rings, everything the soul of you is owed, the whole ladder of the world, one rung at a time, and every rung the church's gift. The pit learns together and gets what it gets together. You will learn alone, and what you get will be what you are. That is the church's justice. Now — the method."

---

Feng Si taught him the Soul-Drawing Formula in the small room with the door locked, the way the church taught the things that mattered — to one, and behind stone.

"The world is full of soul power," said Feng Si. "It is in the air and in the earth and in every living thing, the way water is in the world, and the soul drinks it the way the body drinks water, and the drinking is called cultivation. Every soul master on this continent began exactly where you begin — seated, breathing, drawing the world in, the soul power settling in the center, climbing level by level toward the gates at every tenth level, which open only to rings. But your soul is not the common kind, and the common drawing is not the church's way with you. The Formula is the church's own. Watch."

He taught it then — the posture, the breathing, the path the soul power was to walk through the body, down through the center and back, and under the path the other thing, the thing the church put into all its gifts, because the Formula drew through the appetite, and the appetite it drew through was a devouring appetite, and the drawing reached, faintly, beyond the air and the stone, toward the life around it — the warmth of it, the blood of it — the way a root reaches for water it has not been given.

Luo Chen learned it completely, and asked nothing, because the Formula was not a thing to ask about. It was a thing to take apart.

That night, in the room with the lock on the outside, he ran the Formula for the first time, and the soul power of the altar came to him the way the book said it came to souls like his — eagerly, and in quantity, because a devouring soul draws the way a whirlpool draws, and the fastness of it was real, and under the fastness the hook stood up and looked at him, the way the draught had looked at him, the way everything this church gave looked at him, because everything this church gave was a hand, and the hands were all reaching the same direction.

*So this is the leash,* he thought, with the Formula running and the hook reaching. *The speed is real, and the reach is real, and the two come tied together, the way they tie everything. Very well. I keep the speed. The reach, I file down, grain by grain, in the dark, until the day you hand me a longer leash and believe it holds — and the filing is mine, and the keeping of it is mine, and that is the difference between us, that you do not know the file exists.*

He ran the Formula again, and the drawing came clean, and the reach came filed, and the soul power settled in his center like water settling in a cup, and he drank the world in the way the world was never meant to be drunk — quietly, and without owing anyone for it.

---

The feeding came on the seventh day, because the church taught the way the church taught — with the thing itself.

The two grey men brought it at the hour between the bells: a cage of black iron, and in the cage a beast — a boar the size of a calf, grey-bristled, with small eyes and a wound in its flank where the taking of it had happened, and the beast was a soul beast, a hundred years of it at least, because the life stood in it the way life stands in the things of the forest, thicker than the common kind, and it looked at the boy through the bars of the cage with the look of a thing that knew what rooms like this room were for.

Feng Si came behind the cage, and behind Feng Si came the assessor, because the first feeding was not a lesson the servants watched alone.

"Your soul grows by devouring," said the assessor. "The world calls that evil, and the world hunts what it calls evil, and the church calls it the method, and teaches it. The beast's life is soul power standing on legs, and the soul power is yours, if you are what the crystal said you are. The church gives you the taking of it, because the church feeds what is useful. Feed."

The grey men opened the cage.

The boar came out of it wounded and afraid, and the fear went off it in waves the way fear goes off beasts, and Luo Chen stood at the center of the room with his soul still and looked at the beast, and the beast looked at him, and what the beast saw there was the thing beasts see when they see it, because the forest has taught beasts the sight since the forest began — the look of the thing that eats, and the knowing of it, and the stillness before.

He let the dragon rise.

The lamps went low, and the shadows leaned, and the Devouring Dragon stood up behind him, and the boar's legs folded under it the way a beast's legs fold, not from a blow but from the blood's own counsel, because the dragon carries a dominion of lineage and lesser beasts know it the way prey knows. And Luo Chen reached with the soul, and the reaching went further than the Formula's hook ever went, because the hook was the church's teaching and this was the soul's own — the innate, the birthright, the thing the crystal had seen standing in him before any ring or any teacher: Devouring Heaven and Earth, the Devouring Dragon's own art, older than rings and needing none of them. The dragon's pull took hold of the beast's life the way the deep takes hold of water.

The feeding was not a killing the way the world counts killings. It was a drinking.

The beast's soul power came out of it in a current the grey men could not see and the assessor could, and the current walked across the room and into the boy, and the beast grew lighter as the current walked — lighter in the life of it, thinner in the standing of it — and the boy's soul drank the current the way the book said the devouring drinks, completely, and without waste, and the soul power of a hundred years of beast-life settled in his center and was his.

The boar was still breathing when the current ended, and then it was not breathing, and what lay on the floor of the room was a husk, whole in its shape and empty in its substance, and there was no ring above it, because the ring is made of what the beast carries, and the devouring had drunk what the ring is made of, and the assessor looked at the husk, and at the boy, and her grey eyes had the stillness of a woman writing a report in her own place.

"Clean," she said. "It ate cleaner than the beast ate. Most first feedings are a spill and a struggle, and the beast dies screaming and the child vomits. This one drank it the way a cup is filled. And that reach was no Formula — no teaching reaches that far. That was the innate, the soul's birthright, whole and walking before a ring. Write it exactly: Devouring Heaven and Earth, innate and ringless; the devouring takes without waste, and the taking left no ring, because the devouring ate what the ring is made of. Write that too, because the rooms above will want the knowing of it: the boy's road to the gate is not the feeding. The feeding fills him. The gate still wants a killing done the right way, and a ring whole, and the hunt will give him both. Write it."

She turned her grey eyes to Luo Chen one more time, standing in his room with a hundred years of beast-life settling in his blood and the dragon quiet behind him.

"The church feeds what is useful," she said. "Be useful, and be fed. The measure of you goes up tonight, and the word will come down, and when the word says you are ready, the hunt walks for you. Ten days. Be ready in ten days."

She went out with her grey men and her silence, and Feng Si stayed a moment at the door with the lantern, and looked at the husk on the floor, and at the boy, and his soft face did the thing soft faces do when they have been given a thing to keep that is larger than the keeping of it.

"The cup will come every night now," he said. "The welcome of the altar, for the ones the church keeps close. Drink it when it comes, and be warm."

"I will," said Luo Chen.

---

The cup came that night, in the dark wood bowl, dusk-colored and warm, sweet to the nose the way the pit's food was never sweet, and the draught stood up in him the way the book said such things stood up — soft, and warm, and loosening, the hand laid gently on the wall of the mind, the first note of the song that the church sang to its knives until the song was the only music they knew.

The boy's part of him felt the ease of it, the quieting of the stone room's cold, and the man part of him went to work the way the man part of him always went to work, quietly and completely. The engine took the song's measure — its notes, its pull, the place in the blood where it went to root — and began to build the answer in the dark, slowly, the way the wall is built, grain by grain, until the day this song comes again and finds a wall where it expected a door.

*First grain,* said the man inside the child, while the child's face went soft with the warmth of it and the child's eyes went a little heavy with the easing of it, and the lock stood on the outside of the door and believed itself the keeper. *Sing it to me nightly, then. Every note of your song is a brick of my wall, and the church is paying for the church's own wall, and that is the only kind of wall I ever trusted — the kind the enemy pays for.*

And in the dark of the room, with the draught's warmth in his blood and a hundred years of the beast's life in his center and the Formula's speed filed clean, he went over the account the way he went over it every night, because the account was the only thing in this room that was entirely his.

The beast's power sat in him, and it was real, and it was a filling, and the filling was the proof of the road — the devouring gave what the devouring always gave, speed and substance, and never the gate, because the gate wanted a ring whole and a killing done the right way, and the ring came only from the beast the killer's own hands took, with the life standing in it at the taking. Ten days, the assessor had said, and the hunt walked when the word came down, and the word would come down, because the church fed what was useful, and nothing in the pit or above it had ever been as useful as he intended to become.

*Ten days,* thought Luo Chen, and closed his eyes, while the altar slept over him and the forest breathed beyond the altar and somewhere in the depths of it the things with dragon's blood in them hunted their night's hunger, and did not know that the hunger coming for them had a name, and a room with a lock, and ten days, and plans. *The vessel is filled enough, and the gate is tired of waiting. So am I.*
