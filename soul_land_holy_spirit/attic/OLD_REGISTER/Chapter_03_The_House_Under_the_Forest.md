# Chapter 3: The House Under the Forest

The road went down, and the light went back, and the two parted company the way light and dark always part in such places — politely, and forever. The grey man walked Luo Chen down until the road came to a stone, and on the stone was carved the two-skull coin, and under the coin he stopped the way men stop at a line they were never paid to cross.

"Down," said the grey man, pointing into the trees. "Straight down. The house will hear you coming."

"Does it hear everyone coming," said Luo Chen.

"It hears what it wants to hear," said the grey man, and turned, and went back up toward the lamps, and Luo Chen watched the grey go up out of the dark the way he had watched things go his whole new life — with the patient eye of a man taking the measure of what he was being handed. Then he went down, and the forest closed behind him the way water closes.

It was dark under the trees, but the dark was not empty, because the Evil Demon Forest was not a forest the way other forests are forests — it was a kingdom, and kingdoms have eyes. Things watched him from the branches and did not come down, because the things in the fringes know what walks a road, and what walks a road belongs to somebody, and the road bore the church's mark on its stones at intervals, and the mark was respected the way the strong are respected in the wild, which is completely, and from a distance.

The house stood in a hollow where the trees leaned together overhead like old men conspiring, and it was not a house the way the word house means a thing with a door and a roof — it was a long low building grown half into the roots of a tree so vast that its roots were the building's ribs and its bark its walls, and the whole of it sat in the shadow of the trunk the way a barnacle sits in the shadow of a ship. Lights burned in its windows, low and orange, and from it came the smell of broth and herbs, and under the broth and the herbs a sweetness that was not any herb, a sweetness Luo Chen knew now in his blood, because the sweetness was the welcome of the altar, and the house kept the welcome of the altar the way the pit kept obedience.

*So this is the barnacle,* he thought, standing in the hollow with the dark breathing around him. *And the ship is the forest, and what the forest rides on, the book told me, and the book did not need to tell me twice.*

The door opened before he reached it, and the woman in the doorway was old the way iron is old — hard once, and worn into something harder and smoother — short and straight in the Cult's grey with a white apron over it, and her face was kind, and the kindness sat on it the way the cheerfulness sat on Feng Si — fitted, and for use.

"Come in out of the watching," she said. "The woods are rude about staring, and I keep my knives out of their sight."

Luo Chen stepped into the house, and the house was warm, and the warmth was the worst thing about it, because the warmth was real.

---

The common room was long and low-ceilinged, ribbed by the great tree's roots, and it held children the way the pit held children — along its walls, on mats, in the lamplight — but fewer, and quieter, and dressed, and the difference showed the way the difference between a pit and a workshop shows.

There were nine of them, and Luo Chen counted them the way he counted everything, completely, and the counting went into the private place where he kept the things that were his and not the church's. Ages from small to near-grown; eyes that watched without moving. On the bench nearest the fire sat a girl of perhaps ten years, thin and straight-backed, her hands in her lap and her face still, and the room deferred to her the way rooms defer to what has been there longest and worked hardest.

"The word from above," said the old woman, holding out her hand.

Luo Chen gave her the scroll bound in grey cord, because he had carried it down and he had not opened it, and the not-opening was a door he left open on purpose, because the boy who does not open what he is given is the boy the church trusts.

The old woman broke the cord and read, and her face did not change while she read, and when she was done she looked at Luo Chen a long moment, the way one looks at a thing one has been told about and cannot see, and then the kindness came back, more careful than before.

"I am Mother Wei," she said. "This is my house, and the house belongs to the church, and the church belongs to the Holy Emperor's light, and you belong to the house until the house is done with you. We eat at the bells, we work between the bells, we sleep when the work is done, and the sleeping is watched over, because the forest is near. Do you know what the forest is?"

"It is what the house is under," said Luo Chen.

"Good enough for a first night," said Mother Wei. She held up the scroll. "The word from above says what you are, and it says how you came, and it says one thing more, and the one thing more is the reason the house is going to speak of you for a year. It says no number."

The room went quiet the way rooms go quiet, completely, and the eyes on the mats changed the way eyes change when something in the order of things has moved and everybody felt it move and nobody was told what it was.

"The house numbers its knives," said Mother Wei, pleasantly. "The number is the church's kindness — it tells us we are one thing here and not the many things we were before. But the word from above says you keep your own name, and the house does not argue with the word from above. So you will be the only knife in my house with a name, and you will find the gift heavier than the number. What is your name?"

"Luo Chen," said Luo Chen.

"Then Luo Chen," said Mother Wei, "you will sleep at the end of the bench by the fire, because the word from above does not say where you sleep, and the fire is warm, and I am old, and I resolve what the word leaves open the way the old resolve everything — to my own comfort. Thirteen."

The girl on the bench rose the way water rises when the vessel moves, and came, and looked at Luo Chen the way his own eyes looked out of his own face when he thought nobody was watching.

"Show him the house," said Mother Wei. "And then show him the cup."

---

The house had a shape, and the shape was a lesson, and Thirteen showed him the lesson the way she did everything, without waste.

The sleeping places along the wall. The work yard behind the house, where the beaten ground still showed what had been done on it for how many years. The herb room, which smelled of the sweetness under the broth, and which she showed him from the doorway and did not enter. The little door in the back wall, which was locked, and which she did not explain, and which led down, because stairs go down when they go anywhere in the business of this church.

"Nine of us," said Thirteen, in the low voice of one giving an account. "Nine, and you makes ten. The house takes what the altars send down, and the house makes what the church can feed. You eat what you're given, you drink what you're given, you train at the yard bells and at the method they give you, and what you are given depends on what you are, and what you are is measured every month. The ones that climb get more. The ones that stand still get less. The ones that go down go out the little door, and nobody comes back up."

"What's behind the little door," said Luo Chen.

"The kitchen," said Thirteen, and her face did not move at all when she said it, and that was how he knew she was afraid of it, because the house had taught her that the face is given and the fear is kept.

"Ten," said Luo Chen. "You said ten, with me. There were nine on the mats."

"Eight on the mats," said Thirteen. "Me makes nine. You makes ten. And the tenth one is where the trouble is, because the word from above came down about you, and the word from above never comes down. Things come down about us, but the word from above is a different coming down, and the house is watching you to learn what you cost. So you should know, Luo Chen, that you are not the only one being measured here."

She said the name the way one puts a hand on a thing to see whether it is hot.

"I'll try to be worth the watching," said Luo Chen.

"Try quietly," said Thirteen. "The loud ones went down the little door first."

---

The cup came at the evening bell, and it came the way the book said such things come — to everyone, and as kindness.

Mother Wei carried it on a tray, a small dark bowl for each child, and the sweetness of it stood up in the room's air the way it had stood in the hall of the altar, and Luo Chen's blood knew it before his eyes did, and knowing it, stood up in him — soft, and warm, and loosening, the hand laid gently on the wall of the mind.

"Drink," said Mother Wei, giving him the bowl. "The forest's cold gets into the bones of the new ones, and the welcome keeps it out."

Luo Chen drank, and the boy's part of him felt the ease of it, and the man part of him went to work quietly and completely. The engine took the draught's measure — what it was, and what it did, and where it went — and then it went further than the hall, because the house was going to sing this song every day, and a wall built for the same song sung daily is a fortress, and fortresses are built grain by grain, in the dark of the blood, with a man's patience for the mason.

*Second grain,* said the man inside the child, while the child's face went soft with the warmth of it and the child's eyes went a little heavy with the easing of it, and Mother Wei watched him ease the way she watched all her knives ease, with the satisfied eye of the keeper of the house. *Sing it daily, then. The learning of your whole song is the deafness to it.*

He swayed where a child sways, and slept where a child sleeps, at the end of the bench by the fire, and in the dark behind the sleeping face the man lay awake with the scroll of the first step unrolled in his mind, and began the night's true work.

---

The method of the first step was called the Drawing-In of the Hungry Soul, and it was the church's own, and it was good, and that was the danger of it.

He read it by the fire's last light with the house asleep around him, and the reading was slow and complete, because the method was words, but the words were a skin over motions, and the motions were what mattered, and he learned them the way he learned everything — by doing them once in the dark behind closed eyes.

The method drew soul power the way a hungry thing draws breath — not in the straight road the common manuals teach, but through the appetite, using the soul's own hunger as the pump. Where a common method circulates, this method devoured, and gave back more than it devoured, and the speed of it was the reason the church's knives were feared across the empire. And the hook of it was honest to the engine, the way hooks are honest to the fish that can see the whole line: the method fed the appetite it used, and the appetite grew with the feeding, and the grown appetite would want rawer things, and the rawer things were where the church wanted its knives to want.

*So,* said the man inside the child, turning the hook over in the dark of his mind the way one turns over a blade one has decided to keep. *The speed is real and the price is real, and the two come tied together. Very well. I keep the speed, and I pay the price in my own coin and not in theirs — the appetite is mine before it is theirs; it was born mine; it has eaten what I told it to eat ever since. Feed, then. But feed where I point, and the pointing is mine while they believe it is theirs.*

He sat with his legs folded under the last of the fire, and he began the Drawing-In of the Hungry Soul, and the forest's night came into him the way the book said the world's energy came to such souls — eagerly, and in quantity, because the soul that devours draws more than the soul that receives, and the fastness of it was a warmth like the draught's warmth but cleaner, and underneath the warmth the engine kept its ledger.

And he felt the gate.

It stood where the book said it would stand and where his own fullness said it stood — at the top of the tenth rank, a wall of ceiling over a soul already brimful, locked the way mouths are locked, not the way doors are, and it said what the wall says to every soul that comes to it full: *come up, and bring the price.* The price was a taken life, because the world's ladder is built of taken lives, and the ring that comes from the taking is the key and the proof and the debt all at once, and no amount of drawing-in would pass the gate, because the gate does not open to fullness — it opens to killing.

*Not yet,* said the man inside the child, to the gate, quietly and without resentment, the way one speaks to a wall one intends to climb. *I am not ready, and the readiness is a thing of its own time. But I am coming, and the price will be of my choosing and not yours — and when the vessel is proved and the hunt finds its moment, the ring will be of the dragon's own line, because like fuel changes me toward what I am, and strange fuel only fills, and I did not walk into the house of the forest to be filled.*

He slept then, with the fire's last warmth on his face, and somewhere in the depths of the forest the things with the blood of the dragon in them went about their night's hunger, and did not know him yet.

---

The morning's work was the body's work, and it was done at the forest's edge, because Mother Wei said the forest's edge was where the house's knives learned what they were for.

The children ran the yard's road — the carrying and the striking and the falling and the rising — and Luo Chen ran it with them in the body of a six-year-old, and the body did what the body had been trained to do since the orphan camps of the border wars, which is to work without complaint the way a good tool works, and the working drew eyes, because the house's children were measured every month of their lives and knew what a body could do, and what this body was doing was not what this body had any right to do.

He let some of it show and hid some of it, because usefulness is a ladder and its rungs are climbed one at a time in other people's sight, and a knife that shows all its edge on the first morning is a knife the house tests on hard things first. And while the body worked, the other part of him stood at the edge of the forest and felt it the way only that part of him could feel it.

The book had told him the forest's order — the Evil-Eye Tyrants in the deeps and their Emperor over them, the eight-hundred-thousand-year old watching eye that was the forest's king and the church's partner both. The telling had been a thing of words on a remembered page. The feeling was not a telling.

It stood in the deep places like a sky lying down.

It was not a pressure the way fighters' pressures are pressures, aimed and edged. It was a weight of attention, vast and slow and without interest, the attention of something so old and so strong that its mere being bent the world around it the way mountains bend weather, and every living thing in the forest lived inside the bending and had never known it unbent. The children of the house felt it as fear and called it the forest. Luo Chen felt it as shape, because the sea in him was deep for his years, deeper than any eye in this house had the instrument to sound, and deep water does not jump when the wind moves.

*There you are,* he said, in the private place, to the weight of attention in the deeps, the way one says it to a landmark one has been steering by for a long time on the strength of a map. *The book was right about you, then, and I am going to be right about the book. You and your sons and your core are the lamps I do not look at, and my road goes through your forest the way a careful road goes through a sleeping country — quietly, and without waking anything, and with the traveler's gratitude for the dark that hides him.*

And then, under the great weight, closer, in the ten-thousand-year places between the core and the fringes, something else moved in the feeling — smaller and sharper and hungrier, a shape of appetite that his soul answered before his mind did, the way a harp answers a note of its own tuning. The blood of the dragon in him woke to the blood of the dragon in the deeps, and the waking was older than the draught and older than the method — the recognition of kin, and his public soul stirred toward the deeps with a joy that had nothing soft in it, because the Devouring Dragon is a thing of hunger, and hunger is glad of hunger the way wolves are glad.

*Patience,* said the man inside the child, to the dragon in him. *The deeps are not for us yet. The fringes are for us — the hundred-year kinds at the forest's edge, where a first hunt can breathe, and where the wall of the tenth rank stands waiting with its price in its mouth. Our first ring comes from the dragon's own line, when the vessel is proved and the moment comes, and the paying of the price will be our own choosing and no one else's. That is the whole of the plan for now, and the plan is a direction of the heart and not a date on a calendar, and the heart is patient because it has been where it is going before, in another life, and knows that the road arrives.*

"New one," said Thirteen, beside him, and he turned his face from the forest's edge the way one turns from a thing one has not been looking at, and her eyes were on him with the watching the house had promised. "You were standing still. The yard's road doesn't stop."

"The yard's road goes in circles," said Luo Chen. "I was looking at the road that doesn't."

"The forest road doesn't go anywhere for us," said Thirteen. "It goes down for the hunt-master's hunts, and it comes up with the ring, or it doesn't come up. When you reach the wall — the wall at the top of the tenth rank — you go out with the hunt-master, and what comes back is what comes back. The boy before you who reached the wall was afraid, and he chose a weak ring out of the fear, and the house kept him a while and then sent him away somewhere the weak rings go, and the somewhere is not up. So when your wall comes, choose with the whole of you, because the house measures the choosing as much as the climbing."

"When does the hunt-master come," said Luo Chen.

"Month's end," said Thirteen. "Mother Wei will tell you when you're ready, and the telling of it is the last kindness the house gives before the forest gives the rest."

She went back to the yard's road, and Luo Chen went after her, and behind them the forest breathed at the edge of the light the way it breathed at everything, without welcome, and without end.

---

The evening cup came again, and the evening bell, and the fire, and the end of the bench, and the second day of the house closed over Luo Chen the way the forest closed over the road — completely, and as if it had always been closed. And in the dark behind the sleeping face, the man kept his ledger, because the ledger was the thing in him that the house would never keep.

*The house,* he wrote, in the private place, in the way of the writing that leaves no ink. *Mother Wei — the keeper; kind the way a glove is kind; she gives the seasoning as kindness, and the kindness is real and so is the seasoning, and the two are the same thing, and that is the shape of this church. Thirteen — the house's best knife before me; afraid of the little door, and the fear kept, which means the face kept, which means she will climb. Eight others, unnamed so far, and I collect taken things. The forest — felt, not told: the Emperor's weight in the deeps, untouched and untouchable; the dragon-kin in the ten-thousand-year places; the fringes for the first hunt. The wall of the tenth rank, standing with the price in its mouth. The hunt-master, month's end. The vessel, my nightly work, graded and honest. And over all of it the song of the draught, daily — today the second grain of the deafness.*

*Two grains,* he wrote last, with the humor he kept for his own private use. *The church sings to me every day, and I am learning its whole music, and the day is coming when it will sing its loudest and most loving song and find, where the song expects a door, a wall — built of every grain the church itself has given me, which is the only kind of wall I ever trusted, the kind the enemy pays for.*

The fire burned low, and the house slept, and the forest breathed, and the boy at the end of the bench slept with the sleep he was owed, and under the sleep the engine kept working, and the plans stood around him in the dark the way they stood — not as a schedule, not as a promise, but as the direction of the heart.

And far up over the forest the night sky stood with its stars in their places, and one of those stars was a lamp that did not shine on this place and would not shine on this place, and the man inside the child counted on that darkness the way sailors count on their stars, because the darkness of some lamps is the only road a careful boat can sail. Month's end was coming, and the hunt-master with it, and the wall of the tenth rank stood ahead on the road under the trees with the price in its mouth, and the hunger that was coming for the price walked on two legs, and was patient, and had plans.
