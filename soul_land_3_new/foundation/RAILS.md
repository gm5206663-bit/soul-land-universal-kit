## PROSE LAW — "write like canon, don't write boring" (merged house grammar)

Standing law, machine-checked where possible:

1. **SENTENCES:** average ≤ 25 words per chapter; no sentence over 60 words; no
   paragraph that is one monster sentence. Short concrete sentences, named things —
   canon Soul Land is plain and fast.
2. **HARD SCENE:** every chapter shows at least one event in real time (a forging, a
   fight, a test, a deal), moment to moment, not summarized.
3. **REGISTERS:** at least three of the kit's five registers per chapter (kit 07 §2).
   **Dialogue is mandatory** wherever humans share a scene (~8–10 spoken lines per
   thousand words). A chapter with no speech must declare why in its footer.
4. **WRITE IT ONCE / SCOPE LAW:** a beat goes on the page once. No paragraph that
   exists to explain a paragraph; no chapter that closes by re-telling itself; one
   line of consequence after a scene, not a summary. The author's standing word:
   *"write what needs to write, not everything — you can skip."*
5. **BUDGET:** target 2,400–3,200 words per chapter (hard floor 900, hard defect
   3,400+ unless the footer names the reason — a season's turn).
6. **NO OVERVIEW OPENINGS:** a chapter does not open on a season, a summary, or a
   status report. Open inside the scene, in motion. End scenes on a turn or a hook.
7. **THREAT AND FACE:** every chapter names what can be lost and who is pushing back.
   If the OC's only verb is "keep," the chapter is not written yet.
8. **LEARNING THROUGH FAILURE FIRST:** the record shows what was PAID, not what was
   contemplated. Mastery second.
9. **FIGURES OUT OF PROSE:** exact numbers live in the footer/STATUS_PANEL; prose
   carries felt weight ("his arms shook on the tenth hammer fall"), not spreadsheets.
10. **MOTIF = TEXTURE, NEVER ENGINE:** recurring images may flavor a chapter; the
    subject of a sentence is a thing or a body, not an idea. Never the same metaphor
    twice in one chapter.
