# STYLE_GOLD.md — what "like canon" (L-6) actually means, operationally

## THE SHAPE (the S1 episode rhythm — the template every slot shadows)
1. **The wronged:** a person with a small, human grievance loses the thing that day
   (the contest, the photo, the part, the credit).
2. **The akuma:** Hawk Moth's voice + the black butterfly; it bonds to the object the
   grievance is wrapped in (the parasol, the phone, the costume…).
3. **The deal:** always the same — become the best [X], but bring me the Ladybug and
   Cat Noir Miraculouses.
4. **The escalation:** the villain tests power in three rising beats (prank → property
   damage → city-scale threat), with a monologue or broadcast between them.
5. **Failure #1:** the heroes try the obvious thing; it fails on physics (the yo-yo
   slips on ice).
6. **Failure #2:** the combined attack; the wind throws them into a bus.
7. **The object:** Lucky Charm gives the improbable object (a bath towel).
8. **The plan:** a physics-honest scheme built from the object + the location's
   infrastructure (pipes, crane, billboard, HVAC).
9. **The purify:** the akuma's object breaks, the butterfly is caught, the damage
   unwinds (the towel repairs everything).
10. **The denouement:** a small domestic beat where the real story was all along
    (Marinette misses the shoot; Manon takes her place; everyone is a little warmer).

## THE TONE (canon, per user ruling)
- Warm and fast; comedy carries the tension, never replaces it.
- Heroes banter MID-fight (Cat Noir's puns and hand-kisses; Ladybug's swats).
- Kwami are tiny food-motivated personalities with big opinions.
- Villains are dramatic and theatrical; their monologues are genuinely entertaining.
- Destruction is real (cars, buildings, a city's weather) and rescue is the point —
  the trapped civilians (Alya & Manon in the ice) are the emotional center.
- Stakes stay personal at the ends: a missed photoshoot, a balloon, a hug.

## THE REGISTER (sentence level)
- Short action sentences in fights. One beat per line when the pace is high.
- Internal monologue is wry and self-aware (Marinette-class).
- Dialogue is spoken-English natural: contractions, interruptions, callbacks.
- No purple prose, no author voice, no meta (Z1 is a build failure).

## THE OC'S ADDITION (what we bring to the texture)
- The OC's talent beats are written as **witnessed excellence — his nature working
  on every single part of him**: the crowd notices, the camera finds him, the
  numbers are impossible but legal (strongest in the year, top of the class by a
  distance, the calm in the ice, the hands that hold a toddler steady while a city
  freezes). Human-level as his nature, never a superpower (L-14, FINAL 2026-09-04:
  the talent is his nature — part of him, dependent on him, working on every part of
  him; the "human ceiling" framing is REPLACED — do not use it).
- **The dial:** he shows 80%. The gap between eighty and one hundred is the season's
  slow reveal — moments where a room sees ninety, and someone writes it down.
- **Adaptation under pressure:** in a crisis the talent adapts him to the crisis —
  breath, hands, mind, three moves ahead; he is the steadiest, strongest, sharpest
  person in the frame, and the frame keeps his name.
- The OC's talent vocabulary (config: talent_vocab) is the fingerprint.

## THE OC IS A PERSON (the 2026-09-04 correction — how to actually write him)
*User ruling: "the OC needs to be another character — he should feel real, he has
everything the others have, he should be PART of the world; everything should feel
natural and real." This is the fix for writing him as a prodigy the narrator describes.*
- **A whole life, not a showpiece.** He has a home (the four-color boxes, his mother),
  a school (his seat, his group, his gate time), a thing he's into (the map in the
  notebook), small flaws (finishes sentences, the snort, always hungry, always cold),
  wants (to stop being the new kid; to tell his mother about the cuff), and small
  embarrassments. Give him the same KIND of life as Marinette/Adrien — the "everything
  the others have."
- **Never announce the talent.** The narrator does NOT say "the talent did its quiet
  work" / "that was the talent" / "the city adapted around him" / "the class was
  deciding what he was." The talent is his NATURE — show it through (a) how his
  body/mind/voice actually work in the moment (steady hands, a fast mind, a voice
  people trust) and (b) how OTHER people react (the vendor's second croissant, the
  class's silence, the camera finding him) — as lived reality, not commentary.
- **He is PART of the world, not an observer of it.** He's in the seat, in the group,
  at the gate, on the bench — a member, in the middle of things. Don't frame the world
  as looking at him from outside; frame him as being inside it.
- **Concrete, sensory, specific.** The four-color boxes, the croissant, the map in the
  notebook, the snort behind a cough, sleeves to the knuckles, the gate at eight fifty.
  Real dialogue: short, warm, funny, natural. Dry and practical, never braggadocious.
- **He is allowed to be not-amazing.** Tired, hungry, flustered, a little awkward,
  wrong, late, missing something, a little stung. The dial is how much of himself a
  room can hold — not a guarantee of perfection. A real 13-year-old has flat moments;
  write them.
- **Warmth is specific and small.** He finds Manon's face funny; he finds the red-haired
  girl who runs late cute and amusing; he tells his mother nothing yet. The big feelings
  live in the small, concrete gestures.

## THE VOICE LAW (upgraded 2026-09-04 — measured against ch1–3)
*Chapters 1–3 are the voice. Everything is measured against them — not "the early
chapters were charming and we grew out of it." Drift from them is the largest craft
failure.*
- **Third person, close on Keal.** He does not narrate to a reader; he is not writing a
  memoir. The narration is a story, not a record of a story.
- **No journal tics.** Banned outright (machine-checked, `config.json` `voice_tics`):
  "I would like you to notice" · "on the record" · "for the record" · "it's worth noting"
  · "as we will see" · "the answer arrived fully formed" · "his body computed" · "a picture
  assembled itself" · "the certainty came before the evidence." When a number matters to
  the scene it arrives through a named in-world object (a clock, a screen, a dial) — never
  narrated at the reader.
- **Scene and dialogue carry it.** If two people are in a room, they talk. ch1–3 run warm,
  fast, funny, with real stakes (L-6). The inner life is SHOWN — a decision, a physical
  beat, a line, something he does with his hands — not summarized.
- **Sentences vary; the short ones do the heavy lifting.** Mix long flowing sentences with
  two-word stops. Cut atmospheric-but-generic filler ("the air shifted", "fate changed
  forever") — mood is built from concrete, cause-grounded detail, not lyrical weather.
- **End on concrete.** A chapter closes on a concrete image, decision, or line — never the
  banned abstract register ("nothing would be the same", "the edge of something").

## CANON-FIRST, OC-WOVEN CRAFT (upgraded 2026-09-04 — how a chapter is built)
*The canon scene is the spine and the bulk of the chapter. Keal is a parallel thread
woven AROUND it — never the center, never a replacement.*
1. **Build on the real canon scene(s).** From the title-verified ore, make the actual canon
   beat the spine and the majority of the chapter — setting, every participant, the cause,
   the mandatory beats, the dialogue, the action, the consequence, the reveal-function.
   Never compress a canon scene into exposition.
2. **Keal runs as a parallel additive thread.** His own scenes run alongside the canon
   (his seat, his map, his hunger, his group); he intersects the canon naturally — by
   proximity, the classroom, the park — on his OWN beats, not by taking a canon character's
   beat. **Parallel ≠ duplicate.**
3. **Braid the threads.** Cut between the canon thread and Keal's thread with scene-breaks.
   **End by braiding** — a short forward-looking passage that ties both lines to what's
   coming, on a concrete image.
4. **Scene ownership is absolute.** Ladybug and Cat Noir own their iconic beats (the
   transform, the lucky charm, the de-transform, the rescue). Keal may witness, support,
   complicate, or run parallel — a canon character's defining action, line, or emotional
   climax stays theirs unless a recorded D-row has genuinely displaced its cause.
5. **Full scenes, never summary.** Every beat is dramatized: a concrete place, characters
   who want something, action/resistance/discovery, character-specific dialogue or
   meaningful silence, a change by the end, a consequence that connects to the next scene.
6. **Living, voiced ensemble.** The whole class/cast is present and individually voiced —
   each with role, voice, agenda, and knowledge limit. Humor comes from personality and
   familiarity, not generic one-liners. (This is L-17's perspective panels.)
7. **Restraint before the Cuff / before a reveal.** Show Keal's nature through instinct,
   competence, magnetism-hints, and an uncanny stillness — never active Miraculous powers
   before the BOUND beat, never a system voice, never a reveal slid into self-recognition
   (L-5, L-4, L-20).
8. **Dramatic irony through the dual thread.** Because the canon thread and Keal's thread
   run side by side, the reader sees more than Keal — the omen, the wronged person, the
   coming akuma — while Keal stays believably unaware. That is the engine of tension.

**Self-check before delivering a chapter:** Is the actual canon scene the spine and bulk?
Is Keal a parallel additive thread, not the center? Did Keal steal any canon beat? Is every
beat a full scene, not summary? Is the ensemble voiced and is there a non-Keal panel
(L-17)? Is Keal's power restrained where the locks hold? Does the chapter braid and end
concrete? If any answer is **no**, rebuild before the gate.
