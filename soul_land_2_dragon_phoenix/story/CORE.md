# The story and its protagonist

## The story in one paragraph

Yan Jingheng is a native boy born to Yan Shaozhe and Cai Mei’er. His two parental
martial-soul foundations, dragon–phoenix inheritance and Adaptation Talent have
developed within a secure Shrek upbringing, with real resources, teachers and
relationships. He meets Huo Yuhao for the first time while traveling with Tang Ya
and Bei Bei. Their different circumstances give the encounter weight: one boy
expects a home and help to be available; the other has learned to depend on
himself. Neither boy exists merely to prove the other’s worth.

This is **Soul Land II fanfiction**, not a continuation of another OC serial.
The working title is *The Dragon and the Phoenix*. Gaurav Meena is the author.

## Identity

**Yan Jingheng / 言景衡** is male, native-born and human. His surname follows his
father’s 言. There is no reincarnation, Earth education, remembered source novel,
internal system, guiding intelligence or missing-childhood explanation.

The [opening dossier](../OPENING_BUILD.md) gives his current physical description,
exact working age, power, equipment and trip. Those values are generated from
one [current record](../state/CURRENT_STATE.json), not independently kept here.
His age-appropriate body and ordinary appearance remain recognizably a child’s;
advanced development does not make him a small adult or justify sexualized depiction.

## How he behaves

Jingheng is securely loved and accustomed to competent people. He expects a
question to receive an answer, a correction or a genuine refusal. He respects
his elders without treating their titles as a reason never to speak. Around
trusted company he is lively, competitive and capable of humor or impatience.

He enjoys finding out why something works. In a workshop, that means examining
a failed part and trying a correction. In a fight, it means noticing distance,
stance and timing, then acting. He does not narrate an internal laboratory report
or silently diagnose every person he meets. His confidence comes from lived
instruction and results, not from knowing he is a protagonist.

Adaptation improves emotional regulation without removing pride, irritation,
fear, attachment or the desire to be praised. Native intelligence is not adult
experience. He can understand that poverty exists before understanding what
having no replacement clothes or reliable meal feels like.

## What can challenge him

He may offer a practical solution when the other person first needs a choice.
He may want approval for an invention whose implications are not yet understood.
A genuine disagreement can concern risk, ownership, incomplete information or
how help is offered. These are scene possibilities, not mandatory humiliations
or permanent social defects. A lesson that is learned must change later behavior.

Routine danger should become routine when his real abilities solve it. Tension
comes from the actual situation, not forgotten powers, manufactured starvation,
constant exhaustion or every hunt nearly killing him. Equally, strength cannot
supply private knowledge, the right spiritual interface or another person’s consent.

## Story commitments

- His family and prior development matter in present action.
- Canon characters keep independent motives, skills and choices.
- The first Yuhao meeting is with Tang Ya and Bei Bei; there is no prior contact.
- Existing Ma Xiaotao and elder bonds are already lived relationships, not new introductions.
- Ma’s future direction remains open. No pairing, engagement or compulsory sibling label is assigned.
- Childhood appears through relevant recollection, not an eleven-year opening chronicle.
- Changed causes can change outcomes. Canon is the narrative spine, not a machine forcing identical injuries and rescues.
