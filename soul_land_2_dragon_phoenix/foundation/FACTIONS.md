# Institutions and factions — authority, access and independent motives

## Shrek and the household

Jingheng belongs to a powerful academy family. This supplies resources, teaching
and social familiarity; it is not a private kingdom he commands. The parents,
Mu En, Xuan Zi and faculty retain duties and differing opinions. Actual workshop
results or unusual progress can affect those opinions through witnessed history.

Separate family permission, faculty supervision, student status, workshop access,
public achievement and formal certification. None automatically proves the others.
The opening design establishes a household-raised training/workshop pupil, not
an automatically enrolled inner-court student or a numbered professional title.
Formal later admission requires its own actual route; current ability is real
regardless of the absence of such an institutional badge.

## Tang Sect

Tang Ya and Bei Bei have their own relationship to its aims. Jingheng meeting
Yuhao beside them does not make him a member, inheritor or financier by default.
Membership would require an actual invitation, informed choice, applicable rules
and consequences for his existing commitments. Do not replace their agency with
an instant resource solution supplied by the deans’ son.

## Duke household / external power

Yuhao’s private history remains his. Jingheng does not acquire intelligence on
it from the reader’s opening scene. Later intervention needs a disclosure,
a motive, information and real political/institutional consequences.

## Hidden ancestral organizations

Ancestry is not membership, allegiance or contact. No secret delegation recruits,
trains or protects Jingheng during the pre-opening years in this foundation.
Research any later revelation on its actual clock; do not make every external
actor aware of hidden parentage or twins without a route of information.

Future faction outcomes remain conditional. Do not preselect reform, destruction,
allegiance or universal gratitude as a reward for the premise.
