# Tang Ya, Bei Bei and Huo Yuhao — the first meeting

## What is sourced

Novel receipts N03–N04 establish Yuhao’s stream/cooking encounter with Tang Ya
and Bei Bei and their later choice to follow him. The author puts Jingheng with
those two at his first meeting with Yuhao. No earlier encounter exists.

## Bei Bei — working prior acquaintance

The natural design is a known academy connection rather than strangers who
instantly trust the deans’ child. Their ease can show in a brief disagreement,
an understood joke or a practical division of attention. No fabricated shared
hunt, sect membership or kinship is accepted by this design. Bei Bei retains his
judgment and capability; he does not need to be made foolish to show the OC’s strengths.

## Tang Ya — her own participant

Her humor and immediate interest in the fish are part of the researched source
encounter. She also has her own goals. Jingheng’s presence does not turn her into
a second Bei Bei, an exposition device or someone automatically authorized to
recruit him into the Tang Sect. Their accepted shared academy/social acquaintance and trip arrangement live
in the current state; neither is a canon source event. Do not embellish them
with invented Tang Sect membership or an unrecorded shared hunt.

## Yuhao — a stranger with agency

A well-provided child may notice worn equipment or fatigue. He cannot identify
an abusive household, hidden parentage, innate reading or a future bond on sight.
An offer should leave Yuhao a choice. Food and conversation need not become a
rescue performance in which Yuhao owes permanent loyalty.

The first difference between the boys can be practical: how they speak of a
journey, what they assume can be replaced, or why someone hesitates before
accepting help. Do not make Jingheng gratuitously cruel or Yuhao incapable in
order to stage a lesson about privilege.

## Fourth-person logistics

Track who approaches, where each sits, how much fish is available, what is said,
what Yuhao withholds and who knows his intended route. The decision to follow
must include Jingheng’s motives and ability. No convenient absence is invented
to reproduce a later source injury. Novel 2.1–2.4 have now been read for forest consequences; later prose still
requires a specific changed-cause plan rather than automatic source staging.
