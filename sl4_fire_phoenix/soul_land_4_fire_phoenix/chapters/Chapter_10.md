# Chapter 10: The Building That Held Its Breath

Horizon Building did not look like danger from the outside.

It looked like a promise made out of glass.

The upper floors caught the daylight and broke it into pale silver strips. Flying cars curved around its parking levels in clean, obedient lines. Advertisements flowed across transparent panels, telling shoppers where to eat, where to buy clothes, where to watch demonstrations, and where children could jump until their laughter became the loudest thing in the room.

Lan Xuanyu pressed both hands to the car window.

"Mommy, is that it?"

"That is it," Nan Cheng said.

"It is too tall."

"Buildings are allowed to be tall."

"If I jump from the top trampoline, will I reach the roof?"

Nan Cheng looked at him.

Lan Xuanyu immediately sat properly. "I was asking for science."

"Science says no. Mommy says absolutely no."

The Soul Guidance flying car guided itself into a parking place. The windshield lit with arrows showing the shortest path to the shopping center entrance. Lan Xuanyu stared at every glowing sign as though the building had been created entirely for him.

He had trained until his body ached. He had eaten until Nan Cheng's grocery plans surrendered. He had heard a song that made his chest feel strangely hollow. Now there were trampolines.

Trampolines were simple.

You jumped.

You came down.

You jumped again.

No one asked what was inside your blood when you jumped.

No one asked why your right hand sometimes made people fall.

No one asked whether Teacher Nana's ancient technique hurt so much because it was powerful or because power always wanted payment first.

"World of Trampolines!"

The moment they entered, Lan Xuanyu's eyes brightened.

The place was wide enough to feel like an indoor playground had swallowed a sports field. Trampolines stretched in neat squares, some small and close to the floor, others deep and elastic enough to send children several meters high. There were sponge pits, a balancing rope, padded walls, and staff members performing flips that made nearby children cheer.

"Socks first," Nan Cheng said, catching his collar before excitement carried him past the staff desk.

Lan Xuanyu accepted the special trampoline socks with the solemn expression of someone being armed for battle.

"Anti-slip dots," he said, inspecting the bottoms.

"Yes."

"If I use footwork, will they still work?"

"If you use Teacher Nana's footwork in the trampoline park, Mommy will ask Teacher Nana to give you extra homework."

Lan Xuanyu placed one hand over his mouth.

Nan Cheng almost laughed.

After they paid and entered, Lan Xuanyu stepped onto the nearest trampoline and bounced once.

Then he vanished upward.

"Wah!" a child nearby shouted. "He jumps so high!"

Lan Xuanyu himself was startled. His body rose higher than expected, nearly three meters before he began to fall. The first landing wobbled, but training corrected him before fear could. His knees bent. His waist shifted. His arms opened just enough. He landed, sank, and the trampoline sent him up again.

The second jump was cleaner.

The third made him laugh.

Nan Cheng watched from the side, arms folded lightly, heart softening until it almost forgot to worry. She was a Spirit Emperor. She could jump far higher than this if she wished. Yet watching her son discover a safe imitation of flight felt better than showing him anything.

Lan Xuanyu turned in the air.

He had become heavier after training, but not clumsy. The new weight made the trampoline answer him more strongly, and his body learned the answer quickly. He bounced from one square to another, tried a turn, failed, landed on his backside, laughed, and climbed back up.

"Careful," Nan Cheng called.

"I am being careful!"

He was not being careful in the way mothers preferred.

He was being careful in the way children believed counted.

Several streets away, Yan Shuo was arguing with a measuring tape.

"It is shrinking on purpose."

Yan Ke took the tape from his child, stretched it across the padded forearm guard on the counter, and showed Yan the markings. "It is not shrinking. Your arm guard is thicker because your mantle control drills keep damaging the lining."

"Then the arm guard is growing."

"Equipment does not grow. Children grow. Bills grow."

"Bills should train less."

The owner of the training-supply shop chuckled behind the counter.

This arcade was not part of Horizon Building. It was smaller, older, and much less shiny, built along a lower commercial street where ordinary families came for repair kits, practice clothes, inexpensive protective gear, replacement grips, and food stalls that smelled better than the prices looked. Yan Ke preferred it because the owners knew him, the exits were clear, and the stock did not pretend that every child needed equipment made for a clan heir.

Yan Shuo preferred it because two shops down sold fried dough twists.

They had already bought replacement stitching thread, a padded shoulder wrap, two heat-resistant practice cloths, and no fried dough twists.

This was, in Yan Shuo's opinion, a grave failure of planning.

"If I hold the mantle closer, the cloth lasts longer," Yan Shuo said.

"Yes."

"If the cloth lasts longer, we save money."

"Also yes."

"If we save money, we can buy fried dough twists."

Yan Ke looked at him with the deep sorrow of a father who had watched logic become a thief. "One bag after we finish."

Yan Shuo brightened so quickly that the Dawnflame Kite stirred warmly inside his chest.

"Control," Yan Ke said at once.

Yan Shuo coughed and tucked the warmth back down before the shopkeeper could notice the tiny shimmer at his shoulder.

"It did not flare."

"It considered flaring."

"Considering is not a crime."

"It is a warning."

Yan Shuo wanted to object, but then the lights flickered.

Once.

Twice.

The floating advertisement panel above the shop door shuddered and went blank.

Everyone looked up.

The arcade's main guidance screen turned black, then flashed emergency blue before breaking into lines of static. A sound like a distant insect nest crawled through the Soul Guidance devices around them. Yan Ke's communication bracelet lit, failed, lit again, and displayed no signal.

The shopkeeper frowned. "That is strange. Backup should catch immediately."

Outside, someone shouted.

A second later, a siren began.

Not the clean school bell.

Not the public weather alert.

A real emergency siren.

Yan Shuo's spine straightened before he decided to be afraid.

At Horizon Building's hundredth floor, fear had a different face.

A man in a leather outfit walked with loose shoulders and a smile that did not belong beside security guards. His short bluish-gray hair and sunglasses made him look careless, almost bored. The guards in small mechas warned him away from the restricted elevator.

He raised both hands as if surrendering.

Then a black helmet appeared.

A silvery-white crystal on its forehead caught the light.

"Begin."

The word was quiet.

The response was not.

Invisible interference spread from the helmet. Outside the building, the roof of a large Soul Guidance transport split open, revealing a huge electronic device. Six Spirit Masters around it pressed their soul power into the machine until the invisible wave sharpened and drove itself into the upper floors.

Systems isolated.

Signals cut.

The Horizon Building, which trusted technology for almost everything, began to learn what happened when technology stopped answering.

Guards fell.

Panels opened.

Threads from a mechanical hand drilled into protected metal.

The man smiled wider.

"Start the invasion."

Elsewhere in the same building, staff shouted around dead screens. Elevators stalled. Internal access locks failed in ways no one had practiced because modern buildings preferred believing failure was old-fashioned.

On the 111th floor, the man in leather entered the think tank center as if arriving at a party.

"Don't shut it down," he said cheerfully when staff tried to cut the system. "That would make things troublesome. Why not just take me away?"

Then the helmet pulsed.

Staff collapsed one after another.

The world became clean for him.

He moved to the core control room, drilled through security, stripped firewalls layer by layer, and hummed to himself while data poured across the screen. Sixty-four layers of decryption did not frighten him. They entertained him.

The final barrier opened.

He expected wealth.

He saw zero.

For the first time since entering the building, the smile froze.

"We got fooled."

He ran.

The glass wall rushed toward him.

A voice stopped him before the impact did.

"What's the rush?"

The supervisor who had panicked earlier no longer looked like a supervisor. Heavy gray armor covered him like a mountain. Spirit rings rose beneath his feet, two purple and six black, pressing the air until Zhan Jing's bones remembered fear.

Ye Feng stood in the only escape route.

"Zhan Jing," he said. "City of Sin envoy. Master of mind control. Expert in committing crimes."

Around them, the staff who had supposedly fallen rose in standard Federal mechas and leveled Soul Guidance weapons.

For one breath, the trap looked perfect.

In the old supply arcade, nothing looked perfect.

People were trying to leave through the same doorway at once. The shopkeeper shouted that there were side exits. Someone knocked over a display rack. A child began crying because her mother had let go of her hand for half a second and half a second was long enough for terror to invent separation.

Yan Ke's voice changed.

It became the voice he used at work when alarms meant procedure, not panic.

"Yan Shuo, left wall. Keep your hand on my belt. Do not run ahead."

"Dad, what is happening?"

"We do not know yet. That means we follow safety rules instead of guesses."

A pale lantern rose behind Yan Ke's shoulder. His Signal Lantern martial soul did not blaze like a battle spirit. It steadied. Warm yellow light spread low along the floor, marking a path toward the staff corridor.

"This way," Yan Ke called. "Not the front entrance. Parents with children first. Elderly next. Do not push. There is another exit through the storage hall."

Some people obeyed because they recognized command.

Some obeyed because the light gave their feet something to believe.

Yan Shuo's heart beat too fast.

Horizon Building.

Lan Xuanyu was at Horizon Building.

He had told him.

The memory came with unfair clarity: Can we compare now? Adults will say no. Then we compare homework? I take back friendship.

"Dad," Yan Shuo said.

"No."

"I didn't ask."

"You were going to ask to go toward the tall building. No."

"Lan Xuanyu might be there."

Yan Ke's jaw tightened. "Then his mother is with him, and the police and military will go there. You are still a child. Your duty is here."

"But if—"

A ceiling panel cracked loose above the crying child.

Yan Shuo moved before the argument finished.

He did not run toward Horizon Building.

He stepped under the falling panel.

Dawnfeather Mantle opened close across his shoulders and forearms, not wide, not glorious, just a tight layer of fire-and-light feathers catching the sharp edge before it struck the child's head. Heat ran up his arms. The panel was not heavy enough to crush him, but its corner bit into the mantle and pushed him down to one knee.

The Dawnflame Kite fluttered hard inside him.

Close.

Hold.

Do not flare.

His breath found the root shape before fear could drag fire outward. Shoulders, ribs, back, ankle. The drill he hated arrived like a stubborn adult inside his bones.

Yan Ke caught the panel's side and shoved it away.

"Good," he said once, and the single word struck harder than praise usually did.

Yan Shuo's arms trembled.

The child's mother snatched the little girl close, sobbing thanks.

"Move," Yan Ke ordered gently. "Thank him after you are outside."

Yan Shuo wanted to look toward Horizon Building again.

Instead, he held the corridor door until the next group passed.

Protecting the people beside him felt smaller than running toward a disaster.

It also felt heavier.

At the 111th floor, Ye Feng learned that heavy things could still be taken from his hands.

Zhan Jing laughed after being cuffed.

He should have been afraid. His Spiritual Power was sealed. His storage device was taken. The Valiant Dragon Special Forces had him surrounded.

Instead, he looked at Ye Feng and asked whether the commander believed he would remove the handcuffs personally later.

Then he spoke Ye Feng's name.

His age.

His daughter's age.

Ye Feng's blood cooled.

The internal communication device rang.

The voice on the other end did not belong to his people.

"In the City of Sin, everyone calls me the King of Eternal Darkness."

The deeper trap opened.

There were 12,345 people in the Horizon Building. There were 160 criminals inside. Bombs were being installed at load-bearing points. The power system was down. Main exits were sealed. A barrier separated the upper layers. The commander had minutes to release Zhan Jing or let more than ten thousand hostages die with him.

Ye Feng's hand nearly crushed the device.

He had prepared for months.

The enemy had prepared for years.

When the lights went out at World of Trampolines, Lan Xuanyu was in the air.

For a heartbeat, he thought it was part of the game.

Then the darkness swallowed the room.

The trampoline vanished beneath him.

A familiar scent caught him before fear did. Nan Cheng's arms wrapped around him, carrying him down as screams rose around them.

"Power outage," Nan Cheng said near his ear. Her voice was steady because mothers sometimes built walls out of voice before their hands knew what to do. "Xuanyu, be careful."

Staff members shouted for everyone not to panic. Emergency lights glowed near the safety passage, thin and greenish in the darkness. People ran toward them anyway. Children cried. Adults cursed. Somewhere outside the play area, metal shrieked.

Then came the Soul Guidance gunfire.

Nan Cheng's face changed.

She knew that sound.

She dropped with Lan Xuanyu into the trampoline's soft dip and pressed him under her body. Ice formed across her back, a defensive shell in a room where children had been laughing moments before.

"Mommy," Lan Xuanyu whispered. "What is happening?"

"Don't be afraid," Nan Cheng said.

She did not say she was afraid.

The screams became fewer after an icy voice ordered everyone not to move. Searchlights cut through the dark. Black small mechas hovered overhead, their beams passing over trampolines, sponge pits, and trembling families.

Nan Cheng held her breath.

Ice protected.

Ice also shone.

The searchlights found her.

"Spirit Master. Kill."

The world became bright with death rays.

Nan Cheng moved.

Six purple rings rose around her. Ice fog burst outward, hiding her and Lan Xuanyu. An ice shield hung over their heads as she rolled, leapt, and drove toward the emergency exit she vaguely remembered. She was not a combat specialist. She had never wanted to be one. Defense had always been enough.

Enough for home.

Enough for ordinary danger.

Not enough for this.

A suffocating pressure waited near the exit.

Nan Cheng turned aside on instinct. Three ice walls rose.

A six-meter black mecha shattered them with a sledgehammer.

She used her strongest ice ring. Frost crawled over the mecha. For a breath, it slowed.

Then red light burned through the cold.

The hammer fell.

Nan Cheng curled around Lan Xuanyu and gave her back to the blow.

The ice shield broke.

Pain and blood filled her mouth.

"Mommy!"

Lan Xuanyu felt her weakness more clearly than he understood it. Something inside him went quiet and terrible. He slipped from her loosened arms and stood before her with his small body, arms spread, lips pressed tight.

Gold-patterned Blue Silver Grass wrapped around his right arm.

His left hand threw an ice awl.

It bounced from the black mecha without leaving a mark.

The cannon on its shoulder turned toward him and Nan Cheng.

"Do not hurt my mommy!" Lan Xuanyu shouted.

He was terrified.

He did not move away.

At the supply arcade, Yan Shuo heard a far-off rumble through the walls.

Not thunder.

Too deep.

Too wrong.

People outside screamed again. The crowd surged, then stopped because Yan Ke's Signal Lantern brightened along the floor and a nearby security worker finally found enough courage to help direct them.

"This way! Service exit! Keep moving!"

Yan Shuo's arms ached from holding the door. His mantle had withdrawn to a dim line at his shoulders, but the heat under his skin still wanted to run. Every distant sound pointed him toward the building where his friend might be.

He imagined Lan Xuanyu laughing in the air.

He imagined the lights going out.

He imagined nothing, because his mind refused to make the next picture.

"Dad," he said, smaller this time.

Yan Ke did not answer at once. He was guiding an old man around broken floor glass.

When the corridor cleared, he put one hand on Yan Shuo's shoulder and knelt enough to meet his eyes.

"If Lan Xuanyu is there, wanting to help him is not wrong. Running into an unknown terrorist attack with one ring and no plan is wrong. You understand the difference?"

Yan Shuo wanted not to.

His breath shook.

Inside his chest, the Dawnflame Kite pressed warmth against his ribs, not urging flight, not urging fire, only staying.

"I understand," Yan Shuo said, and hated how much it hurt.

Yan Ke squeezed his shoulder. "Then help me get these people out."

So he did.

On the 111th floor, Ye Feng stared at fire bursting from the middle of the building.

Walls, glass, and worse fell away in the explosion.

The King of Eternal Darkness laughed through the communication line.

Ye Feng's fists trembled.

He had no clean move.

A Spirit Douluo could break enemies. A Three-word Battle Armor Master could stand like a mountain. But mountains could not swing freely when ten thousand civilians were tied to their wrists.

"Let him go," Ye Feng said.

The words tasted like blood.

Zhan Jing's restraints were removed.

He walked back to the system like a performer returning to the center of a stage. The Horizon Building's connection to the central bank stabilized. Digital doors opened. A planted seed woke inside the data path.

Above the city, satellite signals warped.

An invisible battleship descended through distorted sky toward the roof.

City of Sin mechas rose through shattered windows, retreating toward the welcoming beam.

The plan had teeth inside teeth.

For the first time in his life, Ye Feng felt truly powerless.

In the trampoline park, the cannon fired.

Lan Xuanyu shut his eyes.

A familiar voice entered his mind.

"Don't be afraid."

The light stopped.

Sound stopped.

Fear stopped moving but did not disappear.

Lan Xuanyu opened his eyes.

The cannon beam hung in midair, bright and impossible. Around him, screams had become silent shapes. People were frozen with mouths open. Nan Cheng's hand reached toward him, terror fixed in her eyes.

A delicate hand stroked his head.

Silver hair shone in the halted light.

"Teacher Nana!"

Lan Xuanyu threw himself into her arms and cried because bravery had ended and he was allowed to be a child again.

"They hurt Mommy. Teacher Nana, Mommy is injured."

"Don't cry," Nana said, holding him. "Everything will be fine with me around. You were very brave. You stood before your mother. You are already a man."

Her voice was soft.

Her eyes were not.

The black mecha cracked without being touched.

The cannon beam vanished with it.

One by one, the airborne bandits shattered under crisp sounds like glass ornaments breaking in winter. Lan Xuanyu tried to look, but Nana covered his eyes with her palm and held him against her.

Then time breathed again.

Nan Cheng's cry tore free.

"Xuanyu!"

Lan Xuanyu ran to her. Blood still marked the corner of her lips. Nana knelt and placed a hand against Nan Cheng's back.

"You were really good," Nana said.

Cool power moved through Nan Cheng, easing the pain so quickly she could not understand it. Silver halos spread from Nana's body. Injured people across the trampoline park stopped moaning so loudly. Bleeding slowed. Broken fear found breath again.

Everyone stared at the silver-haired woman.

They had seen too much.

They understood too little.

Nan Cheng understood only one thing with absolute certainty: if Nana had not come, her son would be dead.

"Xuanyu," Nana said. "Come with me. We cannot let the people who hurt you go."

Nan Cheng nodded again and again before Lan Xuanyu could ask. "Go. You are safest with Teacher Nana. Mommy will be fine."

Lan Xuanyu hugged Nan Cheng once, then Nana.

"Teacher Nana, thank you."

"Hold tight," Nana said.

Silver light folded them away.

In the supply arcade, the last civilians reached the service exit.

The street outside was chaos held back by distance. Emergency vehicles screamed past. Traffic screens showed only warning colors. Far ahead, between two buildings, smoke marked the direction of Horizon Building.

Yan Shuo stood under the open sky and felt useless.

His arms hurt.

His shoulders burned.

A little girl's mother had bowed to him so many times that embarrassment nearly replaced fear, but none of that changed the fact that Lan Xuanyu might be inside the building where smoke climbed.

Yan Ke tried his communication device again.

No signal.

"Police channels are jammed too," a security worker said, pale. "This is not a normal outage."

No one laughed at the obvious.

Above Horizon Building, figures in black mechas streamed toward the roof.

A compact battleship floated there, sleek and dark, drinking them into a beam of light. Zhan Jing boarded last, laughing, relieved by the thought that speed and invisibility meant freedom.

The King of Eternal Darkness ordered retreat.

He did not want pointless fireworks. He named forces even City of Sin feared: War God Temple, Tang Sect, Shrek Academy. The city below was a prize, not a grave they wished to dig for themselves.

The battleship lifted.

Its engines roared.

It broke the sound barrier and blurred toward invisibility.

Silver light appeared on the roof.

Nana stood with Lan Xuanyu in her arms. The engine blast reached her and became a spring breeze.

She looked up.

"Want to go?"

The dark blue ring on Lan Xuanyu's thumb slipped to her palm without him noticing. Nana held his small right hand and made a simple throwing motion.

A dark blue shadow crossed the sky.

No one below knew what it was.

Most would never remember asking.

Near the edge of the city's emergency zone, Yan Shuo suddenly looked up.

He did not know why.

The smoke seemed to pause.

The sirens thinned until they sounded very far away.

Something vast and cold-bright moved above the city, too high for his eyes, too quick for thought. The Dawnflame Kite tucked itself close inside him. His fire did not rise. It folded around his heart like a chick beneath a wing.

Then the sky bloomed.

A fireball opened near the upper atmosphere, huge and silent for one impossible heartbeat before the sound arrived late. People around him cried out. Someone whispered, "Fireworks?"

Yan Shuo knew fireworks.

Fireworks were bright and noisy and friendly.

This was not friendly.

But the thought came anyway, soft at the edges, as if the city itself preferred it.

Fireworks.

Maybe fireworks.

A wave passed through Zi Luo City.

It was not heat. It was not wind. It brushed across fear, memory, panic, and pain with a silver gentleness that made adults blink and children stop crying for a breath. Yan Shuo felt it touch the place where his own warmth had crouched. His body wanted to stand firm, then forgot what it was standing against.

His father put an arm around his shoulders.

"Stay with me," Yan Ke said.

"I am," Yan Shuo answered.

He was not sure whether he had been about to step away.

On the roof, the dark blue ring returned to Lan Xuanyu's thumb as though nothing had happened.

"Teacher Nana, what was that?" Lan Xuanyu asked, looking at the fading fireball.

Nana smiled faintly.

"It should be the fireworks."

After that, the city remembered in fragments.

Military vehicles arrived in numbers that made streets feel smaller. Police cordons spread outward from Horizon Building. Bomb disposal experts entered with pale faces and steady hands. Injured civilians were carried out. Families found one another and cried. Officials spoke carefully, then more carefully, because careful words were sometimes the first tools used to build a wall around panic.

Nan Cheng and Lan Xuanyu spent hours answering questions.

They were released earlier than many because Lan Xuanyu was a child and Nan Cheng was injured. When they finally reached home, it was already noon.

Lan Xuanyu ran in first.

"Teacher Nana! Teacher Nana!"

Nana came from her room in the pajamas Nan Cheng had bought for her, silver hair tied simply as if she had spent the whole morning at home.

"You are back," she said.

Lan Xuanyu hugged her legs. "Teacher Nana, you are awesome. When can I become as strong as you?"

Nana smiled. "Train well. One day you will become strong."

Nan Cheng stood behind him, still shaken in ways healing could not reach. "Teacher Nana."

Nana gave her the same gentle smile. "It was nothing. It is good that both of you are fine. Rest."

Lan Xuanyu shook his head.

"No. I want to train. Teacher Nana, I want to become stronger."

Nan Cheng's lips parted, but she did not stop him.

She remembered the sight of her son standing before a cannon with his small arms open. She remembered wishing he would run. She remembered being proud and terrified at the same time.

"If you are not tired," Nana said, "then of course."

In the Yan home, the news began before dinner and did not stop.

Horizon Building had been attacked by terrorists from the City of Sin. The building had suffered severe damage. Hundreds of bombs had been found and dismantled. Casualty numbers rose from uncertain to unbearable as official confirmations arrived: 131 dead, over 800 injured.

The report said the terrorists had been captured.

It did not explain how.

It did not explain the sky-fire people had seen.

It did not explain why some witnesses remembered a silver-haired woman and then looked confused when they tried to say more.

Yan Shuo sat on the sofa with both hands on his knees.

"Lan Xuanyu was there," he said.

Yan Ke's communication device finally worked again in the evening. He had already tried the Lan family number twice, then stopped because emergency survivors did not need neighbors clogging their devices. A short message arrived after the news confirmed names would be released later.

Nan Cheng: We are alive. Injured but safe. Xuanyu is safe. Thank you for asking.

A second message came much later, shorter and unmistakably from Lan Xuanyu.

Lan Xuanyu: I am okay. Mommy is hurt but better. I need to train harder.

Yan Shuo stared at the words.

His thumb moved.

Yan Shuo: You are not allowed to be dead.

A long pause.

Lan Xuanyu: I know.

Yan Shuo: That is an important rule.

Lan Xuanyu: En.

Yan Shuo: Your mommy too.

Lan Xuanyu: En.

Yan Shuo: I helped people leave a shop. My arms hurt.

Another pause.

Lan Xuanyu: You were brave.

Yan Shuo swallowed.

He suddenly did not want jokes.

Yan Shuo: You too.

No answer came for a while.

Then:

Lan Xuanyu: I was scared.

Yan Shuo looked at the message until the letters blurred.

Yan Shuo: Brave people are scared. My dad says stupid people do not notice danger.

Lan Xuanyu: Your dad is smart.

Yan Shuo: Do not tell him. He will become unbearable.

Lan Xuanyu sent no smiling face this time.

Yan Shuo understood.

Some days did not make room for little pictures.

At the military command center, Ye Feng sat under light that made every wall colder.

He gave his account from the beginning: the months of preparation, the bait, the trap, Zhan Jing's capture, the King of Eternal Darkness, the hostages, the bombs, the explosion, the release order, the battleship, the impossible destruction of that battleship, and the communications returning.

He did not invent excuses.

He did not hide failure.

That was not enough to make the dead return.

"Commander Ye," the investigator said, "you will remain in isolation until the inquiry determines responsibility."

"Understood."

He stood, saluted, and walked back to confinement with a steadiness he did not feel.

He thought of Ye Lingtong.

He thought of telling her that not provoking was storing strength.

He wondered what strength meant when stored strength still failed to protect everyone.

At home, Ye Lingtong did not know the full story. Children of soldiers learned early that some silences had uniform edges. She knew her father was under investigation. She knew Horizon Building had burned. She knew people had died. She knew adults looked away when she entered a room.

She trained anyway.

White light wrapped her body closer and colder than before.

If Lan Xuanyu was a mystery, if Yan Shuo was becoming stronger, if her father could be taken away by duty and blame, then she would not stand still.

The next day at school, no one joked.

Even Huang Qifan kept his mouth closed when the teachers gathered both classes for the safety talk. Gong Yinghao's face was harder than Yan Shuo had ever seen it.

"A Spirit Master is not someone who runs toward every fight," he said. "A Spirit Master is someone who knows what can be protected, how, and at what cost. Some of you think a ring makes you heroes. Yesterday, adults with far more than one ring died or failed. Remember that."

Yan Shuo looked at Lan Xuanyu across the room.

Lan Xuanyu looked pale but calm. Too calm, maybe. Nan Cheng had not come to school with him; Teacher Nana must have sent him. Or perhaps his mother had insisted he go because normal days had to continue even when normal felt damaged.

Their eyes met.

Neither smiled.

Yan Shuo raised two fingers slightly beneath the desk.

Still alive.

Lan Xuanyu blinked once, then copied the gesture.

Still alive.

That was enough for the morning.

After class, Gong Yinghao checked Yan Shuo's arms.

"Bruising along the forearms. Light soul-power strain. Mantle edges held close."

"That sounds good," Yan Shuo said carefully.

"It is good."

"No extra drills?"

"Extra rest."

Yan Shuo stared. "Is this a trick?"

"Yes. The trick is that children heal better when they rest."

"Adults are learning new methods."

Gong Yinghao almost smiled. Almost. "Your father reported that you wanted to run toward Horizon Building."

Yan Shuo looked down.

"I didn't."

"Wanting to and doing are different. Yesterday you learned that. Do not forget it."

The words stayed with him longer than praise.

That night, Yan Shuo opened his training notebook.

He wrote the exact numbers first because exact numbers were anchors.

Rank 13.

Spiritual Power 39.

Spirit Origin Realm.

Dawnflame Kite: original acquisition paperwork exactly 100 years; private actual living age at this point is actual living age of 163 years, yellow tier.

Dawnfeather Mantle: improved, still unmastered. Emergency use: held close under falling panel. Arms hurt. No new rank/SP/ring follows from the emergency, but body memory and restraint improve.

He paused, then added a line beneath it.

When the sky burns, protect what is beside you first.

He frowned at the line.

It sounded too much like an adult sentence.

He almost scratched it out.

Then he left it there.

In the Lan home, Lan Xuanyu practiced until exhaustion dragged him down. Golden Dragon Shocks the Heavens hurt more after fear. It also mattered more. Each reversal of blood flow reminded him of the cannon. Each ache reminded him of Nan Cheng's blood. Nana guided him with even more care, but he could feel something quiet behind her eyes, something like leaving before the word had been spoken.

Three days passed.

Zi Luo City cleaned broken glass and hung black ribbons.

Horizon Building stood behind military lines, no longer a promise made of glass but a warning that reflected the sky.

In the Yan home, Yan Ke rewrote emergency contact cards and made Yan Shuo memorize three more evacuation rules. Lin Mei inspected his mantle control twice and said only that fear had not ruined his shape.

In the Ye home, Ye Lingtong waited for news of her father and trained until her knees shook.

In the Lan home, Nana sat quietly after Lan Xuanyu went to school.
