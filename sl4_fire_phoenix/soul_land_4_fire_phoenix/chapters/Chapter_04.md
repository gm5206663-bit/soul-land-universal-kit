# Chapter 4: The First Record

The first night after awakening taught Yan Shuo that having fire was not the same as using it.

He learned this by nearly setting his sleeve smoking.

It was not a large fire. It was not even a proper flame, if one asked Lin Mei, who had opinions about what did and did not deserve the dignity of being called flame. It was a red-gold shimmer that gathered around his fingers while he sat on the edge of his bed, too excited to sleep, trying to coax the Fire Phoenix back out because closing his eyes felt like abandoning it.

The shimmer crawled over his knuckles.

Yan Shuo held his breath.

The shimmer brightened.

A thin thread of heat kissed his cuff.

The cloth darkened at the edge.

His door opened.

Lin Mei stood there in silence.

Yan Shuo looked at the sleeve. Then at his mother. Then at the sleeve again, in case the universe had decided to hide evidence out of pity.

It had not.

"I was checking," he said.

"With your sleeve?"

"If it was strong."

"Your sleeve has submitted its report."

Yan Ke appeared behind her, hair still damp from washing, expression halfway between alarm and the helpless pride of a father whose child had done exactly the kind of foolish thing he had feared. "Do we need the suppression foam?"

Lin Mei did not look back. "If you spray suppression foam in my child's bedroom for a cuff mark, you will sleep in the hallway."

Yan Ke lowered the canister he had somehow already obtained.

Yan Shuo stared at it. "We have foam?"

"We have a responsible father," Yan Ke said.

"We have a nervous father," Lin Mei corrected. She crossed the room and sat beside Yan Shuo. "Show me your hand."

He obeyed at once.

His palm was warm, but not injured. The skin showed no burn. The heat had gone into the world before it harmed him, which was better than the opposite and still not acceptable as a habit.

Lin Mei turned his hand over. "What did I tell you?"

Yan Shuo lowered his head. "Fire listens to breath."

"And what were you doing?"

"Not breathing."

"You were breathing enough to live. Not enough to guide. There is a difference."

The Fire Phoenix inside him stirred, young and bright and far too pleased with itself. Yan Shuo felt it as a warmth behind his ribs, like a bird stretching in a room too small for wings.

"It wanted to come out," he said softly.

Lin Mei's expression gentled. "Of course it did. It just woke up. Everything new wants room. That does not mean you give it the curtains."

Yan Ke muttered, "Or the furniture."

"Or Father's paperwork," Yan Shuo added.

Yan Ke put a hand over his heart. "My child has turned cruel after awakening."

Lin Mei squeezed Yan Shuo's fingers once, bringing his attention back. "Again. A little warmth only. Breathe first."

He inhaled.

This time the warmth rose slowly. Not because it was weaker. Because he was listening. A small red-gold glow gathered under his skin, visible only where the room light touched his palm. It wanted to leap. His excitement pushed at it. His mother's hand held his wrist, not restraining, only reminding him that a body had edges.

He breathed out.

The glow steadied.

"Good," Lin Mei said.

The word settled deeper than the staff member's praise. The awakening center had called his martial soul promising. His mother called his control good, and that meant the fire had not only appeared; it had answered him.

Yan Shuo grinned.

The glow flared.

Lin Mei raised an eyebrow.

He panicked and breathed too hard. The glow vanished.

Yan Ke coughed into his fist.

"Do not laugh," Lin Mei said.

"I am not."

"You are laughing internally."

"Still not illegal."

Yan Shuo fell backward onto his pillow, exhausted and delighted. His sleeve smelled faintly of hot cloth. His hands tingled. The place inside him where the Fire Phoenix had awakened felt too large and too empty at the same time, as if something had opened a window in his chest and the whole sky expected to come in.

He did not know that in another apartment, his friend was sleeping under a stranger mystery.

He did not know that a dark ring had appeared on Lan Xuanyu's thumb in the night, born from forces no parent could file safely.

He did not know that Lan Xiao had woken to a question that had no good category, had reached toward the ring, and had been pushed back by a power that did not care about a father's fear.

He did not know that Nan Cheng, fierce enough to challenge a whole institute for her son, would hear about it and choose family over report again.

Yan Shuo knew only the smell of his sleeve and the warmth of his mother's hand.

That was enough for his world that night.

For Lan Xiao, the night did not end when the child slept.

The ring on Lan Xuanyu's thumb looked too small to ruin a life and too impossible to ignore. Dark blue, fitted perfectly, patterned with designs too fine for ordinary decoration, it sat on his son's hand as if it had always belonged there. Lan Xuanyu could not remember putting it on. It could not be removed. Touching it had forced Lan Xiao back with enough power to make his heart crawl into his throat.

He checked the windows. The door. The room logs. The floor. The air vents.

Nothing.

Nothing was worse than something.

Something had a handle. Something could be reported, contained, measured, negotiated with. Nothing meant the impossible had entered his son's room without passing through the world.

Lan Xuanyu watched him with sleepy confusion. "Father, is it bad?"

Lan Xiao turned back too quickly and put on a smile too gently. "Not if it does not hurt you. Does it hurt?"

Lan Xuanyu shook his head. "It looks nice."

Of course it did. Everything dangerous in their life had arrived beautiful.

Nan Cheng listened when he told her, face draining of color and then hardening around the same decision they had already made once. Do not hand him over. Do not let the child become a specimen. Watch. Guide. Hide what must be hidden until hiding harmed him more than exposure.

It was not the clean decision a scientist wanted.

It was the only decision a father could live with.

Morning training began anyway.

Lan Xiao taught his son the foundation cultivation method that generations of spirit masters used because steadiness mattered more than speed. He placed his palms on Lan Xuanyu's back, guided spirit power slowly through tiny meridians, and spoke with patience he did not feel. Lan Xuanyu followed as well as a child could, trusting the warmth behind him, trusting the voice that said do not resist.

For a while, the world behaved.

Then Lan Xuanyu released his martial soul again.

Blue Silver Grass rose from both palms.

Two white rings rose with it.

Lan Xiao forgot how to breathe.

One ring without a soul spirit or hunt would already have been enough to overturn common sense. Two rings, one for each side of a spirit that looked like one spirit and did not behave like one, was worse. The grass had changed too, faint gold on one side, faint silver on the other, patterns so subtle an ordinary examiner might dismiss them and so impossible that Lan Xiao's mind ran through recultivated spirit beasts, twin spirits, mutation theory, ancient bloodlines, and every explanation that collapsed as soon as he touched it.

Lan Xuanyu only looked at the rings and asked what the circles were.

A father could break under that kind of innocence.

Lan Xiao did not break.

He sat on the floor of the cultivation room with his son and chose, again, the narrow path between truth and safety.

"You must not show everything casually," he told Lan Xuanyu.

"Why?"

Because the world is not always kind to miracles.

Because I do not know what you are.

Because I love you more than my answers.

Lan Xiao said, "Because some things are better learned slowly. Father will help you."

Lan Xuanyu accepted this with the open trust that made lying feel like swallowing glass.

Across the housing district, Yan Shuo was also learning slowly.

His lesson began with standing still.

He hated it.

"How can standing be training?" he demanded.

Lin Mei stood opposite him in the practice room, hands folded behind her back. "Badly, if you keep talking."

"Fire does not stand."

"Fire that cannot stand becomes smoke."

That sounded wise enough to be unfair.

Yan Shuo planted his feet on the mat and glared at the wall. The Fire Phoenix stirred whenever his attention touched it. It wanted motion: leap, flare, flash, show. His soul power, newly awakened and still shallow, answered emotion before discipline. If he imagined wings, his shoulders warmed. If he imagined Lan Xuanyu asking to see, his hands glowed. If he imagined Ye Lingtong from the housing courtyard saying rank mattered more than spirit shape, the heat sharpened in a way Lin Mei saw before he did.

"Again," she said. "Breath down. Warmth in the chest. Do not throw it into your hands."

"But the hands are where it comes out."

"Today. Later, who knows? Do not let the first door convince you the house is only one room."

Yan Shuo frowned because that was another sentence he would understand later and dislike now.

Yan Ke watched from the doorway with a tablet full of school documents. "The academy replied."

Yan Shuo lost the breath immediately. The practice room warmed.

Lin Mei clicked her tongue. "See? One sentence, and your fire runs to the window."

Yan Shuo dragged the warmth back with great effort and greater embarrassment.

Yan Ke waited until Lin Mei nodded. "They accepted his preliminary record. Fire Phoenix, attack category. He will be evaluated again during registration."

"Same academy as Xuanyu?" Yan Shuo asked.

"Likely, if the transfer documents stay clean."

"Same class?"

Lin Mei answered before Yan Ke could soften it. "Probably not. Your martial soul is attack type. Xuanyu's public record is control type."

The disappointment hit so fast the Fire Phoenix folded inward.

The room cooled by a fraction.

Lin Mei saw that too. This, more than excitement, mattered. Fire that only knew pride was easy to train. Fire that withdrew from hurt could become dangerous in quieter ways.

"Different class is not different friendship," she said.

Yan Shuo looked at the mat. "But if something happens?"

"Then you learn what happened before you run at it."

"If he needs me?"

"Then be someone useful enough to help, not someone dramatic enough to arrive first."

Yan Ke lowered the tablet. "That may be the best sentence anyone has said in this house."

"Write it on your safety forms," Lin Mei said.

"I might."

Yan Shuo did not smile. He was thinking about Lan Xuanyu's pale face after awakening, about the pressure behind the sealed door, about grass and fear and the promise of together after. He did not know his friend's real problem. That ignorance made him want to stand closer, which was exactly why his mother was teaching him not to move before understanding.

He breathed.

The Fire Phoenix unfolded again, not outside him, but enough that his chest warmed with steadier heat.

"I will be useful," he said.

Lin Mei's eyes softened. "Good. Start by standing properly."

He groaned.

She smiled.

Life took on a rhythm after that.

Lan Xuanyu trained privately under Lan Xiao's careful eye, learning the foundation method, learning not to display both sides, learning that adults sometimes asked children to hide for reasons larger than a child's shame. His white rings remained unimpressive to anyone who only valued color.

Before the academy registration, Lan Xiao made the lie simple enough for a child to remember and painful enough for a father to say twice.

If a teacher asked, Lan Xuanyu was seven, not six.

If anyone questioned why he had entered a year late, the answer was illness.

Lan Xuanyu had looked up with clear eyes and asked whether good children were allowed to lie. Lan Xiao had told him that some lies existed to protect people.

So his public story became clean enough to pass through registration: one year older on paper than his true age, gifted but not impossible, a Blue Silver Grass mutation with a low-level ring and enough promise to enter a good academy without setting the Federation on fire.

Yan Shuo trained openly.

That was the difference between them.

When Yan Shuo released a faint Fire Phoenix aura under Lin Mei's supervision, neighbors heard about it by dinner. When the academy confirmed his attack-type placement, Yan Ke received congratulatory messages from people who had never cared about facility safety until facility safety produced a promising son. When Lin Mei corrected his posture in the public practice room, two parents watched from the hallway and whispered about phoenix-type spirits.

Visibility had its own pressure.

At first Yan Shuo liked it.

Then a boy from the housing block asked him to show the phoenix three times in one afternoon, and another child asked whether he could burn through a metal railing, and a parent Yan Shuo barely knew told him he had to become a great battle armor master one day. By evening, the word phoenix felt heavier than it had in the awakening room.

He found Lan Xuanyu by the courtyard fountain, arranging toy cars along the edge.

"Everyone wants me to show," Yan Shuo said, dropping down beside him.

Lan Xuanyu considered this. "Do you want to?"

"Sometimes. Then not when they ask."

"Say no."

Yan Shuo stared.

Lan Xuanyu carefully adjusted a blue car. "You taught me."

The unfairness of being taught by his own teaching left Yan Shuo speechless.

Lan Xuanyu looked proud of himself.

Yan Shuo groaned and lay back on the soft courtyard ground. Above him, the protective dome filtered the evening into warm gold. Beside him, Lan Xuanyu hummed under his breath, and the old heaviness that sometimes lived around him brushed the air like a sleeping beast's breath.

Yan Shuo's chest tightened.

He noticed now. Not in words, not as knowledge, but as pattern. Sometimes near Xuanyu, especially when Xuanyu was tired or thinking about things he could not say, the world pressed differently. Yan Shuo did not like it. He also did not fear it the way he had on awakening day.

He breathed through his nose.

The warmth in his chest steadied. The Fire Phoenix held its shape inside him, small and proud, refusing to scatter.

Lan Xuanyu looked over. "Are you tired?"

"A little."

"Because phoenix?"

Yan Shuo almost said yes. Then he thought of his mother's eyes when she asked if something hurt. "Because day."

Lan Xuanyu nodded as if days were known enemies.

"I have a ring," he said suddenly.

Yan Shuo sat up. "You got one?"

Lan Xuanyu held up his hand and showed the dark ring on his thumb.

Not a spirit ring. A finger ring. Yan Shuo leaned closer before remembering not to grab other people's things. It was dark blue, subtle, and strange enough that his Fire Phoenix went very still.

"Where from?"

"I don't know."

"You don't know?"

Lan Xuanyu shook his head. "It was there. Father says don't worry."

Yan Shuo studied his friend's face. Lan Xuanyu seemed not worried because his father had told him not to be. That kind of trust was strong and fragile at the same time.

"Does it hurt?"

"No."

"Then good."

"It won't come off."

Yan Shuo revised his answer. "Then maybe good."

Lan Xuanyu laughed.

A week later, registration brought a different kind of pressure.

Heaven Luo Elementary Spirit Master Academy did not look like Yan Shuo's old kindergarten. It rose in metal and glass, efficient and polished, with lifts, testing stations, teacher lines, security gates, and parents wearing clothes that made Yan Ke stand straighter. Soul guidance cars moved through ordered traffic outside. Children entered with records, expectations, and martial souls that would become public categories before they had even learned what those categories meant.

Yan Shuo held his mother's hand until he saw other children looking. Then he let go and pretended he had not needed to hold it.

Lin Mei allowed the lie because it harmed no one.

Ye Lingtong entered the waiting area like someone already certain the floor belonged under her feet.

Yan Shuo noticed her before her name was called. She had curly brown hair, light green eyes, and a chin lifted high enough to challenge weather. Her father stood beside her in military uniform, broad-shouldered, stern, the kind of man whose silence made people arrange themselves better.

When her test result appeared, the teachers' attention sharpened.

High innate power. Dragon-line mutation. A father with rank and reputation.

The room learned her name quickly.

Yan Shuo felt the Fire Phoenix stir, competitive before he had permission to be. He pressed his toes against the floor inside his shoes and breathed.

"Do not measure yourself from another child's mirror," Lin Mei said quietly.

"I wasn't."

"Your shoulders were."

Yan Ke looked at Yan Shuo's shoulders, then wisely decided not to comment.

Yan Shuo's own registration came with less silence and fewer shock waves, which was both disappointing and relieving. Fire Phoenix made the examiner sit straighter. His innate level made the record bright enough to matter and not bright enough to become a storm. His Spiritual Power score, twenty-three points, earned a second look but not a whispered alarm; it was still Spirit Origin Realm, good and steady rather than strange. Attack category settled beside his name. Recommended focused training. Strong fire compatibility. Good future potential.

Useful words.

Heavy words.

Then Lan Xuanyu's turn came, and the room changed again.

Not because of flames. Not because of a dragon girl's pride. Because the test showed what the public record needed it to show and still enough to make parents turn their heads.

A child registered as older, standing at a level that made teachers pause.

Lan Xuanyu looked polite and a little confused by the attention. Lan Xiao wore calm like armor. Ye Lingtong watched from the side, and Yan Shuo saw the moment irritation entered her face: her father had looked at Lan Xuanyu gently.

Yan Shuo did not understand adult rank well, but he understood wanting a parent's eyes.

Ye Lingtong's anger had a lonely edge.

That did not make it kind.

Inside the school, before the class allocation finished, Yan Shuo was pulled toward the attack-type line. Lan Xuanyu went toward the control-type group. Their hands did not touch before the split. They only looked at each other across moving children.

"After," Yan Shuo mouthed.

Lan Xuanyu nodded.

Then Ye Lingtong stepped into Lan Xuanyu's path.

Yan Shuo saw only part of it. A lifted chin. A hand reaching toward Lan Xuanyu's face. Lan Xuanyu saying something too soft to hear. The girl's fingers pinching harder than play. Lan Xuanyu stepping back, one hand to his cheek, eyes wide with hurt more than pain.

Heat flashed under Yan Shuo's skin.

He took a step.

Lin Mei was not there to stop him. Yan Ke was outside with other parents. No adult hand landed on his shoulder.

But his mother's training had left a hand anyway.

Learn what happened before you run at it.

He stopped after one step, shaking with the effort of it.

A teacher guided the children apart before anything more could happen. Ye Lingtong turned away with proud anger. Lan Xuanyu stood there rubbing his cheek and looking honestly confused, as if meanness were a rule he had not been taught.

Yan Shuo wanted to cross the room.

The attack-type teacher called his name.

He went where he was told because the first day of school was full of rules and breaking the first one would not help Xuanyu.

But at lunch, he found him.

Lan Xuanyu sat with two control-type classmates who had already discovered that he was easy to like. Yan Shuo approached carrying his tray with the focus of a small soldier crossing hostile territory. The classmates looked at his uniform mark, then at his face, then made room because children recognized intensity even when they did not understand it.

"Your cheek," Yan Shuo said.

Lan Xuanyu touched it. "It doesn't hurt now."

"She was mean."

"I told her."

"What?"

"I don't like mean people."

Yan Shuo stared, then nodded slowly. "Good."

Lan Xuanyu brightened. "Father said I can refuse."

"I said first."

"You did."

This restored some justice to the world.

Across the dining hall, Ye Lingtong sat with the attack-type children, back straight, expression fierce. She caught Yan Shuo looking and lifted her chin as if daring him to say something.

He looked away.

Not because he was afraid.

Because his mother had also said being useful was better than arriving first. Today, useful meant sitting with Xuanyu until the confusion left his eyes.

Classes began.

Attack-type children learned quickly that having an attack spirit did not mean attacking whenever they wanted. Their teacher made them introduce martial souls, then spent half the lesson explaining control, range, stamina, and the difference between courage and stupidity. Yan Shuo felt personally targeted by several examples.

When he released a faint Fire Phoenix aura under supervision, the other children reacted exactly as the courtyard children had: admiration, questions, challenge, envy. The flame rose behind him in a young red-gold outline. It did not become wings. It did not fill the room. It stayed close because he kept breathing and because Lin Mei's training held in his bones.

"Good restraint," the teacher said.

Yan Shuo glowed more from that than from the fire.

Then the teacher added, "You will need it. Fire users who trust talent too much burn out early."

The glow became less comfortable.

In the control-type classroom, Lan Xuanyu released his Blue Silver Grass when asked. A white ring circled it. The teacher used him as a demonstration because public records made him the class goal: a child already across the first threshold, a visible ring, a spirit skill waiting to be understood. The other children saw an ordinary plant made special by mutation and effort.

Lan Xuanyu saw grass he still did not understand.

At the end of the day, the two boys met at the school gate while their fathers found parking excuses and their mothers' messages arrived through wrist devices.

"What did you learn?" Yan Shuo asked.

Lan Xuanyu answered seriously, "Ranks. Rings. My grass is ordinary but not ordinary. White ring can become better if upgraded."

Yan Shuo frowned. "Your grass is not ordinary."

"Teacher said ordinary type."

"Teacher is not your grass."

Lan Xuanyu considered this with deep respect. "Oh."

"I learned fire users burn out if stupid," Yan Shuo said.

Lan Xuanyu looked worried. "Are you stupid?"

"No!"

"Then good."

Yan Shuo opened his mouth, closed it, and decided friendship required forgiveness.

Lan Xuanyu smiled at him.

The hidden heaviness brushed Yan Shuo again, softer than before, almost lost beneath school noise and evening air. His breath caught, then steadied. He was tired from standing properly, from not running at Ye Lingtong, from holding his Fire Phoenix close while everyone looked. The pressure made his chest warm and his stomach hollow.

He breathed anyway.

Fire did not bow. It did not need to shout that it refused.

It simply kept its shape.

Lan Xiao arrived first, smiling down at them with eyes that still carried private sleeplessness. Yan Ke came a moment later, complaining about the school's parking logic with the passion of a man who had found a safe target for all his anxiety.

The boys parted at the gate.

Different classes.

Different records.

Different secrets.

The same promise, carried now into a wider world.

That night, Yan Shuo did not try to burn his sleeve.

He stood on the mat while Lin Mei watched, released a glow no larger than a candle around his palms, and held it through three slow breaths.

On the third breath, the Fire Phoenix inside him stopped pushing toward the window and settled behind his ribs like a young bird willing, for now, to learn the shape of its nest.

Across the district, Lan Xuanyu slept with a dark ring on his thumb and two white rings no public record would ever fully explain.

School had begun.

So had the difference between what the world recorded and what the children truly carried.
