## Part 1: The Cemetery

The trees over the wall were part of it.

That was the first thing Lin Hao noticed, and he noticed it because he had been assuming for about forty metres that he was walking past a park, and then the wall ended and the trees went on in rows, and the rows had stones in them.

**Heaven Dou Public Cemetery.**

Large trees, orderly, arranged between the gravestones the way a thing is arranged by somebody who had a plan and had the time to carry it out. A few people scattered through it, paying respects. Nobody speaking.

Wu Zhangkong's robes went behind him in the wind, and Lin Hao understood the word *spectre* for the first time in his life, because the man walking in front of them did not look like somebody visiting. He looked like something that had always been there and had only now been noticed.

It was Wulin's first time in a place like this. Lin Hao could tell, because Wulin's eyes went everywhere at once — which was what Wulin's eyes did when he was somewhere he had not decided how to be yet.

Wu Zhangkong did not stop.

He walked past row after row, past the centre, and he did not slow down or look around or do any of the things people do when they are trying to find something, and Lin Hao understood about sixty metres in that he was not finding anything at all.

He knew exactly where he was going.

He had been here before.

---

## Part 2: The Stone

It was just off the centre, and it was tall, and it had a few simple words on it.

**The grave of Long Bing.**

Lin Hao read it, and then read it again, because the name did not go with the stone, and he could not immediately work out why.

*Long Bing.* It sounded like a girl's name.

"Wait here," Wu Zhangkong said.

And Lin Hao stopped breathing for a second, because he had been listening to Wu Zhangkong for four months and he had never — not once, not in a corridor, not at a grave, not in front of a bear — heard that voice.

It had **thawed.**

It was not warm exactly. It was the voice of a man who had put something down.

Wulin moved to the side without being asked, because he was Wulin and because he could hear it too.

Wu Zhangkong took a **white cloth** out of his sleeve.

---

## Part 3: What He Did

The stone was not dirty.

That was the part Lin Hao would remember for the rest of his life, and he would not be able to explain it to anybody, because it was such a small thing.

There was a thin layer of dust on it. A thin one. The kind that gets on a stone in a week, or maybe two, in a city with old trees over it. It was not dirty. It did not need cleaning. Anybody walking past would have looked at that stone and seen a stone that was fine.

Wu Zhangkong cleaned it.

He cleaned it from the top down, with the white cloth, and every movement was — Lin Hao did not have a word for it, and he had a great many words, and none of them were the right one — **careful.** Not slow. Careful. The way you handle something that is not fragile but that you have decided is.

He cleaned it until it was **sparkling clean as jade.**

And then Lin Hao looked at the other stones, in the rows on either side, and he understood what he was looking at, because this one was **brighter.** Not cleaner. Brighter. It had been cleaned like this many times, over a long period, by somebody who did it the same way every time, and stone remembers that.

Wu Zhangkong stood there for a while with the cloth in his hand.

He did not say anything.

He did not say anything for long enough that Xie Xie shifted his weight, and Gu Yue put a hand on Xie Xie's arm without looking at him, and Xie Xie stopped shifting his weight.

Then Wu Zhangkong folded the cloth, and put it back in his sleeve, and turned around, and his face was his face again.

"Come along," he said.

---

## Part 4: What Nobody Said On The Way Out

They did not ask him.

That was the thing Lin Hao would be proudest of, later, and he would not be proud of it at the time, because at the time he was simply too astonished to speak.

Nobody asked. Not Xie Xie, who would have asked a stone where it came from. Not Zhang Yangzi. Not Wulin, who was the worst of them at not asking, who asked everything, who had asked Wu Zhangkong about the other continents in a corridor four weeks ago and been told to drop it.

They walked out of Heaven Dou Public Cemetery in a line behind a man in white, down a street with no tall buildings in it, and nobody said a word about **Long Bing**, and Wu Zhangkong did not thank them for it, and did not need to.

About four streets on, Gu Yue said, without turning her head, "He has been there before."

"Yes," Lin Hao said.

"How many times."

"I don't know." And then, because the not-knowing had been bothering him for four streets: "The stone wasn't dirty, Gu Yue. The moss was on the *north* face and the rest of it was clean, and there's no rain in this city that falls only on one side. Somebody has been cleaning that stone on their knees for long enough that they've worn a place to kneel, and they've been cleaning the part that doesn't need it, because the part that needs it would mean admitting there's a reason to be there at all."

Gu Yue was quiet for long enough that Xie Xie, three paces ahead, turned around to check they were still there.

"That's the longest thing you've ever said to me," she said.

"Yes."

"Say less of it to anybody else."

"I had worked that out."

And Wulin — who had been walking at the back with his hands in his sleeves and who had not said a word since the gate, and who had asked Wu Zhangkong about the other continents in a corridor four weeks ago and been told to drop it — caught up and walked between them for six paces, and said, "My mother has a stone."

Neither of them said anything.

"It's not in a cemetery. It's in a yard, because we couldn't afford the cemetery, and she goes out to it on the ninth of every month and she *talks* to it, and she has been talking to it for five years, and I have never once been able to work out whether she thinks he can hear it." Wulin looked at the street. "I used to think she was doing it wrong. I've been thinking she was doing it wrong for five years. And today I watched a man with six rings clean a stone that was already clean, and I would like somebody to notice that I have stopped thinking she was doing it wrong."

Xie Xie, from three paces ahead, without turning around: "Are we doing the feelings thing. Because I'll come back. I'll come back and I'll do it properly, I just need to go and stand near a wall first."

"Go and stand near a wall," Lin Hao said.

"I'm going to stand near a wall."

"We know."

And Xie Xie went and stood near a wall, about eleven metres on, and waited there with his arms folded and his face turned up to the sun like a person who had been asked to hold something, and did not say one thing about it for the rest of the day.

"Many times."

"Yes."

"The stone was not dirty."

"No."

Gu Yue was quiet for a while.

"I have never seen him do anything twice," she said, "except that."

## Part 5: The Sculptures

The Tang Sect's hall in Heaven Dou City had statues in it, and Wulin stopped in the doorway.

Lin Hao understood why in about two seconds, because he had seen the face before, in a room with no windows, four days ago, attached to a cauldron and one soul ring.

She was carved in stone and she was older, and she was holding a flute.

"**Xiao Xiao,**" Wulin said. "The Flute Cauldron Douluo."

He was not guessing. He had known the stories since before he knew what a martial soul was, the way everybody in Glorybound City had known them, and he was reciting now, in the voice of a boy who had been given something he had wanted for a very long time and was trying very hard not to be strange about it.

"Twin martial souls. **Three Cycles Soul Cauldron** and **Nine Phoenix Flute.** She was in the Spirit Pagoda founder's generation of the Shrek Seven Monsters." He looked along the row. "So these are the rest of them."

⚠️ *The Seven are named as history and as sculpture. No living person's origin is named. The lock holds.*

And then he got to the middle of the row and he stopped being able to speak at all.

"**Tang San.**"

The **Thousand Hands Douluo.** Leader of the first generation of the Shrek Seven Monsters. **Founder of the Tang Sect.** He had Bluesilver Grass, and there was a statue of him in a hall in a city with no skyscrapers, and Wulin stood in front of it with his mouth slightly open and did not close it for about eleven seconds.

Lin Hao stood next to him and did not say anything clever, which was possibly the most difficult thing he had ever done.

Then Wu Zhangkong, from behind them, in the flat voice:

"The first generation founded it. The generation ten thousand years later rebuilt it — **Huo Yuhao**, the Spirit Ice Douluo, who founded the Spirit Pagoda and pulled the Tang Sect back out of a crumbling husk." He looked at the statues. "The federation does not regard them kindly. The Spirit Ice Douluo led his comrades to force the Sun Moon Empire into a peace treaty, and empires do not forgive being made to stop."

There was a silence.

"That's why it's in this city," Wulin said. It was not a question.

"That's why it's in this city."

---

## Part 6: What Lin Hao Understood

He understood it on the walk back, and it was not a large understanding, and it undid something.

He had spent four months being afraid of Wu Zhangkong.

Not frightened — he had stopped being frightened around the time of the bear, because fear is a thing you run out of. He had been *careful*, which was different and worse, because careful is a thing you have to keep doing. He had kept a ledger of the man the way he kept a ledger of everything else: the flat voice, the folded arms, the nine seconds, the *"you are going to have to be smarter than the thing that comes for them,"* the white robes and the nothing in his hands.

He had built a complete model of Wu Zhangkong.

And the model had not had **Long Bing** in it.

Lin Hao walked down a street with no tall buildings and understood that he had made the exact mistake he had made with the Hall of Heroes, which was the mistake he kept making, which was that he had taken a **record** of a person and assumed it was the person.

The record said: cold, exact, demanding, kind in the only way he had.

The record did not say: **has been cleaning the same stone for years and does not need to.**

He had taken a dead man's record apart in four seconds and been told afterwards that the Hall had run out of files on him. He had spent four months against a living man's record and been too clever in exactly the same way, and nobody had told him, because nobody had known.

He wrote it that night.

**Wu Zhangkong cleaned a stone that was not dirty, and his voice thawed, and none of us asked him, and that was the right thing and I only did it because I was too astonished to speak.**

**I have been keeping a record of him for four months and the record was wrong, and the way it was wrong is the same way the Hall of Heroes was wrong: a record is what a thing did, and a person is what a thing does when nobody is scoring.**

Then he looked at the page and added the line at the bottom.

**Long Bing.**

*I don't know who that is and I am never going to ask, and I think that is the first thing in this book I have decided not to find out.*

He closed it.

The **hawk-gold lock** at his temple lay across his cheekbone, and the **marks on his forearms** were faint under his sleeves, and the hawk turned over in him — **one thousand one hundred and eighty-six** — and did not lean toward anything.

Lin Hao lay on his back above a forge in a city with no skyscrapers, and thought about a stone that was sparkling clean as jade, and about the fact that he had three rings and could not think of a single thing they were for that anybody he loved needed.
