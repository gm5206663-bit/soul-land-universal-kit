## Part 1: What The Last Day Was

The last day of the tournament was not difficult.

Lin Hao had been building toward it for thirteen days, and the last day was not difficult, and the not-difficult had become a pattern, and the pattern was the part he did not know what to do with.

Class zero won.

The shape worked. Xu Xiaoyan's starwheel made five seconds happen, and Gu Yue put earth and fire into the five seconds, and Xie Xie put the Twin Dragon Storm into the part after, and Wulin's Bind held the rest. Lin Hao used the Track twice and did not need it a third time, because the third time he used the stroke Gu Yue had given him at four in the morning nine days ago.

He had never used it in front of anybody. He had practised it eleven times in a field behind a dormitory and taken the tops off two bars and a post, and each time it came out of the Stormbringer quieter than the one before, and he had begun to suspect that the quiet was not the technique getting worse but the technique getting *finished*.

It left the sword with no sound in it at all. Forty metres of air stopped being one thing and became two, and the man at the end of it went down with his shield in two pieces and no idea what had come through it, and the stands — who had come to watch a harbour-city team be decent — went quiet in the particular way a crowd goes quiet when it has been shown something it has no word for.

Gu Yue, eleven metres off, said nothing. Her hands were at her sides and her chin was up a fraction. Xie Xie, flat on his back in the part after with his daggers on his chest and his lungs somewhere else, turned his head.

"She taught you that?"

"Yes."

"In nine days."

"Before the light."

"I have known her since the spring," Xie Xie said, to the sky, "and she has never taught me *anything*, and I have asked, and I want it noted that I have asked *repeatedly*."

Four minutes and six seconds.

Then it was over, and there was a ceremony, and a cup, and Lin Hao stood on a stage in Skysea City holding the cup and did not know what to do with it.

## Part 2: What The Invitation Was

It came the next day, in a room, from a man Lin Hao had not met, and the folder was not thin.

That was the first thing he noticed. The folder was not thin.

"You have three purple rings at ten years old," the man said. "You're a fourth-rank Grandmaster. You have a movement that isn't a movement, and a man with a thin folder offered you a fifth-rank teacher and you turned him down. I've read the report about that. I'm not going to offer you a teacher."

Lin Hao waited.

"Shrek Academy," the man said. "All five of you. Not one. Not two. Five."

Nobody spoke.

"I'm offering it rather than waiting for you to ask," the man said, "because I've decided you work things out fast, and I'd rather say it first."

"That's the third person who's said that to me," Lin Hao said.

"I know. I read the report."

---

## Part 3: What He Had To Say

He found them on the seawall, because the seawall was where they went, and because he had started to understand that going to find people was itself a thing worth doing.

Wulin was eating dried fish. Xie Xie was up on the wall with his legs in the air. Gu Yue sat with her hands in her lap. Xu Xiaoyan was sitting down, because that was what she did. Xu Lizhi was eating a pig trotter and had not been invited and had come anyway.

"They've offered all five of us Shrek," Lin Hao said.

Nobody said anything for a while.

"That's the whole thing," Xie Xie said at last, upside down. "That's the thing everybody wants."

"Yes."

"And you said it like it was a problem."

"I said it like it was a thing," Lin Hao said. "I didn't say it was a problem."

Gu Yue turned her head a few degrees.

"You're going to say no," she said.

"I don't know."

"That's the sixth time."

"I know."

"You're going to say no," Gu Yue said, "and I'm saying it rather than letting you work it out, because I'd rather be the one who says it first. I learned that from you. I didn't know I was learning it."

---

## Part 4: What He Said

He thought about it for eleven seconds, which was a long time for him, and then said the thing he had not planned.

"I don't know what I am," he said. "I've said it six times and it's the true answer. Three men have told me they're not upset about it. A man with a thin folder offered me a teacher and I said no. A man with a thick folder has offered all five of us the thing everybody wants. I've been handed four things before I could hold them in four weeks, and every person who's handed me one had already decided what I was."

He stopped.

"Shrek would decide what I am too. They're better at it than anybody — they've been doing it eight thousand years. And I'm not saying no because I don't want it. I want it. I've wanted it since I was six and my father put a hammer in my hand and didn't explain a thing."

He looked at the sea.

"I'm saying I don't know yet. Not-knowing-yet isn't the same as no, and I haven't been able to tell the difference for four weeks, and I'd like to be able to."

Xu Lizhi, from the end of the wall, with the pig trotter, said, "That's the most annoying thing anybody's ever said."

"I know."

"I'm going to go anyway."

"I know."

"I'll tell them you said not-yet."

"I'd like that."

## Part 5: What Wulin Said

He stayed after the others had gone, because Wulin stayed, and because Lin Hao had started to understand that Wulin staying was itself a thing.

"I'm going," Wulin said.

"I know."

"I'm telling you rather than letting you work it out."

"I noticed."

"I'm going because my father makes machine repairs and my mother cooks and I've wanted it since I was six, and because you taught me that saying a thing is the same act as thinking it, so I thought it and I'm saying it." Wulin ate the dried fish.

He did not say the other reason. He never said the other reason, and Lin Hao had known what it was for two years, because you cannot watch a boy put every coin he earns into a tin for four hundred Fridays and not work out what the tin is for. Na'er. Shrek was in the centre, and Shrek had eyes in eleven cities, and Eastsea was a harbour at the edge of the map where a girl could be taken and nobody would think to look twice.

"I'm also going," Wulin said, "because it's further inland than this."

"I know," Lin Hao said.

"That isn't—"

"I know." Lin Hao let it sit for a second, because he had learned that the silence after a thing like that was the part that did the work. "You don't have to say it for me to have it. I've never once asked, and not asking has cost me nothing, and I've started to think not asking is the only decent thing I've done in this city."

Wulin was quiet.

"You're coming," he said.

"I don't know."

"You're coming," Wulin said. "You've said *I don't know* six times and *no* once and *not-yet* once, and I've known you since we were three, and I'm telling you you're coming. I'm telling you rather than asking, because I've decided the telling is the same act as the thinking, and I didn't know I was going to say that until I said it."

Lin Hao looked at the sea.

"That's the first time you've told me something I didn't know," he said.

"I know," Wulin said. "That's the tenth time."

And then Wulin said the thing he said about once a year and never on purpose.

"Do you remember the wall behind your father's yard."

"Yes."

"I came off it when we were four and you caught me, and you were nowhere near strong enough to catch me, and we both went down, and you broke your wrist." Wulin was looking at the harbour rather than at him, which was how he said the large ones. "And you told your mother I'd come off it too, so she wouldn't be cross with me, and you were four years old, and I have never in my life worked out where you got *that* from."

"I got it from you," Lin Hao said. "You'd have done it."

"I'd have done it," Wulin agreed at once, because it was true and because he had never once in seven years pretended otherwise about anything. "But I'd have cried about it afterwards."

"You'd have cried about it *before*."

"Both," Wulin said. "Honestly. Both."

Xie Xie, upside down on the wall, said, "I want it noted that I've been in this class two years and I've learned more about the two of you in the last forty seconds than in the whole rest of it, and I've decided I don't want to know anything further, and I'm going to stay upside down for a while."

Gu Yue said nothing. But she had been watching Lin Hao's hands, and she noticed that they went very still at the word *wall*, and she put it in the place where she kept the things she was not going to use — which was a small place, and which had never before held anything she had to think about putting there.

Because Lin Hao had broken his wrist at four and lied about it inside the hour to protect a boy he had known one year, and had never mentioned it since, and had gone on to become what he was, and she understood — with a clarity she did not enjoy — that everything she had ever measured about him she had measured from the outside, and the inside of it had been finished before she was born.

Wulin was quiet a moment.

"You're coming," he said.

"I don't know."

"You're coming."

## Part 6: What Lin Hao Wrote

That night, in the room that smelled of the sea, he took the book down and wrote the number first, because the number never asked anything of him.

*Two hundred and sixty-four.* It had been two hundred and ninety-four. Nine points had gone somewhere between four minutes and six seconds and one conversation on a seawall with a boy eating dried fish who told him something he did not know. He could not say which had done it. He had written that four times now, and the writing-it-four-times was the only honest part.

*One thousand three hundred and ninety-one.*

*Thirty-six. It came on the last day, in four minutes and six seconds. I told Wulin first. I didn't plan to. Not planning it has become the most common thing about me.*

*Shrek has offered all five of us. I said not-yet. Not-yet isn't no. Wulin told me I'm coming — the first time anybody's told me something I didn't know — and it came from Wulin and not from a man with a folder.*

And then the line at the bottom, which he wrote last because it was the one he had been circling all week.

*I have been handed four things before I could hold them. A sword with an opinion. A Track that isn't a movement. A fourth rank in eighteen days. And Shrek.*

*Three men have told me they're not upset. A man with a thin folder asked me a question after I said no. A man with a thick folder read the report about that and didn't offer me a teacher.*

*And Wulin told me I'm coming. He's the only person who's ever told me something I didn't know, and he learned it from me, and didn't know he was learning it, and I didn't know I was teaching it. It's the thing I've done that I'm most proud of, and I didn't do it on purpose.*

*Zhou Zhangxi and Yun Xiao aren't going. They were never in class zero. Zhou Zhangxi said on the seawall that he isn't upset, and Yun Xiao said from behind him that he is, and that Zhou Zhangxi is lying, and they're both telling me the truth in different ways and I haven't worked out which to believe.*

*Zhang Yangzi and Wang Jinxi aren't going. They transferred out — packed after the exam and went to another academy, the way Wei Xiaofeng went back to his class before them. Wang Jinxi said nothing when he packed. Zhang Yangzi said eleven things and I wrote down nine. Wei Xiaofeng hasn't spoken to any of us since the tournament and I haven't thought about him in twenty-six days.*

He closed the book.

The lock at his temple lay gold across his cheekbone, and the marks on his forearms were raised and had been raised for thirty-six days, and the hawk turned over in him — *one thousand three hundred and ninety-one* — and did not pull toward anything.

Lin Hao lay on his back in a city that smelled of salt and hot metal, and thought about a father who made horseshoes, and about a thin folder and a thick folder, and about the fact that he had said not-yet, and about the fact that Wulin had told him he was coming, and about the fact that he was not frightened.

He slept.
