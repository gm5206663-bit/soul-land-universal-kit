## Part 1: The Sixth

Class zero had five students and one teacher, and it had had five students and one teacher for most of a semester, and then on the first morning of the new term there were six students, and the sixth was a girl Lin Hao had never seen.

She was standing in the doorway with a bag over one shoulder and the expression of a person who had rehearsed an entrance and was finding that the room was not shaped like the entrance.

**Xu Xiaoyan.**

She had a **Starwheel Ice Staff**, and she had two rings, and she had transferred in from somewhere with better resources, and she looked at the five of them in the room that smelled of floor polish and floor polish only, and Lin Hao watched her do the arithmetic on the room and understood from the way her face went that the arithmetic had not come out the way she wanted.

"Teacher Wu," she said. "I'd like to test my spiritual power again. At night."

Wu Zhangkong turned to look at her.

"Does the night affect your spiritual power?"

"Yes."

It was, Lin Hao understood, the first time anybody in that room had volunteered a secret to Wu Zhangkong.

"Alright," he said. And then: "Combat test. In pairs."

---

## Part 2: What Gu Yue Said

"Tang Wulin with Xu Xiaoyan," Wu Zhangkong said. "Xie Xie with Gu Yue."

Xie Xie went silent, in the particular way Xie Xie went silent when he was working out whether he had been insulted.

Gu Yue frowned.

"Teacher Wu, that's not fair."

Everybody looked at her, because Gu Yue did not say things like that. Gu Yue said accurate things. *Fair* was not a word she used.

"We have two rings," she said. "They only have one. You should pair a one-ring with a two-ring. I'll go with Wulin, and Xie Xie can pair with Xu Xiaoyan's Starwheel Ice Staff, because Xie Xie's soul power is the highest **of the four of you** — and I did not say of the five of us, and nobody in this room has said that out loud before."

Xie Xie made a sound.

"Just how much do you loathe me," he said.

"I'm being accurate."

"You're being *murderous.*"

Lin Hao put his hand up.

He had not decided to. That was becoming a pattern, and he had begun to suspect that it was not indecision at all but the thing his body did when his head had already finished.

"There's a problem with the arithmetic," he said.

Wu Zhangkong looked at him.

"Explain."

"Gu Yue's right that the rings are uneven," Lin Hao said. "She's wrong about who has how many." He held up three fingers, because it was easier than saying it, and because he had learned that in a room like this the visual version landed faster. "I have three."

There was a silence.

Xu Xiaoyan's face did something.

"That's not—" she started.

"It's all of them purple," Lin Hao said. "And the spirit soul is at one thousand three hundred and forty-four years, and Wu Zhangkong has said *I don't know* twice about how that happened, and I'm telling you this now rather than in four minutes when you find out in a fight, because I would rather you knew."

Xu Xiaoyan looked at Wu Zhangkong.

Wu Zhangkong did not say anything, which was the confirmation.

---

## Part 3: What Wu Zhangkong Did

"Then the pairing changes," Wu Zhangkong said.

"That's not what I asked for," Gu Yue said.

"I know what you asked for." Wu Zhangkong folded his arms. "You asked for fairness. There isn't any available, because there is no one in this room who can be paired with Lin Hao and have it be fair, and I have spent four months discovering that, and I would like the six of you to discover it in one morning rather than four months." He looked at Xu Xiaoyan. "You will pair with Tang Wulin, because you are new and he is the only one of them who will not take the pairing as an insult. Xie Xie with Gu Yue. And Lin Hao—"

He stopped.

And Lin Hao watched him stop, and understood that this was the third time in four days that Wu Zhangkong had arrived at a place where the answer was not available, and that the arriving was beginning to be a habit.

"—will take all three of you," Wu Zhangkong said, "one at a time."

"That's not a test," Xie Xie said.

"It's the only test I have."

---

## Part 4: What Happened

Xie Xie went first, because Xie Xie always went first, and because he had spent six hours the week before working out fourteen ways to handle something and had decided that the only cure for that was to be in front of it.

He lasted nineteen exchanges.

That was, Lin Hao understood afterwards, the longest anybody had lasted against him in four months, and the reason was not that Xie Xie had got better, though he had. It was that Xie Xie had spent six hours **thinking about him**, which was the thing nobody else did, because everybody else adapted in the moment and Xie Xie had adapted in advance.

"You've been planning," Lin Hao said, afterwards, from the floor, because he had gone down and had not got up yet.

"Six hours."

"It worked for nineteen exchanges."

"It worked for nineteen exchanges and then you did the thing with the wrist that I planned for and then you did a *different* thing with the wrist." Xie Xie sat down beside him. "I have a new complaint, which is that you are not consistent, and inconsistency is the only thing I cannot plan around."

"I'm consistent."

"You're consistent about being inconsistent."

Gu Yue went second, and Gu Yue was the only one who did not lose.

He wants it noted, and he wants the noting to be exact, because the exact version is the one that frightens him. She did not beat him. She could not — she is rank twenty-one and he is rank thirty-three, and there is a realm between those two numbers, and Wu Zhangkong had explained to the class once, flatly, the way he explained everything, that **the wall every ten ranks is not a bigger step, it is a different kind of thing**, and that a Soul Grandmaster who beats a Soul Elder has done something that does not happen. So she did not beat him. She did not touch him and he did not touch her and it lasted four minutes, which was **nine times longer than anybody else in that room had lasted**, and at the end of it he was on his knees with his soul power scraped to the floor and she was standing, and the reason was the only reason she has ever had.

She is the one he cannot read.

*"That is the fourth time,"* she said.

*"You haven't beaten me."*

*"No,"* she said. *"I've outlasted you. Four times. You can read everybody in this room, and you cannot read me, and every one of those four times you spent more than you had because you were still trying."* And then the thing she actually came to say: *"no, you're reading. I told you those are not the same thing."*

Wu Zhangkong, from the doorway: *"That is not a win for her and it is not a loss for him. It is the only honest result available between those two, and I want both of you to notice that I am calling it honest."*

Xu Xiaoyan went third.

She lasted eleven seconds, and she did not say anything afterwards for a while, and then she said, "I thought one month was plenty of time to prove myself," and Wu Zhangkong said, "so did I, about a different thing," and she looked at him and did not understand, and Lin Hao understood, and did not say.

---

## Part 5: What Long Hengxu Did

He came to the room at the end of the morning, which was not a thing he did.

Lin Hao had met Long Hengxu four times and had spoken to him once, and the speaking had been about a class assignment, and the Director had the manner of a man who ran a very large institution and had learnt that most problems could be solved by assigning somebody to a room.

He looked at the six of them.

He looked at Lin Hao for rather longer than he looked at anybody else.

"I have spoken to the President," Long Hengxu said. "The board has been questioning the resources we put into this class since two of you left." He said it to all of them and looked at Wang Jinxi's empty desk while he said it, and nobody mentioned it. "So we are going to display your strength. There is a tournament, and you are going to go, and you are going to be decent, and you are going to garner some prestige for this academy."

"And if we're not decent," Xie Xie said.

"Then the board will stop funding you, and you will all go back to the classes you came from, and I will be right and the President will be annoyed, and neither of those outcomes will affect any of you as much as you think." Long Hengxu folded his hands. "You are young. Be decent."

And he left.

And Gu Yue said, to nobody in particular, "He is not telling us the whole thing."

"He's telling us the part that's ours," Wu Zhangkong said, from the doorway, where he had been standing for the whole of it. "The part that is his is that he has bet his own standing on six children, and he would like us to know that, and he is not going to say it, because he is a Director and Directors do not bet."

---

## Part 6: What Lin Hao Wrote

He wrote it that night, above the forge, and he wrote the number first out of habit, and there was no number, because nothing had been measured, and he noticed that he had written a dash where the number went, and that the dash bothered him, and that this was worth writing down.

*No number this week. I wrote a dash and the dash bothered me and I would like to know why, because I have never cared about the number before, I have only cared about the record, and those are not the same thing either.*

*Gu Yue told me I read and I do not know the difference, and today she was right twice — once about the rings and once about me.*

*Xu Xiaoyan thought one month was plenty of time to prove herself and Wu Zhangkong said so did I about a different thing, and I understood him and she did not, and I did not tell her, because I have decided that noticing the things I don't say is as important as noticing the things I do.*

Then the line at the bottom.

*Long Hengxu has bet his standing on six children and is not going to say it.*

*Wu Zhangkong has bet something too, and he has said it twice, in a corridor and in a forest, and both times in the form of the words I don't know.*

*I am the sixth. I have three rings and a hawk at one thousand three hundred and forty-four and a track I have learned badly and a sword that has stopped being a tool, and there is a tournament, and I am the thing the arithmetic was not written for.*

*I am going to be decent. And then I am going to be more than decent, because I have seen what standing in front of costs, and there are six of us now, and I would like to be one that somebody can stand beside.*

He closed the book.

The *hawk-gold lock* at his temple lay across his cheekbone, and the *marks on his forearms* were raised and had been raised for a week, and the hawk turned over in him — *one thousand three hundred and forty-four* — and did not pull toward anything.

It was waiting.

And for the first time, so was he, and he had begun to suspect that the waiting was the same waiting, and that this was worth noticing too.
