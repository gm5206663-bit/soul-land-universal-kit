## Part 1: What Xie Xie Had Built

Wu Zhangkong had said *"it's the only test I have"* and had then not come, which was, Lin Hao decided, also part of the test.

They practised in the training field behind the dormitory, because the training field was free and because nobody in class zero had ever been given a place to practise that was not free.

Xie Xie went up.

Lin Hao had fought Xie Xie eleven times and had a ledger of every exchange, and he knew the Light Dragon Storm the way he knew the grain of a bar — the shape of it, the timing, the particular way it committed. He had an answer for it. He had had an answer for it since the fourth time.

The Light Dragon Storm came, and he answered it.

And then the **Shadow Dragon Storm** came at the same time.

Lin Hao went down.

Not badly. He went down the way you go down when something you have an answer for arrives with a second thing attached to it, and the answer you have is for one thing. He was on his back in the training field with the sky above him and Xie Xie standing over him looking both pleased and slightly sick, because the Twin Dragon Storm cost what it cost.

"**Twin Dragon Storm,**" Xie Xie said. "Light and Shadow merged. I built it last month."

"I noticed."

"You noticed after."

"Yes."

"That's the point." Xie Xie sat down beside him, which he did when he had done something he wanted acknowledged. "Who wouldn't feel pressured to improve in that room? You've got three rings and a realm on every one of us and Gu Yue outlasted you and Xu Xiaoyan lasted eleven seconds and I have been the fourth-best thing in that room for a month, and I decided I would rather build something than be fourth."

Lin Hao looked at the sky.

"How long did it take."

"Three weeks. And I threw up twice."

"That's the most impressive thing anybody in this academy has done."

"I know," Xie Xie said. "I've been waiting for somebody to say it."

## Part 2: What Mu Chen Wanted

The soul communicator rang that evening, and Lin Hao looked at it for a moment before answering, because he did not own one and had borrowed Xie Xie's, and because the person calling was not a person who called.

"**Lin Hao.** Come and see me when you're free. I have something to talk to you about."

**Mu Chen.**

His voice was lower than usual. That was the part Lin Hao noticed, because he noticed, and because Mu Chen's voice was the voice of a man who had been calm in front of him for eight months and had never once been anything else.

"Yes, Teacher. I'll come now."

"Mn. I'll be waiting."

Lin Hao put the communicator down and stood in the room for about four seconds, because he had worked out that a man does not call you at that hour in that voice to tell you something good, and because he had learned from Wu Zhangkong that the space between knowing and saying was where most of the things that mattered lived.

Then he went.

---

## Part 3: What He Was Offered

Mu Chen's forge was on the fourth floor of the Skysea Alliance's smithy, and it was quieter than the floors below it, and the quiet was the kind that cost money.

"You're forging second-grade," Mu Chen said. He did not look up. He was looking at a bar on his anvil. "At third rank."

"Yes."

"How long have you been making second-grade."

"About three weeks."

"And before that."

"Fourth-grade. For eight months."

Mu Chen put the bar down.

"Do you know what the gap is between those."

"Yes. I don't know the mechanism."

"There isn't one." Mu Chen looked at him for the first time. "That's what I want to talk to you about. There isn't a mechanism. You watched something and your hands changed, and I have been a fourth-rank Grandmaster for eleven years and I cannot tell you what you did, and I have decided that not knowing is not a reason to stop."

He turned the bar around on the anvil.

"**Fourth rank,**" he said. "**Grandmaster.** I'm putting you up for it."

Lin Hao did not say anything for a moment.

"I'm not ready."

"No."

"You just said—"

"I said I'm putting you up for it." Mu Chen said it in the calm voice, which was somehow worse than the low one. "Not that you're ready. Those are different, and you're the only student I've ever had who would notice the difference, so I'm not going to pretend I didn't mean both." He picked the bar back up. "The test is in four days. It has two parts. You know the first — refine to a standard. The second is new to you."

"What is it."

"**The Halo.**"

---

## Part 4: What The Halo Was

"A Thousand Refined metal is metal that has been given life," Mu Chen said. "You know this. You did it at nine, which I was not present for and which I have opinions about."

"Yes."

"The fourth rank asks a different question. Not *can you give the metal life* — that's the third rank, and you can, and it stopped being interesting the day you did it at nine." Mu Chen put the bar down again. "The fourth rank asks whether the metal can hold a **shape** while it's alive. Whether the life you put in it will keep a form you gave it, without you holding it there."

"And the Halo."

"The Halo is what it looks like when it does." Mu Chen said it the way he said everything. "A ring of light around the work. Not bright. Not impressive. It's the metal saying it has decided to be what you asked it to be, and it's going to keep being that, and it does not need you any more."

Lin Hao thought about it.

"That's the same thing as the sword," he said.

Mu Chen looked at him.

"Explain."

"Wu Zhangkong told me a sword that has something to say is not a tool," Lin Hao said. "He said the difference between a blade and a sword is that a sword has an opinion. And I've been making blades for four years and one of them stopped being a blade about six weeks ago, and I did not make it stop." He stopped. "The Halo is the metal having an opinion. That's the same thing. It's the same thing in a different material."

Mu Chen was quiet for a while.

"That is either the smartest thing a student has ever said to me in that chair," he said, "or the smartest way of being wrong, and I have been sitting here for about a minute trying to work out which."

"Which is it."

"I don't know." Mu Chen picked up the bar. "Go and find out in four days."

---

## Part 5: What Happened In Four Days

The metal was **Thousand Refined Blue Coppertite**, and it was the worst bar Lin Hao had ever been given, and he understood immediately that that was the point.

Coppertite was forgiving. It took a shape and kept it and did not argue, which was why third-rank work was made of it, and why a bar of it that had been **deliberately badly refined** was the hardest thing you could put in front of somebody who was good, because every flaw in it was a place where it would rather be something else.

Lin Hao stood in front of it for about eleven seconds.

Then he started.

He did not know what he was doing for the first two hundred strikes. That was the part he would remember. He had made second-grade for three weeks and he had a method, and the method did not work, because the bar had flaws in it that his method went around rather than through, and going around was the thing that had been working and was now the thing that was failing.

At about strike two hundred and forty he stopped going around.

He does not have a good account of the next hour. He has the ledger, and the ledger says he struck nine hundred and eleven times, and that he broke two hammer faces, and that at some point he stopped hearing the room, and that the last thing he consciously decided was to stop asking the metal what it wanted and start **telling it**, which was not a thing he had ever done, and which he had been told four months ago by a man in white was the difference between a blade and a sword.

The Halo came at the end.

It was not bright. Mu Chen had said it would not be. It was a ring of light around the work, the colour of the metal, and it was the metal saying it had decided, and Lin Hao stood in front of it with two broken hammer faces and his hands shaking and understood that he had not made it decide.

He had **asked**, and it had said yes, and the asking was the part he could not describe.

Mu Chen looked at it for a long time.

"Fourth rank," he said. "Grandmaster."

"How long did that take you."

"Eleven years." Mu Chen said it without any particular feeling. "And I want you to notice that I am telling you that, and that I am not upset, and that I have been sitting with not being upset for about eleven years, and that you are the second person who has made me do it."

"Who's the first."

Mu Chen looked at him.

"Your teacher's teacher," he said. "And I am not going to say more than that, because I have decided that the things I don't say are as important as the things I do, and I notice you have started doing that too."

## Part 6: What Lin Hao Wrote

He wrote it that night, above the forge, and he wrote the number first out of habit.

*two hundred and twenty-six.* Still. Not re-measured, because nothing had measured it, and the not-knowing had stopped being a thing he minded — which he noticed, because a month ago it would have bothered him, and the not-bothering was its own small fact.

Then the rest of it.

He wrote it that night, above the forge, and he wrote the number first out of habit, and there was no number again, and he wrote a dash, and the dash did not bother him this time, and he noticed that too.

*Fourth rank. Grandmaster. Eleven years for Mu Chen and four years for me, and he told me that and was not upset, and he said I am the second person who has made him not upset and he would not say who the first was, and I did not ask.*

*The Halo is the metal having an opinion. I said that in the chair and Mu Chen said it was either the smartest thing a student had said to him or the smartest way of being wrong, and in four days I found out it was the first, and I cannot explain why, and I no longer need to.*

*I stopped asking the metal what it wanted and started telling it. That is not a thing I had ever done. Wu Zhangkong told me four months ago that a sword has an opinion, and I thought he was talking about swords.*

Then the line at the bottom.

*Xie Xie built the Twin Dragon Storm in three weeks and threw up twice, and Gu Yue outlasted me for four minutes without beating me, and she has beaten me five times in her life and every one of them came before the third ring, and I still have not worked out how both things are true — and Xu Xiaoyan lasted eleven seconds, and I got a fourth rank in four days, and there is a tournament, and Long Hengxu has bet his standing on six children.*

*We are not ready. Wu Zhangkong said there is no one who can be paired with me and have it be fair, and Mu Chen said I am not ready and put me up for it anyway, and both of those men have said the true thing to me in the same week in the same form, and I have begun to understand that this is what it sounds like when adults decide to stop protecting you.*

*I am going to be decent at the tournament. And then I am going to be more than decent, because I am a fourth-rank Grandmaster with three purple rings and a sword that has an opinion, and I would like to be worth standing beside.*

He closed the book.

The *hawk-gold lock* at his temple lay across his cheekbone, and the *marks on his forearms* were raised and had been raised for eleven days and had become a thing other people had stopped mentioning, and the hawk turned over in him — *one thousand three hundred and forty-four* — and did not pull toward anything.

Lin Hao lay on his back in a city with no skyscrapers, and thought about a ring of light the colour of the metal, and about a man who had been not upset for eleven years.

He slept.
