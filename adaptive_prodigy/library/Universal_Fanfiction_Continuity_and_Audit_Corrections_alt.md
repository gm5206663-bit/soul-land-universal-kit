# Universal Fanfiction Continuity and Audit Corrections

## Scope

This file preserves only transferable lessons for future fanfiction, storyline, canon-divergence, status-panel, and continuity work. It does not preserve any story-specific names, powers, relationships, episodes, villains, devices, locations, or events.

## 1. Retrieve the exact latest continuity before writing

- Recover the exact final narrative scene, not only an older status panel.
- Verify the latest location, time, injuries, reserves, captures, escapes, equipment, relationships, knowledge states, and unresolved threats.
- Read enough of the previous chapters to reconstruct the immediate cause-and-effect chain.
- Never call a continuation authoritative or definitive when the exact prior narrative state has not been recovered.

## 2. Reconstruct canon causes before importing canon events

- Do not move to the next canon episode merely because it comes next in the source material.
- Verify whether the original causes, actors, technology, locations, alliances, and enemy freedom still exist in the altered branch.
- If earlier butterfly effects removed a canon cause, rebuild the event through a new valid cause or let the event change completely.
- Never force canon back into place after the branch has logically changed it.

## 3. Resolve enemy and organization status precisely

- Avoid vague states such as “defeated/captured” when the next plot depends on that character acting.
- Record exact custody, location, condition, security, escape routes, communication access, allies, and contingency systems.
- If an enemy returns, establish how and why before the attack begins.
- Do not use unexplained remote activation, surviving fragments, hidden backups, or undetected infrastructure merely to restore a canon conflict.

## 4. Separate confirmed fact, strong inference, hypothesis, and unknown

Every continuity update should distinguish:

- Confirmed: directly shown or explicitly established.
- Strong inference: well-supported but not yet proven.
- Hypothesis: plausible explanation under investigation.
- Unknown: not established and must not be guessed.

Do not present a tactical concern, theory, or possible risk as a confirmed fact.

## 5. Respect character knowledge limits

- A character may suspect a culprit without knowing with certainty.
- Knowledge requires evidence, prior disclosure, recognition, direct perception, or a valid inference path.
- Do not let characters identify technology, magic, motives, hidden locations, or enemy plans instantly without foundation.
- Extremely intelligent characters still cannot know missing facts from nothing.

## 6. Audit detection and response timing

When a powerful character has advanced senses, networks, allies, or monitoring systems:

- Explain why a large crisis was not detected earlier.
- Establish whether the event activated instantly, bypassed sensors, exploited privacy boundaries, used unfamiliar laws, or began outside monitored scope.
- Show what each detection system noticed and when.
- Use the character’s actual speed, parallel cognition, and available tools during the first response.
- Do not make a powerful character passive merely to preserve dramatic timing.

## 7. Do not invent precise numbers without a source

- Avoid exact percentages, ranges, casualty estimates, signal levels, or technical measurements unless a system could realistically calculate them.
- Explain the data source or use approximate language.
- Precision should communicate real information, not create false authority.

## 8. Define privacy and emergency overrides before using them

- If an AI, suit, sensor, or assistant has privacy restrictions, establish what remains active during privacy mode.
- Distinguish personal recording, emergency alerts, tactical monitoring, and remote network access.
- Do not let systems violate earlier privacy rules simply because the plot needs a warning.

## 9. Avoid premature certainty from first contact

- First exposure usually provides data, warning, partial resistance, or a foundation.
- Do not instantly claim full understanding, complete containment, permanent mastery, or guaranteed safe countermeasures.
- Phrase early conclusions cautiously: “appears,” “may,” “initially indicates,” or “until tested.”
- Separate successful defense against one intrusion from mastery of the entire threat system.

## 10. Growth must be earned and correctly classified

- One rejected attack is not automatically a new power or major permanent upgrade.
- Record initial exposure as data acquisition or active adaptation unless later evidence proves stable growth.
- Keep powers, techniques, modes, resistances, applications, equipment functions, and combined moves separate.
- Do not create a new power by renaming a function of existing abilities.

## 11. Audit older master files instead of copying them blindly

- An older authoritative panel may contain premature wording, future information, ambiguous status, or contradictions exposed by later chapters.
- New scenes override older summaries when they conflict.
- Correct the master continuity rather than preserving an error because it was previously saved.

## 12. Preserve protagonist agency without weakening the stronger character

- The canon protagonist should resist, decide, investigate, and act through their own abilities and judgment.
- Support from a stronger original character must not erase consent, autonomy, competence, or narrative importance.
- Do not make the protagonist stagger, fail, or depend immediately unless the threat and prior foundation justify it.
- Show the protagonist’s own defenses before or alongside assistance.

## 13. Make emergency mental protection explicit and ethical

- Clarify whether a psychic shield protects the boundary rather than entering private thoughts.
- Show consent where possible, or establish a narrow emergency justification.
- State that the protected person can reject or end the link.
- Do not use intimacy or trust as blanket permission for mind access.

## 14. Powerful threats need the right kind of tension

- Do not nerf a powerful character to make a conflict dangerous.
- Build tension through hostages, distributed systems, incomplete information, legal constraints, collateral risk, unfamiliar laws, moral choices, simultaneous crises, or incompatible rescue requirements.
- Make clear why raw force is insufficient without pretending the character lacks it.

## 15. Use a powerful character’s full parallel capacity

- If a character can think, perceive, and act across many targets, show simultaneous actions.
- They may protect civilians, map signals, isolate weapons, scan structures, contact allies, and analyze the enemy at once.
- Do not reduce them to one dramatic action at a time unless a valid overload, interference, or attention constraint is established.

## 16. Family and relationship scenes need natural complexity

- Do not make acceptance, approval, trust, cultural differences, grief, fear, and power concerns resolve perfectly in one scene.
- Include discomfort, questions, misunderstandings, different expectations, and emotional history where appropriate.
- Let family relationships grow across time rather than through one flawless conversation.

## 17. Do not turn continuity rules into dialogue

- Characters should not recite status-panel language about agency, consent, boundaries, scaling, or relationship principles.
- Show those principles through choices, reactions, interruptions, disagreement, and behavior.
- Dialogue should sound like the character, not like an authorial correction note.

## 18. Avoid repeating an already-learned relationship lesson

- Once a lesson has been established, progress the relationship instead of restating the same theme in every chapter.
- Repetition without new consequences makes development feel frozen.
- Return to an old issue only when new circumstances genuinely test it.

## 19. Balance episode plot and personal scenes

- If a chapter belongs to a major canon crisis, introduce or foreshadow that crisis early enough.
- Personal or family material may remain central, but the episode’s main conflict should not appear only in the final paragraph without setup.
- Intercut parallel developments when it improves tension and continuity.

## 20. Dramatize research instead of summarizing it

- Show samples, tests, failures, observations, safety limits, participants, and uncertain conclusions.
- Do not claim a character “learned much” without displaying what was learned and how.
- Research into magic or unfamiliar systems must preserve their distinct rules rather than reducing them to ordinary physics.

## 21. Keep status panels and author instructions separate

A status panel should record:

- current state;
- confirmed abilities;
- injuries and reserves;
- equipment;
- relationships;
- identity knowledge;
- world conditions;
- active threats;
- unresolved questions.

A separate continuity lock should record:

- what future chapters must preserve;
- canon-divergence requirements;
- mistakes to avoid;
- unresolved causes that must be explained.

Do not mix “the character is” with “the writer must.”

## 22. Make status panels usable, not merely long

Use a clear order:

1. Exact checkpoint.
2. Immediate location and condition.
3. Current threat.
4. New confirmed growth.
5. Injuries, reserves, and equipment.
6. Relationship and knowledge changes.
7. Allies and enemy status.
8. Canon-divergence ledger.
9. Unknowns and hypotheses.
10. Non-negotiable continuity locks.

Avoid repeating the same scaling, power count, relationship rule, or ownership rule in many sections.

## 23. “Complete status of everyone” requires real coverage

When asked for the status of the main character and others, include meaningful current information for:

- protagonist;
- original character;
- family;
- primary allies;
- organizations;
- active enemies;
- injured or missing characters;
- political and public conditions;
- technology and equipment ownership;
- knowledge and identity matrix;
- unresolved arcs.

Do not give one complete character panel followed by one-line summaries for everyone else.

## 24. Maintain an unresolved-threat ledger

List active threats separately with:

- threat identity;
- confirmed current status;
- location if known;
- available resources;
- current objective;
- what is unknown;
- why it remains dangerous;
- which prior butterfly effects change the original canon version.

## 25. Include a canon-divergence audit before major episodes

For each important canon event, compare:

- original cause;
- original actors;
- original location;
- original resources;
- original outcome;
- current branch changes;
- required new cause or altered outcome.

This prevents accidental canon restoration.

## 26. Respect prior containment and destruction

- A fully contained AI, destroyed device, dead villain, sealed portal, or confiscated weapon cannot return casually.
- If a remnant, backup, copy, contingency, or successor exists, establish it before use.
- Do not contradict “no surviving fragment” with an unexplained later return.

## 27. Use exact power scaling in action, not only in panels

- A powerful character’s feats, reaction speed, perception, and multitasking must match the latest status.
- Do not preserve the scale in a dossier while writing the character at an older, weaker operating level.
- Restraint should be visible as control, not mistaken for limitation.

## 28. Preserve equipment benchmarks without clutter

- Keep old technical measurements in an equipment appendix or reference table.
- Include them in the main panel only when they matter to the current situation.
- Do not overload an active-crisis checkpoint with unrelated historical figures.

## 29. Use absolute language carefully

Distinguish:

- demonstrated capacity;
- established capability;
- practical operating level;
- authorial comparison;
- current tested peak;
- untested maximum;
- theoretical potential.

Avoid words such as “perfect,” “complete,” “instant,” or “definitive” unless the evidence truly supports them.

## 30. Create and maintain one corrected authoritative checkpoint

- After the audit, save a clean updated continuity file.
- Mark older panels as superseded when necessary.
- Do not leave the latest state scattered across chat messages.
- Future chapters must retrieve the newest corrected checkpoint rather than an older benchmark.

## Final execution checklist

Before delivering a new chapter or status panel, verify:

- exact previous scene recovered;
- canon cause reconstructed;
- enemy status precise;
- organization status precise;
- timeline and travel valid;
- character knowledge valid;
- powerful senses and response timing accounted for;
- protagonist agency preserved;
- abilities used at current scale;
- no unexplained revival, escape, backup, portal, or technology;
- confirmed facts separated from hypotheses;
- growth earned and correctly classified;
- status and author instructions separated;
- repetition removed;
- final checkpoint saved and clearly labeled.

## 41. Audit all growth after the latest status-file checkpoint

- A status file is authoritative only up to its stated checkpoint; it does not erase powers, skills, relationships, equipment, or discoveries established in later narrative chapters.
- Before writing a new status panel, compare the status-file date/checkpoint against every later chapter and user correction.
- Add every later genuine acquisition to the cumulative count instead of freezing an older number.
- When a later chapter contradicts an older panel, preserve the historical panel as correct for its time and create a new current-state update rather than pretending the later growth never happened.
- Distinguish between witnessing a phenomenon, researching it, learning its principles, awakening a compatible power, and achieving mastery. A rule against instant copying does not forbid later earned acquisition through sustained study, exposure, practice, and compatible foundations.
- Never use an older restriction such as “does not automatically possess this force” to deny a later, explicitly earned version of that ability. Preserve the distinction between **not automatic** and **never possible**.
