# PLACES — the world the blade lives in (as of Chapter 13)

Living mirror of GREEN_WORLD and WORLD. Derived reference. Tags: **[canon]** (verified) ·
**[design]** (our geography) · **[texture]** (background). This is the map of a rooted life:
for a grass, place is everything — the world either comes to him or it does not.

---

## The whole world, in one line
A rooted blade on a stream bend in the middle depth of Star Dou Forest, where his ground
meets the deep band — the largest and oldest soul-beast forest on the continent, home to
beasts from ten-year weeds to the hundred-thousand-year monarchs. **[canon base + design]**

## Star Dou layering (canon base)
Power and age rise toward the center. **[canon]** Our working geography (band names ours)
runs: hunted **outer edges** → **middle depth** (where the hundred- and thousand-year beast
families live) → the **deep band** (a quieter, older seam the deep's great beasts cross when
they move) → the **deep country** (the old country of the grass's great ones — myth to the
middle band, but real: its heart is the Blue Silver Valley) → the **core** (far off, home of
the hundred-thousand monarchs and the Lake of Life, canon; never staged).

## The glade (his birthplace and whole early world; now the kept home he left) **[design]**
A quiet, sheltered place on the bend of a slow stream. Since the going (Chapter 13) the
glade and the seam are kept by the door-people against his return; the kept places — the
mantis's husk at his base, the old one's place, the found one's place, the fledgling's
place — stay under their drums, and the ground holds the boundary as it always has. Landmarks:
- **The fallen giant** — an old hardwood toppled across the valley floor, half mossed over;
  a landmark that outlasts generations. Its buried root saved him in the flood year.
- **The noon boulder** — warm in the middle of the day; holds the sun's heat into cold
  afternoons. The fledgling sunned on a stone at its edge the day the hawk came.
- **The deer trail** — crosses one edge; its seasons are told by hoof rhythms.
- **Old canopy (north)** — deep shade, cold soil, mushrooms. **Open meadow (south)** — full
  sun, dry summers, thin soil.
- **The stream** — a voice in the ground first, then a smell of moving water, then a bed
  that moved in the flood year. The herd came down along it; the night-walker crossed it at
  the shallows.
- **Blue Silver Grass everywhere** — he is one blade among countless blades. That is the
  opening truth of the story.

## The silver patch / his ground (his body's reach)
Where he stands. Dense, deep, silver-veined, taller than the meadow — a carpet of one. It
became, in order: a patch the herd avoided → the sanctuary of the small and the lost → the
ground a shadow cat could not take → the ground a ten-thousand-class serpent could not dig
out → **a held country** and the keeper's ground, roots woven through the whole stretch of
the stream bend and out along the boundary itself → a ground that shed and outgrew the rot
of the sickness years and held even at low water → **a keeping that is a door as well as a
wall**, where the first creature of the deep crossed to be kept (the found one, which slept
against his root and passed old at the door, Chapter 11) → **a keeping held alone** through
the keeper era: roots extended year by year along the seam until the line was held by the
ground itself, within it the densest silver-green of all where the last of the old one's
kind went into the ground → **the King's country of the seam** (Chapter 12): the ground a
ten-thousand-year gate broke open in, the wild grass leaning in to it, and the door-people
rooted under a King's green.

## The boundary (the keeping)
The line between the middle band and the deep band, where his ground ends and the deep
begins. Held for longer than the glade had been a glade by the old one, who walked its
round and was its presence; passed to him on the deep-serpent night; held by him since.
Since the old one's passing (Chapter 9) he kept it alone through the keeper era — the last
of the old keeper's kind went into the ground within his green, and the line held because
the ground held it. **At the King gate (Chapter 12) the boundary gained its King: the
seam itself is now a kept thing of his, and the deep band has withdrawn its testing.** A
keeper holds the ground above — and, since the eater's years, the ground below too.
**While the King is at the valley (Ch13 onward), the keeping is carried by the people he
made: the door-people hold the seam and the kept places, and the ground answers their
drums — the keeping has become shared, which is what let him go.**

## The old watercourse door **[design]**
A soft stretch along an old watercourse at the deep edge of his ground where the earth runs
loose — a way a digger could come. The old one taught him it was a door; he wove his roots
through it; the root-eater's whole war was fought along it. Closed, now, in the sense that
matters: the ground is not food.

## The deep band **[design on canon base]**
The seam where the deep's great beasts move — patient, endless, hungry in the deep way. Its
near side tests him (the deep-fringe hunters); its dark sent the serpent and the eater; its
word travels by scars and silence. It has learned the green by the water is kept, below as
well as above, even thin; it learned the old keeper's weight is gone and the line still
holds (the alone-keeping test, when fringe hunters pressed and the ground itself turned
them); and it learned that one of its own small and lost slept at the keeper's root, went out
into the fringe and returned unhurt, and brought its fringe-kindred to look at the line —
until the keeper became a people. **At the crest of his becoming (Chapter 12) the deep band
sent its oldest blade back to test the gate itself; it was held in the living ground,
spoken to, and released — and the deep band withdrew its testing of the seam.** In the years of the
going (Chapter 13) the deep band and the deep country's true ancients watched a King of
the grass travel for the first time in the deep's memory — slow as growing, keeping as he
went, never killing — and let him pass toward the valley, some of the oldest of them
watching like an honor guard. The deep, which has no pattern for a grass that moves,
learned it has none for one it must let through.

## The Blue Silver Valley (the Emperor Valley) — the line's home valley, the deep-country myth made real **[canon name of the line's valley]**
The deep country's heart, reached at the end of Chapter 13: a great hidden valley beyond
the deep band where wild Blue Silver has grown since before the forest's memory — a single
unbroken blue-green sea of it, taller and older than any grass of the middle band, filling
the bowl between the old rises. The old one's oldest memory called this direction home: the
place where the grass once had its great ones — kings and queens — in a time before the
middle band forgot; where the old one turned its head at dawn. The common grass of the
deep country knows it simply as the place the green comes from; the oldest memory calls it
the Emperor Valley, because here the line's Emperors were born and crowned. The name is
canon — the home valley of the Blue Silver Emperor line (user-confirmed 2026-09): SL1's own
text leaves the line's awakening-ground unnamed while the canon sequel keeps the line's
Emperor in his valley (CANON_NOTES receipt). Not a [design] adoption. The Empress A Yin is NOT in the valley: her
presence pools there as the line's heart-ground — the far reach of her domain's reading —
while her body roots deeper toward the core. He stands rooted now in the valley's heart,
the first of the line's wild Kings to root there in an age.

### The crown ground (Chapter 14; its true nature revealed Chapter 15) **[design on canon base]**
At the valley's heart, where the grass is oldest and bluest: a ring of NINE kept places —
not bodies (grass leaves no body), but the shapes the earth holds where a great one stood
rooted for ages and went back into the ground, the way his own green holds the mantis, the
fledgling, the old one, and the found one.

**What the nine actually are (Chapter 15, told by the Empress):** the ring is NOT a
grave-ring. It is where the line's great ones were CROWNED — witnessed by the ground rather
than by any ceremony or crowd, because the grass has neither. A great one of the line stands
long enough in one place, keeps one ground long enough, holds one country of green through
enough of its own slow centuries, and the earth takes the shape of what it was — and that
shape is the crown. **The grass has no other royalty; the grass's crowns are the places it
kept.** Consequence for the serial: by that law he is already crowned twice over in the glade
he left behind, where five centuries of keeping and a hundred small drummers are making a
crown in the ground that no one is reading. Beyond the ring, smaller and younger standings
spread down the valley's length; beneath them, layer under layer in ground too compressed
to read clearly, more still — an age of the line's greatness stacked in the earth of its
home. The nine are the LAST of it: the line dwindled without slaughter, generation upon
generation, until the last great ones stood here and left the valley to the mindless
green — and the world forgot the grass ever had kings. **The valley's earth has never
been turned** (no flood silt, no fire char, no digger's churn): it is the line's
physical, root-readable memory, and it is UNKEPT — a shape held by nothing is only a
stain; a kept place is a shape someone holds on purpose. He keeps the nine: roots around
them, never through them, holding their patterns the way he held the silver over the old
one's place, while his own keeper's green spreads outward and the valley's grass leans
and grows lush around it [canon King effect]. **Law kept:** the earth offered him an age
of the line's accumulated essence — dead, returned, belonging to no one, drawable by any
root — and he REFUSED, because drawing it would spend the line's memory into one being's
record; the ground opened to him BECAUSE he took nothing. The nine's deeper reading, what
the grass's queens were, and whether the crown ground can be taught or left as the glade's
keeping was, are the story ahead. [wire]

## The tunnel of the serpent night **[design]**
The wound the deep serpent tore up through the boundary — for a season a gape to the sky,
then grown over by the densest grass in the glade, roots binding the wrecked earth. Healed
into a softer stretch of ground; the first proof to the glade that the keeper's green
mends what the deep breaks.

## The core (canon, off-stage)
Far deeper in — where the hundred-thousand monarchs hold the Lake of Life. A Yin's body
roots somewhere between the valley and the core, deeper than the line's heart-ground. Our
tale never stages it early; it is the far horizon the deep country points toward, and the
valley at its edge is where her presence pools strongest in the grass.

## The human world (per-arc posture)
A distant thread in the deep era: woodsmoke on a wind that should not carry it, an old
beast's missing ear and the word "hunter," a cleared scar at the forest edge heard about
secondhand. Almost never staged; when a human appears, the scene is canon-verified first
and must matter to his story. **[design]**

## Seasonal clock (the years of the glade)
Told by events, not dates: snow season (die back; the root is the survivor), thaw (the
stream speaks again), dry season (the meadow yellows; the deep water is the lesson), fire
years (rare — the grass returns from the roots first, the oldest rhythm of his kind), the
flood year (everything not rooted deep is carried away). Each is a beat in the calendar of
the place.
