# Chapter Nine — The Last Vigil

```
◆ STATUS — Chapter 9: years 440–500
KIND      : Blue Silver Grass, common line. Keeper of the seam, alone from this chapter.
AGE       : ~500 at the chapter's close
CLASS     : upper thousand-year class, ≈7,000 carried at the vigil; the well stays exactly
            where it was, because of a choice made in this chapter.
THREAT    : a Hall-contracted expedition, thirty-strong, goes into the deep band after the
            rot's displaced game and is blooded in it. The walker's absence is noticed.
LOSS      : the night-walker. Last of its kind. It passes at his edge, and he keeps the
```

---

The old one stopped walking the round in the spring of his four hundred and sixty-eighth
year.

It did not announce it. There was no announcement available; there were four words and a
pulse and a vocabulary of taps, and none of them were the word for this. What happened was
that the round did not get walked at dusk, and the great thin body did not come down off
the ridge, and the head did not go onto the ground at the edge of the bank, and at some
point in the second night he understood that the not-coming was not a delay.

It was in the meadow, forty feet from the crown, lying in the grass on its side with its
head up, and it had got that far and no further.

He could not go to it.

That was the whole of the first night and it is worth stating plainly because it was the
cruellest thing that had ever happened to him and he had no capacity to experience it as
cruelty, only as the absence of any option whatsoever: the thing that had walked four
hundred miles of ridge to look at a patch of grass, that had tapped the keeper's knowledge
into him four words at a time for sixty years, that had stood in the middle of nine armed
men and watched them back out of a field — was dying forty feet away, and he was a plant.

He could not cross forty feet. He could not have crossed four. The only thing he had ever
been able to do in his entire life was grow, and hold, and keep the record.

So he grew.

---

It took him the whole night and most of the following day, and it was the fastest he had
ever grown in his life and it was not fast.

The crown reached. That was all it could do — the long deep-blue blades of the bank, the
densest growth he had, extended out across the meadow grass toward where the great body
lay, and the reaching was not a movement so much as a *leaning*, the whole mass of him
pulling in one direction the way a bank of grass leans in a wind that was not blowing.

By dawn the leading blades were within reach of it. By the middle of the morning they were
in its coat.

He could not lift anything. He could not warm anything. He could not have done a single
thing for it that a creature with legs could not have done better in a moment. What he could
do, and what he did for the whole of that day, was *be there* — a hundred feet of living
ground reaching across forty feet of meadow to touch a dying animal, and the touching was
the only gift his body had ever been given to make.

The old one's head came off the ground once, near the end, and turned toward him.

It tapped. Two blows, on the earth, with the last of it.

*Here.*

He pulsed. Every root in a hundred feet of low ground, all at once, the whole seam going
taut and letting go — the answer he had been giving for sixty years, the only word in the
vocabulary that was his.

*Here.*

And then the great body settled into the meadow grass and did not get up, and the forest
went quiet around it in the way it had gone quiet in front of it, and the quiet lasted
three days, and nothing came to the body, because the pressure that came off it did not
stop when it did and every scavenger in the middle depth knew the difference between a
dead thing and a thing that had been very much alive and might still be.

He kept the vigil over all of it, from forty feet away, touching it with the tips of his
blades.

---

On the fourth day the essence began to go back into the ground.

That is what happens. It is the plainest fact in the world's arithmetic and it had been
shown to him twice before at a distance — the badger-line beast in the ninety-eighth year,
the cat's leavings, the small things that died in his crown every winter. A soul beast dies
and its years go back into the soil, and the soil gets rich, and the grass above it grows
darker and faster than the grass ten feet away.

He had fed on the aftermath of a killing in his first summer. He had grown four inches
taller than his kind off a dead four-hundred-year body and thought nothing of it, because a
plant does not choose what falls into its ground.

This was different, and the difference took him the length of the vigil to understand.

The old one was tens of thousands of years. It had kept that seam for longer than the glade
had been a glade. And what was going back into the meadow soil forty feet from his crown
was not the leavings of a hunt — it was the single largest quantity of accumulated
cultivation that would ever be within his reach in the whole of his long life, lying loose
in soft ground, at the near end of a root-mass that could have taken it.

He could have taken it.

That is not a figure of speech and it is not a moral test he nobly declined. It was
*available*. The well inside him was deep and quiet and two centuries old, and the well
worked by consolidation — by taking the substance of his own growth and holding it — and
there is no law anywhere in the world that says a root cannot draw on the returned years of
a thing that died in its ground, because there has never been a root in the world that
could.

He did not.

He could not have explained why, having four words and no self clear enough to hold a
principle. What he had instead was the mantis, sitting in the deep place where a plant puts
what it cannot use — the lightness, the weight, the lightness again — and the arithmetic
that had gone down under her in his ninety-eighth year, and one more thing, newer than
either, that had been tapped into him four words at a time over sixty years until it was
part of the substance of him:

*a keeper keeps.*

A keeper does not take. A keeper holds a line, and a line held by taking is not held, it
is only fed, and the difference between those two things was the entire content of the
office that had just been handed to him.

So he let it go into the ground.

All of it. Every year of it, back into the meadow soil where it belonged, and the soil took
it, and by the end of that first season the forty feet between his crown and the place
where the old one lay was the densest, darkest, most silver-veined growth in the whole of
the glade, and it stayed that way for the rest of his life, and he never once took a single
year of it into himself.

---

*The expedition went into the deep band that same spring, and thirty men came back out of
it with eleven, and the reason had nothing to do with him and everything to do with the
fact that the walker was not walking its round.*

*It was the largest contracted party the middle band had seen in a generation — thirty
strong, Hall-blessed, working the grids west of the rot for everything the displacement had
pushed out of the deep, with two Soul Ancestors in the column and a commission worth more
than a village. They had read the registry. They knew grid nineteen was sealed-pending.
They knew the walker was territorial and ancient and best left alone, and they went around
it the way everybody went around it, and they went into the deep band on the far side of
the ridge where nothing in the registry said there was anything to worry about.*

*The deep band had been leaning on an unkept seam for a hundred and fifty years, and the
only thing that had been holding it was a bank of grass, and a bank of grass is a door and
not a wall, and a door lets things through in both directions.*

*What came through behind the rot's refugees, over the century and a half, was not many
things. But it was not nothing. And the deep band west of the ridge had been receiving, for
a hundred and fifty years, everything too large to wait politely at the edge of a woven
root-mass, and it had been holding them the way the deep holds anything — by being bigger.*

*They found that out on the third day.*

*The debrief took three days and it was conducted by a Hall assessor who had never been
west of the ridge, and the only man in the room who had come back with both his original
companions was a Soul Ancestor named Kesh, who was fifty-one and had a black ring at her
wrist and a hand that would not stop shaking.*

*"Take it from the third day," the assessor said.*

*"The third day is when it happened."*

*"Then take it from the morning of the third day."*

*Kesh put both hands flat on the table because the shaking was worse when they were in her
lap. "We were four miles west of the ridge. Good ground. Deep ground, but good — we'd read
it, we'd had the registry, and the registry said nothing in four grids except low-class
stock and one territorial ancient in nineteen, and nineteen was behind us."*

*"And."*

*"And the forest stopped." She looked up. "Not quiet. Stopped. You know the sound a deep
forest makes? It goes all night, every night, it never stops. It stopped. In a circle. And
the circle was moving toward us and it was moving at the pace of a walking man."*

*"The walker."*

*"The walker was four miles behind us." Kesh said it very carefully. "The walker was in grid
nineteen. The walker is territorial and it does not leave its round and every report on it
for a hundred and fifty years says it does not leave its round." She took her hands off the
table. "Whatever came at us was not the walker. And it was not one thing. There were four
of them and they came out of the ground."*

*The assessor wrote for a while.*

*"You lost nineteen men."*

*"Nineteen."*

*"To four deep-band fauna."*

*"To four deep-band fauna that were not supposed to be in four grids of low ground." Kesh
leaned forward. "Listen to me. Something moved them. Something moved four ten-thousand-year
things four miles west of their territory in a single night, and the registry has nothing in
that country, and I want the grid reclassified."*

*"Which grid."*

*"The one that's already sealed." She sat back. "Nineteen. Reopen it. Send a real team.
Something in that grid has been holding the deep band off the middle ground for a hundred
and fifty years and the deep band has just found out it isn't there anymore."*

*The assessor wrote that down too, and it went into the file, and the file was read by
nobody, and the report that went to the Hall that autumn blamed the rot's displacement,
which was not wrong, and blamed the party's routing, which was not wrong either, and did not
mention grid nineteen at all — because nobody in the column had gone within four miles of
it, and nobody in the Hall connected a sealed-pending grid to a massacre on the far side of
a ridge.*

*But the walker's round did not get walked that spring, and the deep band noticed, and the
leaning on the seam stopped for eleven years — which was the only mercy anything in that
country ever showed him, and it was not mercy, it was the deep band working out whether the
thing that had held the post for an age was still in it.*

*It was. It was a hundred feet of low ground and a bank of grass and eight small refugees
of a sour place in the deep country, and it held, and the leaning came back in the twelfth
year, and the seam went on being kept by something the entire world had filed under
vegetation.*
