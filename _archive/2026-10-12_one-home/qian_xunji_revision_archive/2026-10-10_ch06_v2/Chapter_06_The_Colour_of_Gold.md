# Chapter 06 — The Colour of Gold

He stayed at the basin four more days, and on the fourth night he did the second ring.

He had meant to do it on the second night. His body had said otherwise. He slept most of a day with the ridge under his first ring throbbing like a bad tooth, and when he woke the cold water did not feel cold so much as indifferent, and that was worse than cold.

So he waited, and ate, and drank from the stream below the rim, and thought.

What he thought about was arithmetic, because arithmetic was the only honest thing left to him. The first ring had cost him blood he could not make back in a week. The blood in him was thinner now than it had been. The second ring would want more than the first had wanted and not less, because the seam went deeper every time he opened one.

He had come to this place believing he would finish all nine of them in a month.

He was going to finish two, and go home, and come back when the blood had filled in again.

That was the shape of the road. It was years long and not weeks long, and the moment he understood it he stopped arguing with it, because a man who argues with the length of a road wastes the stretch of it he is standing on.

---

The second ring was the other yellow one. Seven hundred and sixty years, out of a bird of prey that had carried light along its wings, and it had given him a shield.

He sat with his back against the same stone in the same place, for the same reason he had used the first time. If he went over he would go into the cold, and the cold had already saved him once.

He looked at the seam before he touched it. He had learned that much from the first night.

The seam under the second ring was not like the seam under the first. It was newer, and shallower, and nothing had ever torn it before, and for a while he thought that would make it easier.

It made it worse.

The first seam had a scar running through it, and a scar is a place where the body has already learned to let go. This one had not learned anything at all. He pulled, and it held, and then it tore along its whole length at once instead of tearing in one place, and the pain was so clean and so complete that he heard himself make a sound he had not made since he was eleven years old in another world.

Blood came out of his nose and his ears and from under his nails, and he had nothing left in him to be ashamed of the noise.

The years went down into him anyway. Seven hundred and sixty years of a bird of prey went into the core, and the blood weighed them, and the blood kept less of them than it had kept the first time.

He felt that happen. It was the worst part of the night.

Not the tearing. The weighing. He could feel his blood go through those years as a merchant goes through a purse by lamplight, setting aside the coins he cannot afford. He understood with total clarity that he was poorer than he had been four days ago, and that the difference was not coming back.

The core opened and drank, and the pool dipped a finger's width again, and the two waters went on refusing to mix.

The bird went through all of it and came out the other side still a bird of prey, and not a small one.

The seam re-knit. It took most of the night, and twice he stopped breathing and started again without deciding to.

When it was finished, the second ring was gold.

Two gold rings at his feet now, and seven above them still wearing the colours of dead animals, and he sat in the dark looking at the two of them and did not feel the triumph he had expected to feel twice.

He felt like a man counting what he has left.

---

Before the light went down he tried the shield.

He had not planned to try it. It was the kind of thought a man has at the end of a long night, when he is too tired to be careful and too wired to sleep. The shield came out around him as it had always come out, a slow wall of gold feathers turning, and it looked exactly as it had looked for fifty years.

So he stood up and walked into the cold spring and let the water take him.

The shield held. It kept the cold out of him as a roof keeps out rain, and it did not shudder, and it did not thin at the edges. After a while he walked out again and stood on the stone with the frost steaming off his shoulders, and understood that he had not spent anything.

His soul power was low. It had been low all night. The shield had not taken from it.

He stood there a long time with that.

A soul master's shield runs on his soul power. That is what a shield is. Every man on this continent who has ever raised one has paid for it out of the same purse he fights with. Every man knows that a long defence is a lost fight, and plans his defence short because of it.

His did not cost him that. His cost him blood, and blood is a purse that nobody on this continent knows how to spend, and it fills itself back in between the fights instead of during them.

He sat down on the stone and did nothing else for a while, because he had become aware all at once that he was a thing the world had no word for.

The golden layer came up again, and the halo, and the light, and the pillar over the eastern forest.

He had expected the pillar. What he had not expected was that the second one would be taller.

It stood over the trees longer than the first had stood, and when it finally went down the wings stayed out a while, and the domain rolled out across the forest and did not want to come back in, and he sat with his hands flat on the stone until they stopped shaking.

Then he lay down beside the two waters and slept, and dreamed of a sparrow.

---

He left on the sixth day and walked west.

He did not fly. He had six wings and the strength of a man twice his rank, and he walked, because a golden man in the sky above the empire was not a thing he wanted anybody describing to anybody else.

The country to the west was the same country. Flat, and frozen, and empty, and he crossed it faster than he had come, because he was in a hurry now and because his body had stopped noticing the cold.

It had stopped noticing a good deal else as well, and he had four days of walking to think about that.

He thought first about the ledger of it, because the ledger was the only part of this that belonged to him. Nine slots. Every man on this continent had nine, and the ninth ring was the measure of a man, and the codex said so in the second volume in his own handwriting. He had not changed that. Two of his nine were gold and seven were still the colours of dead animals. When the seventh turned there would be no tenth waiting underneath, because blood does not make new slots, and he was not going to pretend to anybody that it did.

What the blood had changed was what his rings were made of. That was all of it, and it was enough.

He thought about the ring that bursts. He had read the price of the hammer. A man who bursts a ring twice inside thirty-six days may find one of them does not come back at all, and a ring that does not come back is a hole in him until he kills something old enough to fill it. He was not using a hammer. His seams were not the same thing as a broken ring. He knew all of that, and he also knew that a seam torn along its whole length in the dark at the edge of a spring was not a thing to feel confident about.

If one of them went, he would have to go hunting. In a forest where almost everything was under ten thousand years. For something old enough to fill a slot that had held seven hundred and sixty years of a bird of prey and had then been gold.

He walked a long way with that one and did not arrive at an answer, and he let it stay unanswered, because a man who cannot yet pay for a risk should at least know what it costs before he takes it.

What he kept returning to was the blood.

His soul power had been low for six days and he had not sat down to fill it, because there had not been a safe hour to sit down in. None of that had mattered. The shield had not touched it. The light had not touched it. The two gold rings at his feet ran on something else entirely, and they would go on running after a stronger man had taken everything else away from him.

That was the largest thing that had happened to him at the basin, and it had taken him four days of walking to understand it, and he was not yet sure whether it was a gift.

On the fourth day he passed a village, and there were men outside the ale house, and one of them was talking with his hands.

"— over the trees, that high, and it stood there like it had a right to. My wife says it was a god come down to look at something."

"A god. In the eastern forest. What would a god want with the eastern forest, there's nothing in it but snakes and bad water."

"Ask the god. I only know it was there and it was gold and my wife saw it from the yard and she don't see things that aren't there."

A younger man stood at the edge of them with a bronze pin on his sleeve, the kind a branch hall gives to a boy who has passed his tenth level. He was listening with his mouth open. He had the look of a man who will tell this story for forty years and add something to it every single time.

He walked past all of them. Nobody looked at him, and the man with his hands went on talking, and the god over the eastern forest was already taller than the thing he had seen.

---

Spirit City at dusk. The gates, and the white walls, and the bells going for evening prayer with the sound of them coming down the hill as it had come every evening for fifty years of a life that was not his.

The guard at the gate straightened when he saw the face.

Then he saw the rings.

It was a small thing, a man's eyes going down and then up again quickly, as eyes do when they have seen something they will be asked about later. He said nothing at all. He bowed, and he opened the gate.

He walked up the hill with two gold rings around him, and he felt every eye on that street go down and then up again.

---

Qian Daoliu was in the hall of statues.

He was not standing under his own grandfather's face this time. He was standing in the middle of the floor with his hands behind him, facing the door, and he had the look of a man who had been standing there for some hours.

"You are back."

"I am back. Five weeks, not six."

"So the roads held."

The old man did not move. Then he said, "Show me."

He let the rings out.

Nine of them, going up from his feet, and the two lowest were gold, and the seven above them were yellow and purple and black in the order he had worn them since he was a boy.

The hall was quiet for a long time.

Qian Daoliu had ninety-nine levels of power and ninety-nine years of age, and he had seen every ring on this continent worth the seeing, and he stood looking down at his son's feet with his mouth slightly open.

"Gold," he said at last. "Gold is a million years."

"No."

"Gold is the Angel God's ring." The old man's voice did not change, and that was how his son knew the old man was frightened. "It is the tenth ring, and it comes down with the god seat, and it has never in the history of this family come down on any man. I have stood under that statue for sixty years and I have looked at that ring every day of them, and it is not a colour. It is the seat itself, wearing a colour so that men can see it."

"I know what it is."

"Then you know what you have done." The old man took a step closer, and his voice was still perfectly even, which was the worst sound in the room. "The seat does not come down on a man who kills for it. It comes down on blood. It can only ever go to a man of our family who carries the six-winged angel in him, and that is the one law in this hall that has never once been broken, and it is the reason our family has kept that hall shut to every outsider who ever asked. There is one gold ring in this world and it belongs to a seat, and you are standing in front of me with two of them on your feet."

"They are not that ring."

"No," the old man said. "They are worse. That ring is a thing a man may inherit. Those are a thing a man has made out of himself, and I have never seen it and I have never read it and I have no name to put on it."

He had known this question was coming, and he had thought about the answer for four days walking west, and he had decided on the truth, because the truth was the only thing he owned that no man on this continent could take away from him.

"They carry no year," he said. "A ring is a beast's age, and that is what every ring on this continent is, and that is why the ladder is the ladder and why a man's ninth ring is the measure of him. Those two are not a beast's age. They are the concentration of the blood in me, and they run on blood and not on soul power, and they will be as deep as the blood gets and no deeper."

"How deep does the blood get."

"I don't know yet. That is the honest answer and it is the only one I have."

Qian Daoliu was quiet for a while. Then he said the thing his son had been waiting four days to hear, and had not been sure he would hear.

"Then whose blood do you think you are carrying."

"The Angel God's."

"Yes." The old man sat down on the step beneath his own grandfather's statue, which was not a thing he had ever done in his life. "And you have made it deeper."

"Yes."

"In a year."

"In a year."

The old man put his head in his hands for a moment, and then took them away, and looked up, and he was not frightened any longer, and what he was instead was something his son had no name for.

"Then you had better be careful," he said. "There are men on this continent who have spent their whole lives believing that gold means one single thing, and you are walking around with two of them, and they will want to know where they came from."

"I know. And I will tell them nothing at all, because I will tell the codex instead."

---

He wrote it that night, in the third volume, in the hand he used when he wanted the Elders to believe he had been thinking about it for a long time.

A short chapter. A ring is a beast's age, and the ladder is the ladder, and this holds for every ring a man can kill for. There is one other kind. It is not made of a beast. It is made of the blood of the man who carries it, and it has no year, and it runs on blood rather than on soul power, and it deepens as the blood deepens. It is gold.

He did not write that he had two of them.

He did not write how it was done, because it could not be done by any man whose blood was not already what his was, and a rule that no man alive can follow is not a rule but a boast.

He wrote what the thing was, and he set the chapter beside the chapter on the ladder, and he left it there to be found.

That was the whole of the strategy, and it had taken him four days of walking to see it. A man who does not want to be asked a question should not hide from it. He should answer it in public, and early, and in a voice dry enough that nobody enjoys reading it.

He put the pen down and read the page back once, and changed one word, and read it back again.

Then he thought about how the page would travel, because that was the part that mattered. The Elders would copy the third volume within a week. It would reach the Holy Halls in both capitals within a month, the Main Halls after that, and the two empires by spring. Any man who wanted to know what a gold ring was would find his answer in a book, in the driest voice in it, six weeks before he thought to ask the question.

The only man alive who could connect that page to him was his father, and his father would not.

Then he took the nine small pills out of his coat and looked at them for a while, and sent for a servant he had chosen three months ago for the reason that the man had a deaf wife and no children in the city.

The pills went east, to the locked rooms, by a hand that could not be traced.

What the woman did with them was hers.

He sat in the chamber under the Hall with two gold rings around him and eight walls of stone and a daughter due in five weeks, and he was not frightened, and he was not triumphant.

He was counting.

Six weeks between one ring and the next, and seven rings left, and a blood that had to fill in between them. He did the sum, and the sum came out in years, and he had been alive in this body for one.

He had never in two lives been patient about anything at all.

He put the lamp out and went to bed, because the road was long, and he had been alive in this body for one year and intended to be alive in it for a great many more.
