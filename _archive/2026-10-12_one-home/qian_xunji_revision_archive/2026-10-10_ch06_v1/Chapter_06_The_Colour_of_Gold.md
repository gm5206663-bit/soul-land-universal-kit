# Chapter 06 — The Colour of Gold

He stayed at the basin four more days, and on the fourth night he did the second ring.

He had meant to do it on the second. His body had said otherwise. He had slept most of a day with the ridge under his first ring throbbing like a tooth, and when he woke the cold water did not seem so much cold as indifferent, which was worse.

So he waited, and ate, and drank from the stream outside the basin, and thought.

What he thought about was arithmetic, because that was the only honest thing left to him. The first ring had cost him blood he could not make back. The blood was thinner now than it had been. And the second ring would want more than the first had wanted, not less, because the seam went deeper each time.

He had come here believing he would do all nine in a month.

He was going to do two of them, and go home, and come back when the blood had filled in again.

That was the shape of the road. It was years long, not weeks, and the moment he understood it he stopped resenting it, because a man who argues with the length of a road wastes the part of it he is standing on.

---

The second ring was the other yellow one. Seven hundred and sixty years, out of a bird of prey with light along its wings, and it had given him a shield.

He sat with his back against the stone in the same place, for the same reason: if he went over he would go into the cold, and the cold had saved him once already.

He looked at the seam first. He had learned that much.

The seam under the second ring was not like the seam under the first. It was newer, and shallower, and it had not been torn before, and he thought for a while that this would make it easier.

It made it worse.

The first seam had a scar in it, and a scar is a place where the body has already learned to let go. This one had not learned anything. When he pulled, it held, and then it tore along its whole length at once instead of in one place, and the pain was so clean and so total that he heard himself make a sound he had not made since he was eleven years old in another world.

Blood came out of his nose and his ears and from under his nails, and he did not have the strength left to be ashamed of the sound he was making.

The years went down into him anyway. Seven hundred and sixty years of a bird of prey, and the core took them, and the blood weighed them, and the blood kept less of them than it had kept the first time.

He felt that happen, and it was the worst part of the night.

Not the tearing. The weighing. He could feel his blood go through the years as a merchant goes through a purse, and set aside what it could not afford, and he understood with total clarity that he was poorer than he had been four days ago and that the difference was not coming back.

The core opened and drank, and the pool dipped a finger's width again, and the two waters went on not mixing.

The bird went through it and came out the other side still a bird of prey, and not small.

The seam re-knit. It took most of the night and twice he stopped breathing and started again without deciding to.

When it was finished, the second ring was gold.

Two gold rings now, and seven above them still the colours of dead animals, and he sat in the dark looking at the two of them and did not feel the triumph he had expected to feel twice.

He felt like a man counting the money he has left.

Before the light went down he tried the shield.

He had not planned to. It was the kind of thought a man has at the end of a long night, when he is too tired to be careful and too wired to sleep. The shield came out around him as it always had, a wall of gold feathers turning slowly, and it looked the same as it had for fifty years.

So he stood up and walked into the cold spring and let the water take him.

The shield held. It held the cold out of him as a roof holds out rain, and it did not shudder, and it did not thin, and after a while he walked out again and stood on the stone with the frost steaming off his shoulders and understood that he had not spent anything.

His soul power was low. It had been low all night. The shield had not taken from it.

He stood there a long time with that.

A soul master's shield runs on his soul power. That is what a shield is. Every man on this continent who has ever raised one has paid for it out of the same purse he fights with, and every man knows that a long defence loses him the fight.

His did not cost him that. His cost him blood, and blood is a purse nobody on this continent knows how to spend, and it fills back in on its own between the fights instead of during them.

He sat down on the stone and did not do anything else for a while, because he had become aware, all at once, that he was a thing the world had no word for.

The golden layer came up again, and the halo, and the light, and the pillar over the eastern forest.

He had expected the pillar. What he had not expected was that the second one would be taller.

It stood over the trees longer than the first had, and when it finally went down the wings stayed out a while, and the domain rolled out over the forest and did not want to come back in, and he sat with his hands flat on the stone until they stopped shaking.

Then he lay down beside the two waters and slept, and dreamed of a sparrow.

---

He left on the sixth day and walked west.

He did not fly. He had six wings and the strength of a man twice his rank, and he walked, because a golden man in the sky over the empire was not a thing he wanted anybody to describe to anybody else.

The country west was the same country. Flat, and frozen, and empty, and he crossed it faster than he had come, because he was in a hurry now and because his body no longer noticed the cold.

It no longer noticed much else either, and he had the whole of four days to think about that.

He thought first about the ledger of it, because the ledger was the only part of this that he could control. Nine slots, and every man on the continent had nine, and the ninth ring was the measure of a man and the codex said so in the second volume in his own hand. He had not changed that. Two of his nine were gold and seven were still the colours of dead animals, and when the seventh turned there would be no tenth waiting underneath, because the blood does not make new slots and he was not going to pretend to anybody that it did.

What the blood changed was what his rings were made of. That was all, and it was enough.

He thought about the ring that bursts. He had read the price of the hammer, and the price was that a man who does it twice inside thirty-six days may find one of them does not come back at all, and a ring that does not come back is a hole in him until he kills something old enough to fill it. He was not using a hammer, and his seams were not the same thing as a broken ring, and he knew all of that, and he also knew that a seam torn along its whole length in the dark at the edge of a spring was not a thing to be confident about.

If one of them went, he would have to go hunting. In a forest where the beasts were mostly under ten thousand years. For something old enough to fill a slot that had held seven hundred and sixty years of a bird of prey and had then been gold.

He walked a long way with that one and did not arrive at an answer, and he let it stay unanswered, because a man who cannot yet pay for a risk should at least know what it costs before he takes it.

What he kept coming back to was the blood.

His soul power was low. It had been low for six days, and he had not sat down and filled it, because there had not been a safe hour to sit down in. And none of it had mattered. The shield had not touched it. The light had not touched it. The two gold rings at his feet ran on something else entirely, and they would go on running when a stronger man had taken everything else away from him.

That was the largest thing that had happened to him at the basin, and it had taken him four days of walking to understand it, and he was not sure yet whether it was a gift.

On the fourth day he passed a village, and there were men outside the ale house, and one of them was talking with his hands.

"— over the trees, that high. Stood there like it had a right to. My wife says it was a god."

"What would a god want with the eastern forest."

"Ask the god."

A younger one was there too, with a bronze pin on his sleeve, the kind a branch hall gives out to a boy who has passed his tenth level, and the boy was listening with his mouth open and had the look of a man who will tell this story for forty years and add something to it every time.

He walked past them. Neither of them looked at him, and the man with his hands went on talking, and the god over the eastern forest was already taller than the one he had seen.

---

Spirit City at dusk. The gates, and the white walls, and the bells going for evening prayer, with the sound of them coming down the hill as it had every evening for fifty years of a life that was not his.

The guard at the gate straightened when he saw the face.

Then he saw the rings.

It was a small thing. A man's eyes going down and then up again, quickly, as they do when they have seen something they will be asked about later. He said nothing at all. He bowed, and he opened the gate.

He walked up the hill with two gold rings around him, and he felt every eye on the street go down and then up again.

---

Qian Daoliu was in the hall of statues.

He was not standing under his grandfather's face this time. He was standing in the middle of the floor, with his hands behind him, facing the door, and he had the look of a man who had been standing there for some hours.

"You are back."

"I am back. Five weeks, not six."

"So the roads held."

The old man did not move. Then he said, "Show me."

He let the rings out.

Nine of them, going up from his feet, and the two lowest were gold, and the seven above them were yellow and purple and black in the order he had worn them since he was a boy.

The hall was very quiet for a long time.

Qian Daoliu had ninety-nine levels of power and ninety-nine years of age, and he had seen every ring worth seeing on this continent, and he stood looking at his son's feet with his mouth slightly open.

"Gold," he said. "Gold is a million years."

"No."

"Gold is the Angel God's ring." The old man's voice did not change, which was how he knew the old man was frightened. "Your daughter's ring will be gold, when she is a god. I have read it. There is one gold ring in our family and it is not on this earth any more."

"I know."

"Then what are those."

He had known this question was coming, and he had thought about the answer for four days walking west, and he had decided the truth, because the truth was the only thing he had that nobody on this continent could take away from him.

"They are not years," he said. "They are blood, and they carry no year at all."

The old man looked at him.

"A ring is a beast's age," he said. "That is what every ring on this continent is, and it is why the ladder is the ladder, and it is why a man's ninth ring is the measure of him. Those two are not that. They are the concentration of the blood in me, and they carry no year at all, and they will be as deep as the blood gets."

"How deep does the blood get."

"I don't know yet."

Qian Daoliu was quiet for a while. Then he said the thing that his son had been waiting four days to hear, and had not been sure he would.

"Then whose blood do you think you are carrying."

"The Angel God's."

"Yes." The old man sat down on the step under his own grandfather's statue, which was not a thing he ever did. "And you have made it deeper."

"Yes."

"In a year."

"In a year."

The old man put his head in his hands for a moment, and then took them away, and looked up, and he was not frightened any more, and what he was instead was something his son did not have a name for.

"Then you had better be careful," he said, "because there are men on this continent who have spent their whole lives believing that gold is one thing, and you are walking around with two of them, and they will want to know."

"I know. And I will tell them nothing, because I will tell the codex instead."

---

He wrote it that night, in the third volume, in the hand he used when he wanted the Elders to believe he had thought about it for a long time.

A short chapter. A ring is a beast's age, and the ladder is the ladder, and this is true for every ring a man can kill for. There is one other kind. It is not made of a beast. It is made of the blood of the man who carries it, and it has no year, and it runs on the blood and not on the soul power, and it deepens as the blood deepens. It is gold.

He did not write that he had two.

He did not write how it was done, because it could not be done by anybody whose blood was not already what his was, and a rule that no man can follow is not a rule but a boast.

He wrote what it was, and he set the chapter next to the chapter on the ladder, and he left it there to be found.

That was the whole of the strategy, and it had taken him four days of walking to see it. A man who does not want to be asked a question should not hide from it. He should answer it in public, in a book, in the driest voice he can manage, six weeks before anybody thinks to ask.

He put the pen down and read it back once, and changed one word, and read it back again.

Then he thought about how it would travel, because that was the part that mattered. The third volume would be copied by the Elders within a week. It would go to the Holy Halls in both capitals within a month, and out to the Main Halls after that, and into the two empires by the spring. A man who wanted to know what a gold ring was would find the answer in a book, in the driest voice in it, six weeks before he thought to ask.

The only man alive who could connect the page to him was his father, and his father would not.

That was the whole of the strategy. A man who does not want to be asked a question should not hide from it. He should answer it in public, and early, and badly enough that nobody enjoys reading it.

Then he took the nine small pills out of his coat, and looked at them, and sent for a servant he had picked three months ago for the reason that the man had a deaf wife and no children in the city.

The pills went east, to the locked rooms, by a hand that would not be traced.

What the woman did with them was hers.

He sat in the chamber under the Hall with two gold rings around him and eight walls of stone and a daughter due in five weeks, and he was not frightened either, and he was not triumphant.

He was counting.

Six weeks between one ring and the next, and seven rings left, and a blood that had to fill in between them. He did the sum, and the sum was years, and he had been alive in this body for one.

He had never in two lives been patient about anything.

He put the lamp out and went to bed, because the road was long, and the first step of a long road is always the same, and he had been alive in this body for one year and intended to be alive in it for a great many more.
