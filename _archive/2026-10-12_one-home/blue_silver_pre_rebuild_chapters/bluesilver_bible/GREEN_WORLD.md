# GREEN WORLD — the living stage
The place where he is born, and the small lives around him. Status: living. Tags:
**[canon]** **[fan]** **[AU]** **[design]** (our choice, clearly ours).

## Where he is
- Star Dou Forest, **middle depth** — far enough in that the great predators and monarchs of
  the deep core are only rumors and distant roars; far enough out that human settlements are
  a season's walk away and rarely reach here. This is the band of the forest where the
  hundred-year and thousand-year beast families actually live. **[design]**
- His glade: a quiet, sheltered place on the bend of a slow stream. Working picture of the
  stage (to be fixed visually in beats, adjust freely):
  - a **fallen giant** — an old hardwood toppled across the valley floor, half mossed over,
    a landmark that outlasts generations of small beasts;
  - a **noon boulder** by the water that holds the sun's warmth through cold afternoons;
  - a **deer trail** crossing one edge of the glade (hooves, antler-scrape on bark, the
    season's first hoof-prints in mud);
  - **old canopy** over the north side — deep shade, cold soil, mushrooms; **open meadow**
    on the south — full sun, dry summer, thin soil;
  - Blue Silver Grass everywhere: along the stream, under the trees, in the meadow. He is one
    blade among countless blades. That is the opening truth of the story. **[design]**
- **Bands of the forest (working geography, [design]):** Star Dou runs from the hunted outer
  edges to the great core. Between the middle depth — where the hundred- and thousand-year
  families live — and the true deep sits a **deep band**, a quieter, older seam that the
  deep's great beasts cross when they move. His glade lies at the middle depth's inner edge,
  where his ground meets that deep band. An ancient keeper (the night-walker, Ch4–6) long
  held the boundary between the bands; after the deep-serpent night (Ch6) the boundary is
  his. Canon base: forest power rises toward the center (Star Dou layering); the exact band
  names are ours. **[canon base + design]**

## Time on a plant's clock
A grass does not count years the way beasts do. Time is told by what happens:
- **Snow season**: the blades die back; what lives is below the frost line. His first winter
  is his first near-death and his first real lesson — the part of him that must survive is
  the root, the seed, the quiet below. **[design beat seed]**
- **The thaw**: the stream speaks again, the canopy leafs, the world returns from the soil.
- **Dry season**: the meadow yellows, the stream thins; beasts dig for water; a dry year
  tests everything rooted.
- **Fire years** (rare): a dry lightning strike can take a whole slope. The grass grows back
  from the roots first — the oldest rhythm of his kind. **[canon-adjacent imagery, AU use]**
- **The flood year**: the stream overleaps its bank; the glade becomes lake for a week;
  everything not rooted deep is carried away.
Each of these is a natural beat waiting in the calendar of the place.

## The living ground (how he feels his world)
- Blue Silver Grass is the commonest plant on the continent and grows in carpets everywhere.
  **[canon]** In the deep wild it covers whole valley floors and forest skirts.
- Ordinary grass has no soul and no mind **[canon]** — including his kindred. There is NO
  grass "network": the blades around him feel nothing, know nothing, and are not connected
  to anything. Gathering the life and information of surrounding Blue Silver Grass is an
  **Emperor-domain ability** (A Yin's Blue Silver Domain, canon) — hers, not his, and not
  grass's. **[canon]**
- So from his first awakening his loneliness is real: he is one mind inside a silent
  country. What he slowly learns instead is **the living ground** — that the earth under
  him is full of messages that are not minds but motions. Through his roots he reads the
  soil's wet and dry, the deep water's pull, the drink of other roots, and above all the
  **footfall-tremors of the living**: a badger's drag, a rodent's scatter, a hoof's knock,
  rain arriving on the canopy a full minute before it reaches him. This is his first
  language, and it costs him nothing that isn't his own body.
- This ground-reading is also why the Empress knew one impossible blade — not because he
  senses the network, but because her domain reads the grass, and at the King gate (Chapter
  12) there was finally a blade that was no longer ordinary grass to read: she has read him
  for the first time, a two-way wire (their meeting still ahead). **[user design on canon
  base]**
- **The King gate changed this — and only then, with honest labels.** VERIFIED canon (novel
  ch.134): a King has great intelligence, telepathy, true speech, and bloodline sensing; the
  massed-grass bond appears in the second-awakening/ring event the elder King performed. The
  wider powers the user supplied (absolute local dominion, hive-mind, sensory synapse,
  vitality funneling, sub-anchor to the domain) have **no canon source** and are kept as
  labelled **[design]** territory only (BEAST_LAW §10 ledger) — never asserted as canon. The
  "no ordinary-grass network" rail above governed his whole PRE-KING age (root/ground
  sensing only) unchanged; it still governs his ground-feel now — he never reached the
  network early and is not a lesser King: at the gate (Chapter 12) he received the complete
  verified King grade, and grows his own things on top. **[verified King canon + user
  design, labelled]**
- Local animals walk on grass, sleep on it, eat it. To every other creature in the glade he
  is, at first, just part of the carpet. The story's early drama is that he *isn't* — and
  that the living ground lets him feel everything that walks on him without a way to act on
  it. **[design]**

## The neighbors (who the middle forest is made of)
Living texture only — no canon authority needed, all **[design]** until named on page:
- **Burrowers and small herbivores** of the glade edge: a wizened old badger-like beast with
  a territory line that passes within a grass-length of him; a family of digging rodents
  whose seasons of birth and flight shape the glade's years.
- **Birds and the canopy**: nesters overhead, a hunting hawk's shadow crossing the meadow at
  the same hour; droppings that seed new life — small gifts from above.
- **One old predator** whose home range includes the glade and who is, by the era of his
  childhood, more interested in warmth than in prey.
- **Insect life** — the deepest texture of the place: the pollinators of the meadow, the
  leaf-cutters, the singers of the dry season, and the ones who eat grass.
- **Passing powers**: once in a long while something *great* crosses the middle band — a
  thousand-year beast on the move, a hunt heard but never seen. These are the century-scale
  events that teach a rooted being the shape of the world. **[design]**

## Rules that root the place (the real constraints that make this story)
1. **Rooted — until the King's going.** Canon itself: even the 85,000-year Blue Silver King is
   rooted and immobile **[canon]**. For his whole pre-King age — and for any ordinary grass —
   the rule holds absolute: reach is root range and the ground his roots can read; danger
   must come to him or walk over him. At the King gate era (Chapter 13) the serial's one
   exception lands: the GOING, a whole rooted body drawn and re-rooted season by season,
   slow as growing, always a rooted thing at every moment — the adaptation's greatest
   visible miracle, [design on canon base], never claimed as canon grass behavior (BEAST_LAW
   §10). It is priced: every release of old earth answered by a new rooted reach ahead; it is
   not walking, not swiftness, and it is the costliest thing he has ever learned.
2. **Survival is not guaranteed.** He can be grazed, stepped on, burned, dried, carried off
   by the flood. Being a soul beast does not make the carpet sacred to anyone else. Every
   century he survives is earned.
3. **Slow power, fast world.** The beasts around him live, age, and die on timescales he will
   outlast. That asymmetry is a core engine of the story, not a footnote.
4. **The ground is not the only listener.** His ground-reading is how HE senses. But the
   Emperor's domain (canon: A Yin's Blue Silver Domain) reads Blue Silver Grass across its
   reach — so one day, when he is no longer ordinary grass, the domain will find him. Until
   then there is nothing in the grass for the domain to notice, and he stays unread.
   **[canon base + user design on timing]**
