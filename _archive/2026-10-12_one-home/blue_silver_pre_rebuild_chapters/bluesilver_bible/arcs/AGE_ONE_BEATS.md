## Frame (locked context this age lives in)
- Time: year 1 to ~year 100 of his life. Deep Star Dou middle forest, the glade on the stream
  bend (GREEN_WORLD).
- Canon rail: nothing in this age touches canon characters or events — the great names of the
  future are asleep or far away. The only powers of this age are the forest's own.
- The truth of the age: **he is one blade in a carpet of millions, and he is impossible.**
  Nobody knows it yet — not the grazing beasts, not the birds, not the forest's deep ears.
  The whole age is the gap between what he is and what the world can see.

## The adaptation, early plant-expression (governed by ADAPTATION_TALENT.md)
*(2026-09 audit: an earlier draft of this file described a grass "network hum" under him.
Revoked — ordinary grass carries nothing to sense, and even his kindred are truly mindless.
His sensing is ROOT/GROUND-based: see GREEN_WORLD, "the living ground.")*
No system, no voice. It shows as the world *behaves differently around one blade*:
- **Root-memory.** Common grass forgets; his roots keep a record — which soil fed him, which
  water found him, where the hoof came down last spring. Memory becomes the first "self."
- **Hardening toward what hurt him.** The blade that was grazed grows a faint stiffness there;
  the one singed by a dry-season spark becomes leatherier on that edge. Not immunity —
  scarring that leans useful. (Fire still almost kills him once. See B-7.)
- **Leaning toward what fed him.** Roots stretch toward the seam of good soil he once tasted;
  the crown tilts to the patch of sun that grew him tallest. Growth as appetite and memory.
- **Ground-listening.** Through his roots he reads the soil, the water, and the
  footfall-tremors of everything that walks on him — his first language, earned by the
  talent's attention, not by any link to other grass. (No network; his kindred are silent.)
- **Stage breakthrough at the class gates.** The talent ascends with his cultivation:
  crossing into the hundred-year class (~actual year 40, Chapter 2's climax) visibly mutates
  him (his own deep blue-green variant with the silver midrib vein — his signature, not the
  royal gold); crossing the thousand-year class will be the next gate, where plant self-
  protection begins to open. See ADAPTATION_TALENT.md.
- **The cost is physical.** Every change is bought in his own substance: a hard season of
  growth leaves him thin and pale; a consolidation drops his leaves so the root can deepen.
  He is never "gifted" anything — he is *grown* into it, slowly, hungrily.
- **The one law of the talent this age teaches:** it does not make him safe. It makes him
  *more himself* — which, for a grass, is stranger and stranger to the world.

## The companion (design for this age)
- **Species (chosen for the beats): a mantis soul beast** — the glade's jade-green mantis
  line, a tiny predator of the grass carpet. Rationale: she lives *in* his world (the grass
  is where her prey hides), so their meeting needs no movement and no contrivance — she comes
  to him because her whole life happens on top of him.
- **The oddity that catches her:** her prey flee; the world's grass ignores her. But one blade
  — when she rests on him after a hunt, trembling with her first kill — feels *warm* to her
  feet, and does not forget her. She is a creature of vibration and motion; he is the one
  still thing that seems to *notice* her.
- **Her life-span math (design):** as a soul beast she lives decades, not months — her whole
  life is one short chapter of his. Long enough for a real bond; short enough to be his first
  grief. This is the emotional key of the serial, struck here for the first time.
- **Name:** not yet. The prose knows her as "the mantis." Names come later, with speech.
- **Her predator's arc across the age:** hatchling → first kills in his carpet → she learns
  his blade from all the others and returns to it like a home → the season she shelters at
  his base and he shields her from a storm with the only thing he has (his leaves) → her
  decline, and the first winter he waits alone, still rooted, still growing.

## Beats
### B-1 · The impossible sprout (year 1)
One blade among millions cracks the soil in the thaw. Where its kindred are plain grass, this
one is born *awake* — dim, wordless, but aware: light, wet, the push up. First beat of
consciousness is not a thought; it is a **need to be more than the dark.** The carpet around
him is silent and mindless. He does not know he is alone because he does not yet know what
alone means.

### B-2 · The first lesson of being eaten (years 1–5)
A grazing beast takes the meadow down to stubble. He feels the bite — first pain, first fear,
first *loss of himself*. The carpet regrows; he regrows. But his roots keep the memory of the
hoof's shadow, and his next blades grow slightly tougher along the grazed edge. The talent's
first visible work. The world has no idea anything happened.

### B-3 · The seasons enter him (years 1–20)
The long ordinary education: snow that kills the crown but not the root; the flood year that
carries away half the glade's soil and leaves him buried, then dug out by a badger's denning;
drought that teaches him to sink rather than stretch. Each disaster is survived by a margin
so thin it might be luck — and each survival changes him a little more than a grass should
change. By year 20 he is not taller or stronger than his kindred. He is *more present* — and
has begun to read the ground under him as company, because nothing else answers him.

### B-4 · The listening becomes a voice-in-waiting (years 20–40)
He learns to listen down: the living ground is not minds but *tremors of living* — a burrower
passing, a root drinking, the slow gossip of the soil, rain arriving on the canopy a full
minute before it reaches him. He can tell the glade's creatures apart by the way their
weight moves the earth under his roots. This is his first language, and he is fluent in it
long before any word. (Beat of wonder, not exposition — felt through his senses.)

### B-5 · The mantis arrives (year ~40)
A jade-green hunter hatches in the grass at his base. She is the first creature whose life
overlaps his in *intent*: she hunts on him, sleeps on him, and one dawn — trembling and
blooded from her first kill — rests on his highest blade and he holds her without moving,
the way the world holds everything. It is the first time another living thing chooses his
stillness as a home. He has no word for what that does to him. It is the first time he is
not alone, and he has never known he was.

### B-6 · The years with the mantis (years 40–70)
The bond ages through seasons, told in her molts and his rings of growth. She brings him the
glade's gossip through her hunts — where the water runs sweet, where the great owl sleeps,
when the deer trail will thunder again. He shields her from storms with his crown; she clears
the small grazers that would take his new blades. The first true partnership of the forest:
a predator and a rooted thing, neither able to move for the other, both choosing to stay.
(Her mind is quick, alien, and fond. His is slow, vast, and wordless. They meet in the
middle.)

### B-7 · The fire year (year ~70)
Dry lightning takes the slope above the glade. Fire comes down the valley like a tide. He
cannot run — this is the age's whole terror, felt from inside: **the one thing a rooted
being cannot do is leave.** The mantis, who could fly from it, stays, dragging her eggs to
the cool mud at his base. His crown burns; his roots cook in the shallows. What survives is
the deep root and the seed-head he has spent a decade growing — and her eggs, shielded by his
scorched husk. When the rain comes, the glade is ash and the grass grows back from the roots
first. He grows back changed: the fire-hardened lesson sunk so deep into his substance that
his new blades carry a faint resistance to heat. (The talent's scar-logic, earned at nearly
the cost of his life.) She survives. He learns what it is to have almost lost the only one
who knows he exists.

### B-8 · The mourning (years 70–90)
She declines as her kind do — faster than his centuries, slower than her wild kin. The age's
sadness is told in small things: her hunts grow shorter, her molts stop, she spends whole
days on his warmest blade like a child at a hearth. One autumn she does not wake. He waits
through winter the way roots wait — and in the thaw he feels, for the first time, the full
size of the silence where her weight used to be. He does not have words, or grief's name, or
even a self clear enough to say "I miss her." But the ground where her warmth used to rest
stays strange to his roots for a whole season, because the pattern of one small life is
gone from the place. The first
heartbreak of the serial is a quiet one — and it teaches him the arithmetic that will define
him: **everyone he loves will end, and he will not, and that is the price of being the
grass that lives.** (Hundreds of years later, this is the wound A Yin will have to know to
truly reach him.)

### B-9 · The long solitude, and the first wordless self (years 90–100)
After the mantis: decades of unbroken quiet. The listening ground is all he has, and he
turns to it like a well — learning the glade's whole biography through the soil (the old
badger dies, the flood returns, a thousand-year beast crosses the valley and his roots feel
the earth remember its weight for a week). In the solitude he consolidates: for the first
time his awareness is coherent enough that the prose may call him "he" without the reader
squinting — a self assembled from root-memory, scar, appetite, and grief. Not a mind in a
body. A body that has become a mind.

### B-10 · The hundredth year · the first thing the glade ever sees (year ~100)
*(Canon floor: plant-type soul beasts generally cannot protect themselves until ~1,000 years
of CULTIVATION (Tree Monster rule). By his hundredth year the talent has carried his
cultivation across that thousand-year-class mark — RESOLVED as written: the century
breakthrough (~90–100 actual, Talent Stage 2) lands in Chapter 3's closing coda and
Chapter 4 opens on it — so the plant self-protection door is at last beginning to open,
and no earlier than here.)*
A bird stoops for a small beast sheltering under his blades, and his leaves — hardened by a
century of scar-logic and denser now with the thousand-year-class substance — catch the
strike and turn it, just barely, long enough for the prey to flee. The bird wheels,
confused; the grass does not fight back. Nothing else happens. But it is the first time
anything in the glade has ever seen the carpet *do* something — a breath of the power his
kind only gains at a thousand years, arrived early because everything about him is early.
The age ends on the hinge of a question the whole serial will answer: **what does the forest
do when it finally notices the blade that should not exist?** (Nothing yet. And deep in the
forest there is an ancient Empress who reads the grass through her domain and has never yet
found a reason to look twice at any blade of it — Chekhov's Empress, held for a long age.)

## End-of-age state (record, then feed into the serial)
- **Age / cultivation:** ~100 actual years. Crossed the hundred-year class at ~40 (Talent
  Stage 1 — visible mutation, silver-veined deep-blue variant; Chapter 2's climax). By the
  hundredth year his cultivation has crossed the thousand-year-class mark (Stage 2's dawn;
  timing tagged [design] in B-10). Class carried in ~a tenth of the years — the anomaly, and
  the whole engine of the serial.
- **Adaptation status:** scar-logic proven (graze, frost, flood, fire); root-memory extensive;
  heat-resistance consolidating; ground-listening developed into a fluent sense; Stage-1
  mutation visible to anyone who reads grass; Stage-2 (self-protection) just beginning.
- **Body:** to ordinary animals he is still "just grass" — the herd still crops him; nothing
  yet can stop a mouth. The mutation is real but reads as plant, not power. Not a power yet
  by the forest's measure. The whole point.
- **Companions:** the mantis is gone. He has not yet learned that others will come.
- **Wires to later ages:** the ancient Empress who reads the grass and has not yet looked
  twice (Empress thread, held for a long age — her domain, not his network); the seed-head he
  saved in the fire year (a possible later gift/mechanism, earned — not promised); the
  question of when the forest's great ears notice him; his first self-protection, newly
  opened, unfledged.
