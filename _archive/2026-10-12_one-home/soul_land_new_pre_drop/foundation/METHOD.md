# METHOD — the writing system (distilled)
### Franchise-neutral craft, condensed from the two old projects' method docs + the uploaded 56-rule method.
### These are habits, not religion. The story owns the rules; the rules do not own the story.

---

## 1. CORE BELIEF

The job is not "write an exciting scene." The job is:

**Build a coherent living world → put characters in it → simulate what would really happen →
convert that simulation into prose without breaking established truth.**

Priority order:
Consistency > convenience. Causality > coincidence. Character agency > author control.
Earned progression > arbitrary escalation. Consequences > spectacle. Truth > memory.

---

## 2. TWO MODES — ARCHITECT AND AUTHOR

- **Architect mode:** canon, world, causality, character simulation, timeline, knowledge layers,
  power logic, consequences, continuity. Decides WHAT can happen.
- **Author mode:** prose, dialogue, viewpoint, pacing, emotion, scene presentation. Decides HOW it's told.
- **Never let Author override Architect.** If it's not architecturally true, no prose polish saves it.

---

## 3. SOURCE-OF-TRUTH LAYERS

1. Hard truth (verified canon, universe rules, explicit user decisions, established facts).
2. Approved divergence (user-approved changes from source).
3. Current story state (everything that has happened on the page).
4. Character knowledge (what each person knows / believes / misreads / doesn't know).
5. Future information (author knowledge characters haven't earned — protect it).

Knowledge firewall: a character never behaves as if they know what they haven't learned.

---

## 4. CAUSALITY, NOT COINCIDENCE

Every important beat must answer the three questions:
**Why does this happen? Why now? Why this way?**
If you can't answer all three, the beat isn't ready.

Every divergence propagates: trigger → immediate effect → secondary effect → character reaction →
institutional reaction → long-term consequence. A ban is not a butterfly — an unbroken rule changes
nothing downstream. If it changes nothing, don't claim it changed the story.

---

## 5. THE LIVING WORLD

While the protagonist does X, everyone else is doing something. The world functions when the
protagonist is absent. Pressure items age; silence is a decision; an honest "not now" beats
accidental forgetting.

---

## 6. POWER / PROGRESSION HONESTY

Never confuse having power with controlling or understanding it. Every gain answers:
What changed? What caused it? What was already possessed? What failed first? What limitation remains?

Nothing is absolute. Rank/power labels (King / Emperor / Sage …) are *guesses about a fight*, not
fixtures. Victory, failure, and survival must each have reasons — no plot armor.

---

## 7. STORY ECONOMY AND THE FULL-PLAY LAW

Every element must justify the narrative space it consumes. Chapters have body, organs, and real
turns — no thin hinges, no copy-machine rooms, no kit-recap-as-story, no audit-as-story.
**Play full.** Use the whole kit, the whole cast, the consequences. A named job finishes in the turn
it's given.

---

## 8. THE AUDIT LOOP (before you call a chapter done)

- **Canon audit:** location, timeline, characters, knowledge, power, regional behavior. What changed
  *because of our divergence*? Anything not deliberately changed remains canon.
- **Continuity audit:** ages, dates, injuries, promises, relationships, impossible travel, resources.
- **Character audit:** does the voice, values, and decision-making still belong to this person? Change
  must be earned.
- **Power audit:** did strength come from an established cause? What counters remain? Does the world react?
- **Tension audit:** what can be lost? who can fail? what stays unknown? why isn't it easy?

Repair at the smallest possible area, preserve established material, then record the new state.

---

## 9. MEMORY (the ledger habit)

After every chapter: state snapshot (world / characters / power / resources / secrets / open threads /
pressures) + consequence ledger (what changed, who knows, what's now possible, what's now impossible).
Old numbers get corrected at the source, not whispered around the project.

---

## 10. USER-COLLABORATION RULES (learned the hard way, from the old chats)

- Do not **parrot** the user's explanations back into prose or status files — understand the thing,
  then write the thing.
- Do not use skill names as stickers ("Fire-body took the graze"). Show what it means.
- Do not present files instead of talking. Talk like a partner.
- Do not recap after presenting.
- If a user calls a scene trash, find *what it skipped*, not just re-word it.
- Ask before guessing a fork the user cares about; don't gold-plate what they didn't ask for.
