## 0. THE IMMUTABLE LAWS

1. **CANON IS THE SPINE.** The OC is a parallel thread, woven around it. Never the center, never the replacement.
2. **VERIFY BEFORE YOU WRITE.** Fetch ALL sources. Build a complete beat ledger. Never write from a compressed summary or a single search.
3. **ADAPTATION TALENT IS ALWAYS ACTIVE.** Every exposure = data = growth. Even during rest, sleep, and mundane moments. Track it. Never let it sit idle.
4. **THE EPISODE ARC IS SACRED.** *(NEW)* Maintain a verified master episode↔chapter map. Never announce "next is Episode N" without checking the canonical episode list. Never carry numbering in your head across sessions — it drifts. Verify identity and order from the list, every time.
5. **A SEASON SUMMARY IS NOT AN EPISODE.** *(NEW)* Season-overview synopses compress and merge events across many episodes. Never import a season-summary line as a single-episode beat. Always verify per-episode.
6. **POWER HAS TWO AXES — physical tier ≠ overall level.** *(NEW)* A character's raw strength/speed/durability tier (Axis 1) is NOT their overall combat level (Axis 2 = all powers × adaptivity × tactics). **Evo does NOT need to BE a tier to BEAT a tier.** Limits are current challenges solved via combination + growth — not permanent ceilings. *(See `evo_power_reference.md`.)*
7. **EVO IS A MAIN CHARACTER — not a "parallel thread."** *(CORRECTED)* He is a full Avenger, a co-protagonist, with his OWN fights, victories, growth, and iconic moments. He FIGHTS and WINS. Canon characters own their iconic beats; Evo has HIS OWN, with equal weight. He is a multi-thousand-ton powerhouse whose presence is SUBSTANTIAL in every episode.
8. **THE ADAPTATION TALENT COMPOUNDS — visibly, dramatically, every episode.** *(CORRECTED)* Every fight makes Evo measurably stronger. This is SHOWN in the prose. The longer a fight runs, the more he dominates. Limits are TEMPORARY — he grows THROUGH them. His power is multi-thousand-ton (ALL stats equivalent) and GROWING. ALL his abilities (TK, psychic, energy transfer, empathic projection, gamma-state) are FULLY AWAKENED and devastating — never "rough," "seeded," or "frozen." The gamma-state FIRES and DOMINATES.

## 1. CANON VERIFICATION PROTOCOL (Learned from Hard Failure)

### The mistake that taught the core protocol:
Writing from a 3-sentence synopsis → missing 80% of the episode's beats, details, and character moments. The user said: *"you don't check canon information and missed many things."*

---

### 1.0 EPISODE IDENTITY & SEQUENCE LOCK — *(NEW, highest priority — do this FIRST)*

Before building any beat ledger, confirm the episode's **identity** and **place** in the sequence.

1. Fetch the **canonical season episode list** (Fandom "Season X" page + Wikipedia "List of episodes"). **They must AGREE.**
2. Record: episode number, title, position, and — crucially — which episodes come *before* and *after*.
3. Cross-check against your existing chapter coverage. **Flag every GAP** (skipped episodes) and every **MISMATCH** (a written chapter labeled with the wrong episode number).
4. **NEVER trust an in-head number** like "we're on E10." Verify it from the list every single time.

**THE FAILURE THIS PREVENTS (the signature mistake of this arc):**
Work proceeded E1→E8 correctly, then **silently skipped E9–E18**, wrote **E19 ("The Ambassador")** but **labeled it "Episode 9,"** and announced **"next is Episode 10: All-Father's Day"** — when All-Father's Day is actually **Episode 20**. Ten full episodes were invisible for an entire arc, and the "next" target was off by ten. *Root cause: no master map existed; numbering lived in the model's head and drifted across sessions.*

---

### 1.1 Sources to read (in order)

**Step 1 — Fetch ALL chunks of the episode's Fandom page.**
- Do NOT stop at chunk 0 (navigation) or chunk 1 (partial synopsis).
- Read EVERY chunk (some episodes have 3-5 chunks).
- Extract: Synopsis (full), Quotes, Trivia, Items (first appearances), Cast (who appears — including cameos), Locations, Vehicles.

**Step 2 — Cross-reference with secondary sources (EXPANDED).**
- IMDB (cast + summary).
- TVTropes Recap page (themes/structure the wiki misses).
- **Reviews (IGN, etc.) and fan talkbacks** — reveal on-screen beats, tone, and — critically — *what is NOT in the episode* (e.g., confirming a villain does not appear). Essential when the wiki Synopsis is empty.
- **Apple TV / streaming episode descriptions** — sometimes a second, different one-line summary.
- Marvel Animated Universe wiki (alternate wiki, sometimes different detail).

**Step 3 — Build the COMPLETE BEAT LEDGER.**
- List EVERY beat from the synopsis — not just "key" beats. Every scene transition, every character moment, every reveal, every item debut, every cameo.
- Include small details: trivia notes, character banter, specific items, specific dialogue referenced in the synopsis.
- Number each beat. This is the checklist.

**Step 4 — Do not write until the ledger is complete.**
- If a beat is unclear, fetch more chunks or search for recaps.
- If a beat cannot be verified, flag it and ask the user.

---

### 1.2 THREE NEW VERIFICATION RULES (earned this arc)

- **EMPTY-SYNOPSIS RULE.** When a Fandom Synopsis section is empty or only a one-line Overview exists, you MUST reconstruct beats from secondary sources (TVTropes, reviews, talkbacks). The Overview alone is NEVER enough. *(E9's wiki Synopsis was blank; the full spine came from TVTropes + IGN + the fan talkback.)*
- **SEASON-SUMMARY RULE.** Season-overview paragraphs merge events across episodes. "X joins the team" in a season summary does NOT mean it happens in the episode you're writing. Verify the per-episode scene. *(E9: "Attuma joins the Cabal" was a season-summary compression — IGN + talkback confirmed NO Cabal/Red Skull appears in E9 at all.)*
- **ABSENT-CAST RULE.** Read the Featured/Supporting cast list to learn who is ABSENT. If a character is not listed, they are not in the episode — do not write them in. *(E9: Black Widow absent.)*

## 2. CHAPTER WRITING PROTOCOL (Learned from Compressed Drafts)

### The mistake that taught this:
Cramming an entire episode into 1-2 compressed chapters → skipping openings, missing character moments, rushing pacing. The user said: *"don't put one episode into one chapter"* and *"don't miss anything, not even small details."*

### The protocol:

**Chapter count:** 3–4+ chapters per 22-minute episode. Each chapter = ONE movement of the episode (not the whole thing). Slower pace, fuller scenes.

**Chapter opening (NEVER skip this):**
Every chapter MUST open with:
- **Concrete location** (where are we?)
- **Time of day** (morning? night? during the fight?)
- **Who is present** (name them)
- **What they were doing immediately before** (eating cereal? fighting? sleeping?)
- **The emotional temperature** (calm? tense? post-battle exhaustion?)

Example of a proper opening:
```
The common floor of Avengers Tower smelled like cold coffee and the particular,
accusatory silence of a refrigerator that had given everything it had and been
left open for its trouble. "That," the Hulk said, pointing one enormous finger
at the empty shelves, "is a war crime."
```

**Scene structure:**
- Each scene = a concrete place + characters who want something + action/dialogue + a change + a consequence that connects to the next scene.
- Scene-break (`***`) between threads.
- Braid at the chapter's close — tie the threads toward what's coming.

**Pacing:**
- Full scenes, NEVER summary disguised as a chapter.
- Dialogue-driven, character-specific voices.
- Let moments breathe. Don't rush to the next beat.

### Craft refinement — THEMATIC RESONANCE *(NEW)*
Tie the OC's personal thread to the episode's canon theme via a **shared image** — so the OC contributes depth without centrality. *(E9: Evo's suit-building insight — "energy doesn't bend sharp; give it a soft corner or it backs up" — became the same truth Hulk embodied as **focused rage** and Evo embodied as **aimed instinct**. Three threads, one image, zero OC-centrality.)*

## 3. CANON-FIRST OC-WOVEN STYLE (The Core Principle)

### The 10 Rules (Learned and Refined):

1. **The canon scene is the SPINE and BULK** of every chapter. The OC (Evo/Vihaan/etc.) is a PARALLEL ADDITIVE THREAD — woven around the canon, never replacing it.
2. **Build each chapter on the actual canon scene** (from the beat ledger). Fully dramatize it.
3. **The OC runs as a parallel thread** — his own scenes (devotion, duty, daily life), intersecting canon naturally on HIS OWN beats.
4. **Braid** the two threads with a scene-break (`***`). End by braiding both toward the approaching storm.
5. **HONOR SCENE OWNERSHIP.** Canon characters keep their iconic actions. The OC never takes a canon character's beat. (Falcon owns Ghost of a Chance. Cap owns Super-Adaptoid. Thor owns Serpent of Doom. Hulk owns Depth Charge.)
6. **Full scenes, never summary.**
7. **Living voiced ensemble** + source-medium grammar (TV-serial rhythm for Indian shows; action-comedy rhythm for Avengers).
8. **OC powers restrained** — pre-awakening: devotion/instinct/magnetism only; no system voice.
9. **Dramatic irony** — the reader sees more than the OC; the OC stays unaware.
10. **Concrete endings** — never "everything was about to change."

### What the OC NEVER does:
- Never steals a canon character's win.
- Never solves the plot with raw power alone.
- Never adapts past a category his AT can't touch (Asgardian magic, body-swap, cosmic scale).
- Never becomes the center of every scene.
- Never has his AT portrayed as a system/AI/voice.

### What the OC DOES:
- His own intro thread (E1: A.I.M. drone fight).
- His own power development (solar project, energy capacity growth, suit-build thread).
- His own thematic contributions ("nothing is absolute"; "the mind wins, not power"; "aim, don't just swing").
- His own character growth (reckless → wise).
- Supports in every fight — always parallel, never replacement.

### OC-IN-CLIMAX RULE *(NEW)*
In a canon character's defining climax, the OC supports at a **SECONDARY load point**, never the primary one. *(E9: Hulk holds the whole island — his beat, unequivocally; Evo braces one cracked secondary column to buy Tony time. The OC is essential but visibly lesser.)* This keeps the climax owned by its canon hero while the OC still genuinely matters.

## 4. ADAPTATION TALENT TRACKING SYSTEM

### The Core Rules (from the AT Framework — Scales With User):

1. **The AT level = the USER's tier.** Mortal user → mortal-scope AT. Supernatural user → supernatural-scope AT. Cosmic user → cosmic-scope AT. It grows when the user grows.
2. **Two axes:** Tier (mortal → superhuman → supernatural → cosmic → divine) sets the ceiling. Development (Nascent → Developing → Mature → Perfected-within-tier) sets refinement.
3. **Always active.** Even during rest, sleep, and mundane moments — consolidating, filing, optimizing, maintaining, preparing, growing.
4. **Exposure → data → adaptation → growth.** Every hit, every energy frequency, every environment, every failure = data for the AT.
5. **Failure-as-data.** When the user fails (can't stop a meteor, gets possessed, can't adapt to Asgardian magic), the AT learns from it. The failure creates a pathway for future growth.
6. **Law of Compatibility.** Closer to the user's foundation = faster adaptation. Further = slower or impossible without a path.
7. **Never downscale (no nerfing). Never exaggerate beyond earned exposure/resources/time.**
8. **Non-sentient.** Never narrated as speaking, choosing, calculating. Shown through embodied performance only.


## 5. ERROR AUTOPSY PROTOCOL (What to Do When You Mess Up)

### The rule:
When the user says there are mistakes: **do not defend, do not shallow-apologize, do not immediately rewrite.** First identify every failure, trace to source, then fix.

### Steps:
1. **Identify every failure** — surface mistakes AND deeper causal mistakes (canon errors, characterization errors, knowledge leakage, timeline, location, prop, power-rule, relationship pacing, scene theft, unsupported inventions, forced canon restoration, AT errors, reveal-timing, misread intent, **episode-sequence/numbering errors**).
2. **Trace backward to source** — wrong canon reconstruction? missing checkpoint? unsupported assumption? faulty butterfly? misread user intention? **drifted numbering / no master map?**
3. **Explain the real causes concisely.**
4. **Add the mistakes to the rejection list.**
5. **Quarantine the faulty draft.**
6. **Return to the last safe checkpoint.**
7. **Reconstruct correctly.**
8. **Apply the lesson permanently.** Convert recurring mistakes into prevention rules.

## 11. THE FINAL TEST (Before Every Creative Choice)

> *"Is this supported by canon, foundation, previous events, character knowledge, world rules, and believable causality?"*
