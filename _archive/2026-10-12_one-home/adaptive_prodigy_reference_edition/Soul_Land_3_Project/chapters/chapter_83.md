## Part 1: The Flower Grows

Gu Yue reached out and grabbed the air, and opened her hand on specks of blue.

They multiplied, merged, and a sphere of water-light stood on her palm — and then petals, azure, fashioned the way the earth's had been, floating up to circle above the yellow: eight over nine, the two layers overlapping as they turned together. Her three soul rings lit in turn, each aiding its own part of the work.

*Dual element control.* The two middle-aged examiners nodded approval — and then she grabbed the air again, and green winked into being, and the nodding stopped. Wind. Dual-element soul masters existed, even at Shrek; three was *unprecedented* — more was not better, the rings each touched one element, the body strained, and theory said, flatly, that so many was impossible. The whirlpool condensed; the green became seven petals above the water.

The fourth time she clutched the air, the examiner on the right shot out of his seat, and Shen Yi's eyes went wide — she had watched this girl work multiple elements at once before, and never to this degree. Embers gathered; a fireball stood in her palm; sweat rolled down her temples and her eyes did not lose their calm by so much as a hair.

The old lady leaned forward, and the mask of indifference cracked.

Four elements. Earth, wind, water, fire — the four fundamentals, the ones the world itself is made of; to hold all four was to hold a piece of the power of creation. Shrek's own records held one three-element soul master, and he had made Soul Sage before the balancing crushed him. The legends said that if the four ever came into harmony, their joined power could be harnessed. And here, in an examination room, the four turned over one another in a thirteen-year-old's hands — the first in the entirety of the continent's history.

Not much of any element — that was not the point. The point was the mind, split four ways, tending the equilibriums: earth to water, water to wind, wind to fire, each relationship a strain, the burden rising exponentially with each addition. She held it. The fire congealed into six blazing petals, blossoming above the rest, and the quad-colored flower turned slowly at her fingertips.

The class gaped with the examiners. The three people in the room most familiar with Gu Yue discovered, together, that they had not been familiar with her at all — and the tactical part of every mind present did the same arithmetic at the same time: *control like this, and a four-element fusion attack becomes possible in battle.* The examiners had already gone further: only the Spirit Sea realm could do this at all.

Then she grabbed the air a fifth time, and the old lady stood up.

The sunlight beaming through the windows came apart into golden specks and ran to Gu Yue's hand — light, the fifth — and she did not force it into petals. She spun it around the four-colored flower, a halo for a thing that should not exist, and the room understood with one voice that the show had been building to a sentence.

## Part 2: I Present This to You

"I present this to you!"

Her head came up; her gaze went at the old lady like a laser; and the flower vanished from her palm in a flash of silver light and reappeared in front of the examiner's table.

"Careful!" Shen Yi cried — no longer controlled, the four elements in the flower were already sliding toward chaos, a bomb that could go off at any moment, and Gu Yue knew it, and had done it anyway.

She had done it on purpose. The class knew the purpose without a meeting: this was the woman who had put their captain into a wall twenty minutes ago, and of all the class's many ways of saying *that was our brother you hit*, Gu Yue's was a four-element explosive delivered with a bow's politeness. The sixth, at the chairs, filed it with perfect composure as the most honest bill presented all day, and revised, slightly, his private estimate of how the purse spent when it did spend.

The old lady's surprise dissolved into a smile. Her arm waved, sleeves billowing — and behind her a crescent moon stood up into the air of the room, shining down on the flower, and **nine soul rings rose around her.**

This time the class could make out their levels. Four purple. Four black. And one **red** — the red of a hundred-thousand-year soul ring, a color none of them had ever seen on a living person, and the power rolling off her pressed the room flat.

The moonlight took the flower, and the flower froze, caught like a thing in a trance — and then, ever so slowly, melted. The elements went home. Ashes to ashes, dust to dust.

*Nine rings,* every one of class zero thought, in pandemonium, at the same time. *She's a Title Douluo. A nine-ringed Title Douluo — with a hundred-thousand-year soul ring—*

Even Gu Yue had a lifeless look in her eyes. The flower had taken her five minutes and better than sixty percent of her power to build; the old lady had annihilated it in an instant, smiling, without using her full strength — and Wulin, still pale in his chair, quietly finalized the morning's arithmetic: *so that's where the wall came from.* The sixth, who had failed to read her twice, counted the rings instead — four purple, four black, one red — and wrote the red one down in the ledger of things the alphabet does not cover, in ink.

## Part 3: Good, Good, Good

"Good, good, good!" A grin had taken over the old lady's whole face, and she turned to Shen Yi. "Zhuo Shi did quite well to have me extend the exam period for these kids. This girl doesn't need to take the following trials. Let's have her directly enter the inner court. Even I can't help myself at the thought of a six-element soul master — this has to be a first in the entire history of the continent!"

The name landed on the class like a coin dropped in a quiet room. *Zhuo Shi.* Elder Li had said it once, taking their wager — *Zhuo Shi would have liked that* — and here it was again, out of Shrek's own mountain, attached to the reason any of this day had happened at all. The class did not look at each other. The class looked at each other.

And the rest of the sentence caught up: *doesn't need to take the following trials. Directly enter the inner court.* The thing they had crossed a continent and knelt at a gate for — the terms, the work, the years of arithmetic — and Gu Yue had just been lifted over the whole of it, sideways, by a woman who could not help herself. The four of them did the silent arithmetic of what this meant for a team of five with a wager riding on *passing everything*, and got four different answers, and none of them said a word, because the old lady was already walking over to Gu Yue, looking her up and down.

"Girl," she asked, with a smile, "do you want to become my disciple?"

The room held its breath. The flower's dust was still settling. Somewhere in the building a bell rang once, for nothing.

And at the long table, Shen Yi — whose file on class zero was becoming a book — felt the morning shift under her, because the old lady had stopped one examinee short of the roster. There were five chairs. Four had shown. And the fifth examinee, the one with the three pages and the sword-line and the spring report, the one whose word had gone around the hall this morning — *Comprehensive* — was still sitting at the side with his hands folded, last in the custom of them, waiting for a turn that had just been swallowed whole by a discipleship.

The hall that hears everything turned, very slightly, toward the fifth chair.
