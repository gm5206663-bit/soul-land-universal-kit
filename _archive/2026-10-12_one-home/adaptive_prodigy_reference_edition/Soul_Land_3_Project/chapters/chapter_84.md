## Part 1: I Don't Want To

"Girl, do you want to become my disciple?"

Gu Yue was taken aback — and then shook her head. "I don't want to."

"You don't want to?" For the first time all day, the old lady was dumbstruck. "Why?"

"No reason. I just don't want to."

"Gu Yue!" Shen Yi crossed to her at once. "Don't speak like that. Do you have any idea who this is? Elder Cai is an elder of Shrek Academy's most guarded **Sea God's Pavilion**. Her title is Silver Moon. She is the **Silver Moon Douluo**, and she stands at the summit of this continent! You are lucky to have the chance to become her disciple!"

None of them had known the name. All of them understood the weight of the way Shen Yi said it. Six years ago the Silver Moon Douluo had gone through the continent like weather that owed nobody an explanation — a heaven-shaking eccentric, yielding to no one, doing exactly as she pleased, her rampages turning the soul master world upside down until Shrek had sent people to bring her home. Then nothing. Six years of nothing, ending in this room, holding a flower's ashes.

Gu Yue met Shen Yi's eyes, gnawed her lip, and shook her head again. "She hit Wulin. So I don't want to be her disciple."

Neither Elder Cai nor Shen Yi had expected *that* to be the problem. The examiners' table sat in a moment of pure incomprehension — the petulance was real, and its fuel was a boy-shaped dent in a wall.

"Nonsense!" Elder Cai was disgruntled. "That kid's claw is special! If I hadn't used some strength to send him flying, his claw would have gripped my old bones and *injured* me. I didn't even harm him."

The two middle-aged examiners stared. There was an apology hidden in there — an actual apology, to a thirteen-year-old, from the Silver Moon Douluo. Neither of them would ever speak of it.

"Gu Yue, I'm fine! Really! Think about this carefully!" — Wulin, the words tumbling out clumsily, because he understood exactly what was being thrown away.

She turned back to Elder Cai, and her eyes were earnest. "Wherever they are, I will also be. We won't be separated. If they enter the inner court, then I'll enter too."

"Why are you such a handful?" The sleeve flicked. "Fine. Have it your way. I don't care anymore." And the Silver Moon Douluo stormed out of her own trial.

## Part 2: The Zeros

Wulin tugged Gu Yue's arm the moment the door shut. "Why did you do that? She's a nine-ringed Title Douluo! Her *weakest* soul ring is thousand-year! She has a hundred-thousand-year red soul ring! Do you know how rare that is on this continent?"

Gu Yue met his gaze, honest to the bottom. "She hit you. So I'm not happy."

That put a lid on it. Xie Xie exhaled. "You know, I always thought I was prideful. Now I know what true arrogance is. Gu Yue, you're so *awesome.*" And Xiaoyan, laughing despite everything: "Big sis Gu Yue is so honest. Really."

Shen Yi came back ashen, and pointed a trembling finger. "You! Who do you think you are? Do you know how many people *dream* of the chance you just threw away?" Gu Yue lowered her head and said nothing — she had given her one explanation, to Wulin, and nothing else was coming out of those lips.

"I'm sorry." Wulin, anxious, formal. "It was our fault. Gu Yue is tired; she's not thinking properly. Teacher Shen Yi — can we still continue with the exam?"

"Your exam should finish here." A sigh. "But you can continue. I've notified Teacher already. Hopefully he can placate Elder Cai's rage. Let's go."

In the corridor, Xie Xie — confidence intact — asked the question. "Teacher Shen, how many points did we get for the fourth trial?"

"Zero points." Shen Yi glared. "You all got zero points."

"*Zero?*" Xie Xie nearly detonated. "Even if we weren't amazing, shouldn't we get *some*? Or are you telling me Shrek Academy can't be impartial? Is this because we offended—"

"In Shrek Academy, the **Sea God's Pavilion** makes the rules," Shen Yi said, sweeping them with a deep gaze. "If Elder Cai says zero, then it is zero. No one can dispute it — not even another elder. And now that you've scored it, you can't enter the inner court even if you're absolutely amazing in every trial that's left." Her eyes moved to Gu Yue. "Do you understand the consequences of impulsiveness now?"

Gu Yue did not retort. She followed Wulin in silence.

## Part 3: The Sixth's Ledger

Lin Hao had stood through all of it with his hands folded, watching, and inside the quiet he was good at, the ledger did what it always did: it priced things.

*Item.* The academy's summit had just been standing in front of them — the Silver Moon, the Sea God's Pavilion, nine rings with a red one at the top. And in the same room, unremarked by anyone with a rank, sat a boy whose starting four — three perfect purple and one black, at thirteen — would have been the crown of the academy's own showcase, any decade they chose. Beside him sat a girl who had just held five elements in one hand in front of examiners who called three *unprecedented.* His own level read forty-five. That alone, at his age, was absurd; under it sat a foundation nobody here had begun to measure — and the hall's answer to the whole arithmetic of them was a zero, administered twice, by temper.

*Ignored,* the ledger said, without heat. That was the finding. Not *unseen* — three pages of his file said seen. *Ignored.*

*Item.* He had not come for Shrek. He was here because four people had crossed a continent and knelt at a gate, and because one of them had just refused an empire for the sake of a boy in a wall. Take the five of them out of the arithmetic and there was no reason on this earth for him to be standing in this building at all — and the building had done nothing, to this point, to give him one.

*Item, and the sum:* the gate that priced them by quotas. A wall of other men's promises, read and left. A spring priced honestly and filed. A showcase scored zero for a bruise to an old woman's pride. His impression of Shrek Academy, to this point, itemized, totaled, and signed: **negative.**

He put the ledger away. It was not a bitter document; it was a bill, and bills got paid or they got corrected, and either way you kept the numbers. He was thirteen, and he had time, and the next room had tables in it.

*Wulin's* ledger was running too, and it read nothing like his brother's. *Will this affect our exam? Is there anything good waiting for us now that we've offended her?* — the captain's dread, doing arithmetic on five futures at once.

## Part 4: The Fifth Room

The chamber they entered was the same size as the last, and completely different: tables everywhere, and on the tables, mountains of things — the honest clutter of a hundred crafts.

"The fifth trial tests your talent," Shen Yi said. "Well — more like your second profession."

Wulin's eyes went around the room once and lit. Among the many tables, one of them was for **blacksmithing**, and the sight of it went through him like breakfast — and then the elation died, because the examiner's chair behind the table was occupied by a familiar figure in familiar disgrace.

Elder Cai looked at Wulin. She sneered.

"Elder Cai, you—" Shen Yi was out of words.

The Silver Moon Douluo snorted. "For the fifth trial, you all score zero points."

Rage came up in Gu Yue's eyes so fast Wulin got an arm in front of her without looking, and his look at Xie Xie stopped the explosion at the fuse. *This was Shrek Academy. Her home ground.* Behind his face the captain was doing the only arithmetic that mattered now: the team, the futures, the fury swallowed for all five of them.

"Don't say I didn't give Zhuo Shi any face," Elder Cai said, to the room, to nobody. "If they do well in the following trials, they will still be able to enter the inner court despite scoring zero twice. If not — they can go back to where they came from."

*Zero again.* The fifth trial and scored before touching a tool. Shen Yi's face twisted, and she stood still a long moment, because she knew the numbers nobody had told them: **ten trials, ten points each, one hundred possible. Sixty for the outer court. Eighty-five for the inner.** Two zeros meant twenty points gone forever — meant *exceptional*, in every trial that remained, or nothing. In a normal year, one or two examinees in the whole cycle reached the inner court at all.

She did not criticize Gu Yue. There was no point in that now.

And at the back of the five, the sixth looked at the blacksmithing table — the one craft in that room he had carried since he could reach an anvil, the fifth rank of it earned at almost twelve, second-youngest in a Branch's records — and the ledger opened one line, in the margin, where hope lived when it lived at all:

*Zero, twice. Eight trials left. Perfect, or home.*

They would have to be perfect. And one of them, at least, had never been anything else on that side of a forge.
