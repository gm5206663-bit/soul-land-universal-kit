## Part 1: What The Shape Did

The first match was against a team from a city Lin Hao had never heard of, and it lasted two minutes and eleven seconds, and he counted, because that was what his head did.

The shape worked.

He had spent eleven days building it and expected the building to feel like something, and it did not feel like anything. It felt like a thing that had been decided and was now happening.

Xu Xiaoyan's starwheel went up at the start, because it was night, and five seconds happened, and in five seconds Gu Yue put earth under two of them and Xie Xie put the Twin Dragon Storm into the third, and Lin Hao used the Track to be four metres left of where the fourth one was going to be, and Wulin's Bind took the fifth and the sixth, and it was over.

Two minutes and eleven seconds.

Xu Xiaoyan sat down afterwards, because that was what the starwheel cost, and she was smiling, and she said, "That's the first time it's worked the way it's supposed to," and Lin Hao said, "That's the first time we've been somewhere useful when it happened," and she looked at him a moment and said, "That's the nicest thing anybody's said to me today," and he said, "I've said it twice," and she said, "I know."

## Part 2: What Shrek Was Doing

They were standing at the back.

Lin Hao had noticed them in the first match, because he noticed things, and because they were the only people in the stands not doing what the rest of the stands were doing.

Everybody else was watching the winners.

Shrek was watching the shape.

He could not see their faces from the stage. He could see that they were not moving, and that they stood in a formation rather than a group, and that the two at the back were a round boy and a girl, and that the round boy was eating something, and that none of them had reacted to the two minutes and eleven seconds in any way at all.

He used the Track in the second match, and he was four metres left of where he had been, and when he looked up afterwards the two at the back had not moved, and the round boy had stopped eating.

That was the thing he wrote down.

## Part 3: What Gu Yue Saw

She came and stood next to him between matches, which she had done twice now, and he had started to understand that this was what she did instead of saying something.

"They're not watching us," she said.

"I know."

"They're watching the shape."

"Yes."

"I've been watching them watch us," Gu Yue said, "for two matches, and I've worked something out, and I want you to tell me if it's wrong, because you read things and I don't, and I'd rather be told than work it out."

Lin Hao looked at the stands.

"They're not watching who wins," he said. "They're watching who does what without being asked. Xu Xiaoyan sat down and nobody told her to. Xie Xie used the Storm and it cost him everything and he did it anyway. You put earth down before anyone said earth. I went left before anyone said left." He stopped. "They're watching the five of us do things nobody asked for, and counting how many of us do it."

Gu Yue was quiet a while.

"Since Wang Jinxi went," she said, "you've been counting the room before you count the opponent."

"Have I."

"Three weeks. You used to look at the person across from you first. Now you look at where everybody's standing, then at them, and the looking-at-everybody's getting faster and the looking-at-them's getting shorter." She said it the way she said everything, as though reading it off something only she had been given. "I'm not telling you it's wrong."

"No. You'd say it if you thought it."

"I would." She considered the floor a moment, which for Gu Yue was a long speech. "You're going to be very difficult to fight. Not because you're strong. There are four rings in this city that outweigh you and two of them belong to one person. You're going to be difficult because by the time anyone's decided what you are, you've already finished, and the deciding took longer than the fight."

Lin Hao did not answer immediately, because it was the first time anybody had said the true version of it out loud, and because Wu Zhangkong's version had been about *him* — about what he was carrying, about the Kings in the armies, about the problem of being that at ten — while Gu Yue's version had been about *them*. About what it was like to be across from it.

"Is that what it's like," he said, "from where you are."

"I haven't fought you."

"I know."

"So I don't know what it's like from where I am." She picked up her cup and did not drink from it. "I know what it's like from where they are. I've watched four of them. Xie Xie plans for six hours and loses in eleven seconds and then goes and plans for six more, and I've never in my life seen a person do that so cheerfully. Xu Xiaoyan's stopped saying she'll prove herself. Wu Zhangkong's stopped saying *you are not the strongest in this class* — which he'd already stopped believing, and he said it to you twice, and the second time quieter than the first."

"That's not—"

"That's an observation," Gu Yue said. "You can have it. I'm not going to tell you what I think it means, because you'll work it out in about nine seconds and I'd rather you had the nine seconds."

"That's a recruitment," she said then, because she had finished with the other thing.

"Yes."

"They're not here for the tournament."

"No."

"And you knew."

"I knew about four seconds ago," Lin Hao said. "Which is the usual amount of time I know things by. I've stopped being impressed by it."

Gu Yue turned her head a few degrees.

"That's the third time you've said that."

"I know."

"Say it less."

"Yes."

## Part 4: What The Girl At The Back Did

Her name was Ye Xinglan, and Lin Hao learned it the way he learned most things, which was by being told by a round boy with dried fish who had decided the five of them were interesting.

Xu Lizhi came down to the edge of the stage between matches, because Xu Lizhi did not appear to have a sense of the things one did not do.

"You're the one who goes left," he said.

"Yes."

"That's a Shrek thing."

Lin Hao looked at him.

"The Track?"

"We call it something else." Xu Lizhi considered the dried fish. "But it's the same thing. It's a decision about where you're going to be, made before you move. How long did it take you?"

"Nine days. Six of them failing."

"That's fast." Xu Lizhi said it without any particular feeling. "Ye Xinglan took four months. She's standing at the back and she's been watching you go left two matches and she hasn't said anything, and that's how I know she's decided something."

"Decided what."

"I don't know." Xu Lizhi ate the dried fish. "She decides things and then tells me about them later, usually while I'm eating, and I've learned the telling happens when she's already done it."

## Part 5: What Lin Hao Wrote

That night, in the room that smelled of the sea, he took the book down and wrote the number first, because the number never asked anything of him.

*Two hundred and thirty-three.* It had been two hundred and seventy-eight. Eight points, somewhere between two matches and one conversation with a round boy holding dried fish. He could not say which had done it. He had used the Track four times in public and it had not cared that people were watching, and the not-caring was the part he had not expected, because he had assumed performing was different from practising, and it was not.

*One thousand three hundred and fifty-eight.*

*The shape worked. Two minutes and eleven seconds. Xu Xiaoyan said that's the first time it worked the way it's supposed to, and I said that's the first time we've been somewhere useful when it happened, and she said that's the nicest thing anybody's said to her today. I said the same thing twice and the second time wasn't planned.*

*Shrek isn't watching the winners. They're counting how many of us do things nobody asked for.*

*Gu Yue said the true thing today, and it wasn't about me. She said by the time anyone's decided what I am, I've already finished, and the deciding took longer than the fight. I wrote that down twice. The reason is that the first time I made it into a thing about being fast, and it isn't a thing about being fast. It's a thing about what it's like to stand across from me. Wu Zhangkong told me what I'm carrying. Gu Yue told me what I cost. Those aren't the same sentence, and I've been answering the first one for four months.*

*Xie Xie has planned six hours twice this week and lost in eleven seconds twice this week and gone and planned six more, and I've watched him do it and never once asked him why, because I assumed the answer was that he's Xie Xie. I think that was lazy. I think the answer is that he's decided the eleven seconds are his and the six hours are his and nobody gets an opinion about either, and that this is the strongest thing anyone in this class does, and it doesn't look like anything.*

*Gu Yue said that's a recruitment and I agreed, and I agreed quickly, because I've known four months that Wu Zhangkong is taking us somewhere and didn't know where, and now I know he isn't the only one taking us somewhere.*

And then the line at the bottom, which he wrote last.

*Xu Lizhi said the Track is a Shrek thing, and that Ye Xinglan took four months, and that she tells him things after she's already done them.*

*I learned the Track in nine days from a man who said I don't know twice.*

*Wu Zhangkong gave me a Shrek thing and did not say it was a Shrek thing, and he has said I don't know twice about what I am, and I have started to suspect the two I don't knows are not about me at all — they are about where he came from, and that is not a thing I have named, not even here. This is the closest I have come to asking. I did not ask.*

He closed the book.

The lock at his temple lay gold across his cheekbone, and the marks on his forearms were raised and had been raised for twenty-eight days, and the hawk turned over in him — *one thousand three hundred and fifty-eight* — and did not pull toward anything.

But the sea was four blocks away, and there was a girl at the back of the stands who had been watching him go left for two matches and had not said anything, and Lin Hao lay on his back in a city that smelled of salt and hot metal and thought about the fact that he had not asked, and about the fact that not asking had become a thing he did on purpose, and about the fact that he would like to know when he had started doing that.

He slept.
