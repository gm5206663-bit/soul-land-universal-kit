## Part 1: The Name

Special-colored rings were so rare that all of history had produced two names to hang them on — Tang San's gold, Huo Yuhao's white and, by every account, an orange-gold besides, wrung somehow from a vicious spirit soul after the man had begun *creating* spirit souls. Legends, both. So even one golden ring, on a thirteen-year-old, held the room.

The old lady recovered, and her brows went down. "Your soul ring is quite unique. It doesn't seem to be made of soul power. I sense blood essence fluctuations coming from it — but your soul power is completely serene. Just what is going on?"

"Honestly, I don't know either," Wulin said, calm as a report. "I only know that my blood essence is very powerful. I think this ability came from my bloodline — but since it granted me a soul skill, it seems to be a type of soul ring."

The golden ring lit as he spoke, and the roar of a dragon came out of his body; muscle swelled; gold sheathed him.

"Th-this is..." The old lady sputtered. "*Golden Dragon Body?* You have the Golden Dragon **bloodline?**"

Wulin stood very still in the middle of his own light.

*The name of the soul skill appeared in my own mind. So how does this old lady know it? Has someone been in a situation like mine in the past?* The thought ran one lap. *No. That's not possible. She said Golden Dragon. What's sealed inside me is the Golden Dragon **King.** They should be two different things.*

While his mind worked, the old lady appeared in front of him. One flash. No footsteps.

"Punch me."

"...Punch you?"

He knew what a Shrek examiner had to be. He also had eyes. She looked like a woman with one foot in the grave and the other on a walking stick, and his strength had passed five hundred kilograms long ago — with the Golden Dragon Body lit, he made it five thousand.

"Don't worry about me," she said, impatient. "My old bones are quite tough. If you actually kill me, you can just enter the Sea God's Pavilion directly."

Wulin took a breath. "Please be careful, then."

## Part 2: Three Punches

**Bang.**

Something touched his arm — he never saw what — and the punch was swatted aside like a housefly.

"Have you not eaten yet?" she barked. "Use some strength!"

The sidelines had seen it. These were people who had watched him bench a guillotine forty-eight hours ago, and their awareness of his strength made the swat worse, not better. *She simply—*

Wulin drew the breath of it — belly inflating, a sharp whistle in the air, arms swelling past their own swell, scales blazing — and threw the second punch with a grunt like a falling beam. This one boomed ahead of itself and left a golden afterimage hanging where the fist had been.

**Bang.**

It stopped in her palm. She had not moved an inch, and his whole force went into her hand and *dissipated*, like hitting a wall of rubber. Xie Xie, watching, recognized the feeling from the inside and said nothing out of pure fellowship.

"Is that all?" Dry. "You're only this strong?"

The disappointment in her eyes went into Wulin like a fishhook, straight into the thing that was proud on behalf of the whole class. "I'm even stronger than this." The hand changed — golden tendrils — the claw, rumbles rolling off it, the air itself leaning.

Light moved in the old lady's eyes. "Come."

The third punch held nothing back. His blood howled; every drop of blood essence he had converged on the right arm; the roar of it was a regal dragon's; there was no sonic boom because the air got out of the way first.

And this time the old lady punched back.

Light warped around her, and the class saw **nine resplendent rings** rise — nine, in a room where three had been impressive — and then the fists met.

**BOOM.**

Wulin went back like artillery and into the wall, twenty meters, and stuck there.

The old lady returned to her seat. "Next."

The wall let him go in a tumble of debris. *"Captain!" "Wulin!"* — the class arrived as one body, and his brother's hand was the first one in, because it always was, and Gu Yue and Xiaoyan got his other side, and the question *"Are you sure you're okay?"* came from all of them at once in different words.

"I'm fine." A cough, debris in his hair. *This wall is really thick!* And it had been like punching a giant balloon — a simply overwhelming elastic force, everywhere and soft and total — and his body was more than tough enough to be embedded in masonry and walk, but the three punches had emptied the blood essence out of him, and now the hunger was back with interest. *A good meal right now and the blood would come back faster. Too bad there's no food in this building.* The class had stopped being surprised that the fate of the examinations ran on breakfast.

What the students did not see, being busy, was the faces of Shen Yi and the two examiners: jaws dropped, and not at Wulin.

## Part 3: What the Sixth Heard

Lin Hao had watched the old lady move exactly twice — the flash to the center, and the punch.

He had been reading things since before he had words for it, and it worked on seams: everything made, everything alive, everything that moves carries the place where it fails, and he had found them in masters and beasts and a two-word battle armor teacher who had let himself be read like a blade laid on a bench.

Her motion had carried nothing. No seam, no tell, no place where it failed — nothing to read, the way a perfectly balanced scale has no lean. It was the first time in his life the reading had come back empty, and he noted the failure the way he noted all numbers, honestly, and put it beside the nine rings in the same column: *a person the alphabet does not cover yet.*

Then he looked at the wall his brother had become part of, because that he *could* read: good masonry, four hands deep, lime-silver mortar, and it had held a nine-ring elastic counter through a thirteen-year-old cannonball. He filed the mix ratio with the sixth gate's spring, in the report he was not writing, and felt marginally better about the state of the world.

Beside him, Wulin got his breath back and did the arithmetic out loud, quiet, for the class only. "Her strength is unfathomable. I don't think even Teacher Wu could compare."

Nobody argued. The class had exactly one data point on Teacher Wu's ceiling, and it was the boy currently pricing the wall.

## Part 4: The Nod

"Next" had a name, and the name stood up.

Gu Yue walked to the center of the room, expression grim, and did not bow. None of the bows before her had been hers to give. She nodded, once. "I'm beginning now."

Shen Yi frowned — genuinely. In all the years of this room, no examinee had ever dared act that haughty in front of a Shrek examiner. The old lady narrowed her eyes and leaned back in her chair.

"Go on, then."

Three soul rings rose beneath Gu Yue — two yellow, one purple — and her hands turned over and made the shape of a bowl. Yellow light gathered in them: steady, gentle. Earth.

*Elemental control,* the three examiners concluded at once. *She's showing the strength of her spiritual power.* A sensible choice, cleanly begun.

Then the choice left the boundaries of sense.

She took the ball of yellow light into her left hand, and began to sculpt it with her right. Each soft touch of a finger raised a petal — one, then another, then another — until she held a yellow flower with nine petals, turning slowly at her fingertips, otherworldly, the kind of thing that gets museums built around it by accident.

At the table, the examiner on the right nodded, approving. Elemental-type martial souls were rare; her wealth of spiritual power and her steadfast control were impressive.

*However, this still fell within the boundaries of what was considered normal.*

The old lady's eyes had not left the flower. At the chairs, four people who had watched her stop a war-hammer trial with one word kept their faces still, and waited for the boundaries to find out what they were dealing with.
