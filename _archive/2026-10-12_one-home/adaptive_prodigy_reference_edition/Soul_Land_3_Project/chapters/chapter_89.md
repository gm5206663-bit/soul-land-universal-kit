## Part 1: The Instant War

The forgettable boy fought like a dream someone else was having.

His first ring lit; his hand shot into the air, and a sphere of illusory light spun itself out of nothing by his palm — strands of blue, green, and white dancing around his fingers — and when he chanted, a protrusion clawed out of it, dropped, and landed as an enormous hound wreathed in flame, the fire brightening and roughening until the shape said *lion*. Smaller than any lion-type soul beast. Domineering anyway. In the chairs, Wulin did the taxonomy out loud under his breath. "A lion mastiff? Is that a soul skill *or* a spirit soul?" He sat forward. "He's summoning it — that's his spirit soul as the whole army. Nothing's too bizarre for this place."

"His soul has a *dog* in it," Xie Xie said, with feeling.

"Materialize!" Rays of light ran down the boy's finger into the beast, and its edges firmed. "Little Lion, go!"

The mastiff went — a flash of lightning with killing intent nearly solid.

Gu Yue's three rings had been revolving to a controlled beat of her hands. Then the rhythm changed, and all three rings lit at once, trembling together — which skill she was using was anyone's guess, and the correct guess would have been *several*. Azure light; a half-meter sphere of ice in her palm; and she began, unhurried, to slap it. *Slap. Slap.* The sphere shuddered and held.

The mastiff closed, fur streaming like the tail of a meteor, maw yawning a golden-red fireball — and Gu Yue, the picture of boredom, to whom all of this was a chore, threw one ice ball at it, turned her back on the war, and strolled toward Wulin.

The room's collective mind left its body. Fire met ice; the sphere burst into a cloud of homing icicles; and the cloud *changed its mind* in the air — frigid blue to deep green between heartbeats, a third of them vanishing in a flash of silver and reappearing an inch from the summoner's nose, a dome of sharpness closing on the spot. "Not good!" he said, with a prize understatement, having spent everything on his lion's offense and nothing on his own person.

Green wind took the mastiff in the flank; its coat of flame guttered; its body went transparent, then anecdotal. Shen Yi moved faster than the eye could apologize and arrived between the icicles and the boy before the dome could close — otherwise, the evening's second pincushion.

"The match is over," she said, quietly, to the boy whose war had ended inside someone else's sentence.

"Gu Yue — ten points," Shen Yi said. Total forty-eight, two trials left, hope restored.

"She turned her *back* on him," Xie Xie said, to nobody, still processing it.

"She was finished," said the fifth, without looking up. "The back was just the punctuation."

"It's night," Gu Yue said, returning to the chairs, which was her whole after-action report. The stall had done its work; the last fighter of the five wanted her stars.

In the chairs, the fifth examinee was quiet. Among the elements that had obeyed her — ice, wind, that trick of light — there had been the silver one, the flicker that moved things without crossing the space between. He filed it without a word, in the drawer where he kept the things about Gu Yue that were not his to name. The drawer was getting full.

## Part 2: The Weakest

Xiaoyan stood, and her knees made it clear they had not been consulted.

Before she went, Wulin caught her sleeve and whispered. Her head came up in astonishment. "Really? Like *that?*"

"Just try it out." A raised brow, the gentle smile. "It's time for you to go."

"Okay," she said, in the voice of someone agreeing to a practical joke in which she was both the teller and the punchline.

She was, by her own honest ledger, the weakest of the five. Team battles she could assist; alone was another country. So she had done what the captain's cultivation plan had built her for — leaned all the way into her own grammar: where a heavier spear was built to *penetrate*, hers was built to *freeze*; she had stopped apologizing for that a season ago. And she had one more asset the plan had never needed to mention: three years of side-by-side combat had taught her exactly how calm she could be while looking exactly how she liked.

Her opponent was the muscular young man, student number six — the one Xie Xie had nearly picked, the sturdy one. "Begin," said Shen Yi, promptly.

Two yellow rings rose around Xu Xiaoyan, and under them she seemed frail past the point of charity. She *stumbled* catching the thing that congealed in her hands — and the arena's collective heart tilted, because the thing was a jet-black hammer, and she leaned on her staff under its weight like a kitten under a luggage cart.

*Is that a soul device?* went the hall, in unison, silently. *The trial forbids them.*

Shen Yi looked — hard — and held her tongue, because the hammer had no more soul fluctuation in it than a rock did. Whatever it was, it was not a device. It was, in fact, a thousand-refined tungsten hammer wearing duct tape, and what Xiaoyan did next transcended strange on its way to bizarre: her first ring lit, a massive ice wheel spun up before her — and she taped the hammer to the wheel, levitated the whole swaying contraption, and aimed it at a Shrek Academy student.

It flew at him like an inebriated snail. It looked, at every moment, like it was about to be retired by gravity.

"She's going to throw a *hammer* at him," Xie Xie said. "A hammer. On a wheel."

"Please wait a moment!" Xiaoyan called sweetly, to her opponent, about the snail. The line of ten watched with furrowed brows and pinched noses — astonishing things had been seen in this academy before, never once something so uncanny.

"Is she actually—" one of them began.

"Don't blink," said his neighbor, grimly. "I blinked at the fourth one and missed a sixty scored with no soul in it." Her second ring lit; a spear of ice congealed in front of her; and the fifth examinee, in the chairs, put his hand over his mouth and kept it there.

## Part 3: Nice Acting

Number six finally moved, not knowing whether to laugh or cry. *Is this girl a comedian?* — but the guillotine trial walked over his grave, and he did not underestimate her. His tool martial soul appeared: a crystal ball flickering with opaque light, three rings hovering above it.

"Crystal, crystal, *deviate!*"

White filled his eyes; the crystal shone; and Xiaoyan's own ice spear turned traitor in mid-flight, bending away toward her own pathetic wheel. Clever — her spear against her contraption; the girl dismantled by the girl. Her heart thumped once, hard. *If he has his way, I lose.* So she chose that exact moment to reveal her cards.

Purple light shone in her eyes.

His grip on the spear — on *her* spear — stuttered, because her Purple Demon Eyes was every ounce the equal of the captain's, and the stun ran through a spiritual foundation that had been strong before it was ever tested. She waved her staff like a conductor reclaiming the orchestra, and the spear swung back around toward number six, while every eye in the room followed it — and no one at all watched the ice wheel, which had begun, quietly, to speed up.

She raised her staff high. An air of divinity enveloped her, entirely real, and she said the words as though she had been saving them all day:

"The stars in the heavens that shine forever tranquil, grant me strength — *Starwheel!*"

Starlight descended in lines onto the Ice Staff, and it became the thing it only became at night: the **Starwheel Ice Staff**, and the girl underneath it stopped pretending to be clumsy in the same heartbeat — the stumble-act dropped, her every movement suddenly smooth as a cat's. A golden starwheel kindled beneath student number six's feet.

And here was the whole architecture of the trick, the part even the hall's sharp eyes would assemble only afterward: the transformation *cut her connection* to the wheel and the spear both. The spear arrived at number six's face on the last of her guidance — his second ring lit, and his Crystal Ball spiralled light through it, *Decomposition* reducing the ice to warm mist — and with the same breath he raised his eyes to the ceiling and found the actual problem, unguided now, falling toward his skull under nothing but tungsten and gravity.

"What — is that —" he managed, and tried the easy sidestep. The starwheel beneath him brightened, and chains of starlight shot out of the ground and bound him where he stood — the absolute control of astrological-attributed skills, his body a statute, his third ring too slow to matter, sweat jumping off his brow — head tilted up, watching a black hammer spin down at his face.

It stopped an inch above his head.

"I yield!" said student number six, at speed, to nobody in particular, bound and dry-mouthed and entirely alive.

The room exhaled a population's worth of air, and then Xie Xie's whoop arrived late and loud from the chairs.

"Everyone breathe," Wulin said, laughing, sweeping the five of them with one look. "Two more of these and we're students." Shen Yi crossed the floor, took the hammer out of the air, and weighed it curiously — one hand — then stood in front of Xu Xiaoyan with an odd smile escaping around her professionalism.

"A thousand-refined tungsten hammer," she said. Her eyes went down the chairs, to the captain. "Yours?"

"Xiaoyan's," Wulin said, "as of this evening."

"Girl," Shen Yi said, turning back, the odd smile winning, "*nice acting!*"

Xu Xiaoyan smiled sweetly. "Thank you for your praise."

The hammer, the hall now understood, had never been heavy for a two-ringed soul master; any ranged-type could have carried it at a stroll. The stumble had been the entire opening act — curiosity bait, misdirection, the purchase price of one unnoticed minute for a wheel to accelerate. One mistake, any mistake, and she lost; she had bet the trial on everyone's assumptions about a small girl, and the metal thing with no soul in it — the only object in the arena the rules could not touch — was the deciding factor. It had come out of one of the captain's heavy silver bracelet rings, slipped to her in the whisper, and the plan had needed no further instruction: she had known best how to use being *her*. Shrek's students had combat experience in outrageous quantities. What they did not have, a single one of them, was any practice at being charmed.

## Part 4: The Fifth Line

"Ten points," Shen Yi said. Her voice did something complicated on the way out.

So the eighth trial closed with the arithmetic rearranged: apart from Xie Xie — three, and a deduction still pending at the term's end — every one of the five had taken their ten.

"Fifty," Xiaoyan said, rolling the number around like a sweet, and then, honestly, at twice the volume: "I was *so* scared."

"You invented duct tape as a war crime," Xie Xie told her. "Fifty-one, minus whatever the term says."

"Minus," Wulin agreed, rueful.

"Two left," the fifth said, and the class came back to the room. He looked at Xiaoyan, once, the way he looked at finished work. "Which was heavier — the hammer or the act?"

"The act," she said, promptly, and he nodded, because that was the correct answer, and Gu Yue's mouth did something that on anyone else would have been a laugh. Wulin sixty. Gu Yue forty-eight. Xie Xie fifty-one minus pending. Xiaoyan fifty. And the fifth's sixty, banked two fights ago, standing quietly at the top of the column like a nail the wall had grown around.

"Rest for ten minutes," Shen Yi said aloud, to all five, folding the form under her arm. "The ninth trial won't wait for anyone."

"What's the ninth?" Xie Xie asked, because somebody had to.

"You'll survive it," she said, which was not an answer, and was also — the class felt it in the same spine — exactly the syllabus. "Let's move on to the next trial."

And her heart filed itself through a great many folders on the way to saying it.

*Senior brother's students are excellent,* she thought — the martial souls unspectacular and unneeding; Gu Yue the one true standout; Xie Xie's homogeneous twins no match for true twins; Xiaoyan capable, if the restrictions on her were harsh. Four assessments, one line apiece, fair as scales.

And then there was the fifth line, the one her thoughts kept sliding off. The comprehensive-typed one. The 「全能」-classed examinee — the word the whole hall had been passing around since the fourth trial like a coin too hot to hold. The one whose martial soul had never once been seen, whose sixtieth point had arrived without one gram of light, who had asked her strongest student a question about the Tang Sect that the strongest student had declined to answer. *What*, her file's fastest-growing section wanted to know, *is senior brother feeding that class?*

The lamps stood high and bright in the round room, and true night pressed against the academy's windows like an audience. Two trials remained.
