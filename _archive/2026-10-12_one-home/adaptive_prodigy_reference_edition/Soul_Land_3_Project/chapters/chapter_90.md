## Part 1: The Unbelievable

*Tang Wulin is a real freak,* Shen Yi thought, on the walk down. *Full marks in six trials. I can't make heads or tails of him. Plain old Bluesilver Grass, but that golden soul ring — definitely unrelated to his martial soul. Talents not great, strength formidable. Not potential alone — potential married to temperament. Gu Yue is far ahead of the class in talent and potential, but in strength and temperament, Wulin shines brightest, and fully deserves the captain's seat.* A breath. *Senior brother, your insight is so great.*

The room she led them to was unlike any so far: dim, bare, metal-walled, a single large screen hanging in it. The class's stomachs recognized it before their heads did — *this seems similar to the spirit ascension platform.*

"Your goal for the ninth trial is to survive," Shen Yi said. "I believe you've all been in the spirit ascension platform before. Well, this is similar to that. In a sense, all the soul beasts in it are real. If you kill one and are at a bottleneck, then you'll be able to gain a soul ring. Alright. Prepare to enter." She pressed a button, and metal capsules slid out of the wall — five of them, which was the shape of the class these days.

"Teacher Shen, will we be taking this trial together?" Wulin asked.

"Yes. No matter the number of participants, they will all take the trial together. This trial tests your coordination in addition to your ability to survive. You all came together, so you have an advantage here. Good luck."

"Thank you, Teacher Shen."

Around him, his team was already glowing with it. Xie Xie's trial-eight anxieties — three points, a pending deduction — blew off him like dust: they had spent *years* in the platform; anything like it was a walk in the park, so long as nobody lunged head-first at something high-level. GY's eyes and XXY's came to rest on their captain, brimming with confidence. Together, they were unbeatable.

The fifth examinee said nothing, which was usual, and looked at the capsule the way he looked at any tool — checking the seams.

## Part 2: The Elders

The capsules sealed and slid back into the walls, and the room breathed again — and then two people entered it whom the examinees would not have believed.

One was the cranky old lady they knew entirely too well. The Silver Moon Douluo walked in like the room's lighting was a personal insult.

The other was an old man: nose high, deep-blue sunken eyes, standing tall, gray hair down to his shoulders around an unyielding air — a presence that stifled the room by arriving in it.

"Teacher," Shen Yi said, respectfully, and became furniture with excellent posture.

The old man nodded. "How have these kids done so far?"

Shen Yi had prepared several answers on the walk down. All of them fled. She used the true one. "They're unbelievable."

The Silver Moon Douluo laughed. "That's right! They're really unbelievable! I didn't let them take the fifth trial, but there's actually someone who managed to score full points so far! That Tang Wulin is marvelous — his blood essence is strong, almost a bit like yours, Zhuo Shi." She waved a hand at the screen. "Let me be frank. I don't care about the other four. I've already set my eyes on that Gu Yue."

*The other four* — a count that had grown by one since any canon of this exam was written, and Zhuo Shi's sunken eyes said he had done the arithmetic.

"And the fifth one," he said. Not a question. The page Cai had been reading all day finally came up, in the worst company: between the two of them, with Shen Yi listening.

"Mm." The Silver Moon Douluo's expression did something complicated and private. "The one your students keep failing to inconvenience. Sixty points without one gram of soul light, and a question your number two still won't repeat above a whisper." She looked at the screen, at the small figures already forming up in an ancient forest. "Ask them. I've been reading that page since the buns, old man, and it keeps getting longer. I want to see the next line before I say anything aloud."

Zhuo Shi said nothing at all — which in a man like that was a decision — and let the matter fall into the same pocket where, his look suggested, he kept several others. Then he shot her a different look, the Grandteacher one. "What do you mean, *set your eyes on her*? Don't forget that they're all my granddisciples. They don't have anything to do with you."

"What?" Her brows arched. "Are you trying to go back on your word?"

"I'm not going back on my word! I'm stating a fact. Eye them as much as you want — that doesn't mean I'll hand them over. They have to be willing too. You'll need to ask Gu Yue if she *wants* to be your disciple. If she rejects you, there's no use forcing it."

"That's nonsense! I *like* how stubborn she is." She stomped, furious, delighted. "What would separate kids from walking corpses if they didn't have strong wills? Her temperament is precisely what attracts me!"

Shen Yi watched the two of them the way one watches weather over open water.

"We can talk about this after they finish this trial," Zhuo Shi said. "If they can't pass it, it doesn't matter how high they scored on the others." And to the Silver Moon's credit, the vexation reined itself in; she held this trial in high regard too.

"Teacher," Shen Yi ventured, softly, "what test level are they taking?"

Zhuo Shi contemplated the screen a moment.

"The highest level."

Shen Yi's thoughts had to be steadied like a struck bell. *The highest level? Even adults find that level hard! That's—*

"Hurry up now," Zhuo Shi urged.

"Yes."

## Part 3: The Ancient Forest

Space warped; a flash; and class zero stood together in a world that arrived around them — a lush forest, greener than green, and *older* than anything the platform had ever shown them. They fell into the usual formation by instinct: Wulin center, the grass already alive under his feet and probing outward; GY and XX on the flanks; Xiaoyan deep. The fifth took the outer seam, the unassigned edge, the place where the formation met the unknown — which had quietly become his address over three years.

"So the ninth trial tests our ability to *survive*," Xie Xie said, rejoicing. "Fantastic. Isn't this the easiest possible trial for us?"

Wulin raised a hand, and the class went quiet.

"This place is different from what we're used to. At least from the elementary one. I've been in the intermediate level once, with Teacher Wu — the plants here and there are similar." He let that sit. "Don't forget this is still part of the Shrek entrance exam. Teacher Shen said we have to survive. She never said for how long. And if this is like the intermediate platform, we'll run into a lot of thousand-year soul beasts." A beat. "Maybe even some at the ten-thousand-year level."

Three of his teammates drew a careful breath. The fifth did not, having already priced the forest.

Xiaoyan's voice came out small and hopeful. "Captain — if I can break through to rank 30, can't I get a soul ring here? If there's a ten-thousand-year soul beast—"

"Don't even think about it." Wulin's frown was the load-bearing kind. "With your current abilities, absorbing a ten-thousand-year ring is impossible — let alone *killing* one. This would be your third ring. Normally, soul masters don't take a ten-thousand-year ring until rank 50. Your body isn't that strong to begin with. Don't think so wishfully, or you'll encounter a problem. Like the time I evolved my spirit soul to the thousand-year level."

Xiaoyan stuck out her tongue. "Got it!"

"Let's go. Vantage point first. Once we have a good line of sight, we rest and recover what the other trials spent."

## Part 4: The Evacuated Quiet

They moved carefully. Gu Yue scouted, wind birds lifting off her fingers and threading the thicket; Wulin watched their immediate surroundings; the fifth walked the seam and read the ground the way he read everything — along the grain.

The wind birds found nothing. Not beasts, not tracks, not birdsong — a forest as blank as a held breath. Wulin did not believe it for a second; this was part of Shrek's exam.

A while on, they found their hill, wide-viewed, and the captain dug the steamed buns out of his storage device and dealt them around like cards. The fifth ate exactly his portion — the body's price, computed and paid — and took his turn resting with his back to the others and his attention on the treeline.

Two hours. Fully recovered. All tranquil, without the slightest hint of trouble.

"Captain, something's strange," Xie Xie said at last, low, nervous. "It's been two hours already and we still haven't met a single soul beast. Do you have any idea what's going on?"

Wulin didn't answer at once. But it was the fifth who spoke, from the edge of the group, quietly, without turning around.

"It's not empty," Lin Hao said. "It's evacuated."

The class looked at him. The oldest thing he owned had spent the last two hours reading the quiet, and the quiet had a grain like everything else: no fear-signals, no territorial calls, no scavengers working the margins — not a forest without beasts but a forest *cleared by something*, every living thing gone from it the way crowds go from streets when the weather turns.

"An empty forest is a forest something bigger owns," he said. "We're not early. We're late. Everything here moved out ahead of something that's still coming."

The wind moved the ancient canopy, agreeably, like a thing with nothing to confess; and on the screen in a dim metal room, two old monsters and one teacher leaned in.
