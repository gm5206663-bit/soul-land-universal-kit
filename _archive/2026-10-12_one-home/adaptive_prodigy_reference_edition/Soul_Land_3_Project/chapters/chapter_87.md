## Part 1: The Line of Ten

They were already waiting, as promised — ten current students of Shrek Academy in a line along the circular wall, numbered, still as fence posts, each one doing the small work of not looking at class zero and doing it badly. The strongest hid in the middle of the rank and file: a slender boy holding the number two like it was an insult. The tenth was a girl with snow-pale eyes and a stillness that matched Wulin's own.

Judgement, Shen Yi had said. The choice is most of the score.

Wulin walked the line once, unhurried, the way he shopped for vegetables, and stopped at the girl. "Number ten."

The slender boy's eyebrow moved a millimeter, and Wulin came back down the line and murmured to Xie Xie, "Take number two."

"*Number two?*"

"Trust me."

Which left the order, and the class turned inward to draft it in whispers.

"Girls last," Gu Yue said immediately. "We watch everything first."
"Captain first," Xiaoyan said. "He scouts, we choose after. That's just sense."

And then they found the fifth chair already empty, because its owner was already standing, smiling.

It was a smile none of them had ever seen on him. Not the margin's flat arithmetic, not the smith's closed face. Something lighter. Something with its sleeves rolled up.

"You're supposed to go *last*," Xie Xie hissed. "It's practically your catchphrase—"

"The margin goes last when it's arithmetic." The fifth rolled one shoulder, once, like a man settling a coat he liked. "This isn't arithmetic."

"Six trials of points," Gu Yue said, studying him the way she studied prices.

"Six trials of points," he agreed. "And finally a shape I know."

Six trials of points, and finally a shape he knew. He had been waiting for this one since the gates — through spiritual power and talent and buns and all the quiet rooms where nothing could be answered properly, there had been this door at the end of the day, and behind it: somebody from the academy on the bill.

The order went Wulin, the fifth, Xie Xie, the girls. Shen Yi looked at the submitted list, looked at the fifth examinee, and wrote it down without comment, which for a person with her day was a kind of screaming.

## Part 2: The Captain's Thirty Minutes

The tenth trial of the tenth — no. The snow began almost immediately, and it did not stop.

What the room watched for twenty-five minutes was a boy being slowly, patiently buried: the girl's blizzard eating his Bluesilver Grass down to a dim spinning net, frost climbing the strands, icicles rising like teeth. She was good — conservative when her kills failed, a cheetah stalking, whittling, waiting for the one strike. At ten minutes the six-point mark passed; at twenty-five she had nine points banked and her prey rigid with cold, one white hand reaching his shoulder.

Shen Yi had already written the ending in her head — *not that talented after all, ignoring the golden ring, merely Bluesilver Grass* — and was being puzzled by the rest of class zero, who watched their captain's impending defeat without a single eyelid moving.

"How is he still *breathing*?" Xiaoyan whispered.

"He isn't," Gu Yue said. "He's shopping."

The moment the hand touched his shoulder, Wulin smiled. *It's about time.*

Rings and grass vanished; a tyrannical blood-essence aura blew the blizzard sideways. He had not eaten all those steamed buns for nothing. Purple eyes; the world's slow spin; a golden hand closing gently on a carotid; the girl went limp and he caught her before the floor did, because he was like that. The snow dispersed on a room gone silent.

Xie Xie smacked his forehead. "Captain is really getting sneakier and sneakier! Thirty entire minutes to lure her into a trap — that girl never realized Captain excels at brute force, not control!"

"Tang Wulin… ten points." Shen Yi's eyes were wide. *Fifty, plus ten,* said the arithmetic. Sixty. Passed.

"Was he waiting for this the entire time?" Xie Xie muttered — and heard, again, by the teacher whose file on this class grew a page a day. *He's thirteen,* she thought. *How is he this patient?* She did not know about the six years of hammering temper, the grindstone behind the patience. There was a great deal she did not know, and the next match was going to make it worse.

## Part 3: The Fifth's Turn

"Next."

He passed Xie Xie going down, and the class watched the fifth examinee walk into the arena the way you watch a kettle you have been warned about. He stopped in front of the line of ten with his hands behind his back, reading them — unhurried, the way he read metal, along the grain. Number two was the strongest; that had been true since the room opened, and number two was promised. Of the rest, the read said *proudest*, and pride was the grain that split cleanest.

"Number one," Lin Hao said.

Number one was a long-limbed boy with a spear type — he released it the moment the word fell: three rings rising, two yellow, one purple, a shaft of deep-ice blue settling into his hands like it had grown there. A real weapon, real rings, a current student of Shrek Academy who had won his place at this academy the honest way and had never once in his life needed a second exchange against a soulless opponent.

Across the arena, the fifth examinee stood with nothing. No light. No rings. His hands were loose at his sides, and he was still smiling, faintly, the way you smile at weather you have decided to enjoy.

"Begin," said Shen Yi, and her pen stopped.

Number one was fast and his lines were clean — three thrusts off the back foot, each one a question a spear had asked for a thousand years. The fifth put his Question inside the third thrust: an empty hand offered where an answer had to go. The body answered, the way bodies do, before the boy could forbid it — and the answer had a grain to it, the way everything answered has. The Answering Stroke arrived along that grain, unhurried, and stopped a finger's width from the wrist that owned the spear, having decided not to exist.

"Again," Lin Hao said, kindly.

Along the wall, the ten had stopped pretending not to lean. "*No soul light,*" one of them said. "*His control — is that a ten-thousand-year soul skill?*" And another, lower: "There's no fluctuation at all. That's not a soul skill. That's—" and there the sentence died, because there was nothing in the room to call it.

"Spear's honest," Lin Hao called across the sand, pleasantly. "You lean on it like family. Elbow in, it'd be perfect."

Number one was done being careful — and past being mocked. "Shrek does not lose," he said, "to a boy who won't even release his soul."

"It's rude to release everything on the first date," the fifth said. "Show me."

The purple ring flared; the spear's ice-blue became an aurora down its length, the thousand-year skill, the one that had put him on this line — a thrust that arrived in three places in one motion, honest, committed, brave.

Still Water. The fifth did not move until movement was meaningless — and then he was inside the aurora rather than under it, where the grain of the whole beautiful sequence ran to one point — and a hand that had gripped hammers since it was small closed on the shaft. It did not grip hard. It gripped *exactly*, and turned, and number one's own honesty did the rest: the strongest student of the line sat down on Shrek's floor with a sound like the trial gavel.

The spear stayed upright beside him. The fifth had set it so, of course. He waited, politely, while the boy got his breath and his pride into the same room.

"You're faster than your friends think," Lin Hao told him. "Show me the one you're hiding."

What came then was the best thing the line of ten did all day — the hidden technique, everything on it, pride and all, aimed with a spear-soul's full intention at a boy with no light on him anywhere. The Question went out to meet it, deeper this time, and was answered, and the Answering Stroke crossed the answer's grain and stopped — one inch from the throat, in front of the entire academy that could hear it not land.

The Unwritten stroke. The mercy that humiliates.

"Twice," Lin Hao said, quietly, so that only number one heard it. "That's twice Shrek's best line has shown me nothing I couldn't read standing still. Go be angry about it — properly — and be better than this when it matters."

Number one looked at the not-wound for one second. Then he raised his hand. "…I concede."

"Lin Hao. Ten points." Shen Yi wrote it down. Her pen had not moved in four exchanges; the form would show it later, a blank where the notes should be, and she would file that blank with everything else in a file that now had its own weight class.

The fifth helped his opponent up, returned the spear handle-first — the politest insult the academy had taken all day — and bowed to the ten of them, exactly as deeply as the trial deserved. "Thank you," he said, and meant it. "That was the first thing today worth waiting for."

The line of ten said nothing at all. It is a particular silence, being Shrek's pride in a room where somebody's bill is being read out line by line — *your academy has shown nothing worthy enough* — and the ledger's collector standing in the middle of it, unhurt, unlit, unbored, handing the spear back.

## Part 4: Not You

When he came back to the chairs, number two had stepped forward half a pace out of the line — the strongest of them, appetite in the set of his shoulders, the question halfway up his throat.

Lin Hao smiled at him and shook his head, and pointed at Xie Xie.

"Not you. You're his."

The twilight was gone; the round room held its lamp-light like a bowl. Xie Xie stood, rolled his shoulders until they cracked, and walked toward the slender boy with the pleasant expression of a man about to be told something interesting about himself.

"Apparently I'm ambitious," he told number two, conversationally. "My captain says so. Let's find out."

"Should we warn them?" Xiaoyan asked, watching the two of them settle into their stances.

"Which side?" Gu Yue said.

Neither released a soul. Both waited for the opening, ten meters apart, while the girl with snow-pale eyes watched from the floor where she sat wrapped in a shock blanket, thinking about purple eyes, and Wulin sat cross-legged with the Mysterious Heaven Method turning, recovering for the teammates who had nearly lost this chance for his sake.

"Begin!"
