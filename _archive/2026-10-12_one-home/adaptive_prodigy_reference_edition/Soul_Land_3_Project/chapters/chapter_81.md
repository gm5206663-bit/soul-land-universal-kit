## Part 1: The Verdict

Clone skills were not, in themselves, anything Shrek hadn't seen — agility-types produced them like weather. Three clones was impressive and no more. Six would have been a different story, especially with half of them invisible.

Thirty seconds, and the three became one sweating Xie Xie, who bowed three times. "I'm finished with my performance."

The old lady looked at him with interest. "Kid, your twin martial souls are similar to one another, correct?"

"...Yes, that's right." Not surprised — this was Shrek Academy, after all.

"Not bad, but you still lack control. You've only scratched the surface of the Tang Sect's Ghost Shadow Perplexing Track. Don't rush your progress and don't bite off more than you can chew — refine the Track before you give all of your clones the same abilities. And your spiritual power is too weak at the moment. Complete control of three clones at once is impossible for you. Strengthen it first. If you can reach the Spirit Sea realm within three years, your future will be boundless."

Xie Xie had been rather proud of himself, as a general rule and specifically now. He could not refute one word of it. Every nail, squarely hit. He bowed again, head nearly below his knees, admiration welling up from the bottom of his heart. "Thank you for your guidance. I will do my best to improve myself."

"Mn." The old lady nodded. "Next."

He came back to the chairs with his shoulders around his ears and his face bitter, and the next one up was Xiaoyan, coached like the rest of them.

"What's wrong with you? Are you depressed?" Wulin asked softly.

"That's right! I thought I was pretty amazing, and I don't think I did too well."

"It's the opposite," Wulin said, calm as a Tuesday. "I bet you scored quite highly. That grandmother is clearly the central figure of the three. If she thought you were rotten wood, would she have given you so much advice? Besides — we're students. We came to Shrek to *learn.* What would be the point of coming if we already knew everything?"

Xie Xie opened his mouth. Closed it. The arithmetic reorganized itself in his face. *I'm not horrible. She was telling me how to get better.* Wulin patted his leg. "Let's watch."

## Part 2: Words

Xiaoyan stood in the center and bowed. "You may start," the old lady said.

"My sincerest apologies to the three examiners." Xiaoyan took a breath, and put her coach's plan through its steps. "I am unable to show you my specialty, because it is still daytime. My martial soul is different at night. From a certain perspective, I can be considered a *monster* of a soul master. That is why I have come to Shrek."

Moving examiners with words alone is not an easy task. Wulin knew that — which is exactly why the instruction had been to lead with the uniqueness. *Monster* was Shrek's motto; the word was a key cut for this lock specifically.

"Oh?" It was the examiner on the old lady's left. "Then how are you different at night?"

Xiaoyan summoned the ice staff — two yellow rings rising. "During the day, my martial soul is just the Ice Staff, which excels at controlling the battlefield and long-ranged attacks. At night, I gain the power of starlight, and my martial soul transforms into the Starwheel Ice Staff, changing my soul skills."

The spark in the old lady's eyes was instant. "I know of the Starwheel Ice Staff. It came from the Xu family long ago — though not many of them had it. Your surname is Xu?"

"That's right!" Xiaoyan blinked. "You know about my family?"

The old lady smiled calmly. "There are very few things in the world of soul masters that Shrek doesn't know about. You can go back now."

"But I'm not done explaining—"

"Since I know your origins, why wouldn't I know about the absolute control of your starlight soul skills? Go on back to your friends."

"...Oh." She bowed again and went, and Wulin was standing to meet her. "You did great. Relax." And the tension went out of her the way air leaves a tire that has been given permission.

## Part 3: My Speciality Is Power

Wulin walked past her to the center of the room.

The old lady inspected him curiously. "You seem quite confident!"

"Huh?" It caught him off guard — and then he understood, and bowed. "Greetings to the three examiners."

She smiled strangely. "They seem to trust you. Show me what you can do. Show me *why they have so much faith in you.*"

"Yes." No explanations. He understood now that the three of them had likely heard every word he had whispered into Xie Xie's and Xiaoyan's ears — truly formidable, worthy of Shrek. There was nothing to do about it but be worth the whispering.

"My speciality," he said, "is power."

Golden light flashed in his eyes, and his blood essence erupted outward. In a world of his own he could hear his blood moving in his veins like a river in flood; his right arm swelled, golden scales sheathing it — and then a golden soul ring rose into the air above him.

"Ah!" Shock wiped the smile off the old lady's face; her eyes went wide at the resplendent thing. Beside her, the two examiners gaped with her.

Every color a soul ring could be had been settled for ten thousand years. White, ten years. Yellow, hundred. Purple, thousand. Black, ten-thousand. Red, hundred-thousand. Those five, and no others — except that across the whole history of the Douluo Continent, rings of *special* colors had appeared, and their appearance signified immense power.

In the history of Shrek Academy, a golden soul ring had appeared exactly once.

Tang San — of the original Shrek Seven Monsters, founder of the Tang Sect — had killed a million-year soul beast and taken a golden ring from it. Million-year rings had appeared only twice in the continent's history: the second had belonged to the legend the plaza was named for, the founder of the Spirit Pagoda itself — Spirit Ice Douluo, Huo Yuhao. His had been white, disguisable as a ten-year ring — fused, the analysts of later generations said, with the million-year Skydream Iceworm that had slept its way to the million-year level, where Tang San's had been taken directly from the beast, and that was the supposed reason for the difference in color. The Spirit Ice Douluo's ring had become very powerful indeed.

It had still not been on par with Tang San's gold.

The hall stood in that history, with a thirteen-year-old at the center of it and gold turning over his head, and the silence had weight, and the old lady's stick had stopped tapping altogether.

## Part 4: The Word

At the chairs, the class did the only thing available to people watching their brother make history on four hours' sleep and no breakfast: they held on to each other's shoulders and said nothing that would distract him.

Which left the whispering to Xie Xie, who turned to the last of them with his eyebrows already complaining. "What are you going to show? The sword? The iron? They've got three pages on you and this lot reads pages for sport—"

"One thing," Lin Hao said, quietly, which in that hall was a different kind of silence. "Directly related to my strength."

"That's not an *answer,* that's the *rules—*"

"Comprehensive."

The word sat there. Xiaoyan looked at Xie Xie. Xie Xie looked at Gu Yue.

"There's no such System," Xiaoyan said, gently, the way one breaks bad news to the brilliant.

"There is," said Wulin — flat, immediate, brother-certain, not looking up from the gold.

And Gu Yue, who of all the people in the world had the best reason to know what it was when somebody wrote a word in a blank line that had no square — *Elementalist* was on a form somewhere, and had been true ever since — looked at Lin Hao with her cold clear eyes and said, flat as a ruled line: "You wrote it in a blank line."

"Eleven seconds," he said. "The clerk said *right.*"

Up front, the golden ring turned, and the old lady's hoarse voice came down the room without her looking away from it. "Enough, children. Next."

Which meant Gu Yue. She rose, and put a hand on Lin Hao's shoulder for exactly as long as it took to be real and no longer, and went up to make her own kind of noise.

And the hall that hears everything had heard the word.
