# Chapter 21 - The Meaning of a Plus Sign

The metal walls moved like they had all the time in the world.

Lan Xuanyu planted his feet, spread his arms, and pressed both palms against the cold surface. The apparatus hummed. The teacher's warning still rang in his ears: no Soul Power, no strengthening through soul skills, only the strength of the body itself.

That should have made the test simple.

It did not.

The walls did not attack. They did not roar like Lava Hounds, did not breathe fire, did not bare teeth. They simply closed in, little by little, patient and exact.

Pressure entered through Lan Xuanyu's palms.

At first, it was not much. He could hold it. His arms and legs formed four braces, and his waist tightened to keep his body from folding. The stance was copied from Lu Qianxun, who had already held the apparatus for two minutes and thirty-two seconds. Lan Xuanyu had watched the Golden Lion King boy use the whole body rather than only arms, and now he understood why.

The walls pressed harder.

Ten seconds.

A slight increase.

Another ten seconds.

Another increase.

The test did not punish impatience with pain at once. It punished it by making every bad angle worse.

Lan Xuanyu adjusted his knees.

He did not use Soul Power.

He did not need to.

Something in his blood stirred instead.

The feeling was familiar and not familiar at the same time. He had felt it when the Lava Hounds came for him. He had felt it when heat and danger closed around his body and his hands moved before he had time to think properly. It was not the cool flow of water, nor the clean bite of ice. It was deeper, heavier, as if his bones remembered a shape the rest of him did not yet know.

The walls pressed harder.

His skin warmed.

His breathing grew rough.

One minute passed.

The teacher conducting the test leaned forward slightly.

Lan Xuanyu was registered as a control-type Soul Master. A control-type child with Rank 14 Soul Power was not supposed to look comfortable in a pure strength test. Yet the walls still had not closed.

Around the apparatus, whispers began to rise.

Yan Shuo stood with Dorm 336 and watched without blinking.

He had seen Lan Xuanyu do impossible things in public before. Water turning to ice. Small hands with strange strength. A calm expression in situations where other children would rush or shout. He knew his friend was not ordinary.

Knowing did not make the sight smaller.

No Soul Power.

No ring.

No Blue Silver Grass.

Just Lan Xuanyu's body holding the metal apart.

Yan Shuo's own shoulders tightened in sympathy. The month of close-body armor and mecha-interface training had taught him something unpleasant: strength was not simply how hard one pushed. It was whether knees, waist, spine, shoulders, and breath agreed on the same answer.

Lan Xuanyu's answer was strange.

Not perfect.

Strange.

As if a quiet thing inside him became louder when pressure tried to fold him.

Yan Shuo did not know what it was.

He did not try to name it.

Teacher Ji's lessons had done one good thing at least: they had made Yan Shuo more careful with names.

Two minutes.

A few classmates gasped.

Lu Qianxun's gaze sharpened.

Ye Lingtong's fingers curled at her side. Her own result, one minute and seventeen seconds, had already been the best among the girls. She knew better than most what the metal walls felt like after the first easy stretch ended.

Qian Lei stared with his mouth slightly open.

"Is he human?" he whispered.

Liu Feng did not answer. His eyes stayed fixed on Lan Xuanyu's trembling arms.

Lan Xuanyu was not relaxed now. Sweat slid down his forehead. The walls had begun to move inward, and his elbows bent by a fraction he could not fully recover. The pressure rose again. Something in his chest surged in response.

His blood felt hot.

Not painful.

Hot.

His eyes brightened.

He clenched his teeth and pushed.

The walls stopped.

Then, impossibly, they moved outward.

Not far at first. A finger-width. Then more. The apparatus hummed sharply as if the machine itself had not expected resistance to become reversal.

The walls that had been closing in returned toward their original position.

"He pushed them back?" Luo Haoran blurted.

Song Yichen's usually steady face changed. Only a little, but enough for Yan Shuo to see it.

Yan Shuo let out a breath he had not realized he was holding.

Pushing back after the pressure had already risen was different from holding. Every frame lesson of the past month told him that. When weight had momentum, correcting it cost more than stopping it early.

Lan Xuanyu's body shook.

But he did not quit.

The teacher's expression had gone from professional to openly pleased.

Three minutes.

The whispers stopped being whispers.

Four minutes.

"Alright, stop."

The teacher pressed the control button.

The walls froze.

Lan Xuanyu's arms dropped the instant the pressure disappeared. His knees softened, and he almost fell before catching himself with one hand. His whole body looked suddenly limp, as if the strength that had filled him had drained back into a place no instrument could see.

The teacher opened the apparatus and helped him out.

"Not bad," the teacher said, and there was real approval in his voice. "Very good. You are physically strong, but more importantly, tenacious. That is an exceptional trait. Continue to work hard, but remember not to overexert yourself. You must leave yourself some room, understood?"

Lan Xuanyu bowed. "Understood. Thank you, Teacher."

The teacher wrote on his assessment card and handed it over.

Lan Xuanyu looked down.

Strength test: ten points.

Beside the ten was a plus sign.

He did not understand the meaning immediately.

The class did.

Or at least, some of them understood enough to stare harder.

Yan Shuo understood only part of it. A full score was obvious. The plus sign was not. It looked too small to make the air around the card change as much as it did.

Qian Lei leaned sideways until he could see. His eyes widened. "A plus?"

Liu Feng's voice was low. "Willpower."

Lan Xuanyu rubbed his arms, still recovering. "What does it mean?"

"It means," Qian Lei said solemnly, "that you are becoming more and more unreasonable."

The teacher called for the next student before Lan Xuanyu could respond.

The test continued.

It did not become less important just because one result had shocked everyone. If anything, Lan Xuanyu's four minutes made the rest of the class more aware of the machines waiting in line.

Strength.

Speed.

Spiritual Power.

Soul Power.

Four simple columns.

Four places where a month's sweat could become a number.

Yan Shuo stepped into the strength apparatus later with Lan Xuanyu's result still sitting in the room like a challenge no one had asked him to accept.

The teacher repeated the rule.

"No Soul Power. No skill. Pure bodily strength."

"Understood," Yan Shuo said.

The walls began to move.

His first instinct was wrong.

It wanted heat.

His shoulders wanted to open. His back wanted the familiar feathered answer of Dawnfeather Mantle, copper-red pressure and pale-gold edges spreading to catch force before it reached the joints.

Not allowed.

The test did not ask what his first ring could do.

It asked what the body beneath it had become.

Yan Shuo breathed out.

The month in the half-frame rig returned to him. Knees did not lock. Waist did not twist too early. Shoulders stayed connected to the spine instead of trying to flare. Heat rose beneath his skin and stopped there, unused, like a bird folding its wings because the room was too narrow.

No pale-gold motes appeared.

No white-gold feather edges formed.

That was also control.

The walls pressed harder.

Yan Shuo held.

He was stronger than he had been a month ago.

No—that was too small a way to say it.

The month had revealed what had been building since before awakening: a body that corrected around pressure, a Fire Phoenix foundation that made heat and bone answer each other, a living soul spirit that did not sit outside him like a borrowed ring, and an adaptation talent that worked in sleep, hunger, soreness, fear, and repetition. Dawnfeather Mantle was not active; that was the rule. But the ring was not somewhere outside him waiting to be switched on. The absorption had already changed the road his soul power used, the way his shoulders held recoil, the way heat settled along bone, the way breath found a stronger shape before thought caught up. Morning practice mattered. Meal plans mattered. Frame corrections mattered. Song's shield timing mattered. Luo nearly crossing the wrong lane and then not crossing it the next time mattered. But none of those things started from an empty child.

They landed on a foundation that had been growing for years.

The walls pressed harder.

His arms trembled after the second minute, but the tremble did not break him. It traveled into his back, then into his waist, then down through legs that had learned how not to waste force.

He wanted to push back.

Pride suggested it.

Lan Xuanyu had done it.

Yan Shuo's jaw tightened.

This was not Lan Xuanyu's body.

This was not his friend's answer.

His answer was steadier and hotter, not as miraculous at the peak but far less narrow. He adjusted his breath, let the old Fire Phoenix warmth settle into structure rather than flare, and held until the apparatus forced him to give ground one finger-width at a time.

The teacher stopped the apparatus.

"Three minutes and six seconds," the teacher announced. His pen paused for once. "Excellent whole-body structure. You did not use Soul Power. Strength score: ten."

Ten.

Not because the Mantle had helped him.

Because the body that had absorbed it was no longer an ordinary one-ring body.

The plus sign was not written at that station yet, but the teacher's pause said the result had weight.

Yan Shuo stepped out with burning arms and a calm he had to choose deliberately.

It was an excellent result.

It was not Lan Xuanyu's miracle of forcing the walls back.

Both facts were true.

Song met his eyes and nodded once.

Luo whispered, "If you had pushed back too, I was going to pretend I did not know either of you."

Yan Shuo flexed his fingers. "You already pretend that when homework is assigned."

"That is survival."

The speed test came next in batches.

Soul Power was still forbidden. No Mantle, no wind of flame behind the heel, no white-gold edge trimming a turn. Just body, balance, and reaction.

Liu Feng's run cut through the test like a drawn line.

He was thin, sharp, and direct. When he moved, there was no extra gesture. His White Dragon Spear was not in his hand, but the habit of it lived in his spine. Every turn looked as if he had already stabbed the air before arriving.

The teacher's pen paused after Liu Feng finished.

A plus sign went beside his speed score.

This time, no one looked surprised that Liu Feng was fast.

They looked surprised at how cleanly the teacher acknowledged it.

Qian Lei clapped twice before remembering he had no reason to be smug about physical tests.

Liu Feng returned with a face that pretended not to care and ears that betrayed him.

Yan Shuo ran later.

He was not Liu Feng. He was not a pure speed-type. That did not mean slow. Speed was not only a martial-soul label; it was ankle strength, balance, reaction, fear control, line choice, waist recovery, and whether the body wasted half a breath correcting a mistake. The month of frame training had sharpened those things, and the years before it had given them somewhere to root.

He kept the line tight.

He lost to Liu Feng on the straight sections.

He gained back distance in the turns because his shoulders no longer betrayed his feet.

Speed score: nine and a half.

No plus.

Fast.

Useful.

Not Liu Feng's kind of rare, but far beyond ordinary.

Luo's run came after, all flicker and narrow-footed correction. He nearly overreached at the third marker, caught himself, and finished with a grin that said he had survived both the test and his own personality.

Speed score: eight and a half.

Still no plus.

Liu Feng's remained the fastest.

Luo accepted this with theatrical suffering.

"I have been oppressed by spear-speed," he whispered.

Yan Shuo did not know what that meant. "By what?"

"By reality," Luo corrected instantly.

"Good. Keep your complaints in reality."

Song's speed result was lower, as everyone expected. The Stoneplate Tortoise route did not become quick because a month passed. But his steps were less heavy than before, and the teacher marked the improvement without mockery.

That mattered too.

Then came Soul Power and Spiritual Power.

Those were the tests that turned the month's hidden work into harder facts.

Lan Xuanyu's Soul Power result was the worst part of his morning. Rank 14 held. That alone let him breathe easier, because the missing rank had returned before any teacher could notice the old drop. But in the scoring of the class, Rank 14 was low.

Soul Power score: four and a half.

The lowest in class.

Lan Xuanyu accepted the card without changing expression.

Qian Lei winced on his behalf.

Liu Feng's mouth tightened.

Yan Shuo, watching from another line, saw only the public surface: Lan Xuanyu's Soul Power score was low and his expression did not move.

He did not know why Rank 14 mattered more today than it should have.

He did not know there had been a Rank 13 shadow behind it.

He only knew that Lan Xuanyu had looked relieved for half a breath before becoming quiet again.

That was enough to notice.

Not enough to ask.

Yan Shuo placed his hand on the Soul Power testing plate when his turn came.

The instrument hummed.

His yellow ring answered only as a measured resonance, not a skill. Copper-red warmth moved beneath the skin. The first ring's public color remained ordinary yellow. No one tested its deeper living age. No one updated the original paperwork. No device in this station asked how a perfectly compatible Dawnflame Kite had changed under pain, pressure, and training.

But the change was there. It was in the density of the warmth that returned after each breath, in the way the soul power did not splash wildly through his meridians, in the cleaner pull between Fire Phoenix and Dawnflame Kite. Rank measured height. It did not fully explain weight.

This machine asked one thing.

Soul Power.

The number settled.

Rank 19.

For a moment, Yan Shuo did not move.

He had felt close. Of course he had felt close. The month had not been empty. His sleep had been heavier. His appetite had grown. His breath after training had changed. The half-frame rig that had pulled him crooked at first now punished smaller mistakes. His body had been telling him something.

Still, seeing the formal number made his chest tighten.

Rank 19.

One step from the second-ring threshold.

Not at it.

Not over it.

One step.

The teacher wrote without drama, but the mark he wrote was not small. "Rank 19, dense circulation, stable resonance. Soul Power score: ten. Continue to consolidate. Do not rush the next bottleneck."

"Yes, Teacher."

Yan Shuo stepped back.

Luo's eyes were wide. Song's expression softened into the kind of approval that did not need words.

"Rank 19," Luo whispered. "You are going to become insufferable."

"Going to?" Song asked.

Yan Shuo glared at both of them.

The glare failed because he was smiling.

Then the Spiritual Power test came.

For most students, this station was a reminder that fifty points was already the Spirit Connection threshold and that the world above one hundred belonged to monsters, geniuses, or people who made teachers look at files twice.

Lan Xuanyu's updated number was one hundred and thirty-seven.

Qian Lei's was one hundred and fifty-two.

The room changed again.

Everyone had known Qian Lei's Spiritual Power was high. He had said so often enough that even people trying to ignore him had failed. Everyone had known Lan Xuanyu's previous public value, ninety-nine, was already shocking for his age.

One hundred and thirty-seven made ninety-nine look like a door he had walked through without telling anyone how far the corridor went.

One hundred and fifty-two made Qian Lei look ridiculous in a different way from usual.

"Ten points and a plus sign," the teacher marked for Lan Xuanyu's Spiritual Power.

Qian Lei received the same score and plus sign at that station.

He tried to stand with dignity afterward.

His earlier strength result of seventeen seconds ruined the effect.

Yan Shuo laughed under his breath, then stopped when his own name was called.

The Spiritual Power instrument was colder than the Soul Power plate.

He placed his hand where instructed, closed his eyes, and let his mind settle.

Do not chase Lan Xuanyu.

Do not chase Qian Lei.

The thought came so clearly that he almost smiled.

A month ago, maybe he would have tried to force his mind brighter, as if Spiritual Power were a flame one could flare for a better number. Now he knew better. A forced edge blurred. A clean edge held.

Copper warmth settled below thought.

Pale-gold awareness traced the boundary of breath and pressure, not as an outward light, but as the familiar internal steadiness that had begun with Dawnflame Kite and become part of how he endured strain.

The instrument finished.

Eighty-eight.

Spiritual Power: eighty-eight points.

Still Spirit Connection Realm.

Still far below Lan Xuanyu and Qian Lei.

But among children who did not live in that ridiculous spiritual-power peak, it was a number that made the teacher look twice.

The teacher looked at the screen, then at him. "Excellent growth. Stable foundation. Spiritual Power score: nine and a half."

Excellent growth.

Not a miracle.

Not a plus sign.

Foundation made visible.

Yan Shuo's fingers curled once and relaxed.

That word was better than he expected.

Song crossed the threshold too.

His Spiritual Power, once forty-eight, now read fifty-five. Spirit Connection Realm. The number made him blink twice, which for Song Yichen was nearly a public celebration.

Luo's result read sixty-six.

He looked smug for three seconds before Song reminded him that smugness did not increase score.

"It should," Luo said. "Mine is very refined."

"Your smugness leaks outside the lane," Yan Shuo said.

Luo pointed at him. "You are using my own training against me."

"Team education," Song said.

The three of them laughed quietly before the next student was called.

It was not the kind of scene that would make the whole class explode into discussion.

That was exactly why it mattered.

Dorm 336 had grown in ways that fit them.

Yan Shuo: Rank 19, Spiritual Power 88, first raw total, one plus sign for rare integrated foundation, and still not at the second-ring line yet.

Song: Rank 18, Spiritual Power 55, newly steady across the Spirit Connection threshold.

Luo: Rank 16, Spiritual Power 66, sharper in perception and speed without becoming Liu Feng.

One plus sign did not make the whole dorm safe.

No secret technique.

Just a month of work becoming numbers.

Yan Shuo folded his assessment card and placed it carefully in his pocket.

The paper felt heavier than it should have.

After the physical data tests ended, Mu Zhongtian gathered the class.

By then, most students looked as if the morning had wrung them dry and then asked them to sit politely while being labeled.

Mu Zhongtian held the result sheet.

"Physical fitness test results," he began.

The room quieted.

"First place, Yan Shuo, thirty-nine points, and a plus sign."

The room changed shape around the words.

Yan Shuo's team had not merely become not weak. That description was no longer honest. Strength ten, speed nine and a half, Soul Power ten, Spiritual Power nine and a half: the card lay near the top in every column, and the plus sign did not come from one flashy skill. It came from the rare foundation the teachers could no longer pretend was just good balance—Fire Phoenix body, dense circulation, perfect Dawnflame Kite compatibility, Spirit Connection control, and a growth response that kept turning pressure into usable structure.

Song's Spirit Connection breakthrough drew quieter surprise from students who had thought of him only as a shield; Luo's speed and Spiritual Power growth made him lift his chin until Song nudged his elbow. Dorm 336's total had real weight now, and Yan Shuo's monitor title no longer looked like school politeness pinned to a uniform.

Mu Zhongtian continued.

"Second place, Lu Qianxun, thirty-eight and a half points, and a plus sign."

Lu Qianxun's expression did not change much, but the students near him straightened anyway. Second did not make the Golden Lion King weak. His group had been first at the beginning of the term. He had clearly not relaxed. But the half-point gap was real, and so was the pressure it placed between the class's strongest team block and the boy standing at Dorm 336's center.

Then Mu Zhongtian's voice shifted.

"Thirteenth place, Lan Xuanyu, thirty-one and a half points, and two plus signs."

Many heads turned.

Lan Xuanyu looked up.

Mu Zhongtian glanced at him intentionally. "Lan Xuanyu is also the only student who received two plus signs. He was recognized for strength and Spiritual Power."

The air became noisy again.

Thirteenth place was not first.

Thirty-one and a half points did not beat Yan Shuo.

Thirty-one and a half points did not beat Lu Qianxun either.

It did not even place Lan Xuanyu near the top of the raw total.

But two plus signs.

Yan Shuo looked at Lan Xuanyu, then at his own pocket where the card rested.

Rank 19.

Spiritual Power 88.

Thirty-nine points.

One plus.

He did not feel safe.

He felt the academy's meaning press against him in a new way.

Scores measured where a student stood.

Plus signs marked what a teacher believed might matter later.

Those were not the same thing.

Mu Zhongtian read on.

"Twenty-ninth place, Liu Feng, twenty-six and a half points, and a plus sign."

Liu Feng lowered his head slightly.

The plus sign could not erase being near the bottom, but it changed the shape of the result. Speed had been seen. Speed had been valued.

"Thirtieth place, Qian Lei, twenty-five points, and a plus sign."

Qian Lei's face went through three expressions in two breaths: shame, protest, and relief that the plus sign existed at all.

A few students laughed.

Mu Zhongtian did not.

He looked across the room.

"The total points of Dorm 333 are the lowest in class."

Qian Lei shrank.

Liu Feng's jaw tightened.

Lan Xuanyu stayed still.

Mu Zhongtian continued, "However, for this physical test, the group with the most plus signs is Dorm 333, with a total of four plus signs. According to the academy rule, the group with the most plus signs will not be eliminated at the end of this term. Let us congratulate them."

For a heartbeat, no one spoke.

Then the room erupted.

Dorm 333.

The group everyone had thought most likely to be eliminated.

The group with the lowest total score.

Protected.

Qian Lei stared as if someone had opened a treasure chest in front of him and told him it had always belonged to his bed.

Liu Feng looked stunned despite himself.

Lan Xuanyu blinked, then seemed to understand the rule's weight all at once.

Yan Shuo heard Luo inhale beside him.

"Four plus signs," Luo muttered. "Their total is lower than ours, but they are safer than us. I am offended by arithmetic."

Song said quietly, "It is not arithmetic. It is evaluation."

Yan Shuo nodded.

He did not like every feeling in his chest, but he understood the lesson.

Lan Xuanyu's team had almost collapsed in ordinary totals. Yet each of them had a rare edge the academy refused to ignore. Lan Xuanyu's strength and Spiritual Power. Qian Lei's Spiritual Power. Liu Feng's speed.

Four points of future weight.

Teacher Ji would probably have called that meaning.

Not decoration.

Jin Xiang stood up.

His face was flushed, and anger gave him back the volume Teacher Ji had stolen the day before.

"Teacher, this is not fair! Their group has the lowest points in overall strength. Why should they get such a privilege because they are slightly better in some aspects?"

The class quieted enough to listen.

It was not only Jin Xiang who felt it. Yan Shuo could sense that. Teams that had scored higher now had no protection, while Dorm 333 had escaped the elimination shadow through special marks. It was easy to call unfair when a rule hurt after one finally understood it.

Mu Zhongtian's expression remained calm.

"This is the academy's decision. If you are dissatisfied, you may report it to the school through proper channels. Also, this afternoon's actual combat test remains. The top team in actual combat will also not be eliminated this term. If you want proof of strength, defeat them there."

Jin Xiang's mouth closed.

Not because he agreed.

Because that answer left him somewhere to put his anger.

Actual combat.

Mu Zhongtian ended class and told them to rest.

The moment he turned away, Qian Lei exploded.

"We got ashore! Wahahaha!"

The laugh lasted until Liu Feng nudged him and Qian Lei noticed the number of unfriendly stares aimed at Dorm 333.

His laughter died with impressive speed.

Lan Xuanyu grabbed both roommates. "Let's go eat."

"Yes," Qian Lei said immediately. "Strategic retreat to the canteen."

"You mean hiding," Liu Feng said.

"Tactical hiding."

Yan Shuo watched them leave.

For a moment, he wanted to call out. Not to ask about secrets. Not to join them. Just to say something ordinary enough to remind Lan Xuanyu that a plus sign did not have to become another wall.

Lan Xuanyu glanced back first.

Their eyes met.

Yan Shuo lifted his assessment card slightly from his pocket, just enough for Lan Xuanyu to see he had one too, then tucked it away and grinned.

Lan Xuanyu's mouth curved.

Small.

Tired.

Real.

Then Dorm 333 left for the canteen.

Dorm 336 followed at a slower pace.

Luo was still offended by arithmetic. Song was explaining, with infuriating patience, that one plus sign protected a rare direction, not an entire team from bad choices. Yan Shuo walked between them, feeling the card in his pocket tap against his leg.

Rank 19.

Spiritual Power 88.

Thirty-nine raw points.

One plus sign.

He thought he would feel more triumphant about the last part.

Instead, he kept thinking about Lan Xuanyu's hands pushing metal walls back while his whole body trembled.

A plus sign, Yan Shuo decided, was not praise for being better than everyone.

It was a teacher saying: this trait might change a fight later.

He wondered what kind of trait would make a teacher put that mark beside fire.

The thought warmed him.

He folded it away before it became pride.

The canteen was louder than it had been during Teacher Ji's lunch disaster.

Dorm 333 sat together. Qian Lei had recovered enough to brag again, though the hostile air around them kept his volume slightly more survivable than usual.

"With four plus signs," Qian Lei said, "we are number one in a certain sense. Correct, Xuanyu?"

Lan Xuanyu's cheeks were already full of food.

He gave Qian Lei a look that said he had opinions but no available mouth.

Qian Lei pointed at him. "Can you stop for a moment? How did your parents raise you?"

Lan Xuanyu swallowed with difficulty. "If I am not full, how are we going to fight later?"

Qian Lei immediately changed sides. "Correct. Please eat more. Eat for the team."

Liu Feng looked disgusted. "You have no principles."

"I have flexible principles."

Yan Shuo sat with Dorm 336 nearby. Close enough to hear public foolishness. Far enough not to become part of Dorm 333's strategy.

Ye Lingtong entered the canteen.

She walked toward Lan Xuanyu's table.

The chatter around that area thinned, because even students pretending not to watch wanted to know what she would say. Ye Lingtong and Lan Xuanyu had too many old tensions for a simple conversation to look simple.

She knocked on the table.

"Lan Xuanyu."

Lan Xuanyu looked up with both cheeks full.

Ye Lingtong stared at him for half a breath, then laughed. "Why do you look so foolish?"

Lan Xuanyu swallowed, indignant. "Who are you calling foolish?"

Her expression shifted back to seriousness. "Don't ignore kind advice. If you cannot beat your opponents this afternoon, just concede. Your team is already safe from elimination anyway. I overheard Jin Xiang and the others discussing how to humiliate you after winning."

After saying that, she turned and left.

Qian Lei stared after her. "That is called kindhearted? She clearly thinks we will be humiliated. Jin Xiang is truly abominable."

Liu Feng snorted. "Then we will let them see who is strongest. The problem is that we still do not know how the test will be run."

Lan Xuanyu lowered his eyes to the food again, but Yan Shuo could see the thinking settle over him.

Not panic.

Thinking.

Yan Shuo looked away before his attention became rude.

Luo leaned closer over Dorm 336's table. "If Jin Xiang is planning humiliation, what are we planning?"

"Passing," Song said.

Luo sighed. "Your dreams are shaped like furniture."

"Furniture supports people."

Yan Shuo nearly choked on soup.

Song looked at him. "What?"

"Nothing. I am respecting structural stability."

Luo pointed his chopsticks at both of them. "This is why our team has only one plus sign. The teachers fear our conversation."

Yan Shuo tapped the table lightly. "Our team has growth. That is enough for morning. Afternoon is different."

The words settled them.

Afternoon was different.

The physical data test had measured children in pieces: strength, speed, Spiritual Power, Soul Power. The afternoon would put teams back together and ask whether pieces could survive movement, fear, timing, and other people.

Yan Shuo's Rank 19 would matter.

So would Song's new Spirit Connection steadiness.

So would Luo's sharper reaction.

None of that guaranteed anything.

That was the point of a fight.

When the Elite Junior Class gathered in the simulation pod room, the mood had changed completely.

Thirty students stood beside their pods. The morning's numbers still clung to them, but now the numbers had to become choices.

Mu Zhongtian stood at the center.

His gaze swept across the class and paused briefly on Lan Xuanyu.

The last time a major simulation had gone wrong, Lan Xuanyu had not woken immediately.

Everyone knew some version of that.

Yan Shuo knew the public version well enough: mental attack, coma, hospital, reserved place, recovery. He did not know the hidden depth. He did not need to know it to understand why Mu Zhongtian's eyes looked heavier in the pod room.

"The following test is your actual combat assessment," Mu Zhongtian said. "The scenario is called the Maze. Your pods are arranged by serial number. Each team of three will be teleported to the same location. Within the maze, you may encounter all sorts of attacks, and you may encounter other teams. Your task is simple: find other teams and eliminate them. The earlier a team is eliminated, the lower its score."

Some students exchanged glances.

A royal rumble.

Not a clean bracket.

Not a polite duel.

Mu Zhongtian continued, "Teachers will observe the entire process. Outstanding acts in battle will receive corresponding points. The final winning team will receive an additional ten points and will also be exempt from elimination this term. Understood?"

"Understood!"

The answer was loud, but not confident in the same way from every mouth.

Yan Shuo looked toward Song and Luo.

Song's face had become calm in the way it did before he expected impact.

Luo rolled his shoulders, nervous energy sharpening rather than scattering.

Yan Shuo felt copper warmth answer in his chest.

This time, skills would be allowed.

This time, the Mantle might open.

If it did, the flame would not be wide for the sake of being wide. The month had taught him better. Pale-gold motes, white-gold feather edges, clean lanes, no wasted wash over allies.

Rank 19 did not mean burn more.

It meant he had one more reason to spend carefully.

"We do not chase the first sound," Song said quietly.

Luo opened his mouth.

Song looked at him.

Luo closed it. "I was going to agree. Dramatically."

Yan Shuo smiled. "Agree quietly. Save the drama for the enemy."

Across the room, Dorm 333 stood together.

Qian Lei looked both excited and terrified. Liu Feng's fingers twitched as if already holding his spear. Lan Xuanyu's expression was thoughtful, and that made him look calmer than he probably was.

Dorm 333 had protection now.

They also had more enemies because of it.

Dorm 336 had one layer of protection now, and only because Yan Shuo's foundation had finally become impossible to file as ordinary balance.

That did not protect Song or Luo by itself. It did not win the Maze. It did not turn one plus sign into four.

Different pressure.

Different shape.

The pod lids opened.

Students climbed in.

The gelatinous liquid rose around Yan Shuo's body, familiar now and still unpleasant. The lid closed. Darkness covered his vision for a breath.

Then light came from all directions.

Douluo World accepted them.

Lan Xuanyu opened his eyes in a stone passage.

Liu Feng and Qian Lei stood beside him.

The walls rose around them, about five meters high, with a ceiling overhead and small lamps set along the stone. There were no side exits nearby. The corridor stretched forward into darkness, and behind them was a dead end.

Qian Lei looked around. "What do we do now?"

Liu Feng also looked to Lan Xuanyu.

The habit had formed without ceremony over the past month. When the situation was unclear, they looked to him. Not because he shouted loudest. Because his choices had kept making sense.

Lan Xuanyu thought for a moment.

"I do not know this Maze either," he said. "But since all teams are inside, we may have to fight many times. Soul Power is limited. If we want to win, we cannot waste strength on every fight we see. We should find a way to conceal ourselves and reduce unnecessary battles. Save strength for later."

Qian Lei nodded quickly. "Good. Hiding is one of my strongest skills."

Liu Feng gave him a sideways glance. "That is not something to be proud of."

Before Qian Lei could argue, a heavy rumbling came from the darkness ahead.

Bang.

Bang.

Bang.

A two-meter stone figure rolled into view, eyes glowing red, a huge battle axe clutched in its hands. In the narrow passage, it blocked the way completely.

Qian Lei swallowed. "It looks rather strong. Xuanyu, do you want me to summon?"

"No," Lan Xuanyu said immediately. "You are our secret weapon. Do not summon yet. Liu Feng, test its attack pattern. Do not commit. Let it show us how it fights. It should be here to harass us or slow us down, not necessarily to decide the whole test."

Liu Feng nodded.

White Dragon Spear appeared in his hands.

He rushed forward.

At the same moment, in another part of the Maze, Yan Shuo opened his eyes under the same dim stone lamps.

Song stood ahead of him.

Luo stood to the side, already scanning the walls.

The corridor before them forked into three paths.

No enemies were visible yet.

That did not make it safer.

Yan Shuo let his breath settle.

His yellow ring rose.

Copper-red flame gathered close, held narrow by pale-gold motes that ran along the first feather edges of the Mantle. White-gold lines touched his shoulders and upper back, warm but not bright enough to announce them down every corridor.

Small flame.

Clean edge.

Rank 19 did not change the rule.

Meaning, not decoration.

Somewhere far away, stone struck stone.

The Maze had begun.
